# **Prologue**

### **A Small Moment**

A team is in a meeting, and a mistake is on the table for everyone to
see. No one speaks first. Then one person says, "Let's move on," and the
room accepts it.

No policy changed, no speech was given, and no formal decision memo was
written. But something important happened: a boundary moved.

### **What Was Learned**

In that moment, people learned more than the immediate decision: they
learned what gets named and what gets buried, what costs can be passed
forward, and which discomforts are tolerated and which are not.

This is how leadership often operates: not through dramatic declarations,
but through repeated signals under attention.

### **The Same Pattern at Different Scales**

The pattern appears in small and large settings:

- A parent deciding whether to correct or excuse.
- A manager choosing whether to protect dissent or punish it.
- A congregation responding to a breach of trust.
- An institution deciding whether performance outranks accountability.

The scale changes. The structure does not.

### **Why Begin Here**

Many leadership conversations begin with identity:
Who is the leader, what kind of person are they, and what values do they
claim?

Those questions matter, but they are incomplete. This book begins with a
different question: what happens to everyone else when others look to this
person for direction?

The answer is rarely contained in one event.
It appears in patterns, and those patterns are the subject of this book.
