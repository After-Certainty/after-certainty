# **Chapter 9 — Exceptions are Forever**

Over time, the crack stops looking like damage. It becomes part of the
building.

A line that was once out of place begins to feel familiar. People work
around it. Adjust to it. Stop noticing it altogether.

The structure still stands. But it is no longer what it was.

The same thing happens in groups. A workaround is introduced. It makes
things easier in the moment. A step is skipped. A rule is bent. A
shortcut is taken to keep things moving.

It works.

The problem is resolved quickly. The pressure is reduced. Progress
continues.

Nothing breaks. So it happens again. The same situation comes up. The
same shortcut is used. No one questions it. It worked before. Over time,
something changes. What was temporary becomes expected. The workaround
is no longer seen as an exception. It becomes part of how things are
done.

**Exceptions are Forever.**

No decision is made to make it permanent. It just isn’t removed. This is
how exceptions persist. Not through intention. Through repetition. And
through the absence of correction.[^c9-temporary-becomes-permanent]

Once established, they are difficult to undo. Not because they are
necessary. Because they are familiar. People build around them.
Processes adjust. Expectations shift. New work assumes their existence.

Removing them now creates friction. It slows things down. It disrupts
what has formed around them. It requires coordination that no longer feels
urgent. So they remain. Like a crack that has been painted over, the
structure adapts.

What began as a break becomes something the group quietly reorganizes
around. Nothing collapses. But something has changed. The constraints that
once held things in place are weaker. The boundaries are less clear. The
structure is more difficult to reason about.

And this change accumulates. One exception becomes many. Each one small.
Each one justified. Together, they reshape how things operate.

> What was meant to be temporary outlasts its emergency when nothing removes it.

This is the final stage of erosion. Not failure. Not collapse.
Normalization. The group continues to function. But it is no longer
operating within the same boundaries. What happens next will build on
this. People are watching. What they see will be repeated.

[^c9-temporary-becomes-permanent]: Giorgio Agamben, *State of Exception*,
  trans. Kevin Attell (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2005), on how
  emergency and exception logic can normalize into standing practice.
