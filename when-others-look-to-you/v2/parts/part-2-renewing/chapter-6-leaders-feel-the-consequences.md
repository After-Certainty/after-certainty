# **Chapter 6 — Leaders Feel the Consequences**

The adjustment has been made. The sail has shifted. The course has
changed. The boat continues forward.

What matters now is not the decision itself. It is what happens next.

The wind pushes differently. The boat responds. The line holds—or it
doesn’t.

Someone notices.

Not from a distance. From where they are. They can feel it in the
tension of the rope. They can see it in the angle of the sail. They can
tell whether the change worked.

If it didn’t, they adjust again. The loop is short. Decision. Result.
Adjustment.

Nothing is separated. This is what keeps the boat aligned.

The same thing happens in groups.

A decision is made. Something changes. Work moves forward. A plan is
executed. A path is taken.

What matters next is whether the people who made the decision remain
close to what happens.

Do they see the result? Do they feel the impact? Do they hear what
changed? If they do, something stays intact.

**Leaders Feel the Consequences.**[^c6-proximity-feedback]

The loop remains short. A choice leads to a result. The result is
visible. The next choice reflects it.

Nothing drifts far from reality.

The people deciding are not relying only on reports. They are not
separated by layers. They are not shielded from what happens next.

They remain close enough to adjust. This does not require perfect
information. It requires proximity. Seeing what worked. Noticing what
didn’t. Recognizing what changed.

The connection does not need to be complete. It only needs to be
present. Without it, something else begins to happen.

The decision moves forward. But the result does not come back clearly.
It is filtered. Delayed. Softened.

The signal weakens.

At first, this is difficult to notice. The decision still appears to
work. Progress continues. Nothing fails immediately.

But the loop has changed. Decision. Delay. Partial signal. Something is
missing. The next decision is made without fully seeing the last.

This is where distance begins. Not in space. In feedback.

When **Leaders Feel the Consequences**, correction is continuous.
When they don’t, correction becomes delayed. The difference is small at
first. But it compounds. A small gap becomes a larger one. A delayed
signal becomes a missed one. Then a missed signal becomes a repeated
mistake.

The boat still moves. But it is no longer clear whether it is aligned.

> When decision and consequence stay in the same loop, drift cannot hide for long.

This is what completes the loop.

**Dissent is Welcomed.**

**Feedback Drives Change.**

**Leaders Feel the Consequences.**

Signal. Adjustment. Result.

When all three hold, the group remains responsive. When this one
weakens, something else begins to happen. The signal fades. The gap
grows. The first thing you notice is not failure. It’s that nothing is
said.

That quiet is often where **Disagreement is Suppressed** begins.

[^c6-proximity-feedback]: Karl E. Weick and Kathleen M. Sutcliffe, *Managing the
  Unexpected: Sustained Performance in a Complex World* (San Francisco:
  Jossey-Bass, 2001), on staying close to operations and weak signals rather than
  managing only from abstracted reports.
