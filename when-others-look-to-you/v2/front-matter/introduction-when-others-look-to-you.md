# **Introduction — When Others Look To You**


A leader is someone others look to when deciding what to do next.[^intro-working-definition]

That’s where leadership begins. Not with a title. Not with authority.
Not with responsibility. Leadership begins when **Attention Finds a
Focus**.

A question comes up, and the room slows until **Attention Finds a
Focus** on one person. What they do next matters more than it seems,
because **Examples Accumulate**. Others begin to adjust around it.
Over time, without anyone deciding it explicitly, **Leadership
Coalesces**. The group starts to rely on that more often.

If things are working well, **Dissent is Welcomed**, and problems show
up while they can still be handled. When that happens, **Feedback Drives
Change**. The group adjusts instead of just continuing. Things stay
grounded when **Leaders Feel the Consequences**. The people deciding
remain close to what actually happens next.

But this can shift. At some point, **Disagreement is Suppressed**.
Decisions move forward without being tested. As that continues,
**Learning Collapse** takes hold. What is really happening no longer reaches
the people making decisions. Small workarounds start to stick. What was meant
to be temporary doesn’t get undone. Before long, **Exceptions are
Forever**.

People notice all of this. They see what moves things forward and what
persists. Eventually **Leadership Reproduces Itself**, carrying it into
the next moment when **Attention Finds a Focus** again.

### **How the dynamics stack**

Those same dynamics also gather into four groups. **Appendix A** summarizes
the full catalog under **Forming**, **Renewing**, **Eroding**, and **Repeating**.
The diagram legend uses **Adjusting** instead of **Renewing** for the second
group; the **Renewing** part follows that territory. That is the loop that keeps
decisions tied to reality.

![Diagram: leadership pattern groups](export-assets/diagrams/pattern-groups.png)

This happens everywhere—in meetings, in conversations, in families, in
teams—whether anyone intends it or not.

Leadership is not something you step into once. It happens in moments. A
pause. A glance. A question that needs an answer. And in that moment,
what you do does not stay contained. It is seen. It is interpreted. It
is repeated.

This book is about those moments. It shows how leadership forms when
**Attention Finds a Focus**. It shows how leadership stays connected to
reality—or drifts away from it. It shows how what gets copied shapes what
comes next.

These dynamics are not prescriptions. They are not steps to follow. They
are observable conditions that appear again and again, from small groups
to large institutions.

You do not need to become a leader for them to apply. You only need to
notice when others look to you.

Because when they do, something has already started. What happens next
will not stay there. It will be learned. It will be repeated.

[^intro-working-definition]: Foundational framing for authority, legitimacy,

  Authority?" in *Between Past and Future* (New York: Viking Press, 1961).
