# **Appendix A — Legitimacy Transfer in Practice**

This appendix should illustrate legitimacy transfer with a contemporary
case sequence while preserving the manuscript's non-partisan, structural
posture.

Purpose:

- Apply the book's lens to visible public examples
- Show how legitimacy can expand, harden, or be constrained over time
- Demonstrate transfer dynamics without reducing analysis to personality

Required framing:

- This is an illustration of structure, not a ranking of leaders.
- The appendix does not substitute for the core argument in Chapters 4-10.
- Cases are used to clarify transfer mechanisms, not to produce partisan
  verdicts.

At minimum, include:

- Procedural legitimacy baseline
- A post-9/11 to modern-day presidential transition sequence as the core
  case arc
- Crisis-driven expansion dynamics in the post-9/11 period
- Restraint without full structural retraction in the following period
- Personalization and insulation risk in the next period
- Restoration attempts under structural inertia in the current period

Suggested sequence for the core illustration:

- George W. Bush (post-9/11 emergency expansion and precedent-setting)
- Barack Obama (restraint posture with substantial inherited authorities
  retained)
- Donald Trump (personalization and contestability stress under inherited
  authority)
- Joe Biden (procedural-restoration posture amid structural inertia)

Use this sequence to trace transfer dynamics, not to assign moral rankings
to individuals.

Cross-context note:

- Include at least one non-presidential contrast paragraph (for example,
  institutional, organizational, or civic context) to reinforce
  generalizability beyond one office.

Closing aim:

- Show how legitimacy transfer is easiest to expand and hardest to
  contract unless active constraints are renewed.
