# **Appendix B — Leadership Patterns**

This appendix summarizes recurring structural patterns described
throughout the book. Each pattern is presented as a compact diagnostic:
when it appears, what is happening, and what tends to follow.

These patterns are not prescriptions; they are observable conditions
that recur across domains, from small groups to large institutions.

### **Attention Gathering**

**Context:**

A group encounters uncertainty, ambiguity, or a moment requiring
direction.

**Problem:**

Coordination is required, but no explicit structure has yet defined who
will lead.

**Forces:**

- People seek orientation under uncertainty
- Silence creates pressure for someone to act
- Early signals carry disproportionate weight
- Leadership can emerge without intention

**Observation:**

Attention concentrates around a person, and others begin orienting their
behavior in response to that person's actions, tone, or timing.

**Effect:**

Influence multiplies immediately, and the person being looked to
becomes structurally significant whether or not they claim authority.

**Resulting Context:**

A reinforcing loop forms: attention leads to influence, influence
reinforces attention, and authority begins to consolidate.

**Related Patterns:**

- Tone Sets the Room
- The One We Default To

### **Tone Sets the Room**

**Context:**

A leader reacts to a mistake, tension, or ambiguity in a shared setting.

**Problem:**

Group members need to interpret what is acceptable, but explicit rules
are absent or incomplete.

**Forces:**

- People look for social cues under uncertainty
- Tone is faster and more influential than formal policy
- Early reactions shape perceived boundaries
- Silence and emphasis both carry meaning

**Observation:**

Others watch how the leader responds and use that response to interpret
what is acceptable.

**Effect:**

Norms form quickly without explicit agreement, and they shape how truth,
error, and risk are handled.

**Resulting Context:**

Behavior becomes patterned, and future situations are interpreted
through the established tone.

**Related Patterns:**

- Attention Gathering
- Bad News Travels

### **The One We Default To**

**Context:**

A group repeatedly turns to the same person when decisions are unclear.

**Problem:**

Decision-making responsibility is not formally assigned or consistently
distributed.

**Forces:**

- Repetition creates familiarity and trust
- Speed favors known decision paths
- Avoidance of ambiguity reinforces habit
- Informal authority can be easier than formal delegation

**Observation:**

The group defers to the same person automatically, even in situations
where no formal authority exists.

**Effect:**

Authority consolidates through repetition and becomes harder to
redistribute.

**Resulting Context:**

The person becomes the default decision-maker, and alternative
leadership pathways weaken.

**Related Patterns:**

- Attention Gathering
- Authority Becomes Untouchable

### **Someone Else Pays**

**Context:**

A decision creates cost, risk, or harm within a system.

**Problem:**

The location of decision-making and the location of consequence are not
aligned.

**Forces:**

- Incentives favor visible success over visible cost
- Systems distribute impact unevenly
- Those closest to harm often have less influence
- Harm is easier to ignore when indirect

**Observation:**

The cost of decisions is absorbed by people other than the
decision-maker.

**Effect:**

Accountability weakens, and harm becomes easier to normalize or repeat.

**Resulting Context:**

Costs are displaced across the system, reducing trust while increasing
the hidden accumulation of risk.

**Related Patterns:**

- Consequences Don't Come Back
- Authority Becomes Untouchable

### **Bad News Travels**

**Context:**

Problems, risks, or failures emerge within a group or system.

**Problem:**

Timely correction depends on whether negative information can reach
decision-makers.

**Forces:**

- Social and professional risk discourages speaking up
- Hierarchies filter or delay information
- Positive signals are easier to share than negative ones
- People adapt to what is safe to say

**Observation:**

In healthier systems, bad news moves upward and reaches authority in
time to influence decisions.

**Effect:**

Early correction remains possible, and the scale and cost of failure can
stay lower.

**Resulting Context:**

When this pattern weakens, signals are delayed or suppressed, and
problems grow before they are addressed.

**Related Patterns:**

- Tone Sets the Room
- Consequences Don't Come Back

### **Authority Becomes Untouchable**

**Context:**

A leader or leadership structure gains stability, success, or repeated
validation.

**Problem:**

Maintaining authority begins to compete with maintaining correctness.

**Forces:**

- Success increases confidence and reduces perceived need for challenge
- Social cost of dissent rises
- Loyalty signals are rewarded over correction signals
- Narratives stabilize around leadership identity

**Observation:**

Questioning becomes difficult, and dissent is reduced, softened, or
avoided.

**Effect:**

Correction pathways narrow, and the risk of hidden errors and delayed
failure increases.

**Resulting Context:**

Authority becomes insulated from feedback, and structural drift
accelerates.

**Related Patterns:**

- The One We Default To
- Consequences Don't Come Back

### **Consequences Don't Come Back**

**Context:**

Decisions are made at a distance from where their effects are
experienced.

**Problem:**

Feedback from real-world consequences does not reliably reach
decision-makers.

**Forces:**

- Scale increases distance from impact
- Reporting systems filter or delay signals
- Negative outcomes are costly to surface
- Success signals travel more easily than failure signals

**Observation:**

Those making decisions do not directly feel or receive the full impact
of the outcomes.

**Effect:**

Feedback loops weaken, and decisions drift away from reality while
appearing stable.

**Resulting Context:**

Errors repeat, harm accumulates, and correction becomes delayed and more
expensive.

**Related Patterns:**

- Someone Else Pays
- Bad News Travels

### **Temporary Becomes Permanent**

**Context:**

A group adopts emergency measures or exceptions during a crisis or
period of urgency.

**Problem:**

Actions taken for speed or survival are not automatically reversed when
conditions stabilize.

**Forces:**

- Urgency justifies bypassing constraints
- Short-term success reinforces the exception
- Reversal requires effort and coordination
- New practices quickly become normalized

**Observation:**

Practices introduced as temporary responses persist beyond the original
conditions.

**Effect:**

Exception logic becomes default structure, and deliberate re-evaluation
stops happening by default.

**Resulting Context:**

Authority expands, constraints weaken, and structural drift increases
over time.

**Related Patterns:**

- Authority Becomes Untouchable
- Consequences Don't Come Back
