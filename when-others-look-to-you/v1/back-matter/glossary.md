# **Glossary**

When a word appears in **bold** in the main text, it usually marks a term the
book is using in a precise, structural sense—[**renewal**](#renewal) and [**erosion**](#erosion),
[**vibrant group**](#vibrant-group) and [**decaying group**](#decaying-group), [**Circulation**](#circulation) and [**Correction**](#correction), [**harm**](#harm),
[**legitimacy**](#legitimacy), and others. This glossary collects those terms in
one place so you can find them without hunting backward through the chapters.

<a id="authority-circulation"></a><a id="circulation"></a>**Circulation.** Whether influence can move through temporary or shared leadership (including [**guest leadership**](#guest-leadership)) so that leadership can scale without fixing every role at the top—how leadership spreads and reproduces itself, including what **Leadership Reproduces Itself** tracks when people imitate what last worked. Not the same as [**Correction**](#correction): who may step in to lead is a different question than whether warnings and dissent can change decisions without punishment.

<a id="correction-circulation"></a><a id="correction"></a>**Correction.** Whether warnings, dissent, and on-the-ground information can reach decision-makers and change direction without punishment—return paths for feedback and revision. Contrasts with narrow or one-way channels; not the same as [**Circulation**](#circulation). In running prose, ordinary words like *correction* or “course correction” are not this term unless context makes the structural sense clear; when in doubt, use [**Correction**](#correction) and link here.

<a id="circulation-rich-constricted-circulation"></a><a id="correction-rich-constricted-correction"></a>**Correction rich; constricted correction.** Shorthand for how the [**vibrant group**](#vibrant-group)
and [**decaying group**](#decaying-group) tend to route [**Correction**](#correction): rich [**Correction**](#correction) keeps
warnings and dissent able to move and revise direction; constricted [**Correction**](#correction)
narrows those paths.

<a id="adaptability"></a>**Adaptability.** The capacity of coordinated action to change direction when reality
pushes back. One of two capacities (with [**scalability**](#scalability)) used to read [**renewal**](#renewal)
and [**erosion**](#erosion); together they yield the four states [**Adaptive**](#adaptive),
[**Entrenched**](#entrenched), [**Regenerative**](#regenerative), and [**Stalled**](#stalled).

<a id="adaptive"></a>**Adaptive.** A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: lower
[**scalability**](#scalability) with high [**adaptability**](#adaptability). Part of the [**vibrant group**](#vibrant-group) with
[**Regenerative**](#regenerative).

<a id="decay"></a>**Decay.** The state of a group when capacities shrink: influence no longer
revises readily under pressure, coordinated action no longer sustains reach, or
both. Contrasts with [**vitality**](#vitality); sustained [**erosion**](#erosion) tends toward [**decay**](#decay).

<a id="decaying-group"></a>**Decaying group.** The pair of grid states [**Entrenched**](#entrenched) and [**Stalled**](#stalled):
together they cover high or low [**scalability**](#scalability) with low [**adaptability**](#adaptability), on
the [**erosion**](#erosion) side of the scalability–adaptability map.

<a id="effectiveness"></a>**Effectiveness.** The dimension of the argument that tracks
what gets amplified, deferred, or mistaken for control when people look to
leadership—not the same as morality or intent.

<a id="entrenched"></a>**Entrenched.** A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: high
[**scalability**](#scalability) with low [**adaptability**](#adaptability). Part of the [**decaying group**](#decaying-group) with
[**Stalled**](#stalled).

<a id="erosion"></a>**Erosion.** The direction in which a group moves when [**scalability**](#scalability) or
[**adaptability**](#adaptability) shrinks—toward insulation, narrowed correction, and broken
reach. Contrasts with [**renewal**](#renewal); it describes structure, not moral worth.

<a id="guest-leadership"></a>**Guest leadership.** Depends on [**Circulation**](#circulation): people closest to an issue can
temporarily step in to lead a response, then return to their usual role. The
phrase follows usage in the guest-leadership literature (see Bibliography).

<a id="harm"></a>**Harm.** The dimension of the argument that tracks where cost
lands when influence is exercised: who absorbs it, who is kept in view, and
what displacement is treated as acceptable.

<a id="legitimacy"></a>**Legitimacy.** What makes authority feel usable to those who
follow: standing grounded in conduct, procedure, office, or something closer to
identity and story.

<a id="regenerative"></a>**Regenerative.** A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid:
high [**scalability**](#scalability) and high [**adaptability**](#adaptability). Part of the [**vibrant group**](#vibrant-group)
with [**Adaptive**](#adaptive).

<a id="renewal"></a>**Renewal.** The direction in which a group stays open to course correction
and keeps coordinated action working as reach grows. Contrasts with
[**erosion**](#erosion); sustained [**renewal**](#renewal) tends toward [**vitality**](#vitality).

<a id="revisability"></a>**Revisability.** Room to change plans once they are in motion—often what drops
first as [**decay**](#decay) sets in.

<a id="scalability"></a>**Scalability.** The capacity of coordinated action to grow in reach. One of two
capacities (with [**adaptability**](#adaptability)) used to read [**renewal**](#renewal) and [**erosion**](#erosion);
together they yield [**Adaptive**](#adaptive), [**Entrenched**](#entrenched), [**Regenerative**](#regenerative), and
[**Stalled**](#stalled).

<a id="stalled"></a>**Stalled.** A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: low
[**scalability**](#scalability) and low [**adaptability**](#adaptability). Part of the [**decaying group**](#decaying-group) with
[**Entrenched**](#entrenched).

<a id="vibrant-group"></a>**Vibrant group.** The pair of grid states [**Regenerative**](#regenerative) and [**Adaptive**](#adaptive):
together they cover high or lower [**scalability**](#scalability) with high [**adaptability**](#adaptability), on
the [**renewal**](#renewal) side of the scalability–adaptability map.

<a id="vitality"></a>**Vitality.** The state of a group when influence can sustain coordinated action
as reach grows—how open, revisable, and workable the group is in the moment.
Contrasts with [**decay**](#decay); sustained [**renewal**](#renewal) tends toward [**vitality**](#vitality).
