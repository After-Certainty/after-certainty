# **Glossary**

When a word appears in **bold** in the chapters, it usually marks a term the
book uses in a fixed sense—**renewal** and **erosion**,
**vibrant group** and **decaying group**, **circulation** and **correction**, **harm**,
**legitimacy**, and others. This glossary gathers those terms in
one place so you can look them up without rereading earlier chapters.

**Circulation** — Whether influence can move through temporary or shared leadership (including **guest leadership**) so that leadership can scale without fixing every role at the top—how leadership spreads and reproduces itself, including what **Leadership Reproduces Itself** tracks when people imitate what last worked. Not the same as **correction**: who may step in to lead is a different question than whether warnings and dissent can change decisions without punishment.

**Correction** — Whether warnings, dissent, and on-the-ground information can reach decision-makers and change direction without punishment—return paths for feedback and revision. Contrasts with narrow or one-way channels; not the same as **circulation**. In running prose, ordinary words like *correction* or “course correction” are not this term unless context makes the structural sense clear; when in doubt, use bold **correction** as in this entry (capital **Correction** when the term opens a sentence—the same rule as other glossary labels).

**Correction rich; constricted correction** — Shorthand for how the **vibrant group**
and **decaying group** tend to route **correction**: rich **correction** keeps
warnings and dissent able to move and revise direction; constricted **correction**
narrows those paths.

**Adaptability** — The capacity of coordinated action to change direction when reality
pushes back. One of two capacities (with **scalability**) used to read **renewal**
and **erosion**; together they yield the four states **adaptive**,
**entrenched**, **regenerative**, and **stalled**.

**Adaptive** — A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: lower
**scalability** with high **adaptability**. Part of the **vibrant group** with
**regenerative**.

**Decay** — The state of a group when capacities shrink: influence no longer
changes course readily under pressure, coordinated action no longer sustains reach, or
both. Contrasts with **vitality**; sustained **erosion** tends toward **decay**.

**Decaying group** — The pair of grid states **entrenched** and **stalled**:
together they cover high or low **scalability** with low **adaptability**, on
the **erosion** side of the scalability–adaptability map.

**Effectiveness** — The dimension of the argument that tracks
what gets amplified, deferred, or mistaken for control when people look to
leadership—not the same as morality or intent.

**Entrenched** — A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: high
**scalability** with low **adaptability**. Part of the **decaying group** with
**stalled**.

**Erosion** — The direction in which a group moves when **scalability** or
**adaptability** shrinks—toward insulation, narrowed correction, and broken
reach. Contrasts with **renewal**; it describes structure, not moral worth.

**Guest leadership** — Depends on **circulation**: people closest to an issue can
temporarily step in to lead a response, then return to their usual role. The
phrase follows usage in the guest-leadership literature (see Bibliography).

**Harm** — The dimension of the argument that tracks where cost
lands when influence is exercised: who absorbs it, who is kept in view, and
what displacement is treated as acceptable.

**Legitimacy** — What makes authority feel usable to those who
follow: grounding in real behavior, in rules and roles, in the office itself, or in something closer to
identity and shared story.

**Regenerative** — A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid:
high **scalability** and high **adaptability**. Part of the **vibrant group**
with **adaptive**.

**Renewal** — The direction in which a group stays open to course correction
and keeps coordinated action working as reach grows. Contrasts with
**erosion**; sustained **renewal** tends toward **vitality**.

**Revisability** — Room to change plans once they are in motion. Often the first
thing lost as **decay** sets in. (The chapters usually say this in plain words
instead of using this label.)

**Scalability** — The capacity of coordinated action to grow in reach. One of two
capacities (with **adaptability**) used to read **renewal** and **erosion**;
together they yield **adaptive**, **entrenched**, **regenerative**, and
**stalled**.

**Stalled** — A practical state in the scalability–adaptability grid: low
**scalability** and low **adaptability**. Part of the **decaying group** with
**entrenched**.

**Vibrant group** — The pair of grid states **regenerative** and **adaptive**:
together they cover high or lower **scalability** with high **adaptability**, on
the **renewal** side of the scalability–adaptability map.

**Vitality** — The state of a group when influence can sustain coordinated action
as reach grows—how open the group is to revision and how workable coordination
remains in the moment. Contrasts with **decay**; sustained **renewal** tends toward **vitality**.
