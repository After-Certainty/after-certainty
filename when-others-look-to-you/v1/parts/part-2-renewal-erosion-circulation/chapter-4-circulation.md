# **Chapter 4 — Circulation**

### **What Circulation Is — and What It Isn't**

**Circulation** is not a calendar for who sits in which chair next. Delegation
hands off a task; succession names an heir; rotation swaps names on a roster.
Those can be orderly without ever letting influence move *through* people in
the middle of the work.

**Circulation** asks whether the permission structure allows someone to lead
temporarily, share the lead, and hand it off when the problem demands
it—without treating every step as a challenge to formal authority. Influence
needs to move *through* the group, not only *outward from* a fixed center, if
coordinated action is to stay revisable as reach grows.

Guest-style leadership—stepping up for a stretch, then stepping
back—depends on that permission.[^c4-guest-leadership] Where **circulation** is
narrow, the org chart can look healthy while every real decision still waits
for the same gate.

### **Why Correction Alone Isn't Enough**

**Correction** keeps the center honest: warnings and dissent can still change
what the person with formal power decides. **Circulation** is what allows the
*center* to move—who holds the live decision point when the work shifts shape.

A group can keep **correction** open and still stall. Dissent may reach the
named leader on time while every fork in the road still routes through that
same leader. The bottleneck is no longer closed ears; it is a fixed routing
table. **Circulation** is whether someone else can credibly hold the decision
for a phase, carry the authority that phase requires, and return influence
without being punished for having held it.

### **Circulation in the Vibrant Group**

When **circulation** works, temporary leadership reads as legitimate work, not
as a coup. The lead passes to whoever is closest to the shifting facts. People
cover for each other without turning coverage into humiliation or
invisibility: the person who stepped in is visible, credited, and welcome to
step back when the crisis passes.

They learn leadership from inside the role—not only from watching someone else
hold it. What spreads is the capacity for **correction**, adaptation, and shared
authority. When formal leadership turns over, the new person inherits the
position *and* inherits a culture where being challenged in the chair is still
normal. **Renewal** at the center and **circulation** through the bench
reinforce each other.

### **Circulation in the Decaying Group**

**Circulation** does not disappear under **decay**. It tightens.

Influence still moves, but through channels the center already trusts. The
people invited to “step up” are the ones who have shown they will not disturb
the pattern. Temporary authority becomes a reward for alignment, not a test
of judgment. The group can look busy developing leaders while it rehearses the
same blind spots: the same exceptions, the same insulation, the same quiet
limits on what may be said.

What travels is not flexibility to lead but a template for how leadership is
performed here. **Erosion** can persist without a single villain; it only needs
each cohort to learn, before they ever hold the title, whether stepping into
the gap is safe.

Under acute pressure, the same posture often shows up as “discipline.”
Coordination outside the official chain is treated as bypass or leakage, not as
the backup the work needs. Temporary leadership never earns room to form; the
story stays about alignment with the chart. That is **circulation** captured
under another name: pressure spikes, the official chain becomes the only
legitimate story, and exceptions harden into habit.

### **Who Gets Credit**

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
A product team’s manager is out on leave. A junior member runs the vendor call,
holds a hard line on scope, and keeps a release from slipping. In the recap
thread, the director thanks “the team” and moves on. Standups return to normal.
Within a month, her lane quietly shifts toward execution and away from
decisions. People stop asking her into rooms where tradeoffs get made.
Nobody announces a demotion. Everyone learns what stepping up cost.

Elsewhere, the same gap in coverage: she runs the call, names the tradeoff, and
commits. When the manager returns, the public recap quotes her decision as the
right call and leaves her name on it. The manager’s first comment is
correction on tone, not erasure of authorship. The next time the group is under
strain, more people believe they are allowed to hold the center for a week
without paying for it forever.
:::

Same competence can read as threat or as gift, depending on whether **circulation**
is open or captured. The difference shows up first in credit, absence, and who
still gets invited back into the decision ring.

### **Leadership Reproduces Itself — The Circulation Version**

**Leadership Reproduces Itself** is not only mimicry of mannerisms. It copies
permission. If stepping in was treated as out of line, the next person to hold
formal authority will usually defend the same boundary—often sincerely,
because that is what “leading here” was shown to mean. If shared leadership was
welcomed and credited, the next person tends to inherit that habit too.

**Circulation** teaches people whether they are allowed to lead before they
ever hold the role. By the time someone is promoted, the lesson has already
landed.

### **A Practical Circulation Check**

Read the group without the org chart in front of you.

When someone holds the center temporarily, does the room treat it as useful or
as a problem to manage? When the formal lead is away, does work keep moving or
freeze until the usual name returns? Who gets offered those temporary turns—
only people already aligned with the center, or people who might disagree and
still be trusted? When authority is handed off, what travels with the title:
open paths for **correction**, or a warning that only certain people may steer?

If the answers skew narrow, **circulation** is likely captured even when
**correction** still works on paper. What gets reproduced next is not a mystery.
It is whatever this group just taught about who may lead, for how long, and at
what price.
