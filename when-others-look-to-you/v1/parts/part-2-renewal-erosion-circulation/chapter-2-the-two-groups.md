# **Chapter 2 — The Two Groups**

### **Two Directions**

What a group does under attention does not freeze in place.[^c2-adaptive-systems]
Attention can gather without guaranteeing compliance; influence can stay
strong when people only partly go along. The real question is what that
influence keeps producing.

Once **Attention Finds a Focus**, the group moves in one of two directions.
Either course correction stays possible and coordinated action can still grow in
reach, or insulation sets in—**correction** weakens and reach starts to break
down.[^c2-organizational-defenses] The first is **renewal**. The second is
**erosion**, which tends toward **decay**; **erosion** and **decay** get their
own treatment once **renewal** is clear.

**Renewal** names which way influence is moving. **Vitality** names the
condition that tends to show when that movement holds. Direction and
condition are not the same thing. Neither is a verdict on character: good
intentions can still yield **erosion**, and flawed people can still sustain
**renewal**. The distinction is structural—what the group does under pressure—not purity.

### **Two Capacities**

Two capacities carry most of the weight.

**Scalability** asks whether coordinated action can grow in reach—whether more
people, sites, or layers can stay aligned without the center having to hold
every thread by hand.

**Adaptability** asks whether the work can still change direction when reality
pushes back—whether plans stay revisable after they are in motion.

**Erosion** shows when either capacity shrinks. How those capacities combine
into two groups—by whether **adaptability** stays high or falls low—is the next
step; the pattern chapters stay with what each direction looks like in practice.

### **The Vibrant Group and the Decaying Group**

Together **scalability** and **adaptability** produce four combinations at the
corners of the grid—**regenerative**, **adaptive**, **entrenched**, and
**stalled**. They are not personality types. They are readings of structure
under load.

What matters for the fork in this book is the split down the middle: high
**adaptability** on one side, low **adaptability** on the other. Each side is a
*pair* of corners, not a single box.

The **vibrant group** is the **renewal**-side pair: **regenerative** and
**adaptive**. Together they cover high or low **scalability** as long as
**adaptability** stays high—coordinated action can still change course when
reality pushes back, whether reach is broad or still thin.

The **decaying group** is the **erosion**-side pair: **entrenched** and
**stalled**. Together they cover high or low **scalability** when
**adaptability** is low—changing course is hard or gone; the group may still
look coordinated for a long time, or coordination itself may be fraying.

### **Vitality and Decay**

**Vitality** and **decay** are not extra directions beside **renewal** and
**erosion**. They name how things tend to *feel* on the ground when **renewal**
or **erosion** is holding—whether friction still buys safety, whether confidence
tracks what people on the edges can still say.

The chapters that follow take each direction into pattern-level detail: first
what **renewal** looks like when **vitality** holds, then what **erosion**
looks like when **decay** deepens.
