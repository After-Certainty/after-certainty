# **Chapter 7 — Effectiveness and Its Illusions**

### **When the Numbers Look Good**

Two teams finish the quarter above target.

In one, weekly reviews still reopen decisions when field evidence shifts. A metric slips, then steadies for stronger reasons.

In the other, the dashboard stays green while objections go quiet.
Work still ships. More fixes move into side channels and private
workarounds.

Both look effective from a distance.
Only one preserves the conditions that keep effectiveness real over time.[^c7-performance-paradox]

### **What Results Can Hide**

We usually judge leadership by visible outcomes.
That is necessary.
But it is not enough.

A group can hit goals while narrowing **correction**.
It can look coordinated while trust, learning, and adaptability thin
below the surface.

The key question is not only "did this work now?" It is "what did this
result do to future capacity?"

### **After the Citation**

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
The public library’s literacy program receives a state citation; the director’s photo runs in the paper, and the board’s annual dinner is a warm night. A children’s librarian mentions afterward, in passing, that the new family intake still asks for documents the district stopped accepting in June; two families have already been turned away at the desk. The director thanks everyone for the year, pivots to dessert, and the room follows.

She does not press it. Nobody ruled her out of order. The celebration just had no place left for that sentence.
:::

The result was real. So was what the room could no longer hold in public.

That split is the point: visible success can grow while channels for
inconvenient signal narrow.

### **Effectiveness Over Time**

**Effectiveness** is about whether the work actually produces results.

The stronger read is whether outcomes will hold, or whether they are
borrowed from conditions the group is quietly burning through.

When **renewal** holds, groups pay smaller costs early so direction can
still adjust.
When **erosion** deepens, groups protect short-term smoothness and pay
later in larger forms: preventable **harm**, rework, trust loss, and
expensive repair.[^c7-deferred-costs]

In short, **renewal** tends to spend early to preserve capacity.
**Erosion** tends to borrow against the future.

### **When Success Narrows Correction**

Success increases authority. It can improve coordination. It can also
make dissent look unnecessary.

After visible wins, conflicting signals often arrive later, softer, or
not at all. People learn what is easy to raise and what is safer to
keep to themselves.[^c7-escalation]

This is one of leadership's recurring traps.
The same run of success that proves capability can narrow the feedback
channels needed to sustain it.
That same run also teaches the next leaders what counts as normal
leadership here—**Leadership Reproduces Itself**.

### **Correction as Time-Bound Effectiveness**

Leadership sends influence outward. **Effectiveness** over time depends
on whether feedback comes back while costs are still small.

**Correction** means warnings, dissent, and what people on the ground
see can reach decision-makers and change direction without
punishment.[^c7-voice]
**Dissent is Welcomed** means people can still challenge without losing
their place.

When that path stays open, groups can absorb smaller visible costs now
to avoid larger failures later.

It shows whether the group can still change direction.
It shows whether the group is staying healthy or moving toward decay.

Without **correction**, authority still exists, but learning slows.
In the short run, outcomes can still look strong.
Over time, recurrence, rework, and repair costs rise. Groups move
toward the **decaying group**.

In **vibrant** groups, warnings and dissent can still move and revise
direction.
In **decaying** groups, **correction** narrows.
The same care may exist privately, but the public path for changing a
decision narrows until only workarounds remain.

When **correction** stays open, **deferred effectiveness** becomes
possible.
It is patient judgment that pays a smaller cost now to avoid a larger
one later.

### **The Fast Rule Pauses**

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
A regional claims center rolls out a rule that auto-denies incomplete submissions after forty-eight hours. Week one looks efficient. Pending counts drop, managers hit daily closure targets, and the operations report finally reads as stable.

In the second week, a medical-review analyst sees a repeat pattern in escalations from dialysis patients. Documents are arriving, but from clinics that upload in batches, not in the window the rule expects. The denials are technically compliant and operationally wrong.

The claims VP pauses the auto-denial trigger for that category, shifts two teams to same-day manual review, and accepts that month-end closure rates will fall. The drop is immediate and public. Three weeks later, so is what it prevented: appeal volume does not spike, provider complaints stay low, and the unit avoids a broad reopen of closed files.
:::

That is **deferred effectiveness** in practice: pay a smaller visible
cost now to avoid a larger failure later.

Some of the most effective choices look inefficient in the week they
are made.
Leaders may slow implementation, reopen assumptions, or absorb awkward
public correction to preserve reliability later.

### **Performative Compliance and Real Coordination**

What gets measured can hide whether people are actually with you.

People can produce good-looking counts while privately withdrawing
trust. They can repeat approved language while quietly routing around
decisions they do not believe in.

In these conditions, the group appears coordinated, but its ability to
act together is already splitting underneath.
The real story can hide behind tidy paperwork.
**Learning Collapse** follows.

Selective followership shows up when you look closely at effectiveness. Open
disagreement can strengthen results. Quiet divergence wears them down
over time.

### **Reading Effectiveness While It Is Happening**

To understand what is really happening, look at both outcomes and the
conditions underneath them.
Watch whether **correction** stayed open while work was underway.
Watch whether concerns changed decisions or only decorated meetings.
Watch whether success increased learning capacity or reduced it.
Watch whether trust held while results were achieved.

Without that read, results mislead.

### **Legitimacy Next**

**Effectiveness** is what worked.
**Legitimacy** is why people keep following: how authority forms,
hardens, and renews over time, and what makes that sustainable.

::: {custom-style="Pull Quote Block"}
Performance can certify capacity while masking decay.
