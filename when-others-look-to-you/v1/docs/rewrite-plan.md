# Rewrite Plan: *When Others Look to You*

This document describes the structural changes, content migrations, new writing, and editorial notes for the revised edition. It is intended as a working guide for implementing the changes chapter by chapter.

---

## Overview of Changes

The core restructure does four things:

1. **Splits Chapter 2** (Renewal and Erosion) into three focused chapters: Renewal, Erosion, and Circulation.
2. **Moves "Why We Misjudge Leaders"** from its current position (after Chapter 2) to Part IV, where it functions as a culmination rather than an early warning.
3. **Expands Circulation** from a recurring mention into a fully developed chapter with its own analysis of both the vibrant and decaying group.
4. **Migrates analytical content** from the current Chapters 2 and 3 into later chapters where it lands with more weight, allowing Chapters 2, 3, and 4 to focus on pattern introduction and vignettes.

## Part II — Renewal, Erosion, and Circulation

### Chapter 2 — Renewal *(split from current Ch. 2)*

**Goal:** Introduce the renewal direction, name the three adjusting dynamics, and make the pattern feel human-scale through a vignette. Do not yet analyze implications — those migrate to Part III.

**Keep from current Ch. 2:**
- "Two Directions" — condense significantly. Its job is now only to frame the split between renewal and erosion, not to introduce all four states or the full grid. One short paragraph establishing that influence moves in one of two directions is enough. Save the four-state grid (Regenerative / Adaptive / Entrenched / Stalled) for Chapter 6 or the Appendix.
- "What Vitality Looks Like" — keep, trim lightly.
- The three adjusting dynamics: **Dissent is Welcomed**, **Feedback Drives Change**, **Leaders Feel the Consequences** — keep as named pattern blocks.
- Pull-quote closing.

**Remove from current Ch. 2 (migrating elsewhere):**
- "The Cost Gradient" → Chapter 6 (Effectiveness)
- "How Harm Moves" (vibrant group half) → Chapter 5 (Harm)
- "Selective Followership as a Signal" (full treatment) → Chapter 7 (Legitimacy)
- "A Practical Discipline" → condense into pull-quote or drop; the fuller version of this belongs in Chapter 11

**New writing needed:**
- **Vignette** — one short scene showing renewal at human scale. Suggested direction: a manager receives feedback that contradicts what they just reported upward, takes it seriously, corrects the report publicly, and absorbs the embarrassment. The team notices. The vignette ends before analysis. One to two paragraphs, visually separated. After the vignette, one sentence tying it back to the adjusting dynamics before the chapter closes.

### Chapter 4 — Circulation *(new chapter, significantly expanded)*

**Goal:** Establish Circulation as a major theme with the same weight as Correction. Show what it looks like in both the vibrant and decaying group. End on the stakes — what gets reproduced when Circulation is captured.

**Current state:** Circulation is mentioned throughout the book alongside Correction but never developed on its own. The clearest existing seeds are in Chapter 9 (the workplace example where coordination outside the official chain is shut down) and the repair section. Draw on those but do not leave them only in Chapter 9.

**Proposed section structure:**

1. **What Circulation Is — and What It Isn't**
   Distinguish from delegation, succession planning, and rotation. The specific claim: influence needs to move *through* people, not just *outward from* a center. Circulation is not about who is officially next in line. It is about whether the permission structure allows people to lead temporarily, share the lead, and hand it off when the work demands it.

2. **Why Correction Alone Isn't Enough**
   Correction keeps the center honest. Circulation is what allows the center to shift. Without Circulation, a group can have functioning Correction and still stall — because every decision still routes through the same gate. Briefly distinguish the two: Correction is about whether warnings and dissent can return to those who decide; Circulation is about whether the role of decision-maker can itself move.

3. **Circulation in the Vibrant Group**
   What it looks like when it works. Temporary leadership is treated as legitimate, not as a threat to formal authority. The lead passes around when the work demands it. Guest-style handoffs happen without requiring a formal transfer of role. People who step in learn that they are allowed to lead — and learn what good leadership looks like from inside the role, not just from watching it. What gets reproduced is the capacity to correct, adapt, and share authority. New people in the role inherit both the position and the openness to being challenged in it.

4. **Circulation in the Decaying Group**
   Circulation still happens — but it is captured. Influence moves through narrower, more controlled channels. Step-in leadership is available only to those who have already demonstrated alignment with the center. The lead passes to people who will not disturb the existing pattern. What circulates is not the flexibility to lead but the template for how leadership gets performed here: the same exceptions, the same insulation, the same suppressed dissent. The group looks like it is developing leaders. What it is actually doing is replicating its own blind spots. This is the mechanism that makes erosion persistent — it does not need a single bad actor to continue. It just needs the next person in line to have learned from the last one.

5. **Leadership Reproduces Itself — The Circulation Version**
   What gets copied is not just behavior — it is the permission structure. If step-in leadership was treated as out of line, the next person to hold formal authority will treat it the same way. If shared leadership was welcomed, the next person inherits that permission too. Circulation teaches people whether they are allowed to lead before they ever hold the role.

6. **A Practical Circulation Check**
   Three to four observable questions. Examples: When someone steps in temporarily, is that treated as useful or as a threat? When the formal lead is absent, does work continue or stall? Who gets offered temporary leadership — only those already aligned with the center, or others too? When leadership is handed off, what gets passed along with the role?

**New writing needed:** This chapter is substantially new. Draw on existing Circulation mentions throughout the book for language consistency, but the structure, examples, and analysis are all new. Add one vignette — suggested direction: a project team where the formal lead is out and a junior member steps up, handles a difficult call well, and is later quietly sidelined rather than credited. The team learns what stepping up costs. Contrast version (vibrant): same setup, the step-in is credited publicly, and the formal lead returns and references what the junior member decided as the right call.

**Part II opening bridge:** *From Formation to Movement.* Short — two to three paragraphs. The job is to frame the Part II naming arc (renewal, erosion, circulation) without pre-running Part III analysis.

### Chapter 10 — Why We Misjudge Leaders *(current Ch. 3, moved and expanded)*

**Goal:** By this point the reader has seen harm displaced, legitimacy transferred, scale accelerating drift, and tradeoffs compressed under pressure. Every distortion this chapter names has just been demonstrated. The chapter now reads as a culmination rather than an early warning. Reframe the opening accordingly — the judgment problem is not just that observers miss things; it is that scale and pressure systematically make the relevant signals harder to reach.

**Keep from current Ch. 3:**
- "The Judgment Problem" — reframe the opening. The reader no longer needs the problem introduced from scratch; they have just lived through the conditions that produce it.
- "Outcome Bias" — keep.
- "Structural Blindness" — keep.
- "The Intention Trap" — keep.
- "Selective Followership and Misread Signals" — keep.
- "Why Confidence Is So Persuasive" — keep.
- "The Principal's Turnaround" vignette — keep.
- "What to Watch Instead" — keep. **Add** "Early Indicators" here, migrated from current Ch. 2. They land harder after the reader has seen the full pattern.
- "A Better Standard" — keep.

**Add: new section — "Why Erosion Feels Rational"** (migrated from current Ch. 2, expanded). This is the interior counterpart to structural blindness. Structural blindness explains why *observers* miss erosion; "why erosion feels rational" explains why *leaders* produce it without noticing. Each local choice that contributes to erosion — narrowing channels to move faster, avoiding dissent to stay aligned, simplifying the story to reduce confusion — can be defended on its own terms. The pattern only becomes visible through repetition. Place this after "The Intention Trap" and before "Selective Followership and Misread Signals."

**Add: new section — "Misjudgment at Scale"** (new writing). Connect the chapter to Chapters 8 and 9. At scale, the signals that would reveal misjudgment travel farther, arrive later, and warp more on the way. The same distortions that cause misjudgment at human scale — outcome bias, structural blindness — become structurally harder to correct as reach grows and pressure cycles faster. This section should be short (three to four paragraphs) and land as the reason the chapter appears here rather than earlier.

**Part IV opening bridge:** *From Structure to Scale and Judgment.* Short. The job: move from lens findings to scale pressure, showing why the same dynamics become harder to read as distance and speed increase.

**New bridge needed after Chapter 10 (in Part V):** *From Misjudgment to What Remains.* Short. The job: after naming why the pattern is so persistently missed, turn toward what is still possible. Not a pivot to optimism — a pivot to realism. Something like: knowing how misjudgment works does not automatically repair the structure. But it changes what you can see, what you can refuse to excuse, and what you can keep open. That is where Chapter 11 picks up.

## Part V — What Remains

### Chapter 11 — What Happens Next *(current Ch. 9, significantly rewritten)*

**Goal:** Rewrite from synthesis to reckoning. The current chapter summarizes the five lenses and describes two trajectories and partial repair. That is a good penultimate chapter. As the closing chapter — after the reader has just been through the misjudgment argument — it needs to do more. The reader now understands not just how structures erode but why no one catches it. The question the chapter needs to answer is: given all of that, what is still worth doing?

**Keep from current Ch. 9:**
- "Two Trajectories" — keep, expand slightly.
- "When the Baseline Has Already Shifted" — keep.
- The partial repair list — keep, but reframe. Currently reads as a checklist. Rewrite as a sequence: what to do first when the structure has already drifted, in what order, and why the order matters.

**Rewrite:**
- "The Integrated View" — currently a bullet-point summary of the five lenses. Rewrite as a reckoning: here is what the full picture looks like when you hold all five lenses at once. Not a recap — an honest assessment of how much a decaying structure can look like a healthy one for a long time, and what that means for anyone who leads inside one.

**Add: new section — "What Circulation Makes Possible That Correction Alone Cannot"**
This is the chapter's most important new section. Correction keeps the center honest. Circulation is what allows the pattern itself to change — because it changes who leads, what they learn from leading, and what gets reproduced next. If Correction is the mechanism of repair, Circulation is the mechanism of renewal. Without Circulation, repair stays local and temporary. With it, what gets reproduced starts to shift. This section ties the Circulation chapter back in at the close and gives the book an ending that is structural rather than exhortatory.

**Part V / closing chapter:** Do not end on repair. End on the question of what gets reproduced — because that is the throughline of the whole book. The last thing the reader should feel is not hope or warning but clarity: what happens next is not fate. It is what gets repeated until it hardens. Whether Circulation stays open is the question that outlasts any single leader.
