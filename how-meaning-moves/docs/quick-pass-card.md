# Quick Pass Card - Chapter Edits

Use this card for fast chapter-by-chapter passes. Default target voice is
Part I: Chapter 1 (Signal), Chapter 2 (Compression), Chapter 3 (Restraint).

## 1) Voice check (first read)

- Does this read like calm social observation, not theory explanation?
- Does the tone stay serious, restrained, and human?
- Would a reader think "I recognize this" instead of "I am studying a model"?

## 2) Abstraction check

- Replace abstract noun chains with observable actions when possible.
- Translate model wording into human wording:
  - interpretation/narrative/sensemaking -> what people assume or decide
  - certainty/context stabilization -> what starts feeling true
  - acceleration/mechanism language -> people settling faster under pressure
- Keep core terms only where they are structurally necessary.

## 3) Grounding check

- After any abstract claim, add a recognizable human moment quickly.
- Favor ordinary settings: work, relationships, text threads, authority,
  disagreement, repair attempts.
- Prefer what changed in the conversation over what changed in the framework.

## 4) Progression check

- Remove adjacent paragraphs that explain the same mechanism twice.
- Keep section movement: claim -> observable consequence -> next pressure.
- Stop early when a line lands; do not over-explain.

## 5) Compression + restraint language check

**Compression**

- Prefer:
  - "people settle on what they think happened"
  - "the story starts feeling true" / "stops feeling chosen and starts feeling obvious"
  - "people trade depth for speed"
- Avoid:
  - dense process/mechanism exposition
  - academic synthesis cadence
  - corporate, self-help, or therapy tone

**Restraint** (re-ground moral or abstract passages)

- Prefer behavior and cost: delay, not replying yet, tolerating not knowing, carrying discomfort.
- Prefer: "leave more room for revision," "without the protection certainty usually provides," "no rule can fully automate restraint."
- Avoid: "proceduralized," schematic without/out contrast strings, philosophy-default stacking.
- Hinge sentences allowed sparingly; do not chain aphorisms.

## 6) Rhythm check

- Mix short and medium sentences for calm readability.
- Avoid long runs of one-sentence paragraphs.
- Avoid repeated sentence openings and repeated abstract keywords.

## 7) Architecture check

- Preserve chapter structure, pattern blocks, vignette blocks, citations, and
  core thesis.
- Preserve book progression (keep it felt, not lecture-y):
  - signal shapes what arrives before words settle
  - compression settles meaning quickly
  - restraint delays closure so contact can hold
- Improve readability without rewriting framework intent.

## 7b) Vignette check (when present)

- Strongest when nothing dramatic happens, conflict stays subtle, cost is social or internal.
- Analysis stays outside the block; scene does the emotional calibration.

## 8) Final lock question

If a sentence sounds "correct" but not lived, rewrite once more.
