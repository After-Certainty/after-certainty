# Chapter 10 — Fringe

The fringe corridor was viable until it wasn't, and Nadi lived in the margin between.

*Quick Margin* hummed with Meridian freight gone and return cargo waiting—nothing precious, nothing fragile, everything time-sensitive in the way clients lied about time when they meant money. Her maps showed the line she'd filed weeks ago: archive adjacency space, ugly, underbid, wrong in the way pilots meant wrong when they couldn't price loneliness.

Wrong was often cheaper than honest.

Today wrong was also the only corridor that didn't eat her quarter.

She ran margin math on the nav table with coffee gone cold and the loader purring for once, a sound she trusted more than bulletins.

Fuel burn to fringe entry: acceptable.

Berth fees avoided by not sitting in Markets while Vesper sulked: necessary.

Premium from the mirror-tech firm already spent on patches and bribes: gone.

Long corridor as emergency only—eleven days, two fee tiers, jurisdiction adjacency on reflective language, BRICK's cousin swearing Tuesday like Tuesday was a religion.

Vesper as wait.

Fringe as go.

The numbers said go. The numbers always said go until they said crash, and pilots who confused the two didn't get to update maps.

Her loader clicked through preflight with the smugness of machinery that had been bribed correctly. Nadi ran gloved fingers along the patched hull seam—not keeper marks, just repair memory. Three years since the dock collision kissed port side. Three years of creative routes paying for the next repair.

Creative was a polite word for risk priced below fear.

The auction bay foreman had tried to sell her long corridor insurance with separatist riders. Nadi had laughed in his face—not cruel, educational.

"Insurance pays after the story settles," she'd said. "I get paid before."

"Fringe isn't a story."

"Fringe is a fee schedule with loneliness attached." She'd signed the manifest without insurance. BRICK would call her crazy. BRICK ran long corridor and sent cousin memos. Different religion.

Jun had been at the lip again that morning—chart updated, confidence climbing, crowd small. Nadi had walked past without stopping. Stopping was berth burn. Jun measured belief. Nadi measured fuel. Belief didn't open throats. Fuel did.

---

Her comm lit with BRICK before she lifted.

*Separatists definitely. Cousin says internal memo.*

Nadi typed one-handed: **Cousins sell memos. I sell delivery.**

*You're flying fringe again.*

**Viable.**

*You're crazy.*

**Crazy's underbid.**

BRICK sent the respect string. Nadi almost smiled.

Crew banter was the Markets' other wall—the one that didn't update in public, only in dialect between people who burned fuel for a living.

On the public ribbon Jun's cascade chart had landed—separatist cluster, confidence climbing, elegant curves that made fear look intelligent. Nadi had seen the shape on the auction bay display before publication. The wall had priced it first. Jun got credit for describing weather already raining.

Nadi didn't mind Jun.

She minded Jun thinking description was early.

---

She lifted on schedule because schedule was a fiction you enforced with thrust.

The Markets fell away—stations bright, dice thrown, fees still climbing on boards she'd stopped watching because watching without flying was berth burn.

Vesper suspension scrolled past.

Separatist briefing pamphlets scrolled past after it—wrong borders dressed as geography, the kind of lie that moved surcharges before it moved facts.

Nadi didn't believe it. She didn't disbelieve it either. Belief was cargo she couldn't stack.

She believed fuel.

She believed the fringe fee schedule that hadn't spiked yet because not enough pilots were desperate enough to bid it up.

She believed the gate network was stressed—not broken, stressed—the way a corridor hummed before it cleared, the way admins said *administrative* when they meant *we don't know and won't say*.

Ground truth in a cockpit: pressure without catastrophe. Reroutes without explosions. Marks on dock clamps from hands steadier than hers.

Not sabotage-shaped. Neglect-shaped. Hurry-shaped. The shape of a civilization forgetting to notice until noticing cost more than fees.

---

Halfway through, the corridor did what fringe corridors did.

Silence too long.

Echo wrong.

A reading on the adjacency sensor that flickered green and shouldn't have, the kind of flicker keepers marked and accountants ignored.

Nadi didn't panic. Panic burned fuel.

She slowed, logged the flicker, sent it to her archive with dock clamp photos—dot, stroke, third hand steadiest yet. Not theory. Habit.

BRICK pinged again: *You alive?*

**Alive. Fringe being fringe.**

*Gate network's stressed not dead,* he wrote. *Admin friend says wait state.*

**Wait state pays analysts. I fly.**

She flew.

---

Entry was where fringe earned its name.

Too few ships. Too much silence. Admin geometry that felt like a hallway pretending to be a throat. Nadi kept one hand on fuel and one on the comms for BRICK's theories because theories were free and loneliness wasn't.

*Separatists blew intake housing,* BRICK wrote.

**Intake housing has marks**, Nadi sent. **Dot stroke. Steadier hand than mine.**

*You think keepers did it?*

**I think fuel gauge is green.**

That ended the theology. BRICK sent respect instead of argument.

Halfway through, the adjacency sensor flickered—green, wrong green, the kind of reading that meant pressure not catastrophe. Nadi slowed, logged coordinates, photographed the console. Archive note: **stressed not broken**.

Not sabotage-shaped.

Neglect-shaped.

The shape of a civilization forgetting to notice until noticing cost more than story tax.

She flew on because stopping in fringe cost more than wrong readings when no alarm followed.

---

Meridian Arc corridor for the return premium was clean knife again—Ribbon noise, Lake Orin doubled in a viewport she didn't linger on. Tourists failing stairs. A courier with a compass patch somewhere in the habitat maybe, maybe already committed around, maybe eating fees Lena couldn't see from Nadi's altitude.

Not Nadi's job.

Job was margin.

Job was filing **viable** on a corridor that felt wrong so the next pilot paid more.

Job was not mistaking stressed for broken so you could sell sabotage on a pamphlet.

---

Back in Markets she updated maps with grease on her fingers and certainty in her fuel gauge.

Red lines where routes thinned—more red than last week.

Green where prices jumped—ugly-beautiful green.

Yellow going red on Vesper and long corridor both.

She filed fringe as **viable**, long corridor as **emergency only**, Vesper as **wait**.

Business decision, not heroism.

Commit to route knowledge—her knowledge, earned in silence and flicker and dock marks—not Jun's chart, not separatist pamphlets, not BRICK's cousin's Tuesday.

The wall at the auction bay showed reroute density up twenty-four percent. Someone had stopped lying about eighteen. Story tax on housing ticked higher. Wrong-border pamphlets still two for one.

Collision at the dock: fee spike on the public board, separatist chatter on comms, fresh mark on her clamp beside the others—same grammar, steadier hand, checking continuing while the wall screamed.

Nadi touched the mark and didn't photograph it.

Trust when habits repeated.

---

That night she messaged Lena because margin was margin.

**Fringe still viable — long corridor emergency only — fees jumped again — hire margin not lecture — certainty not included**

No reply by morning. Couriers slept on their own schedules or didn't sleep.

Nadi lifted again because lifting was the answer she'd chosen—not outrun fear, outrun missing the window.

*Quick Margin* smelled like coffee and patched hull and the ozone after a crossing.

Jun would publish an update. BRICK would send another cousin theory. Vesper would stay **wait**.

The gate network would stay stressed—not broken—until admins found a word that didn't cost them admission.

Nadi adjusted course and flew fringe because she'd decided route knowledge was worth more than being right in public.

That was the business.

That was also, in Markets dialect, a kind of ground truth.

---

Morning brought the decision back in numbers, not philosophy.

Fringe fee schedule up four points overnight—still cheaper than long corridor emergency math. Housing story tax up two—couriers bleeding standing-up margin while analysts published confidence. Wrong-border pamphlets at the lip unchanged in lie, updated in ink.

Nadi spread the public board photo beside her private map and circled the gap.

Price lead time: fringe moved before narrative.

Narrative lead time: Jun's chart, separatist podcasts, BRICK's cousin's roommate's memo.

Ground truth: stressed network, marks on clamps, green readings that lied politely, fees that told the truth early.

She messaged Lena again because business was business.

**Hire margin or eat long corridor — fees won't wait for your brother**

No reply by lift window.

Couriers committed around on their own schedules.

Nadi committed to fringe on hers.

The loader sulked once, then settled. Dock foreman stamped the manifest. Jun's updated brief scrolled on the ribbon—**0.78**—and the wall had already priced seventy-eight before the animation rendered.

Nadi didn't look.

Looking without flying was berth burn.

She looked at fuel instead.

*Quick Margin* rose.

The corridor opened wrong—loneliness, echo, admin silence dressed as geometry—and she flew it because she'd decided route knowledge was worth more than being right in public.

BRICK pinged mid-entry: *Separatists definitely.*

**Definitely priced**, Nadi sent back. **Still flying.**

Respect string. Quiet cockpit. Coffee gone cold.

Halfway through, the sensor flickered again. She logged it, photographed the nav reading, added it to the archive beside dock marks—dot, stroke, steadier hands. Not sabotage-shaped. Neglect-shaped. The shape of a network forgetting to notice until noticing cost more than story tax.

Gate network stressed not broken.

She could live with stressed.

Broken didn't pay.

---

Meridian return premium cleared. Client lawyers satisfied. Premium in account.

Nadi stood one night at Northhook, watched Lake Orin double the city, didn't look for compass patches.

Couriers and their brothers weren't her margin.

Back in Markets she filed **viable**, filed **emergency only**, filed **wait**, touched the clamp mark without photographing it, and lifted again before Jun finished his sentence on the public lip.

Business decision.

The Markets fell away like a problem she'd chosen to outrun without pretending outrunning solved it.

That was honest enough to fly on.

---

She didn't think about Lena at lift.

Couriers committed around on family and folders and foolish ledger words. Pilots committed to margin. Intersection was hire margin not lecture and certainty not included.

Nadi had sent both messages. No reply wasn't insult. No reply was courier weather.

If the compass-patch courier made long corridor tier two instead of fringe referral, that was the courier's fee to pay. Nadi had files to update and BRICK to ignore and Jun to outrun by flying before the chart finished rendering.

At dock clamp four the mark waited—dot, stroke, steadier hand.

She touched it once, lifted twice in one day because the second contract paid enough to forgive the loader's sulk, and flew fringe again with separatist podcast noise on comms muted and fuel gauge green.

Stressed not broken.

Viable not heroic.

Business decision holding through the corridor's wrong loneliness.

The wall would update when she landed.

She would read it.

She would adjust.

That was the job.

That was ground truth enough for a pilot who'd decided route knowledge beat being right in public—and who didn't need Vesper to speak to know the network was humming wrong while administrators practiced wait.

On the ground before the second lift, a courier with a compass patch crossed the bay too fast to hail—committed face, folder weight, eleven days in her ledger if Nadi read bodies the way she read walls.

Lena didn't look up.

Nadi didn't wave.

Pilots and couriers shared routes, not rescue.

Nadi filed the fringe viable one more time and climbed.
