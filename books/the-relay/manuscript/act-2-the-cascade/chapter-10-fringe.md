# Chapter 10 — Fringe

The fringe corridor was viable until it wasn't, and Nadi lived in the margin between.

*Quick Margin* hummed with Meridian freight gone and return cargo waiting—nothing precious, nothing fragile, everything time-sensitive in the way clients lied about time when they meant money. Her maps showed the line she'd filed weeks ago: archive adjacency space, ugly, underbid, wrong in the way pilots meant wrong when they couldn't price loneliness.

Wrong was often cheaper than honest.

Today wrong was also the only corridor that didn't eat her quarter.

She ran margin math on the nav table with coffee gone cold and the loader purring for once, a sound she trusted more than bulletins.

Fuel burn to fringe entry: acceptable.

Berth fees avoided by not sitting in Markets while Vesper sulked: necessary.

Premium from the mirror-tech firm already spent on patches and bribes: gone.

Long corridor as emergency only—eleven days, two fee tiers, jurisdiction adjacency on reflective language, BRICK's cousin swearing Tuesday like Tuesday was a religion.

Vesper as wait.

Fringe as go.

The numbers said go. The numbers always said go until they said crash, and pilots who confused the two didn't get to update maps.

Her loader clicked through preflight with the smugness of machinery that had been bribed correctly. Nadi ran gloved fingers along the patched hull seam—not keeper marks, just repair memory. Three years since the dock collision kissed port side. Three years of creative routes paying for the next repair.

Creative was a polite word for risk priced below fear.

The auction bay foreman had tried to sell her long corridor insurance with separatist riders. Nadi had laughed in his face—not cruel, educational.

"Insurance pays after the story settles," she'd said. "I get paid before."

"Fringe isn't a story."

"Fringe is a fee schedule with loneliness attached." She'd signed the manifest without insurance. BRICK would call her crazy. BRICK ran long corridor and sent cousin memos. Different religion.

Jun had been at the lip again that morning—chart updated, confidence climbing, crowd small. Nadi had walked past without stopping. Stopping was berth burn. Jun measured belief. Nadi measured fuel. Belief didn't open throats. Fuel did.

The auction bay at pre-dawn was where pilots learned what the wall already knew. Nadi stood at the public board with coffee gone cold and read fringe premium before Jun's animation rendered—four points lower than long corridor emergency math, six points higher than yesterday, ugly-beautiful green in the margin column. Separatist pamphlets at the lip. Wrong-border fee table on the back. Story tax on housing ticked up overnight. Reroute density twenty-two percent on the ribbon, twenty-four by the time she reached clamp four.

She didn't need Jun to tell her fear had a shape.

She needed fuel, a manifest, and a corridor that didn't eat her quarter.

Her comm lit with BRICK before she lifted.

*Separatists definitely. Cousin says internal memo.*

Nadi typed one-handed: **Cousins sell memos. I sell delivery.**

*You're flying fringe again.*

**Viable.**

*You're crazy.*

BRICK sent the usual one-word reply. Nadi almost smiled.

Crew banter was the Markets' other wall—the one that didn't update in public, only in dialect between people who burned fuel for a living.

On the public ribbon Jun's cascade chart had landed—separatist cluster, confidence climbing, elegant curves that made fear look intelligent. Nadi had seen the shape on the auction bay display before publication. The wall had priced it first. Jun got credit for describing weather already raining.

Nadi didn't mind Jun.

She minded Jun thinking description was early.

Dock clamp four before lift: someone had checked the housing—Nadi ran her fingers along the clamp housing, felt the fresh torque seal, nodded once. Plot work, not spectacle. The foreman stamped her manifest. Separatist podcast noise leaked from a kiosk—*definitely, definitely*—and she muted it with her thumb. The lie was already priced into fringe. She didn't need the soundtrack.

She lifted on schedule because schedule was a fiction you enforced with thrust.

The Markets fell away—stations bright, dice thrown, fees still climbing on boards she'd stopped watching because watching without flying was berth burn. Drift at pilot altitude wasn't a market. It was a swarm: modules the size of cities drifting on economic currents, throat openings like mouths that forgot to close, freight lines braided between stations that orbited no star. From the cockpit *Quick Margin* looked like an insect crossing a cathedral floor. Nadi had flown fringe enough to find that comforting. Insects got through arches. Cathedrals didn't care.

Below her a throat opened—brief wrong-light visible even at distance—and swallowed a freighter. Another throat delayed with *ADMINISTRATIVE* scrolling beside a name that wasn't hers. Reroute density on the nav ribbon looked like capillaries under stress, Vesper's silence a blank box in the mesh every pilot pretended wasn't the center of the map.

She believed fuel.

She believed the fringe fee schedule that hadn't spiked yet because not enough pilots were desperate enough to bid it up.

She believed the gate network was stressed—not broken, stressed—the way a corridor hummed before it cleared, the way admins said *administrative* when they meant *we don't know and won't say*.

BRICK: *Premium client lawyers watching tracking.*

**Tell lawyers tracking's green.**

*Green and?*

**Green and none of your business.**

Respect ping. Fringe entry approaching. Loneliness ahead, billable if misread.

Ground truth in a cockpit: pressure without catastrophe. Reroutes without explosions. Checked clamps on dock housing from hands steadier than hers.

Not sabotage-shaped. Neglect-shaped. Hurry-shaped. The shape of a civilization forgetting to notice until noticing cost more than fees.

Fringe entry was where the network showed you its bones.

Too few ships. Too much silence. Nadi had flown fringe for three years and still felt small every time. The corridor opened like a hallway pretending to be a throat: too straight, too quiet, admin geometry that made *Quick Margin* feel like a coin dropped down a well. No Ribbon noise. No Markets chatter. No Jun on a ribbon explaining weather already raining. Just pressure and echo and the hum of something vast being asked to hold more than its maintainers had been funded to check.

From inside a fringe approach the gate mesh wasn't a map. It was stress made visible—reroute density bleeding into throats that weren't designed to announce weight, Vesper silent somewhere in the lattice. Not broken. Not speaking. Stressed the way a hull seam stressed before it cracked: slowly, greenly, with fee clerks calling it administrative until pilots noted the flicker.

BRICK pinged: *You alive?*

**Alive. Fringe being fringe.**

*Gate network's stressed not dead,* he wrote. *Admin friend says wait state.*

**Wait state pays analysts. I fly.**

*Separatists blew intake housing.*

**Intake housing got checked**, Nadi sent. **Clamp seal fresh. Steadier hand than mine.**

*You think keepers did it?*

**I think fuel gauge is green.**

That ended the theology. BRICK sent respect instead of argument.

*How's loneliness?* BRICK wrote.

**Billable if I misread it.**

*Send coords if you bail.*

**If I bail you'll auction my manifest.**

*Obviously.*

She almost smiled. Almost.

Nadi kept one hand on fuel and one on the comms. Echo came back wrong—a half-second delay that shouldn't exist in a throat this short. Silence lasted a beat too long between her ping and the corridor's answer. The adjacency sensor held green and wrong green in the same breath, the kind of reading keepers logged and fee clerks ignored.

She ran the numbers she trusted: fuel burn acceptable, margin holding, premium on the other side if she didn't chicken out. Fringe wasn't heroism. It was a fee schedule with loneliness attached and a gauge that told the truth its bulletin didn't.

The corridor stretched. Admin geometry pressed. *Quick Margin* felt smaller than she was—three years of patched hull, dock collision memory on port side, creative routes paying for the next repair. From here the gate network wasn't history or politics. It was pressure in her bones: reroute density like weight on old joints, Vesper silent in the lattice, throats asked to hold more than maintainers had been funded to check.

Wonder at pilot altitude wasn't pretty. It was scale without comfort—the mesh vast enough to make a cockpit feel like a coin, stress visible only as flicker and echo and the hum of something that had worked for centuries because someone kept checking while everyone else priced fear.

Nadi flew the first fringe crossing the way she flew everything: fuel first, archive second, theology never. The corridor gave her nothing to look at except readings and echo and the pressure tick that honest gauges admitted when bulletins didn't. She counted seconds between ping and return. She counted flickers. She counted margin on the other side if she didn't bail.

BRICK tried theology again at the worst moment: *My cousin says Vesper intake.*

**Your cousin sells memos. I sell delivery.**

*Marks on intake aren't memos.*

**Checked clamps are checking. Gauge is green. I'm flying.**

Respect ping. Silence. Then: *You're noting this for Lena?*

**I'm noting this for the next pilot who chickens out.**

*Lena's the compass patch courier.*

**Lena's not my margin.**

Fair enough. Fringe didn't care about brothers. Fringe cared about loneliness priced below bankruptcy and readings that lied politely and fuel gauges that didn't.

Halfway through, the adjacency sensor flickered.

Green. Wrong green. The kind of reading that meant pressure not catastrophe—the gauge telling the truth its fee clerks didn't want filed. Nadi didn't panic. Panic burned fuel.

She slowed. Logged coordinates. Photographed the console with her glove edge in frame—the habit of someone who'd learned archives outlived bulletins. Sent the packet to her private map with dock clamp photos from last week: fresh seals, third check steadiest yet.

BRICK again: *Reading?*

**Flicker. Green. Logged.**

*Admin geometry?*

**Admin geometry.**

*You're still flying.*

**Still flying.**

She flew on because stopping in fringe cost more than wrong readings when no alarm followed. Neglect-shaped. Not sabotage-shaped. The shape of a network forgetting to notice until noticing cost more than story tax.

The corridor narrowed. Pressure ticked on the gauge—not red, not alarm, the honest twitch of a system carrying more reroute than its bulletin admitted. Nadi ran through the checklist she'd learned the hard way: fuel burn holding, loader secure, archive packet staged, BRICK muted until he had something useful besides cousin theology.

Echo returned late again. Half second. Then three quarters. The adjacency sensor flickered a third time—green, wrong green, polite lie—and held.

*Third flicker?* BRICK.

**Third flicker. Logged.**

*You're going to get us both killed.*

**You're going to miss the premium window.**

Silence. Then respect.

The mesh leaned around her. Not broken—Nadi would have known broken, broken had a taste like metal and alarm tones and claim forms. This was stress: centuries of gate network asked to carry reroute density while Vesper stayed **wait** and administrators practiced words that didn't cost admission. From the cockpit it felt like flying inside someone's deferred maintenance budget.

She noted pressure ticks the way keepers logged checks—coordinates, time, glove edge in frame—not for Jun, for the next pilot who might chicken out. Ground truth was pilot dialect: flicker, echo, fee spike, clamp seal, fringe **viable**, long corridor **emergency only**.

She flew on margins she'd earned in silence.

BRICK: *Reading still green?*

**Green and wrong.**

*Meaning?*

**Meaning pressure not catastrophe. Meaning fly or pay berth.**

*Admin friend says wait state.*

**Admin friend isn't flying fringe.**

*Fair.*

Stress without break. That was the altitude lesson: from a cockpit you didn't see gates fail. You felt them lean. The mesh held—had to hold, had held for centuries—but holding wasn't the same as healthy. Neglect-shaped readings didn't shout sabotage. They whispered *someone should check this* in a dialect fee clerks ignored.

Nadi flew.

The flicker came again—same wrong green, same half-second lie. She noted it, photographed the console, added coordinates to her private archive beside dock clamp photos from last week. Not theory. Habit. Ground truth pilots sold to each other when walls sold fear.

*Still alive?* BRICK.

**Still billing.**

*Respect.*

Not Nadi's job.

BRICK one last ping before dock: *Fringe still crazy?*

**Fringe still viable.**

*Long corridor?*

Respect ping. Dock approach. Loader waking sullen. Markets noise flooding back in—fees, auctions, separatist kiosks, the beautiful ugly swarm of Drift doing what Drift did best: moving money before narrative caught up.

Business decision, not heroism.

Commit to route knowledge—her knowledge, earned in silence and flicker and dock checks—not Jun's chart, not separatist pamphlets, not BRICK's cousin's Tuesday.

The wall at the auction bay showed reroute density up twenty-four percent. Someone had stopped lying about eighteen. Story tax on housing ticked higher. Wrong-border pamphlets still two for one.

At dock clamp four the fee board had jumped four points since lift—fringe premium climbing because she'd proved the corridor again and proof was a product in the Drift. Separatist chatter spilled from a kiosk speaker—definitely, definitely, borders updated before lunch—and Nadi muted it with her thumb because she'd heard the lie priced into the board already.
