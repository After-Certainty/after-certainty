# Chapter 21 — I See You Seeing

Cai counted marks on the approach corridor before dawn because counting was how he thought.

Fourteen yesterday. Eleven fresh overnight from night shift—good hands, consistent angles. Two vendor patches flagged for rework. One tool slip scarred through an old dot and had been refreshed beside the damage instead of over it, which told him Lio was learning the grammar beyond the stylus.

The First Gate hummed in the deck plates.

He trusted the hum.

He did not trust what trust meant before coffee.

---

The thread wall in the keeper break room was nobody's job and everybody's weather.

**SEPARATIST SABOTAGE — EIGHTY-TWO PERCENT**

**ROUTINE MAINTENANCE — SEVEN PERCENT**

Lio read over Cai's shoulder. "Seven's up a point."

"People read marks when couriers ask loud enough." Cai heated water. "Doesn't mean they believe them."

"Courier's back today. The long-route one."

"I know." Cai had seen the berth notice cross the crew board. "She asks about Arin. She asks about Vesper. She asks like questions are cargo."

"Is that bad?"

"It's a job." Cai poured coffee into a cup that had lost its logo years ago. "Different from mine."

The night shift keeper—Corridor Four in Cai's head, real name Dessa when she appeared at housing 9-A with a thermos and a grudge against vendor polish—nodded at him across the break table.

"You counting my marks again?" she asked.

"Appreciating them." Cai slid his log over. "Tool slip on manifold two. You refreshed beside the scar."

"Grammar matters." Dessa poured coffee black. "Supervisor says we're over-logging."

"Supervisor can climb."

She almost smiled. "Courier still asking loud questions?"

"Still learning to read." Cai glanced at the crew board where Lena's berth extension had posted. "Makes the thread move a point. Doesn't fund roster hours."

"Nothing funds roster hours except shame or catastrophe." Dessa capped her thermos. "We're aiming for shame. Cheaper."

---

Housing 7-C first.

Green board: **WITHIN TOLERANCE**.

Gauge: inside published band by microns that would matter in autumn load.

Keeper: rework the vendor patch before autumn load anyway.

Lio wrote the flag note in full sentences because Cai had trained him to write for the archive person who might someday care. The Toma query chain had produced one reply—not from supervisor, from a guild remnant inbox auto-forward:

**CROSSWALK RECEIVED — HISTORICAL ROSTER MATCHES CURRENT SHIFT GAPS — THANK YOU — TOMA**

A thank-you from an archivist was practically applause.

Cai forwarded it to Lio without comment. Evidence that looking backward and looking at seals could rhyme.

---

The ecosystem showed itself when you knew how to read a corridor.

Mark at the manifold lip—dot, stroke, angle family three. Not Cai's angle. Night shift keeper he called *Corridor Four* in his head because he'd never been introduced.

Mark at the pressure guide—Cai's, yesterday, refreshed by Lio on the second pass because apprentices learned by doing.

Mark at the crew intake housing—older, worn soft at the edges, date code from a keeper who'd retired before Cai's first climb. Still legible. Still honored.

Mark at 7-C—two hands, two angles, *I see you seeing* without words.

An ecosystem of attention so easy to forget because it worked quietly.

Lena met him at housing 9-A with her bag and a question that wasn't really a question.

"Fourteen marks yesterday," she said.

"Fourteen verified sites. Some had more than one mark." He opened the housing. "You counting?"

"I'm learning to read." She leaned in, careful of the harness zone. "Toma says the guild thinned over decades. You say budgets cut last quarter. Sera says communities put names on rosters when keepers die. Same shape?"

"Same slope." Cai ran the gauge. "Different rooms."

"Jun says sabotage cluster fits eighty percent of the signal."

"Jun sells clusters." Cai didn't look up. "I sell seals. If sabotage were real, adjacent corridors would show signature asymmetry. They don't. Vesper's quiet isn't a blown housing. It's coordination failure upstream—roster hours, vendor boxes, silence where no official word should be."

"You could publish that."

"So could administrators." He photographed a clean seam, logged it, made a dot anyway because checked was checked. "They publish green."

---

The keeper counter-narrative on the thread was late and weak.

Cai had seen the posts—pilots who remembered marks, one freight captain citing fringe corridor viability, a maintenance log excerpt someone had leaked without context. **ROUTINE MAINTENANCE** crawled from five percent to seven, not because evidence had changed, because fear had fatigued.

Belief cascades didn't reverse on proof. They slowed on repetition.

On the public thread, a pilot Lena didn't know had posted a photograph of a dock mark with the caption *someone was here before the panic*. The comments argued about separatists anyway. **ROUTINE MAINTENANCE** crawled another half point while Cai watched and said nothing.

Lio wanted to post the housing 7-C flag series. Cai told him no.

"People will think we're campaigning."

"We're checking."

"We're keepers." Cai closed the housing panel. "Campaigning is what analysts do. We leave marks. If the marks survive, someone learns the grammar."

"That's slow."

"Yes."

---

Midshift, Cai walked the corridor with Lio and counted aloud—not superstition, inventory.

Manifold lip: verified.

Pressure guide: verified.

Crew intake: verified, legacy mark honored.

Freight rail brace: new flag, vibration wear, autumn problem.

Housing 7-C: vendor patch queued for rework, marks from three hands.

Housing 9-A: clean, dot anyway.

Each site logged. Each photograph uploaded into an archive almost nobody opened, which was the insult and the faith in one file format.

Lena caught them at housing 9-A with her bag and a question that wasn't really a question. in the form of a meeting invite and a vendor liaison with polished shoes.

Conference room glass overlooked the aperture—bad design, Cai thought. Tourists above, keepers on scaffolds below, administrators in between believing visibility was work.

The funding sheet projected on the wall rhymed with the one he'd shown the courier—roster cuts, vendor contracts, quarterly confidence slides.

"We're allocating visibility resources," the administrator said. "Public reassurance during Vesper suspension."

"Publish tolerances," Cai said. "Publish mark schedules. Fund roster hours."

"We've heard your position."

"It's not a position. It's a seal."

The vendor liaison smiled. "Our teams meet all published standards."

"Your teams meet boxes." Cai kept his voice level because level was cheaper than rage and rage got you replaced. "Keepers meet corridors."

Lena wasn't in the room—couriers didn't get invites—but her question lived in his head anyway: *why don't they teach the grammar publicly?*

Because grammar required admitting someone had to learn it.

Because learning required hours.

Because hours required lines on a funding sheet that didn't fit quarterly slides.

He left the meeting without victory and with the quiet anger that named exactly which seal nobody wanted to hear about.

In the corridor outside, Lena waited with her courier badge and the expression of someone who'd learned not to expect theaters to change weather.

"They offered visibility budget," Cai said.

"They didn't offer roster hours."

"No."

"Jun's briefing tomorrow."

"I know." He clipped his kit. "Analysts need stages. Keepers need ladders."

"Will you be on the ladder or in the room?"

"Ladder." No hesitation. "Seals don't improve in meetings."

---

He found Lena at the viewport deck at shift end, where tourists took pictures and keepers took measurements.

She had her slate open—not the thread wall, a map with fringe corridors marked in pilot handwriting he didn't recognize. Nadi's work, maybe. Ground truth from someone who didn't care why if the fee cleared.

"You went to the meeting," she said.

"Nobody wins those."

"You sound like Sera."

"Cai sounded like Cai." He stood beside her, not close. "Pilgrim says people need stories. Keeper says people need seals. Courier says people need routes." He watched a hauler thread the aperture. "We're complementary jobs. Not the same job."

Lena turned the slate so he could see—Orinreach Basin marked eleven days out by long route, Vesper still blank, Arin's last message timestamped. "I need all three. Story to walk on. Seal to trust. Route to arrive."

"You'll get two some days."

"Today?"

He thought about fourteen marks. Two vendor flags. Zero catastrophes. Toma's thank-you. Pell's chalkboard. Mer teaching Jory before winter.

"Today you get seals," he said. "And a hum you can feel in your teeth. Don't confuse it with proof."

"That's the most honest thing anyone's said to me since Northhook."

"Don't spread rumors."

She almost smiled. "Cai, what are you afraid of?"

He looked at the aperture—ancient, maintained, humming. "That the hum outlasts the keepers who listen to it. That habits pass for safety until load shifts. That zero catastrophes reads like permission to cut the roster again."

"And you still climb."

"Tomorrow I climb." He clipped his kit. "That's the job. Being right isn't enough. Being here is barely enough. Still—" He touched the viewport glass, wiped the smudge. "Still I climb."

---

Lena had returned from Two Valleys that morning with soup smell still in her hair and chalk on her fingers. She told him about Pell's roster and Mer teaching Jory before winter without sermonizing, which Cai appreciated. Pilgrims sermonized. Couriers reported.

"Same marks," he said when she finished. "Different housing."

"Same grammar," she said. "Jun's briefing tomorrow. Will you say seals again?"

"If asked." He looked at the aperture. "Analysts want clusters. I have tolerances."

"Nadi will be there."

"Nadi has ledgers." Almost a smile. "You have routes. Toma has slope. Sera has soup. We're an ecosystem nobody funded."

"That's a sentence for a log, not a wall."

"Logs survive walls." He started toward the lift. "Come back after. I'll show you housing 9-A if you're still counting."

"I'm still counting."

---

Night shift took the corridor at dusk.

Cai logged the handoff—sites pending, vendor rework queued, Lio's notes attached with full sentences. The thread wall in the break room had ticked again: **ROUTINE MAINTENANCE — EIGHT PERCENT**.

Late. Weak.

A point anyway.

He walked home along the approach deck feeling the hum in his bones—trusted, feared, insufficient, necessary.

Somewhere Vesper stayed silent.

Somewhere a courier reserved a long route.

Somewhere an archivist struck names through and called it slope.

Somewhere a pilgrim ladled soup.

Somewhere a child taught another child a mark before winter.

The ecosystem of attention persisted without permission from administrators or analysts or thread walls.

Cai was still afraid.

He set his alarm for dawn anyway.

In the morning he would climb again.

---

He dreamed in tolerances—not failure, never failure, only margins narrowing while slides stayed green.

When the alarm sounded, the hum was still there.

That was the whole bargain keepers made with civilization: climb while the hum holds; log when it doesn't; trust nothing that can't be gauged.

Cai made coffee and went to work.

---

At housing 7-C the vendor patch waited like an unpaid invoice.

He would cut it before noon. Lio would write full sentences. Dessa on night shift would add her angle beside his. The green board would still say **WITHIN TOLERANCE**.

The hum would still be there.

Cai trusted the hum.

He did not trust what trust meant.

He climbed anyway.

---

Jun's briefing filled the afternoon news ribbon—**ANALYST REVISES MODEL**—without changing the wall percentages much.

Cai read the headline on the scaffold lip and went back to the gauge.

Headlines were weather.

Seals were work.

Housing 7-C still needed the patch cut.

He cut it.

Lena texted from the berth corridor—*Jun broke. Nadi had ledgers. I had slope. You had seals. Thank you.*—and Cai stared at the message long enough to annoy the scaffold safety AI before typing back: **FOURTEEN MARKS TODAY IF TRAFFIC ALLOWS. DON'T CONFUSE HUM WITH PROOF.**

He sent it.

He climbed.

That was the whole answer he had.

Fourteen marks if traffic allowed.

Two vendor flags if vendors kept cutting corners.

Zero catastrophes if the hum held and the roster didn't thin again before autumn load.

Cai put the comm away and touched the housing seam—dot, stroke, date code—and went to find Lio before intake four got impatient with truth.
