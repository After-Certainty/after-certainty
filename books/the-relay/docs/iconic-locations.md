## The Ribbon

**Theme:** How meaning moves · **Act I anchor** · Lena's home port

A looping orbital ribbon thousands of kilometers long — not a wheel. Interior surface holds cities, forests, lakes, transit lines. At certain curves, other districts hang overhead.

**Canonical image:**

> The far shore of Lake Orin wasn't a shore at all. It was another district, forty kilometers away, suspended overhead by the curvature of the Ribbon. At night its lights reflected in the water below while their own reflected in the water above. Visitors often became dizzy trying to determine which city was real and which was merely its reflection.

**Social practices (only possible here):**

- **Meeting above:** appointments on opposite curvature — friends wave across sky-gap
- **Upside-down geography:** children learn overhead districts by name
- **Navigation by reflection:** reading the city in water above and below
- **Paired festivals:** opposite districts synchronize celebrations across the curve

**Daily life:** maintenance kites between habitat mirrors; seasonal migrations inside the ribbon; transit lines along the inner surface.

**Implication rule:** name kites, mirrors, reflected districts in use — don't explain engineering.

## Orinreach Basin

**Theme:** Depth · **Arin's world** · one place held completely

A curved habitat world on the gate mesh — southern basin, mirror-navigation corridors, storm seasons. Named for its reach toward Lake Orin's trade orbit (historical, not geographic proximity). Locals say *the Basin*; couriers file it as **Orinreach Basin**.

> Orinreach Basin at evening was a place of depths — real stone and reflected stone, the city's particular trick of making you feel both grounded and duplicated.

**Gate link:** **Vesper Gate** is the primary corridor Arin and Lena assume for southern routes.

**Social practices:**

- **Reflection navigation:** mirrors make paths honest; tourists treat them as games
- **Storm cycles:** maintenance rosters swell seasonally; funding does not
- **Southern mirrors:** Arin's district — catwalks, ladders, knees saved before policy

## The Drift Markets

**Theme:** Incentives · **Nadi's home**

Trade stations orbiting no star, drifting between systems on economic currents.

> Every wall carried arrivals. Departures. Exchange rates. Freight auctions. Entire fortunes rose and fell before breakfast. Nobody asked where you came from in the Drift Markets. The answer changed too often to matter.

## The Archive Moon

**Theme:** Memory · **Toma's station**

Small moon dedicated to record-keeping — libraries, vaults, memory gardens. Mountains hollowed for storage.

> The oldest records were stored beneath three kilometers of stone. Nobody expected them to survive a war. The builders had expected them to survive civilizations.

Forgetting is civic failure here.

## The Pilgrim Road

**Theme:** Community / motivation · **Sera's route**

Not a planet — a route of stations and worlds linked by tradition. People travel for years by choice.

> Messages moved faster through the quantum relays. Everybody knew that. The pilgrims carried something else. Nobody could explain exactly what it was, only that settlements visited by pilgrims seemed somehow less alone.

## The First Gate

**Theme:** Who stays when everyone else leaves · **Cai's work** · climax location

Oldest surviving gate. Ancient **and maintained** — stranger than ruins.

> The gate occupied the space where a moon ought to have been. Ships crossed its aperture like insects passing through the archway of a cathedral. Every surface gleamed with evidence of continuous repair. Whatever else humanity had forgotten, someone had remembered this place.

**Prose rule:** Enter through character on scaffold (Cai, Lio). Narration uses gate · crossing · scaffold · passage—not coined symbol labels.

**Social practices:**

- **Keeper shifts:** scaffold crews, vendor flags, zero catastrophes as honest metric
- **Tourism vs work:** tourists photograph the aperture; keepers call the approach *the bones*
- **Stay leg:** Cai stays; couriers cross

## Gate network (backdrop)

**Theme:** Hidden assumptions

Connective tissue. Disruptions cascade. People assume autonomy; history suggests otherwise.

**Naming convention:** Gates use astronomical or temporal names (**Vesper Gate**, Meridian Gate, Zenith Gate). Settlements use geographical names (**Orinreach Basin**, Two Valleys, Northhook, Meridian Arc). Consistency is felt, not explained on page.

**Story present:** **Vesper Gate** suspended without official explanation — cascade trigger for the novel.
