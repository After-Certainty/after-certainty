# Voice specification — The Relay

## Tone

Clear, elegant, reflective, visual, emotionally grounded. Wonder and melancholy; hope without cynicism. Contemporary diction — not ornate, not jargon-heavy.

## Implication over explanation

One vivid unexplained detail beats a paragraph of mechanism.

> A flock of maintenance kites drifted between the habitat mirrors, their silver wings catching sunlight from a star thirty light-years away.

No glossary. Reader infers scale.

## Dialogue

Velorum-inspired: subtext, disagreement, conversational. Characters rarely name themes. Ideas through friction.

**Per-character registers:** see [character-bible.md](./character-bible.md) and **Profanity** below.

**Plain dialogue on polish:** speakable under stress; stakes before ornament. Readability passes flatten **metaphor and jargon**, not **heat** — see [agents/01-dialogue-agent.md](./agents/01-dialogue-agent.md).

## Profanity

Adapted from [Velorum voice-spec](../../velorum/docs/voice-spec.md) — same principle: **experience stays lyrical; speech stays contemporary and human.**

### Split narration vs speech

- **Narration** (especially Lena's reflective camera): comparatively clean — wonder, place, interior — not curse-laden.
- **Dialogue** carries heat: docks, berths, pilots, siblings under strain, administrators dodging questions.

### When swearing belongs

- Conflict, fear, absurd bureaucracy, fees, gate silence, public-pressure beats.
- Humor under load (courier hangar, Drift Markets wall banter).
- **Not** every page. Escalate with scene; don't carpet-bomb.

### Approved register (dialogue)

**Light / frequent:** *hell*, *damn*, *shit*, *bullshit*, *no shit*, *Goddamnit*, *God damn* — when stress, humor, or contempt earn them.

**Heavy (sparing, scene-earned):** *fuck*, *fucking hell*, *fuck almighty* — pilots, couriers, keepers when the mask cracks; **not** in Sera/Jun/Toma default voice.

**Avoid:** sanitizing to *heck* / *darn* on polish passes. **Avoid:** body-part insults unless a future scene earns harm (this book has no explicit sex; insults are not default texture).

**Vary shape:** standalone breath, opener, mid-line hammer — lived speech, not one repeated tic.

### Per-character heat (dialogue)

| Voice | Profanity |
|-------|-----------|
| **Lena** | Moderate when impatient — clerks, fees, silence; sharp, not constant. *Bullshit* at administrators; rare *fuck* when fear breaks through. |
| **Nadi** | **Highest heat** in cast — margins, delays, bad charts; blunt consequence-first; *hell* / *shit* / *fuck* when the wall lies. |
| **Arin** | Dry warmth; rare *hell*; saves bluntness for tourists and budget reviews. |
| **Dael / pilots / dock workers** | Hangar-real; can match Nadi in banter. |
| **Cai** | Quiet until angry — administrator fights; rare *fuck* when seals are ignored (see exploratory Ch. 5 close). |
| **Jun** | Almost never; precision instead. Corin might slip *hell* as a student. |
| **Sera** | Very rare; cuts without swearing. |
| **Toma / Veya** | Measured; profanity out of character unless broken. |
| **Administrators** | Smooth euphemism — politeness as blade; swearing only when mask slips. |

### Brittany / read-aloud

Swearing is **not** a readability failure. If Speechify sounds stiff, add **spoken** heat in dialogue before simplifying vocabulary elsewhere.

## POV

Third-person limited. One camera per chapter. Lena primary; others when necessary.

## Worldbuilding on the page

- Places before systems
- Ribbon culture practices lived, not lectured
- No "As everyone knows..." history

## Anti-patterns

- Technobabble, exposition dumps, purple prose
- Theme lectures in dialogue
- Generic corridors and stations
- **Staff-engineer voice** in non-specialist POV — see [agents/14-brittany-agent.md](./agents/14-brittany-agent.md) · [agents/15-language-drift-agent.md](./agents/15-language-drift-agent.md)

## Accessibility rule

> Future technology should be easier to understand than present technology.

Prefer *the gate stopped answering* over incident-management fields. Prefer *no official word* over *no reason code* in narration and bulletins unless a specialist is quoting a screen verbatim — then ground it immediately in plain speech.
