# Guide 3 — World Design

*The Relay Design Handbook · the civilization. Implements the Foundational World Assumptions in the [Novel Constitution](./01-novel-constitution.md) (frozen) and makes the failure in [Plot & Integration](./02-plot-and-integration.md) (frozen) physically possible. Owns the world as a system; does not own artifacts (Guide 4), people (Guide 5), or prose (Guide 6).*

> **Status:** **Draft v1.2 — structural decisions locked; cultural details still open.** Not frozen, but the load-bearing world decisions are no longer fluid: the failure mechanism (uncommunicated local corrections compounding), two speeds of travel, the climax-site exclusion (Vesper, not the Cardinal Crossing), and the species *count* (exactly two, culture-first, decoupled from Move/Stay). Still open and genuinely fluid: the two species' names and finishing cultural detail, and the world count. Items marked **[DECISION]** are author-level calls.

World Design does not invent gadgets, governments, or species for their own sake. It builds a civilization that **feels old and lived-in**, and whose machinery for learning has decayed in a way that makes the plot inevitable. Everything here serves one law from the parking lot: **the physical failure must mirror the social failure.**

---

## Design Philosophy

*Written first, because it governs every concrete detail below. When inventing anything — a food, a doorway, a holiday — check it against these.*

Three questions the world must always be answering:

- **What makes it feel old?** Layers. Nothing here was built once and left alone; everything has been repaired, repurposed, re-meaninged. A gate plaza is also a market is also a shrine because seven centuries piled uses on it. Age is shown through *accretion and wear*, not through dates.
- **What makes it feel lived-in?** Workarounds. Real people have quietly adapted around every official system — the unofficial path everyone takes, the form nobody fills out correctly, the slang that replaced the proper term. Lived-in is shown through *the gap between how things are supposed to work and how they actually work.*
- **What should readers constantly notice?** That the civilization is **enormous, working, and not understood by anyone in it** — and that this used to be fine. The recurring sensation is competent people maintaining things whose origins they no longer know.

From these flow food, architecture, markets, humor, holidays, and signage. **Do not invent a detail that does not express age, lived-in adaptation, or the working-but-unexplained sensation.** That rule is the guard against decoration.

A standing requirement (from the parking lot): **What does humanity systematically fail to notice?** Humans are a species, not the invisible default. **[DECISION] Proposed:** humans over-trust the *written and the recent* and under-notice the *slow and the tacit* — they believe a thing is known once it is recorded, and mistake the absence of a record for the absence of a fact. This blind spot is the cultural root of the whole failure, and it must shape human spaces, schools, and bureaucracies. Other species' blind spots are defined with each species below.

## The Relay — the literal network

*(The bottom rung of the meaning ladder: transport. The word will migrate; the thing it starts as is defined here.)*

- **Shape.** A mesh of **gates** linking many worlds — not an empire, not a hub-and-spoke, a web. There is no capital, no central authority that truly coordinates it, though most people assume there is. **The exact world count is deliberately left unspecified** (Le Guin never numbers the Ekumen); "many" is the spec. If a scene ever benefits from a number, it can be chosen then — it is not load-bearing.
- **Age.** Roughly seven centuries old. Built across generations, by people whose methods are partly lost. It is **inherited infrastructure** — used fluently, understood shallowly.
- **How it is kept alive (officially).** Local **keepers** tend local gates (Cai's world). Couriers (Lena) and pilots (Nadi) move goods, people, and word between worlds.
- **Two speeds of travel — and what "dark" means.** A gate is an *instant doorway*; the fast network is the gates. But worlds remain reachable the **slow way** — by ship, along the ordinary routes between worlds that pilots like Nadi fly (days to weeks, not instants). When a gate **goes dark**, it severs the *fast* connection, **not all connection**: a darkened world is cut off from the instant mesh but can still be reached the slow way, at a cost in time and risk. This is load-bearing. It is how Arin's stayer-web trades observations at all while the gates fail; how people and the first new-form manifests physically reach a dark crossing to converge there; and why the recovery "spreads the way real things spread" — slowly, the hard way, by ship and hand — *before* the gates themselves come back. (A *dark* gate is closed traffic; a *failing* gate is the symptom; the slow routes are the lifeline that keeps the climax physically possible.)
- **How it was *actually* kept alive (the lost part).** For most of its history, the mesh adapted because a now-decayed practice carried *observations* between worlds — what each keeper noticed was relayed to where it could be combined. This was the human relay before anyone called it that. Its institutions (relay guilds, oversight networks) eroded by assumption, not design. **This is the engine of the plot:** the mesh is a learning system that forgot it was one.

### The failure — the law, not the mechanism

**Fixed (do not change):** the physical failure must **mirror the social failure** — a breakdown in moving observation into adaptation. Whatever the gates do when they fail must be the same *shape* as a civilization that has stopped relaying what it notices.

**LOCKED (resolved during the synopsis pressure-test):** the mechanism is **uncommunicated local corrections compounding.** Every crossing needs constant small adjustments to stay aligned with the rest of the mesh. Historically, keepers reported these adjustments into the relay practice and they were reconciled across worlds. With that practice decayed, each world keeps correcting **locally and blind** — and the corrections drift out of sync, interact, and fight each other across the mesh. World A compensates for a fault that World B's correction caused; B re-corrects for A's; over generations the adjustments accumulate into a non-local instability that surfaces first at the weakest, most-trafficked crossing (Vesper). There is no saboteur and no single broken part. The cause **is** the sum of everyone's invisible, uncoordinated corrections — which is exactly why no single world can see it, let alone fix it.

This is the cleanest possible mirror of the social disease: a civilization that stopped relaying what it notices, expressed as machinery that stopped relaying how it is being maintained. The two earlier candidates are **folded in as texture, not competing theories**: the corrections are undocumented (candidate 2 — "just this once" workarounds nobody logged), and the skill of cross-world reconciliation died with the guilds (candidate 3 — lost calibration knowledge is *why* the corrections can no longer be reconciled). They are facets of the one mechanism, not alternatives to it.

**What this commits the recovery to:** the gates are not repaired part-by-part. They recover only when the corrections become **visible to each other again** so the people maintaining them can stop working at cross-purposes (see Plot's climax and Artifacts' open manifest). The fix is restored *circulation of local knowledge*, not a central repair — which is the whole point.

## The civilization-learning model

How this society learns, remembers, and forgets — the substrate the plot stresses.

- **Knowledge is distributed and largely tacit.** The people who know how things work mostly cannot articulate it; it lives in hands and habits (Cai), routes (Nadi), and long local attention (Arin).
- **Memory is over-invested in the written.** Because of the human blind spot, the civilization trusts records (Toma's archives) and mistrusts the unrecorded — so when the *practice* of relaying observation lapsed, no one noticed, because nothing was written down to be missing.
- **Transmission decays silently — and unevenly.** Skills pass by apprenticeship (Cai→Lio). **A living line like Cai→Lio is not proof the system still works — it is proof that *some* local lines survived.** What broke is not apprenticeship everywhere; it is the **relay *between* local lines** — the lost mechanism by which tacit knowledge once travelled, was compared, and adapted *across* worlds. A keeper can still train a successor flawlessly to tend *this* gate while the civilization-wide ability to reconcile what all the keepers separately know has quietly died. (This is the human-scale face of the failure mechanism: each line keeps correcting locally and blind.) When such a cross-world line breaks, the loss is invisible for a generation — there is no alarm for "we have stopped being able to do this *together*."
- **Meaning travels faster than fact.** Stories, broadcasts, and reputations move quickly across the mesh; verification does not. This is the medium Varik exploits and Jun studies.

## Belief vs. truth

What the civilization assumes versus what is so. The gap *is* the mystery.

| People widely believe… | The truth is… |
|---|---|
| The gates are self-maintaining. | They are kept alive by human stewardship that has quietly thinned. |
| Someone at the center coordinates the mesh. | There is no functioning center; coordination was always distributed and has eroded. |
| A failure means sabotage or an enemy. | The failure is emergent and non-local; there is no culprit. |
| The records contain what we know. | The most important knowledge was never written; its loss left no gap in the archive. |
| If it has worked for centuries, it will keep working. | It worked because of a practice that has now lapsed. |

## Species and cultures

**LOCKED: humans plus exactly two non-human intelligent species.** Small on purpose — two can be specific; five would be decoration. Designed **culture-first**, under the Constitution's two absolute rules: (1) species **enrich** culture, customs, history, architecture, humor, and communication, and **never** determine personality, morality, intelligence, politics, or plot function; and (2) **the species must not re-encode the Move/Stay structure** — neither is "the place species" or "the movement species," because that axis is human-anchored (Lena moves, Arin stays). Their job is to make the civilization feel older, more cohabited, and more *epistemically plural along other axes*. Individuals vary within each species as widely as humans do. *(Names remain TBD — discovered, per the naming rule; the two cultural sketches below are the working implementation, still World Design's to finalize.)*

For each species, World Design fixes only: a cultural signature, a communication texture, a contribution to the shared civilization, and a **systematic blind spot** (so none is wise or complete). Personality stays with the Character Guide.

- **Humans.** Signature: over-trust the written and the recent. Blind spot: mistake "recorded" for "known" and "unrecorded" for "untrue." (Lena and Arin are human.)
- **Species A — the chorus *(working sketch; name TBD).*** Signature: a culture of **collective, simultaneous deliberation** — they think out loud together, holding a question open among many overlapping voices until a shape emerges, and find the lone authoritative speaker faintly indecent. Communication texture: layered, many-voiced, slow to resolve, rich in shared ritual. Contribution: the civilization's habits of consensus, council, and ceremony. **Blind spot:** they undervalue the single dissenter who turns out right, and a comfortable consensus can ratify an error for a long time. *(Epistemically plural — a living image of distributed knowing — but with a real failure mode, so not the "wise species"; and orthogonal to Move/Stay.)*
- **Species B — the revisers *(working sketch; name TBD).*** Signature: a culture built around **the honorable correction** — being wrong, openly, is a mark of standing; they keep their *changes*, not their conclusions, and their records are palimpsests meant to be overwritten. Communication texture: provisional, hedged, full of "as of now." Contribution: the civilization's craft of maintenance-as-revision and its tolerance for uncertainty. **Blind spot:** they revise endlessly and struggle to commit; they undervalue the thing worth preserving unchanged. (Their habit of local correction-without-reconciliation shows up across many worlds as *one civilizational pattern among many* that helped the failure stay invisible — **never *the* cause.** The failure has no culprit, only a shape that many cultures, humans included, fell into; do not let the revisers become the species that "caused it.") *(Contrasts the human written-record fixation directly; orthogonal to Move/Stay.)*

**Anti-essentialism guardrail (test every scene):** if a character's choice can be predicted from their species, rewrite. Pair counter-examples deliberately — a chorus-culture individual who insists on speaking alone; a reviser who refuses to change her mind — so culture never hardens into nature. **And the structural test (Constraint 2):** if either species reads as the embodiment of *Move* or *Stay*, redesign — that axis belongs to Lena and Arin, not to a people.

**Wonder beats are not stealth species.** The vivid textural worlds the synopsis and notebook use for scale — the **weightless market** (a vendor walking the air), the **people who navigate by sound** — are **environmental and cultural human/mixed worldbuilding**, *not* the two non-human species. With the count locked at two, cool details must not smuggle in a third. Assign a non-human species to a scene **only deliberately**, where its cultural presence actually matters; otherwise a striking world is just humans adapted to a strange place. *(Some notebook details — e.g. "three ways to apologize, no word for 'you're welcome'" — are reserved as candidate texture for one of the two non-human cultures, to be assigned when chosen, not multiplied.)*

## Iconic locations

Places revealed through experience, each carrying one of the book's tensions. (Sensory detail and prose are Style's; geography and meaning are here.)

### The naming rule

A standing rule for every place named in this book, now and later:

> **If a reader can roughly guess what kind of place it is from the name alone, the name is probably working.**

Names should carry just enough semantic weight to orient without stopping the story for an explanation (the Le Guin / Bujold move) — *the Drift Markets, Northhook, the Tarn, the Cardinal Crossing, the Vellum Moon* all evoke a picture immediately. Two corollaries:

- **Vary the linguistic texture.** Avoid a palette where everything rhymes (*Xel'Thar / Vorak Prime / Orenthar / Kallix* all blur together). The current set is healthy precisely because it mixes registers — **nature** (the Tarn), **culture** (the Vellum Moon), **infrastructure** (the Cardinal Crossing) — which also makes the civilization feel layered.
- **Names should feel discovered, not invented** — like places that accumulated their names over centuries, not fantasy proper nouns minted for the page.
- **Name *and* orient on first encounter.** A name doing semantic work is not the same as a reader knowing what they're looking at. The first time any place or invented term appears, ground it in one clause of plain language that tells the reader what kind of thing it is and what people do there — *Vellum, a small moon given over entirely to records, run by people who keep what the rest of the network throws away* — then let the name carry it from then on. The test is the opposite of the naming test: assume the reader cannot guess, and give them just enough not to be lost. A guessable name *reduces* how much orientation a first encounter needs; it never removes it. Compressed worldbuilding shorthand (*archive-moon, drift routes*) reads fine to someone who already knows the world and means nothing to a first-time reader — so it is banned on first appearance and earned only after the plain-language version has landed.

- **The Ribbon** — Lena's home; a long-inhabited orbital/transit structure layered with centuries of use. The book's image of *breadth* and the Move leg. Where the silence first lands.
- **The Tarn** — Arin's world (a *tarn* is a still, deep mountain lake); mirror-water navigation, fog, slow seasons; the image of *depth* and the Stay leg. The old **"Orin" names are retired** (Lake Orin / Orinreach Basin) so the geography no longer collides with *Arin* and *Varik*. Use *the Tarn* as the proper name; *the basin* works as an informal descriptor.
- **The Drift Markets** — Nadi's element; trade in motion; incentives and ground truth.
- **The Vellum Moon** — Toma's domain; named *vellum* for the manuscript-skin of old records — a moon made of memory; the over-trusted archive, where the purge-misread occurs. (*Vellum* alone works as the informal short form.)
- **The Pilgrim Road** — Sera's element; community and meaning made by people moving together.
- **Vesper** — the gate that goes dark; the first visible symptom. Not a place of villains; a place where a non-local failure surfaced.
- **The Cardinal Crossing** — the oldest crossing (named for *cardinal* = first, principal); *crossing* rather than *gate* to vary the texture, and because the word reads as something engineers named, not priests; "who stays when everyone leaves." **It is history, precedent, and old infrastructure — *not* the climax site.** *(The climax is locked to the dead Vesper gatehouse so the opening wound becomes the ending's first station; Cardinal may anchor backstory or a Toma-precedent thread, but it is no longer a climax candidate.)*

## Everyday life and economy (as it bears on learning)

- **Jobs are the lenses.** Courier, pilot, keeper, archivist, analyst, pilgrim, historian — the cast's epistemic roles are ordinary professions, so the book's ideas arrive through work, not exposition.
- **The economy runs on trust and routes.** Manifests are promises; reputations cross gates faster than goods. When trust frays, the economy reorganizes (Nadi sees it first).
- **Education transmits skills but not the meta-skill.** People are taught to *do* their jobs, not to notice when the civilization has stopped doing something. That missing lesson is what the new Relay reintroduces.

## Failure Modes

*Ways the world can satisfy this guide and still go wrong.*

- **Species become stereotypes.** Culture hardens into nature; a character's choices are predictable from their species. *Tell:* the road-culture character is *always* restless, the place-culture one *always* patient. *Instead:* deliberate counter-examples; culture enriches, never determines.
- **The world feels invented, not lived-in.** Details exist for color, not from age and adaptation. *Tell:* a detail expresses neither accretion, workaround, nor the working-but-unexplained sensation. *Instead:* every detail answers one of the three Design Philosophy questions.
- **The failure gets a culprit.** Sabotage turns out to be partly true, or a person is to blame. *Tell:* the cause can be pointed at. *Instead:* the cause is distributed and emergent — the physical mirror of the social disease.
- **Humans as the invisible default.** Only the non-human species have a "blind spot"; humanity is the neutral baseline. *Tell:* no human cultural blind spot is ever shown. *Instead:* humans over-trust the written and recent, visibly.
- **The world gets explained.** History arrives as a lecture or a dated chronicle. *Tell:* a paragraph of backstory the reader didn't earn. *Instead:* age and system shown through worn objects and behavior.
- **A center quietly exists.** Someone, somewhere, is actually coordinating the mesh. *Tell:* the search for "who's in charge" finds an answer. *Instead:* there is no functioning center; that absence is the point.

## Dramatization

*How world ideas become scenes — `idea → shown through… / not explained by…`.*

- **Age** → *shown through* a gate-plaza that is also a market and a shrine, worn smooth by centuries; *not explained by* a history lecture or a date.
- **The human blind spot** → *shown through* people trusting a written notice over what is in front of them; *not explained by* the narrator stating that humans over-trust records.
- **The failure mirrors the social disease** → *shown through* small uncommunicated fixes compounding into Vesper; *not explained by* a diagram of the mesh.
- **Species enrich, never determine** → *shown through* two individuals of the same species wanting opposite things; *not explained by* describing "what their kind is like."
- **No center** → *shown through* people looking for someone in charge and finding no one; *not explained by* stating the mesh is decentralized.
