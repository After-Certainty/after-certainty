Why couldn't either

                         to travel. The practice requires both legs at once, plus

**Validation:** every participant abandons a held conviction — integration, not contribution. Necessity test holds (remove Arin → no depth half; remove Lena → the others never meet or understand one another). Derivable in advance? No — but inevitable in hindsight.

## Open production decisions (resolve before/at the outline)

These are the genuine gaps the pressure-test exposed — not handbook failures, but the questions that only emerge once you try to tell the story.

1. **The failure mechanism — RESOLVED.** Locked in [World Design](./03-world-design.md): *uncommunicated local corrections compounding* (each world correcting locally and blind; the corrections drift, interact, and fight each other across the mesh; no saboteur, no single part). The earlier candidates (undocumented exceptions, lost calibration knowledge) are folded in as facets, not alternatives. This also commits the recovery: the gates come back only when the corrections become *visible to each other again*, not via central repair.
2. **The climax geography seam — RESOLVED.** Locked in [World Design](./03-world-design.md) ("Two speeds of travel") and [Plot](./02-plot-and-integration.md): a *dark* gate severs only the **fast doorway**, not the **slow ship-routes** between worlds. Both legs reach Vesper the slow way — Lena's carriers overland, Arin's stayer-web around the dark gate from the far side. Vesper is chosen *because* it is dark: the one place both halves can reach that belongs to neither. This also explains why recovery "spreads slowly, the hard way" before the gates themselves hold.
3. **Seed Sera (and Jun) from Act I — PARTIALLY addressed in the synopsis; the outline must finish it.** The synopsis is allowed to compress: it introduces Sera mid-journey as Varik's counterpart (certainty that is wrong vs. faith that is not enough), carries her through the climax room, and pays her off in the spread (faith given something to hold). Good — but that is the *Complication* stretch, not Act I, and Jun likewise arrives after Sera. Plot's **Movement 1 still calls for an early, low-cost brush with both**, and that architecture stands. So the honest status is: the synopsis seeds them, the **outline must add the very early glimpses** — e.g. Jun seen near the platform or in a public feed, already fascinated by how the stories are forming; Sera glimpsed helping stranded people before Lena knows her name. (Do *not* rewrite the synopsis to force these in; let the outline honor the architecture.)
4. **"The lists" need an owner — RESOLVED.** The stayers' wrong-detail lists are now owned in [Artifacts](./04-artifacts.md) as the **watcher list** — the Stay-side proto-manifest. The invented open manifest is explicitly the *fusion* of two existing half-artifacts: the Move-side **courier manifest** (travels, notices nothing) and the Stay-side **watcher list** (notices deeply, never travels). The climax combines them; the invention no longer comes from nowhere.
5. **POV — RESOLVED.** Locked in [Writing Style](./06-writing-style.md): third person limited, tiered — **Lena primary + Arin + Jun**, everyone else external; **Varik never gets POV**; Arin's POV is *emergent* (staying-as-texture, the web coheres before he names it); Acts I and V are Lena-dominant, Arin/Jun cluster in the middle. *(This was the last structural production decision. What remains — species names/detail, world count, frequency cap — is minor and non-structural.)*

## Outline-stage notes (prose cautions & beats to dramatize)

Things the synopsis can state but the **manuscript** must *earn*. Not synopsis edits — carry these into the chapter outline and drafting.

- **Varik's fade must be dramatized, not summarized.** "He fades" is right at synopsis level; the manuscript needs the concrete beat — he speaks with his usual warmth, people still like him, and then turn toward the manifest, the watcher, the keeper, the process; someone asks *"Who's got the word in from the Tarn?"* **Do not expose, humiliate, or defeat him — displace him.** (Captured in Plot's Varik section as "His fade, dramatized.")
- **Don't let Lena sound complete in the opening.** The synopsis line that she "never once confused what a world says it wants with what it actually needs" works as pitch shorthand, but in prose it risks giving her the book's wisdom on page one. The handbook needs her *observant, not omniscient; connective, not complete.* In the manuscript, render this as habit and instinct (a courier's feel) rather than flawless insight — something she does without being able to explain, not a stated virtue.
- **Keep Sera in Discovery, not just as bookends.** Her Complication contrast (vs. Varik) and her ending payoff are strong; the outline must also place her **Movement 3 beat** — the community that holds beautifully yet cannot generate shared adaptation without a form — so "faith finally has something to hold" is set up, not asserted. (Now in Plot's Movement 3.)
- **The widening beat is required, and small.** The ending must include the one returning manifest carrying an observation that fits none of the new patterns — a reminder the Relay is a way of staying able to learn, not a cure. Not a sequel hook. (Now required in Plot's Movement 5 and present in the synopsis.)

## Load-bearing verdict (from the pressure-test)

The handbook **passed**: a coherent, on-theme story fell out of Plot + Character with little invention, and the gaps it exposed are exactly the decisions worth making before drafting 100k words. The first synopsis draft also revealed a *process* lesson — it came out reading like a design document, because the drafting instinct kept asking "what principle does this illustrate?" instead of "what happens?" The fix was not to change the handbook but to **separate the artifacts**: the handbook (and these notes) document *why it works*; [synopsis.md](./synopsis.md) makes someone *want to read it*. Two different jobs, two different voices.

Style and Craft Patterns were correctly *not* load-bearing for either artifact — they govern prose, which neither a synopsis nor a design note contains.
