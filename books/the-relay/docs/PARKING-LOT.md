## Planted in Act I — owed a payoff in Act II

Concrete promises the drafted Act I makes to the reader. Each is vivid enough that the reader will remember it; Act II must touch it early enough that remembering feels rewarded (not necessarily *resolve* it).

- **Corin's contraband crate (Ch 1 → de-emphasized Ch 3).** The seed-stock with a second lining and a blue ticket under it — *"whatever's under it paying for your seat."* In Ch 3 it is explicitly **left behind**, stranded in the Vesper holding bays, Lena still the courier of record. *Payoff (Ch 6–7, early):* don't open the crate; touch the **consequence** — the lower settlements past Vesper that never got their seed, or word of the blue-ticket man's fate — so the moral-grey opening choice rhymes with the cascade. Keep it a thread, not a subplot; the no-villain thesis means the contraband is a symptom of scarcity, not a clue. *(Plot & Integration.)*
- **Jun's worried map pages (seeded Ch 3).** Jun sends copied *auxiliary / return / legacy / do-not-remove* references after Lena before the *Osprey* casts off — last note: *"None of these go where the records say they go."* Keeps Jun from being a dropped thread and plants **records-don't-match-reality**. *Payoff:* his Ch 11 POV and the Act III archive work (Toma/Veya); the legacy-line/auxiliary-return thread is the physical trail toward "no one erased it." *(Character Guide / Plot.)*
- **Jun watches his hedge get hardened (seeded Ch 2).** His provisional *"not syncing — probably an indexing lag, provisional"* comes back off the market deck as *"Tarn entries not syncing, confirmed."* He is *unsettled*, not calculating — institutions "can't hold a *maybe*." This is the **honest-analyst seed for the Varik tragedy**: in Act II his hedged work is stripped of caveats and weaponized. Keep his moral temperature low in Act I; the danger is the system's, not yet his. *(Character Guide / Plot.)*
- **The unnamed Sennen woman (Sera seed, Ch 4).** Calm in the panic; gives tea, a wall out of the wind, and the next true small thing — no story, no hand. *Payoff:* **Ch 10 — *Kind and Not Enough*** is her proper, named introduction (pairs with the reserved line *"Most true things don't prove enough."*). Confirm she reads as the *same* person Lena filed on the quay — the counter-pattern to both panic and premature certainty — so the Ch 10 reveal lands as recognition, not a new character. **Don't name or over-emphasize her early** (her power is that she appears as a different *kind* of response before Lena has language for it) — but bring her back **before the reader forgets the Sennen scene**; if Ch 10 feels too far, a brief earlier Act II glimpse keeps her warm. *(Character Guide.)*

### Craft patterns established in Act I (carry forward)

- **Choose plainness sometimes (thesis-sentence density).** The prose runs to beautifully compressed, aphoristic thematic sentences — that's the voice, and it's an asset. The risk is *density*: when every paragraph lands a memorable thematic punch, the reader starts to feel the author's intelligence pressing through the scene. On a final pass, watch for **two thesis-shaped sentences doing the same job close together** (or a run of consecutive paragraphs each ending on an aphorism) and let one go plain — keep the sharper line, cut the gloss, and trust the image/scene to carry the idea. This is *not* a "cut texture" rule; it's a "reduce the number of memorable-sentence handles per page" rule. Especially: prefer showing (the machinery, the cost, the object) over a thesis-label that names what the scene already proves. *(Writing Style.)*

- **The Finitude Counter (slow-way scarcity).** On the slow way, every leg costs something irreplaceable — heat, coolant, wash-water, filter life, margin — and the rim "gives nothing back you didn't bring." Seeded Ch 3 (Harl's chalk line on the water gauge, *"a line on a glass that only went down"*) and Ch 5 (Osko bleeding coolant, hand-scraping filters, refusing a corner-cut to save heat). Keep it physical and decrementing, never a thriller clock; it makes dependence and distance *cost* on the page. *(Writing Style / World Design.)*
- **False-authority artifacts (systems that display response without enabling it).** Objects that imply someone is in charge while proving absence: the blank-backed unsigned notice and *Fault Code 0* (Ch 1/3), the painted-over manual override behind unbroken glass (Ch 2), the dead waystation still broadcasting *"maintenance notified, thank you for your patience"* into an empty corridor (Ch 5). Use sparingly — one telling object among others, never admired into a metaphor wearing a hat. *(Writing Style.)*
  - *Override as a planted object (Ch 2):* it reads first as environmental evidence — *a ritual for emergency response, but no living habit of one.* Don't force a literal payoff (no one needs to break that exact glass). If it returns, return it **transformed and thematic**, e.g. somewhere in Act II–III someone *has* broken the glass and the key behind it fits a lock replaced thirty years ago. Optional, and only if it emerges naturally. *(Plot / Writing Style.)*

## Planted in Act II — owed a payoff in Acts III–IV

Concrete promises the drafted Act II makes. Each must be touched at its scheduled payoff (per the outline's seeds-and-payoffs ledger) so the recognition lands as *recognition*, not a new beat.

- **The lists, now a physical artifact (Ch 10 → Ch 12 → 15 → 18 → 24).** The settled folk's scraps appear on the page in Ch 10: coarse grey pulp, blocky charcoal, *no seal, no signature office, none of the purple machine-stamps* — deliberately the **inverse of the false-authority artifacts.** In Ch 12 Lena links them to Jun's pages as "the same kind of thing." *Guard:* keep her at **kinship/texture only** through Act II — she does **not** wield them as a tool, does **not** know whose hand (Arin) until the Ch 18 recognition, and the fusion into the open manifest is Ch 24. Method before meaning. *(Plot / Writing Style.)*
  - *Lexicon evolution — "snag" → intentional language (Act III watch-item).* "Snag" is the **noticing-phase** word — it belongs to Act II, where Lena registers something she can't yet understand (Ch 10 → Ch 12). It's a deliberate transitional motif, **not** a defect; don't retro-cut it. But once she begins **actively pursuing** the question (the Ch 12 agency bridge: *how does a true word reach the person who needs it before they're a name on a board?*), the language should shift toward **testing / carrying / connecting** — *thread, mark, list, route, relay, handoff, signal.* Let the vocabulary track her turn from passive recognition to deliberate practice; "snag" should fade as the intentional verbs arrive. *(Writing Style.)*
  - *Lena's agency (Act III watch-item).* Act II breaks her by demonstration; Act III must not keep only **educating** her. Her Act III reason is *"I need to understand,"* but she should start **experimenting** — finding where the lists come from / who can carry them faster — without solving it early or learning whose hand (Arin) before Ch 18. The Ch 12 close now seeds this (she "walks toward a question," not only away from a shut gate); pay it off with action, not more witnessing. *(Plot.)*
- **Jun's second document (Ch 11 → Ch 23).** A plain, hand-written, *un-sent* account of how a true thing dies in transit — the small wrong turns of his caution. **Not the open manifest; not a gadget.** Its job is Jun's curiosity→responsibility arc: it surfaces as proof he had *already* abandoned the hedge, so his Ch 23 central-model surrender doesn't read as a sudden conversion. *Forcing function:* Cardinal sliding into Varik's orbit dislodges him from the safe reading-room toward the Vellum records thread. Leave it **"addressed to no one"** — a gesture into the dark, not a heroic send-after-Lena. *(Character Guide / Plot.)*
- **Jun's records crumbs, paid and continued (Ch 3 → Ch 11 → Act III).** *auxiliary / return / legacy / do-not-remove;* "none of these go where the records say they go." Lena physically carries the cramped-hand pages behind the dead scanner panel (re-touched Ch 12). The physical trail toward **"no one erased it"** (Ch 14) and the Toma/Veya archive work. *(Plot.)*
- **Nadi's route-data shape (Ch 8 → Ch 22).** In her hands she knows the rim is dying "like an engine starving," but won't name it — *a word is a theory and a theory didn't fly.* Her logbook is the thing **worth more shared than sold**: that's her Ch 22 abandonment (motion-as-survival released). Keep the refusal legible so the release costs her something. *(Character Guide.)*
- **Varik's doctrine left behind as bureaucracy (Ch 9).** His coined phrase *emergency local distribution under route failure* and the grille notice (*kept from them by those who profit from the dark … carried hand to hand until the threads are mended*) persist at Khoury after he's gone. His **grammar outlives his presence** — recurs as the cascade's institutional residue and the explicit contrast to the un-stamped lists; supports the Ch 27 *dramatized fade* (the words stay useful even as the man becomes unneeded). **Loaded-weapon recurrence (do not skip):** at Khoury the phrase opens a hold *correctly*. Show it again at least once in a **worse** setting — used to justify an unjust seizure, a convoy priority that overrides a local claim, or supplies taken where the owner is reachable and simply not asked — so the same five words that saved children at Khoury are seen authorizing harm elsewhere. *That* is the demonstration that his sentences work after he leaves, for anyone, regardless of cause. Candidate sites: an Act III crossing under committee/Watch control. *(Plot / Writing Style.)*
- **Varik's sister — keep open.** Per Character Guide, do **not** resolve her fate in passing; her unresolved silence quietly indicts his certainty and must inform (not contradict) his Act IV absence and Ch 27 fade. *(Character Guide.)*
- **The shrinking circuit / amateur hulls (Ch 8, 12).** The rim "getting shorter every season"; pros abandon routes, amateurs run them — the mechanism that makes a route Lena *correctly* cleared turn lethal (the *Wren*). A facet of the Finitude Counter; carry the cost forward on the slow routes. *(World Design / Writing Style.)*
