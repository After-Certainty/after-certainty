# Guide 4 — Artifacts

*The Relay Design Handbook · how the civilization thinks on the page. Assumes the [Novel Constitution](./01-novel-constitution.md) and [Plot & Integration](./02-plot-and-integration.md) (frozen), and implements within [World Design](./03-world-design.md). Owns the objects and practices through which knowledge is stored and carried; does not own the civilization-learning theory (World), the people (Character), or the prose (Style).*

> **Status:** Draft v1, written to be argued with. Items marked **[DECISION]** are author-level calls.

Artifacts are not set dressing. In *The Relay*, **the civilization thinks through its artifacts** — they are the medium of distributed cognition. A manifest is how trust travels; a bulletin is how attention is directed; a song is how a lesson survives a generation. This guide defines the kinds of artifacts, the rules that keep them dramatic, and the concrete form of the thing the climax invents.

**Core test (applies to every artifact in every scene):** *an artifact should perform work that dialogue would otherwise have had to perform.* If a scene could have conveyed the same thing through two characters talking, the artifact is decoration — cut it or make it do the work.

---

## The three classes

The civilization thinks in three registers. Documents are only one of them.

### Ephemeral — attention and the present

Short-lived artifacts that direct attention and coordinate the now: **broadcasts, transit bulletins, tickets, receipts, menus, route notices, market boards.**

- *What they carry:* what is happening, what to fear, where to go.
- *In the story:* the **suspension bulletin** that opens the book is ephemeral — and it fails at its one job (it carries no explanation). The sabotage broadcasts are ephemeral; they carry *meaning faster than fact* (Varik's medium).
- *Cognitive role:* they are the civilization's short-term attention. When they carry conclusions instead of observations, the civilization panics.

### Persistent — memory and the record

Durable artifacts that hold knowledge across time: **research papers, maintenance manuals, inspection reports, courier manifests, keeper logs, maps, archives, trade ledgers.**

- *What they carry:* precedent, procedure, promises, the record of what was done.
- *In the story:* Toma's **archives** and the **keeper logs** (Cai) are persistent; the **manifest** is the key persistent artifact — a promise and a record of trust. The **purge-misread** happens here: the archive's *gaps* are misread as deletion, because the civilization trusts that the record contains what is known (the human blind spot from World).
- *Cognitive role:* long-term memory — over-trusted, and silent about everything that was never written down.

### Living — culture and transmission

Practices, not paper, through which the civilization thinks and remembers: **traditions, songs, sayings, rituals, festival programs, folklore, apprenticeship.**

- *What they carry:* tacit knowledge, values, and skills that no document holds.
- *In the story:* **apprenticeship** (Cai→Lio) is a living artifact — and its quiet breakage is how skill is lost without alarm. **Courier folklore**, sayings, and the rituals of handoff carry the *receive · carry · pass* ethic before anyone names it.
- *Cognitive role:* the memory the archive cannot hold. The book's deepest point about cognition lives here: **a civilization that thinks only in documents has already begun to forget.**

## Artifacts as cognition

How each class participates in the central mystery and its answer:

- The **failure is legible across artifacts**: ephemeral notices stop explaining, persistent records show gaps that aren't deletions, and a living practice (the observation-relay) has vanished without leaving a documentary trace. The reader assembles the truth from artifacts the way the characters do.
- **Artifact against artifact** is a primary device (from Plot): a trade map (persistent) contradicts a broadcast (ephemeral); a keeper log contradicts the official story. Conflict between artifacts dramatizes distributed knowledge without a narrator.
- The **distributed-knowledge map** (Plot) is carried physically by artifacts: Arin's depth-web is a body of local logs/observations; Nadi's breadth is maps; Toma's is the archive; Cai's is logs. Lena carries them between holders.

## The invented artifact — the new Relay's medium

*(The climax's tactile center. Plot owns the event; this guide owns the form. This is where "reinventing the Relay" becomes a thing you can hold.)*

**LOCKED (resolved by the synopsis pressure-test): the open manifest.** The civilization's existing manifest carries cargo and a promise. The invented artifact is a **manifest remade to carry observations and open questions alongside the freight** — and, crucially, to be **added to by every hand it passes through**. It is the fusion of the two networks made physical: a carrier (Move) brings it; a watcher (Stay) writes the local signal into it; the next carrier takes it on. The synopsis depends on this form load-bearingly (it is the title's payoff and the literal making of the first one is the climax), so it is no longer an open call.

Its properties (each a deliberate answer to the failure):

- **It carries observations, not just conclusions** — the cure for ephemeral panic.
- **It is revisable and additive** — no version is final; it is the opposite of the over-trusted fixed record.
- **It is owned by no center** — it lives in transit, in many hands (the no-magic-boxes guardrail: it *preserves and extends* observation; the people still do the integrating).
- **It is small and portable** — it can spread without authority, the way the old relay practice once did.

**Where it comes from — the two half-artifacts it fuses.** The open manifest is not invented from nothing; it is the union of two artifacts the civilization already has, each crippled by being only half:

- **The courier manifest (Move-side predecessor).** Lena's world: a document that *travels* gate to gate, but carries only cargo and a promise — it moves, and notices nothing.
- **The watcher list (Stay-side predecessor).** Arin's stayers: informal **lists of unseasonals** — *winter-skin on black water, a tide arriving late, Skin first. Gauge late.* — that *accumulate* deep local observation but stay put on one world and never travel or reconcile with anyone else's. A **persistent artifact carried by a living practice** (the watching), and — in the story — the **first visible sign that the Stay-side is building a depth-web**, well before the open manifest exists. *(This is where "the lists" / unseasonals are owned: they are the proto-manifest, not a separate artifact class.)*

The climax's invention is the moment these two finally combine: a thing that both **moves** (like the courier manifest) and **gathers observation, added to by every hand** (like the watcher list). Neither half could have become the relay alone — the open manifest is the Move/Stay fusion made into an object.

**Tactile climax images** (forms, not blocking — Plot owns the scene):

- the dead **Vesper gatehouse** repurposed as the first place where carried manifests and local logs are physically laid side by side;
- a manifest **rewritten in the open by several hands** who minutes ago were arguing;
- the **handoff ritual** — receive · carry · pass — made into a small, repeatable gesture anyone can perform.

*(Alternatives considered and set aside: a relay of physical tokens/markers; a network of way-stations; a revived apprenticeship-chain. The open manifest won because it migrates an artifact the reader already met at rung 1 — transport — up to rung 5 — knowledge. Kept here only as the record of what was weighed; the decision is made.)*

## Usage rules

How to keep artifacts dramatic rather than expository.

- **The dialogue-work test — the redundancy test** (restated, because it governs everything). The practical editing form: *delete the artifact, replace it with two characters talking, and ask — is the scene now basically identical?* **If yes, delete the artifact** and keep the dialogue. The artifact only earns its place when its removal would cost the scene something dialogue cannot recover — a texture, an omission, a contradiction, a thing the reader infers that no one would say aloud.
- **Excerpt, don't dump.** Show the *one telling line* of a manifest or bulletin, not the whole document. A fragment the reader must complete beats a paragraph that completes itself.
- **Implication over explanation** (Constitution): one vivid unexplained artifact detail beats a paragraph of mechanism. Trust the reader to infer the system from the object.
- **Frequency cap.** Artifacts are punctuation, not paragraphs. **[DECISION] Proposed: at most one foregrounded artifact per scene**, and not every scene. Overuse turns a device into a tic.
- **Accessibility** (Constitution): future artifacts should be *easier* to read than present-day ones — legible at a glance, never technobabble.
- **No magic boxes.** No artifact scans, models, or resolves. Artifacts carry and preserve; people integrate. An artifact that produced the answer would break the book.

## Integration linkage

Which artifacts matter at the climax, and how:

- The **open manifest** is the invention itself — the climax is, in part, the making of the first one.
- **Apprenticeship** (living) becomes the template for spreading it: carrier↔watcher pairs.
- The **opening suspension bulletin** (ephemeral) is the motif that closes — a notice that, by the end, carries meaning again.
- Toma's **archive** supplies precedent; Cai's **logs** and Arin's **local observations** supply the fragments; Nadi's **maps** supply breadth — the artifacts are the physical form of the necessity test.

## Continuity rules

Artifacts evolve across the book; track them so the evolution lands.

- **The manifest's arc:** cargo-and-promise (rung 1–3) → carrier of observation (rung 4–5). The same object, transformed — a physical meaning-migration.
- **The bulletin's arc:** carries no explanation (opening) → carries fear/false certainty (middle) → carries meaning again (end).
- **The apprenticeship's arc:** breaking silently (background, early) → consciously revived as the relay's transmission method (climax).
- **One-object rule:** when an artifact recurs, it must have *changed* or the recurrence is wallpaper. Reuse only to show migration.

## Failure Modes

*Ways artifacts can satisfy this guide and still go wrong. (Localizes "do not mistake artifacts for answers.")*

- **Artifacts explain instead of reveal.** A document is a delivery vehicle for exposition. *Tell:* the artifact tells the reader what to think rather than letting them infer. *Instead:* excerpt the one telling line; trust inference.
- **An artifact performs the integration.** A tool scans, models, or resolves the answer. *Tell:* the climax turns on a device producing an output. *Instead:* artifacts carry and preserve; people integrate. No magic boxes.
- **Recurrence without migration.** An artifact returns unchanged. *Tell:* the manifest at the end means exactly what it meant at the start. *Instead:* reuse only to show meaning has moved.
- **Document dump.** The whole bulletin/manifest is shown. *Tell:* a block quote the reader skims. *Instead:* a fragment they must complete.
- **The living register is forgotten.** Only paper artifacts appear; culture-as-cognition vanishes. *Tell:* no song, ritual, or apprenticeship carries knowledge. *Instead:* the living class does real work the archive cannot.
- **Artifact as tic.** Every scene foregrounds an object. *Tell:* artifacts are wallpaper, not punctuation. *Instead:* respect the frequency cap.

## Dramatization

*How artifact ideas become scenes — `idea → shown through… / not explained by…`.*

- **Artifacts-as-cognition** → *shown through* the truth being assembled from conflicting documents; *not explained by* a character explaining how the civilization stores knowledge.
- **The living register** → *shown through* a song or a handoff ritual carrying a lesson the archive lost; *not explained by* stating that culture is also memory.
- **The invented Relay** → *shown through* a manifest being remade by many hands; *not explained by* a speech about a new institution.
- **The human blind spot** → *shown through* characters trusting an archive's silence as proof; *not explained by* narrating that they over-trust records.
