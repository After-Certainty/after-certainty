## Chapter title

Chapter 2 — The Keycard

## Governing question

When is an exception a privilege, and when is it the condition of equal participation?

## Ordinary opening image

An employee keycard that unlocks an office door.

## Case / terrain

A federal judge ordered the Justice Department to let two immigration-court lawyers with serious disabilities continue working remotely while their lawsuit proceeded. The dispute arose after a broad return-to-office directive encountered workers with Type I diabetes and Stage IV lung cancer who had previously performed well from home.

## Conceptual movement

Uniform treatment can look fair because everyone receives the same rule. Accommodation begins from a different understanding of fairness: equal standing may require different arrangements when bodies, risks, and circumstances differ.

## Questions to preserve

- When is an exception a privilege, and when is it the condition of equal participation?
- Why do institutions often treat accommodation as a threat to the rule rather than an application of its purpose?
- What evidence should be required, and how can that requirement avoid working as its own barrier?

## Return image

The keycard works perfectly. The unanswered question is whether entering the building should have been the measure of doing the work.
