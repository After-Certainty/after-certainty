## Chapter title

Chapter 8 — The Appeal Stamp

## Governing question

What makes review independent enough to matter?

## Ordinary opening image

A stamp reading “Reconsidered,” “Reversed,” or “Remanded.”

## Case / terrain

The growth of administrative hearings, internal appeals, judicial review, and the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946. Modern institutions created formal paths through which agency decisions could be challenged, records reviewed, reasons demanded, and legal boundaries enforced.

## Conceptual movement

Appeal is designed institutional humility. It does not assume that every first decision is wrong. It assumes that no consequential decision process should be treated as incapable of error.

## Questions to preserve

- What makes review independent enough to matter?
- Which facts may be introduced after the first decision?
- How much expertise should be required to challenge an expert institution?
- Can revision strengthen legitimacy instead of signaling weakness?

## Return image

The stamp does not erase the first decision. It records that the institution was capable of seeing it differently.

**Historical terrain:** APA / administrative review.
