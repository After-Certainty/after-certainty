## Chapter title

Chapter 6 — The Sentencing Table

## Governing question

How can a system restore judgment without recreating the disparities the rule was designed to prevent?

## Ordinary opening image

A sentencing table made of numbered rows and columns.

## Case / terrain

Federal sentencing reform sought to reduce unwarranted disparities. The Sentencing Reform Act of 1984 created the United States Sentencing Commission and a guideline system. Mandatory minimums addressed unequal discretion but produced disproportionate cases. Congress later created and expanded a limited “safety valve” allowing judges to sentence some defendants below an otherwise applicable mandatory minimum under specified conditions.

## Conceptual movement

**Conceptual hinge of the book.** Rules were strengthened because individualized judgment had been unequal. Structured exceptions were later required because the new consistency could become disproportionate. The safety valve made the exception conditional, reasoned, and reviewable.

## Questions to preserve

- Which differences should a sentencing rule treat as morally relevant?
- When does consistency become disproportionality?
- How can a system restore judgment without recreating the disparities the rule was designed to prevent?
- Who controls access to an exception?
- Does the phrase “safety valve” reveal that mature rule systems must anticipate pressure they cannot safely contain?
