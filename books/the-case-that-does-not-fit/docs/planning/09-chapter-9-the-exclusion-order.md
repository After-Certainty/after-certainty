## Chapter title

Chapter 9 — The Exclusion Order

## Governing question

When does emergency make individualized judgment impossible—and what fails under fear?

## Ordinary opening image

A posted order telling a category of people when and where they must report.

## Case / terrain

Executive Order 9066 and the forced removal and incarceration of Japanese Americans during World War II. Broad emergency authority, racial classification, wartime fear, and limited judicial correction transformed ancestry into a proxy for danger. Later investigations, apology, and reparations acknowledged racial prejudice, wartime hysteria, and failures of political leadership.

## Conceptual movement

A difficult or dangerous circumstance can be used to justify a broad rule that eliminates the very judgment the situation requires. Exceptions to ordinary liberty can become universal within the targeted category.

## Questions to preserve

- When does emergency make individualized judgment impossible?
- Which safeguards are most likely to fail when fear is highest?
- Can a temporary rule remain bounded once institutions organize around it?
- What forms of later repair are possible when the original decision cannot be undone?

## Return image

The order is posted as a general instruction. Every person reading it must live the consequence individually.

**Historical terrain:** EO 9066 / Civil Liberties Act of 1988.
