## Chapter title

Chapter 3 — The Denial Letter

## Governing question

What makes an appeal genuinely reachable rather than formally present?

## Ordinary opening image

An insurance letter beginning, “Your request has been denied.”

## Case / terrain

HealthCare.gov reported that, as of July 1, 2026, the federally administered external-review process was temporarily unavailable for eligible people in several states and territories. Separately, 2026 health-policy research continued to raise concerns about AI-assisted prior-authorization review and the burden placed on patients and clinicians to navigate denials and appeals.

## Conceptual movement

An appeal is not a decorative feature. It is the institutional mechanism for admitting that a rule, model, or first decision may have failed to understand the case. But appeals become fictional when they require expertise, time, persistence, or infrastructure the affected person does not possess.

## Questions to preserve

- What makes an appeal genuinely reachable?
- Who bears the cost of proving that the first decision was wrong?
- When automation accelerates initial decisions, does the institution also increase its capacity for review?
- Should the reviewer encounter the human consequences hidden by the denial code?

## Return image

The letter contains instructions for appeal in smaller type. The instructions are formally present even when the path they describe is practically closed.

## Durability check

Valid if the external-review process resumes or current automation concerns are resolved.
