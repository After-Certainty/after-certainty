## Chapter title

Chapter 1 — The Checkbox

## Governing question

What happens when two categorical presumptions collide in one life?

## Ordinary opening image

A checkbox labeled “Parent notified.”

## Case / terrain

In July 2026, the U.S. Education Department threatened federal funding consequences for school districts in Maryland and Michigan over policies concerning whether a student’s transgender identity could remain confidential from parents. The conflict places two categorical presumptions against one another: parents should have access to important information about their children, and schools may encounter students whose safety or circumstances make automatic disclosure consequential.

## Conceptual movement

Rules create consistency by treating similar cases similarly. Judgment begins with the possibility that the cases are not similar in the way the rule assumes.

## Questions to preserve

- What facts would be morally relevant to disclosure?
- Who is authorized to determine whether a student is safe?
- How can policy avoid both automatic secrecy and automatic disclosure?
- What happens when a difficult local case becomes a national political symbol?

## Return image

A checked box says that notification occurred. It cannot say whether notification protected a relationship or endangered one.

## Durability check

Valid whether courts or agencies ultimately require disclosure, permit confidentiality, or create a more conditional rule.

## Culture-war guardrail

Write for the durable decision tension, not for a preferred political outcome.
