## Working premise

Rules make shared life possible. They allow institutions to act consistently, distribute authority, limit favoritism, and process more cases than any individual person could hold in mind. But every rule is a compression. It identifies the differences that count and ignores the rest.

The book examines what happens when the ignored detail becomes the morally decisive one.

## Core question

How can a system make room for the case its rules cannot understand without turning judgment into arbitrary power?

## Governing claim

Rules protect people from arbitrary judgment. Judgment protects people from rules that have mistaken them for someone else. Legitimacy depends on institutions capable of preserving both protections at once.

## Shared book architecture

Three-part movement:

- **Present** — Current events in which a general rule collides with a particular life.
- **Past** — Historical cases showing the tension between consistency and judgment is older than current controversies.
- **Future** — What history teaches about designing institutions that can reconsider decisions without surrendering fairness or legitimacy.

Nested movement per chapter: ordinary object → event or historical case → philosophical pattern → return to the object.
