# **Part III**

## **Institutions That Can Reconsider**

The history behind us does not settle the argument in favor of rules or judgment. It makes the false choice harder to maintain.

Rules made equity necessary because general commands could not foresee every case. Rules made sentencing reform necessary because local judgment had produced inequalities that could not be excused as wisdom. Rules made accommodation, appeal, and emergency review necessary because institutions needed both consistency and a way to recognize when consistency had become mistaken identity.

Part III turns from the older pattern to the design problem now becoming more urgent. Automated systems can classify cases with a scale, reach, and apparent objectivity that make their categories feel less like judgments at all. But a category that cannot be explained, contradicted, corrected, or reconsidered is not neutral because a machine produced it. It is only harder to reach.

The remaining chapters ask what an institution must build if its first answer is not allowed to be its last answer. The question is not how to escape rules. It is how to design rules, discretion, review, reasons, appeals, and automated decisions so that each protection restrains the failure of the others.
