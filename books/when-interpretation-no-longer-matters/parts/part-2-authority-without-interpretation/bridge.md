# Part II

## Authority Without Interpretation

Part I named the boundary: interpretation can fail as a public medium even when understanding persists. Part II names what replaces it.

These chapters are diagnostic, not taxonomic for its own sake. Each chapter owns one mode and tracks how legitimacy stabilizes when shared reasons no longer do corrective work.

- **Chapter 3 — Alignment:** authority sustained through sorting and belonging signals rather than evaluative agreement across groups.
- **Chapter 4 — Identity saturation:** authority embedded in collective identity so disagreement threatens belonging more than claims.
- **Chapter 5 — Coercion and performative legitimacy:** authority maintained through visible compliance and ritualized participation under threat.
- **Chapter 6 — Narrative enclosure:** authority sealed by a story that absorbs contradiction as confirmation.

Keep the modes distinct. Alignment is not identity saturation; performance under threat is not enclosure; enclosure can intensify interpretation while forbidding correction from outside.

Part III tests these modes in cases at scale.
