# Part IV

## After Interpretation

Part III showed how authority can operate when interpretation no longer mediates legitimacy. Part IV names what remains for those who still judge from outside—or beside—such systems.

When interpretation no longer coordinates consequence, moral clarity can increase without restoring leverage. These chapters stay with that tension:

- **Chapter 11 — Impossible judgment:** Pol Pot and the Khmer Rouge—condemnation without a pathway to consequence.
- **Chapter 12 — Non-repair:** Mao and the Cultural Revolution—interpretation hyperactive while correction backfires.
- **Chapter 13 — Early recognition:** WeWork and Adam Neumann—warning signs before the language of accountability fails.

This is not a rescue program and not an argument for cynicism. It is an attempt to name limits without pretending they are total.

The conclusion gathers implications without introducing a new authority model.
