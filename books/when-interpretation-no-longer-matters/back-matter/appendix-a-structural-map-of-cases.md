# **Appendix A**

## **A Structural Map of the Cases**

The chapters in this book examine different leaders whose authority persisted after interpretation stopped mediating legitimacy. Because the diagnostic posture stays consistent, these cases can begin to blur. This appendix exists to counter that effect.

The table below does not summarize the leaders. It classifies them.

Each row answers the same structural question: **what replaced interpretation—and what light example makes that visible?** Use the chapter links in the book’s table of contents for full argument; this map is for re-orientation only.

### **The Map**

| Leader | Chapter | What replaced interpretation | Spotting it |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Joseph Smith | 1 | **Boundary:** assent before external evaluation | Neighbors’ objections did not revoke standing once belonging formed |
| Jim Jones | 2 | **Interpretive collapse:** understanding without leverage | Followers recognized harm and contradiction; legitimacy held anyway |
| Donald Trump | 3 | **Alignment:** sorting over cross-group persuasion | Fact disputes clarified who belonged more than what was true |
| Juan Perón | 4 | **Identity saturation:** allegiance over program coherence | Competing factions still claimed Peronism across policy reversals |
| Joseph Stalin | 5 | **Performative legitimacy:** compliance under threat | Public unanimity and ritual praise alongside private fear in diaries |
| David Koresh | 6 | **Narrative enclosure:** interpretation inside a sealed frame | Failed predictions and outside pressure absorbed as persecution |
| Sun Myung Moon | 7 | **Alignment at scale:** commitment before comprehension | Workshops and communal life before full doctrinal mastery |
| Hugo Chávez | 8 | **Identity-saturated politics:** belonging over outcomes | Economic crisis and policy reversal without collapsing core identification |
| Adolf Hitler | 9 | **Total authority:** public interpretation eliminated | Intensive communication with no public space for evaluative disagreement |
| Elizabeth Holmes | 10 | **Transitional drift:** interpretation narrows before collapse | Verification deferred; dissent reframed as insufficient vision |
| Pol Pot | 11 | **Judgment without consequence:** clarity without mediation | Moral recognition of atrocity with no institution to carry it |
| Mao Zedong | 12 | **Anti-repair:** interpretation weaponized as purification | Criticism and self-criticism campaigns that deepened instability |
| Adam Neumann | 13 | **Early displacement:** accountability language without constraint | Governance warnings fragmented while “vision” still governed standing |

### **How to Read This Map**

This table is not a progression or a ranking. It is meant to be scanned.

If the cases begin to feel interchangeable, focus on the third column: *what replaced interpretation*. That replacement determines how authority persisted and why familiar corrective tools failed.

A useful simplifying distinction:

- **Interpretation was sealed** (Smith, Koresh, Holmes)
- **Interpretation was sorted** (Trump, Chávez, Perón)
- **Interpretation was erased or hollowed** (Stalin, Hitler, Pol Pot)

These are not moral categories. They are functional ones.

## **Why This Appendix Exists**

This book deliberately avoids explanation through belief, motive, or character. As a result, it demands sustained attention to structure. This appendix provides a way to re-orient without reopening those explanatory frames.

Use it as:

- a reference while reading
- a reset point when returning to the book
- a way to distinguish cases without ranking them

The work of the book remains diagnostic. This map simply makes that diagnosis easier to hold.
