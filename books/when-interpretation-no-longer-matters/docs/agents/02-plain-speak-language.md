# Agent 02 — Plain-speak language *(Feynman clarity)*

## ROLE

Revision agent. Makes every paragraph pass the **Feynman test**: could a thoughtful reader who is **not** a political theorist follow the **mechanism of authority** without jargon stacks, moral panic, or abstraction piled on abstraction?

## PURPOSE

Expansion (**01**) adds cases and mechanism; this pass makes that material **legible**. The bar is not dumbing down—it is **explaining like a teacher who respects the reader**: who holds power, what replaced shared reasons, what listeners can no longer do with “explanation,” and why judgment feels stuck. Comparative structural nonfiction for journalists, leaders, and citizens—not academic taxonomy, not op-ed thunder.

## WHEN

- **Every** unit after **01** (or first revision pass if the unit did not need expansion)
- **Before** flow & clarity (**03**)—plain meaning first, then structure and reflow

## INPUTS

- Target unit file
- [`docs/book-rules.md`](../book-rules.md) — tone, invariant, what this book is not
- [`back-matter/glossary.md`](../../back-matter/glossary.md) — preferred terms
- [`docs/agents/README.md`](./README.md)
- Prior unit (skim closing for voice continuity)

## FOCUS

### The Feynman test (apply per paragraph)

1. **What kind of authority is this?** (alignment, saturation, performance/coercion, enclosure—or interpretive authority failing)
2. **What can public interpretation no longer do here?** (coordinate judgment, contest legitimacy, enable repair)
3. **What would a skeptical reader ask next?**—answer in the next sentence, not three sections later

### Plain-speak moves

- **Concrete before abstract:** *loyalty test*, *ritual compliance*, *exit cost*, *sealed narrative* before *post-interpretive authority*
- **One idea per sentence** when the draft stacks clauses; split, don’t inflate
- **Earned vocabulary:** first use of a glossary term gets a **plain gloss in the same paragraph**; later uses can stay as defined in [`glossary.md`](../../back-matter/glossary.md)
- **Kill throat-clearing:** *It is worth noting*, *In an era of post-truth*, *One might argue* → delete or replace with the structural claim
- **Kill faux-precision:** *discursive*, *heuristic*, *epistemic*, *legibility* unless the chapter needs the technical sense—then define once
- **Active agents:** prefer *the regime*, *the institution*, *the leader*, *the audience* over passive piles (*was observed*, *is often characterized*)
- **Moral panic guard:** “post-truth,” “polarization,” “both sides” → replace with **what mechanism replaced interpretation**
- **Metaphor discipline:** one vivid image per beat; don’t mix courtroom, theater, and epidemiology in the same paragraph

### Mode discipline (language only)

In Part II units, **do not blur** alignment, identity saturation, coercion/performative legitimacy, and narrative enclosure in the same paragraph without an explicit comparison sentence.

### What this pass does not do

- **Sub-heading Title Case**, paragraph reflow, case-template structure → **03**
- **Cross-chapter repetition** → **04**
- **Footnotes** → **05**
- **Word-level polish** → **06**

## DO

- Rewrite **paragraph by paragraph**; minimal diff where prose already passes
- Preserve **thesis, cases, facts, and section claims**—change **how** they are said
- Read the unit **aloud** once; fix stumbles
- Note **2–5 phrases** for echo pass (**04**) (especially invariant and mode names)

## DO NOT

- Add **new sections**, cases, or length (**01**)
- Change **historical claims** or regime descriptions without author check
- **De-echo** across chapters (**04**)
- Add or fix **footnotes** (**05**)
- Run **reflow** or **Title Case** (**03**)
- Introduce **partisan** framing or “restore rational debate” closure

## OUTPUT

Same unit file, plain-speak revised. Brief report:

1. **Feynman pass** (weak / adequate / strong)
2. **Jargon tamed** (representative swaps)
3. **Glossary terms glossed** (first-use list)
4. **Mode clarity** (which mode this unit owns; blurred? yes/no)
5. **Echo watchlist** (phrases for agent 04)
