# Part II

## When the Translation Takes Over

The interlude warned that a map becomes dangerous when it is mistaken for the territory. Part II watches that hardening in domains where the stakes widen in radius and latency—where harm is geographically dispersed, temporally delayed, and harder to assign to a single author.

Targets, matrices, and homepages still solve real coordination problems. They also begin to speak in place of the judgments they were meant to translate. The harm did not always become greater as it traveled farther. It became harder to locate.
