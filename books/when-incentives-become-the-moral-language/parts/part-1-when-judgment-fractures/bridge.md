# Part I

## When Judgment Fractures

Part I asks what substitution looks like when **judgment is still practiced locally** but **cannot survive as the system's moral language**.

You will read four domains as one arc:

**Care** — healing aims survive while clinical judgment loses public standing.

**Engagement** — connection aims survive while editorial judgment is replaced by behavioral metrics.

**Publishing** — truth-seeking survives while productivity metrics replace epistemic speech.

**Targets** — harm reduction survives while climate targets and ESG reporting replace responsibility language.

Part I asks what happens when metrics stop pointing back to values and start **standing in** for values—and when practitioners are left holding moral weight the system can no longer articulate.

When Part I ends, an interlude states what this book is not. Part II then examines fairness, attention, polling, and formation when formula becomes the only public moral language.

Chapter 1 begins with care without caring: how good aims survive while good reasons cannot.
