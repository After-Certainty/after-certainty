# Part II — Leaders Becoming Legible

Part II turns from ordinary rooms to leaders already compressed into
reputation. Each chapter asks a quieter question than verdict: why did
audiences and institutions read this leader this way, and why did that
reading persist?

---

## Two Ways of Looking

An eye exam slips one lens in, then another. Distance sharpens while the
near page softens; the page clarifies while the horizon blurs. Bifocals
join the two without averaging them into one polite haze. The wearer
looks through the portion the task requires.

Judging leaders works the same way—when it works at all.

One way of looking watches how signal travels under compression, how
listeners supply what speech leaves out, and how constraint makes
repair costly. Alone, it tends to stop at coordination failure. The
other watches moral posture toward harm, effectiveness in producing
outcomes, and legitimacy that persists after its justifying conditions
have changed. Alone, it often assumes interpretive clarity—as if harm,
once present, should already be obvious.

The lenses do not correct one another into a softer middle. They
*constrain* one another. Communication analysis keeps moral judgment
from outrunning understanding. Moral analysis keeps effective signaling
from substituting for accountability.

**Judgment arrives early where interpretation is thin, and late where
legitimacy has already hardened.**

The chapters that follow test whether ordinary-room patterns recur when
power, distance, and time amplify them. The point is neither
rehabilitation nor indictment. It is visibility.
