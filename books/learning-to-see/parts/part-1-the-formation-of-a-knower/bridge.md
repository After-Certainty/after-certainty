# **Bridge — The Practices We Build Around Our Limits**

Part I asked how knowers are formed and why no individual perspective is sufficient.

The answer is not a technique. It is a set of practices communities have built around recurring limits: attention that drifts, confidence that hardens, memory that fades, conflict that outruns explanation, suffering that resists tidy meaning.

Prayer, meditation, confession, criticism, ritual, lament, and communal discernment are not interchangeable. They arise from different accounts of reality. They can still confront similar human difficulties.

Part II examines six of those difficulties directly—what each practice understands about the problem it confronts, what capacity it cultivates, and how it becomes distorted.

The goal is not to escape our limits.

It is to build better relationships with them.
