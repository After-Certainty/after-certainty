# **Bridge — What Traditions Carry**

Part II examined practices that respond to distraction, overconfidence, isolation, forgetfulness, conflict, and suffering.

Those practices do not float above history. Communities inherit them, repeat them, reinterpret them, and sometimes forget the conditions that first made them necessary.

A response to danger becomes a virtue. A virtue becomes identity. Identity becomes difficult to distinguish from morality.

Part III turns toward that inheritance: cultural sediment, the discipline of evaluating practices without contempt, and the harder work of integration without reduction.
