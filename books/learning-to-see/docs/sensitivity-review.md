## Domain notes

### LDS history and practice (Ch 1, Ch 10, Ch 13)

**Reviewed for:** conversion narrative tone; anti-Mormon contempt; settled causal claims; AI as spiritual authority.

**Findings:**

- Ch 1 and Ch 10 present Utah/LDS sediment as a **working hypothesis**, not settled history, with scholarly footnotes.
- Ch 13 AI callback treats the model as inquiry companion, not revelation.
- Phase 4 trimmed LDS thread outside anchor chapters.

**Residual:** Author may wish a Latter-day Saint historian or sensitivity reader for Ch 10 hypothesis framing before publication.

### Buddhist traditions (Ch 3, 4, 9)

**Reviewed for:** wellness catalogue tone; implying one Buddhist metaphysics; unnamed meditation lineage.

**Changes:**

- Ch 4 opening names **Theravada-influenced *samatha*–*vipassanā*** as the author’s personal practice context, with footnote clarifying non-monastic, non-representative scope.
- Ch 4 comparative paragraph names Theravada insight practice **and** Zen among schools.
- Ch 9 already distinguishes Buddhist schools on *dukkha*.

**Residual:** A teacher from a named lineage could review Ch 4 opening for fidelity to that tradition’s self-understanding.

### Jewish traditions (Ch 3, 5, 6, 7)

**Reviewed for:** single-position Judaism; machloket as novelty; teshuvah collapsed into Christian confession.

**Changes:**

- Ch 3 footnote: Eruvin 13b, *Pirkei Avot* 5:17, participation boundaries noted.
- Ch 5: separate *teshuvah* paragraph and footnote (Maimonides, Hoffman); Lutheran/Catholic confession forms named explicitly.
- Ch 6: *beit midrash* and *machloket l'shem shamayim* in prose with footnote.
- Ch 7: Passover via Haggadah and Yerushalmi (unchanged); diversity already present in Ch 6.

**Residual:** Rabbinic scholar optional review of Ch 5–6 halakhic references (presented comparatively, not as instruction).

### Indigenous knowledge

**Reviewed for:** generic “Indigenous spirituality”; Haudenosaunee outline material in prose.

**Findings:**

- Haudenosaunee consensus practices from the outline **do not appear** in manuscript prose (appropriate without community consultation).
- Ch 12 **revised:** removed “Indigenous ways of knowing” in favor of non-generic extraction language (marginalized community’s ceremonial knowledge vs. institutional authority).

**Residual:** If future editions add specific Indigenous examples, require **community-named** consultation before drafting.

### Catholic and Protestant confession (Ch 5, 8)

**Reviewed for:** collapsing confession into psychology; ignoring Lutheran/Catholic differences.

**Changes:**

- Ch 5: Roman Catholic/Orthodox sacramental confession, Lutheran examination of conscience, and Jewish *teshuvah* separated.
- Ch 8: confession/forgiveness power dynamics unchanged (already strong); Bonhoeffer cheap-grace footnote present.

**Residual:** Liturgist or theologian optional review of Ch 5 confession taxonomy.

### Islamic practice (Ch 4, 7)

**Reviewed for:** prayer as generic mindfulness; eliding Sunni/Shia and recitation norms.

**Changes:**

- Ch 7: *salah*, five daily prayers, qibla, Quranic recitation in Arabic, Sunni/Shia diversity; Ingrid Mattson footnote.

**Residual:** Muslim scholar optional review of Ch 7 *salah* paragraph.

### Restorative justice (Ch 8)

**Reviewed for:** presenting RJ as universally applicable; forced participation; analogy overreach.

**Changes:**

- Existing misuse warnings retained.
- New `[^c8-rj-limits]` footnote (Zehr, Morrison) on voluntary participation and punitive institutions adopting restorative language.

**Residual:** Practitioner review of Ch 8 school/workplace examples optional.

### AI (Ch 1, 13)

**Reviewed for:** unsupported capability claims; undisclosed AI use.

**Findings:** Ch 1 disclosure footnote; Ch 13 cites FAccT literature; no “AI can never” claims.
