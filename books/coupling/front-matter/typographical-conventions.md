# Typographical Conventions

This section explains how visual cues are used throughout the book to support readers who come from different professional backgrounds.

## Bold Terms

From the Introduction onward, selected technical and structural terms appear in **bold** the first time they are used in the book.

Examples include **Invariant**, **Cohesion**, and **Coupling**.

Bold marks a term as part of the book's shared vocabulary. It does not mean the term will stay bold every time it appears. After the first use, the term returns to normal type unless emphasis is needed for clarity.

When you encounter a bold term you do not recognize, consult the Glossary at the end of the book for a concise definition.

## The Glossary

The Glossary collects domain-specific terms used across software, organizational, and institutional examples. Definitions are written for cross-domain reading: you do not need to be an engineer to read the institutional chapters, or a policy specialist to read the software chapters.

Terms are ordered so that each definition avoids depending on words defined later in the list.

## Section Headings

Subheadings mark major conceptual shifts and, in opening scenes, distinct situations.

Headings are meant to help you navigate the argument, not to decorate the prose.

## Emphasis and Tone

Italics and bold are used sparingly. Bold on glossary terms follows the rule above; other bolding appears only when it aids comprehension.

The book avoids typographic overstatement—excessive capitalization, repeated emphasis, or formatting that substitutes for clear sentences.

## Citations

Superscript notes refer to sources and further reading. They anchor claims, term lineage, or frameworks where a reader may want to go deeper. They are not ornamental; each note is tied to a specific point in the text.
