# **Organizing Question**

Why does collaboration remain fragile even when people are competent, motivated, experienced, and trying in good faith?

That question organizes everything that follows.

If collaboration were fragile only when people were selfish, lazy, or unskilled, the remedy would be selection and training: hire better people, coach harder, remove bad actors. Organizations already invest heavily in those remedies. They still encounter recurring patterns: meetings that consume energy without producing orientation, projects where invisible labor accumulates on a few contributors, initiatives that collapse into compliance when uncertainty rises, and teams that look functional in retrospectives but feel brittle in the middle of the work.

The organizing question assumes a different starting point. It assumes that fragility can persist **even when** the participants are capable and well-intentioned. Something in the structure of shared work—not only in the people—keeps producing strain.

### **How to use the question**

Use it as a diagnostic default when collaboration wobbles.

Instead of asking first, "Who is not aligned?" ask:

- What form of certainty or control has become unavailable here?
- Who can see the work, and who cannot?
- Who bears risk if the shared effort fails?
- What would it cost a participant to name uncertainty openly?
- What stabilizing practice is missing—or present but overloaded?

Those sub-questions do not replace interpersonal skill. They prevent interpersonal skill from being asked to solve structural pressure alone.

In practice, that pressure often shows up as a meeting that ends with nods but no shared picture of who will do what by when—or as resentment that surfaces only after the person holding context has already burned out. Naming the structure first keeps those moments from collapsing into a hunt for the difficult personality in the room.

### **What an answer would look like**

A satisfying answer to the organizing question will not be a single cause. It will be a map: contribution without full ownership, alignment without full understanding, practices that buy time without removing asymmetry, and failure modes where clarity, efficiency, and agreement turn into control, coercion, and threat.

The map should also leave room for why humans keep attempting collaboration anyway. Fragility is real. So is the kind of creation that only emerges through shared contribution no one fully controls.
