# Part I

## Contribution

Part I names what collaboration asks of people before any method arrives.

Readers often assume shared work fails because someone is selfish, disorganized, or not a team player. Part I tests a narrower claim: the same capable cohort can struggle again and again because contribution enters a field no one fully owns, sees, or controls.

Chapter 1 marks shared contribution without centralized authorship. Chapter 2 names why that condition feels unstable rather than merely inconvenient. Chapter 3 examines alignment that coordinates action without full mutual understanding. Chapter 4 follows how contribution moves unevenly—and why invisible maintenance work shapes what groups can sustain.

If Part I works, you should be able to describe collaboration as structural difficulty before blaming character or reaching for tighter management.

Part II then asks what practices actually stabilize shared work—and where stabilizing forms harden into control.
