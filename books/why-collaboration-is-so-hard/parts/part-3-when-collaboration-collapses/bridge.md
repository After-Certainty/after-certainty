# Part III

## When Collaboration Collapses

Part III follows how stabilizing forms fail—not by disappearing, but by inverting.

Part II showed what helps groups survive uneven signal, incomplete control, and work nobody fully owns. Part III shows how the same tools can harden: clarity becomes surveillance, efficiency becomes coercion, disagreement becomes threat, and what remains afterward is **residue** people carry into the next attempt.

Chapter 8 traces clarity that stops orienting and starts controlling. Chapter 9 follows efficiency metrics that substitute for judgment about what the work actually requires. Chapter 10 examines disagreement treated as disloyalty rather than information. Chapter 11 names residue—the fatigue, cynicism, and quiet withdrawal that outlast a single project.

If Part III works, you should recognize collapse patterns without reducing them to one bad actor or one failed workshop. Collapse is often structural: the group's stabilizers start protecting managers, metrics, or reputations instead of contribution.

Part IV then offers diagnostic lenses—and reframes fragility as structure rather than moral failure.
