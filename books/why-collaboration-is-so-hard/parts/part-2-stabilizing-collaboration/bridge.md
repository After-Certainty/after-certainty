# Part II

## Stabilizing Collaboration

Part II asks what groups build when they cannot rely on full ownership, full visibility, or full agreement—and still need shared work to continue.

Part I named the wobble: contribution arrives unevenly, alignment stays partial, and no one holds the whole picture. Part II does not pretend those conditions go away. It maps what people actually construct under them: structures that distribute attention, legibility that makes labor visible without exposing every interior, and trust practices that survive when nobody can verify everything firsthand.

Chapter 5 maps the structures—roles, rituals, documentation, turn-taking—that let contribution continue under constraint. Chapter 6 treats legibility and trust as paired requirements: people need enough signal to coordinate without demanding transparency that collaboration cannot supply. Chapter 7 describes stable collaboration as a maintained condition, not a personality achievement or a culture award.

If Part II works, you should see stabilizing practices as real achievements that still do not erase structural fragility. The scaffolding is worth building. It is also worth watching, because the same tools can harden when pressure rises.

Part III traces that inversion—when clarity, efficiency, and agreement become instruments of control.
