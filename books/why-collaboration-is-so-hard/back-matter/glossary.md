# **Glossary**

Terms used in a **structural and diagnostic** sense across this book—not as team-building slogans or as moral labels for individuals.

## Contribution asymmetry

The uneven distribution of collaborative labor, recognition, and risk. Some participants carry coordination load, translation between functions, or emotional maintenance that others never see; asymmetry produces instability even when timelines look fine.

## Diffuse ownership

A condition in which no single participant can fully predict, justify, or control an outcome, yet many must contribute. Diffuse ownership is structural in complex work, not a failure of accountability design.

## Invisible labor

Work without which collaboration fails but which does not appear in artifacts the organization already counts: tickets, slides, revenue lines. Includes maintenance, trust repair, context-holding, and translation under churn. Related scholarship often calls part of this **emotional labor** when feeling rules are managed for the group's comfort.

## Legibility

The degree to which a contribution can be read by others—especially by those who grade, fund, or promote. Uneven legibility is not the same as uneven value; it is a visibility structure that shapes who feels the collaboration is stable.

## Partial coherence

Enough overlapping understanding to take the next step together, without identical mental models or full agreement on every latent disagreement. Partial coherence is a species of coordination, not sloppy alignment.

## Residue

What remains after collaboration collapses or hardens: mistrust, unspoken corrections, narratives that freeze blame, habits of performed agreement. Residue is structural aftermath, not only interpersonal hurt.

## Shared contribution

Multiple participants introducing labor, judgment, and constraint into a common effort when no one holds the whole picture in advance. Distinct from centralized authorship or a committee version of one person's idea.

## Shared movement

Alignment on what happens next—a timebox, experiment, escalation rule, pause—without resolving every underlying disagreement. Valued when constraints are visible and revision is survivable.

## Stabilizing practices

Facilitation, rituals, documentation, turn-taking norms, retrospectives, explicit credit for maintenance. They help groups survive fragility; they do not remove contribution asymmetry, uneven visibility, or incomplete control.

## Structural fragility

The normal wobble of collaboration under shared contribution: diffuse ownership, partial coherence, and uneven load. Fragility is evidence that collaboration is real, not evidence that participants lack character.
