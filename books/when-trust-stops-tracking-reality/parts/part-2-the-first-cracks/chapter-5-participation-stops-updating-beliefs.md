# **Participation Stops Updating Beliefs**

> *What happens when participation stops teaching?*

Walter continued raising concerns—not constantly, not dramatically. Patient complaints appeared more frequently. Communication problems surfaced across clinics. Processes that once seemed reliable required more work to maintain.

Renata saw the same organization—same meetings, same employees, many of the same reports—yet reached different conclusions. When concerns emerged, she saw dedicated employees solving problems, teams responding to challenges, an organization serving thousands of patients despite inevitable imperfections. The same events that increased Walter's concern reinforced Renata's confidence.

Neither had stopped participating. Both remained engaged, invested, and committed to the mission. Yet participation was doing different work. Walter's participation generated questions. Renata's generated reassurance—and for Renata, that was not irrational. Showing up, staying committed, and continuing to contribute felt like evidence that Calder remained worthy of trust.

Participation does not automatically produce learning. It only produces learning when experience can still change beliefs. When participation itself becomes evidence—proof of loyalty, commitment, or belonging—experience may continue while beliefs stop updating.

For most of Calder's history, participation had worked the other way: outcomes shaped beliefs, beliefs shaped decisions, and experience remained connected to learning.

Over time, participation can begin confirming rather than testing. People continue attending meetings and contributing, but new experiences reinforce existing beliefs. The same activity continues. The learning slows.

The pattern is not limited to organizations. A spouse who keeps working on a struggling marriage may treat continued effort as proof the marriage is sound—even when the same problems keep returning. A parent who keeps showing up for an adult child may treat loyalty as evidence the relationship remains healthy. Multi-level marketing organizations often treat setbacks as evidence of insufficient commitment rather than grounds for scrutiny.[^c5-keep-mlm]

In organizations such as NXIVM, participation increasingly reinforced the beliefs that justified it.[^c5-raine-nxivm] Members remained active and committed, yet experiences that might have challenged trust were interpreted as reasons to deepen it.

John Dewey argued that experience alone does not guarantee learning.[^c5-dewey-experience-education] People learn when experience remains connected to reflection and revision. Trust stays healthy when participation exposes it to correction; drift begins when participation primarily reinforces what people already believe.

At Calder, Walter and Renata embodied two responses to the same environment—participation as information versus participation as evidence of commitment. Both matter. The challenge emerges when one crowds out the other.

People continue showing up, contributing, and caring—but trust begins producing participation that confirms beliefs rather than testing them.

That is often where drift begins.

Not when people stop participating.

When participation stops teaching.

### Core Principle

**Trust Can Interrupt Learning**

Participation can become evidence—and when it does, experience continues while beliefs stop updating.
