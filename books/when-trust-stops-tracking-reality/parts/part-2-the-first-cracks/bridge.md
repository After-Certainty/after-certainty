# Part II — The First Cracks

Calder Family Health continues to grow. Patients still receive care, employees still believe in the mission, communities still trust the organization, and most people still trust David Marsh. From the outside, little appears wrong.

That is often how drift begins.

Major failures rarely emerge all at once. They usually begin as small anomalies, unanswered questions, isolated concerns, or outcomes that seem easy to explain away. At first, these signals are weak—and the organization remains successful, the mission still matters, and the trust is still largely deserved.

Yet conditions begin changing. The organization becomes larger, decisions become more distributed, and fewer people experience the original sources of trust directly. The stories remain, the reputation remains, and the trust remains—but what changes is the environment around them.

The next chapters explore what happens when trust encounters signals that challenge it. Why do some warnings receive attention while others are ignored? Why do some organizations adapt while others struggle to learn? And why do good intentions sometimes make correction more difficult rather than less?

The first cracks rarely appear because trust disappears.

They appear because trust remains.
