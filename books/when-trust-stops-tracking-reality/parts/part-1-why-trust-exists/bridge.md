# **Why Trust Exists**

Trust solves a real problem. No individual can verify everything—and human beings cooperate by relying on knowledge, expertise, and judgment they do not personally possess.

Trust makes that cooperation possible. Without trust, every decision would require endless verification.[^p1-luhmann-verification] Every interaction would become slower, more expensive, and more uncertain. Families, organizations, communities, and institutions depend on trust because verification alone cannot sustain them.

For that reason, trust should not be understood as a flaw in human reasoning. Trust is one of humanity's most important coordination mechanisms.

The purpose of the next chapters is not to criticize trust. It is to understand why trust works. Only after understanding how trust forms can we understand how it sometimes drifts.

[^p1-luhmann-verification]: Luhmann, *Trust and Power: Two Works*, trans. Howard Davis, John Raffan, and Kathryn Rooney (Chichester, UK: John Wiley & Sons, 1979). Luhmann argued that trust reduces complexity by allowing action without constant verification.
