## Chapter title

Chapter 7 — The Launch Schedule

## Governing question

How does a schedule become morally difficult to challenge—and who has stop authority?

## Ordinary opening image

A calendar date that has become increasingly difficult to move.

## Case / terrain

The Challenger accident revealed both a technical failure and an organizational decision failure. Engineers had raised concerns about launching in unusually cold conditions. The later investigation found serious failures in communication, awareness, and decision-making. NASA’s recovery included hardware changes as well as changes to safety culture and management structure.

## Conceptual movement

Deadlines gather social force. A date can begin as a coordination tool and become a promise, a reputation, a budget assumption, and a test of commitment. Concern then has to overcome not merely evidence but the accumulated meaning of delay.

## Questions to preserve

- How does a schedule become morally difficult to challenge?
- Who possesses actual stop authority?
- Why can warnings be present without becoming consequential?
- What conditions allow a person to revise a visible commitment without being treated as disloyal or incompetent?

## Return image

The engineers could see the concern. The constraint had moved into organizational authorization: whether technical uncertainty could become a legitimate reason to stop.
