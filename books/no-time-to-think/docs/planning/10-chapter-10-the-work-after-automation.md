## Chapter title

Chapter 10 — The Work After Automation

## Governing question

As generation becomes cheaper, which scarce forms of work must institutions still resource?

## Ordinary opening image

A queue filled with generated documents, code changes, images, decisions, and recommendations waiting for acceptance.

## Case / terrain

Future terrain: as generation becomes cheaper, the scarce work increasingly becomes selecting, verifying, integrating, maintaining, explaining, revising, and taking responsibility. AI may not simply replace work. It may increase the volume of artifacts requiring these forms of work.

## Conceptual movement

Institutions that automate production while treating review and maintenance as overhead will discover the bottleneck has moved into judgment work they never staffed.

## Questions to preserve

- What happens when institutions automate production but continue treating review and maintenance as overhead?
- Which roles become more important when intermediate output becomes abundant?
- How do novices learn judgment when tools can cheaply imitate competent output?
- Who owns a decision assembled from many automated contributions?
- How do organizations preserve the capacity to operate when tools fail?
- Which forms of work should remain intentionally human because they create capability or consequence contact?

## Return image

The queue is full of finished artifacts. The unfinished work is deciding what any of them should mean.
