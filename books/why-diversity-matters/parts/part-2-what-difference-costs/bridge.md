# Part II

## What Difference Costs

Part I focused on what groups fail to notice. Part II focuses on what it costs to say what you see.

Those are not the same problem.

A person may notice something important and still struggle to make it matter. They may speak and still fail to change the conversation. They may participate and still leave without influence.

The gap between visibility and impact is where many frustrations live. Not every difficulty is a misunderstanding. Some difficulties are structural. Some are constraints. Some are the ordinary costs of standing in a different landscape than the people evaluating your choices.

The chapters in this part follow four forms those costs can take: choices that look free from a distance but are not free up close; words that carry different weight depending on who speaks them and who hears them; inclusion that stops at attendance while power remains unchanged; realities that are true but difficult to explain quickly enough to be believed.

Each chapter asks a version of the same question: what becomes harder when your experience does not match the assumptions surrounding you?

The answer is rarely moral failure alone. Often it is terrain.

Part III then asks what might help — and what remains uncertain even when we learn to see more clearly.
