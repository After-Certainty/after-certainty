# **Helping Shape This Book**

This book is meant to be corrected. Most of what shaped it arrived through real conversations with people whose lives look very different from mine. Most insights entered quietly. A few voices appear directly when their words matter.

The questions that follow are for those conversations — and for readers who notice what I've missed.

Race, gender, class, and disability are not side issues here. They shape risk, power, and how people are treated. They help explain much of what goes wrong. They do not explain everything.

### **What I'm Looking For**

I'm not looking for people to speak for groups, perfect answers, or agreement. I am looking for people who live with risks others don't see, whose perspectives get filtered out, and who can tell me where I'm missing something important.

If this book makes you think, "You're not seeing ___," that's exactly the conversation I want.

### **Blind Spots I Already Know About**

The draft you are reading was built from my perspective first. Early readers have helped me name what that perspective tends to leave out.

The stories in the manuscript are often **clean**. They illustrate a pattern — a garden, a trail, a town meeting — without always showing **what it cost** when the pattern failed. I am looking for stories where the stakes were real: livelihood, health, safety, dignity, a relationship, a family's stability.

I also know the draft **leans toward misunderstanding**. Many chapters describe good people who missed something, or heard something differently than intended. That is often true. It is not the whole truth. Some people will tell me they were understood perfectly — and ignored anyway. Some failures are about **power**, **incentives**, or **indifference**, not confusion. I need those stories too. They do not disprove the book's argument; they define where it stops.

If you have lived through caregiving, chronic illness, disability, poverty, immigration, religious difference, addiction, abuse, gender identity, or the quiet work of holding a family together financially — your experience may be underrepresented here even when the pattern I describe fits. I am not asking you to speak for a category. I am asking whether the book's map matches the terrain you have actually walked.

### **Types of Stories That Would Help Most**

One good story is worth more than ten short answers. The ones I need most often sound like this:

- **Consequence** — not only "people missed something," but "here's what happened because they missed it."
- **Constraint** — a choice that looked simple from the outside but was not simple up close; a risk others did not know you were carrying.
- **Influence without power** — you were in the room, you were heard, and nothing changed.
- **Translation** — you knew something was wrong long before you could make it legible to people who mattered.
- **Boundary cases** — they did not misunderstand you; they understood and did not act, or acting was not in their interest.

You do not need to fit a type. If a moment from your life still sticks with you, that is enough to start.

### **Why I'm Doing This This Way**

Most people who've read my earlier work come from backgrounds similar to mine. That's not a moral failure — but it does limit what I can see.

This book argues that important information often arrives from outside our existing perspective. The interview phase is where the manuscript gets the chance to live by its own thesis.

This book exists because I don't think good intentions are enough anymore.
