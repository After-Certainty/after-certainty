# **How This Book Was Shaped**

<!-- draft: outline only -->

Short chapters.

Real conversations with people who live very different lives than mine.

Most insights shape the book quietly. A few voices appear directly when their words matter.

This book is meant to be corrected.

### **The Shape of the Book**

1. **Why "Having Diverse People" Isn't Enough** — Why being in the room doesn't always mean being listened to, and why inclusion can fail without anyone doing anything wrong.

2. **When Everyone Agrees — and Everyone Is Wrong** — How groups confuse agreement with understanding, and why shared backgrounds make blind spots worse.

3. **Bias Isn't Always What We Think It Is** — Why bias is often about shortcuts and overconfidence, not hatred—and why some people pay the price for those shortcuts more than others.

4. **Why Some Choices Aren't Really Choices** — How money, safety, health, and legal risk change what people can realistically say or do.

5. **Why Words Don't Land the Same Way** — How the same message can feel harmless to one person and threatening to another—and why this isn't just miscommunication.

6. **Being Included Without Having Power** — Why representation often stops at visibility, and how decisions stay unchanged.

7. **The Cost of Being Easy to Understand** — Why systems reward people who sound confident and familiar, and overlook people whose experiences are harder to explain.

8. **Why Diversity Helps Us Catch Mistakes** — How difference helps surface problems early—if we're willing to listen.

9. **Building Systems That Don't Rely on Perfect People** — Why training and good intentions aren't enough, and how we might design systems that expect misunderstanding and still work.

10. **What I Still Don't Know** — What changed in my thinking, what didn't, and where this book still falls short. This chapter leaves things open on purpose.
