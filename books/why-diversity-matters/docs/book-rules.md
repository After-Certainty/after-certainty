# Why Diversity Matters — Book Rules

## Purpose

Architectural constraints for **Why Diversity Matters** (*Why Good Intentions Still Miss So Much*).

This is not a marketing brief. It preserves conceptual cohesion, stylistic consistency, and structural discipline across the manuscript.

## Book Scope and Structure

### Target length

- ~8–15k words (essay edition, consistent with cluster books like Why Collaboration Is So Hard)
- Structure per `index.md`: 3 parts, 3 bridges, 10 chapters, 5 front-matter sections (plus generated title/copyright)

### Markdown file structure

- One markdown file per chapter (and per bridge).
- Filenames must match the chapter slug in `index.md`.
- `index.md` is the hub; all units must be linked from it.

### Front-matter depth

- Front matter should perform its distinct role in the reading sequence (not placeholder blurbs).
- Copyright/legal sections are exempt from depth expectations.
- `questions-for-readers.md` is a conversation guide for interviews, not a quiz.

## Core invariant (non-negotiable)

Diversity is about what gets noticed, trusted, and ignored—not just who is in the room.

Every chapter must map back to this claim. If a section cannot be tied to it, it does not belong.

## Thematic arc by part

**Part I — When Good Intentions Miss:** Presence without listening; agreement without understanding; bias as shortcuts.

**Part II — What Difference Costs:** Constrained choices; words that land differently; inclusion without power; the cost of legibility.

**Part III — What Might Help:** Catching mistakes early; systems that expect misunderstanding; open questions.

## Tone and positioning

### Shared house style (upcoming nonfiction)

- Diagnostic, not prescriptive.
- Observational, not preachy.
- Clear, not academic.
- Serious, not dramatic.
- Calm, deliberate, grounded.

### This book must not be

- A guide to hiring targets
- A moral lecture
- A denial that race, gender, class, or disability matter
- Culture-war framing or manifesto voice

### This book must be

- Grounded in lived difference and real conversations
- Open to correction by people whose perspectives get filtered out
- Short-chapter, essayistic, and honest about what the author still doesn't know
- Diagnostic about why good intentions miss so much

### Plain-speak habits

- Short sentences by default; one clear claim per sentence unless parallelism is deliberate.
- Concrete verbs over abstract noun chains.
- Ground observable behavior before naming concepts.
- Avoid management-blog tone and diversity-as-virtue-signaling framing.

## Chapter construction

Each chapter should roughly follow:

1. Opening pressure (scene, case, or concrete moment—often from a conversation)
2. Structural analysis
3. Return to the core invariant
4. Optional pull-quote (concise, structural; no bold inside pull-quotes)

Most insights from interviews shape the book quietly. A few voices appear directly when their words matter.

## Repetition rules

- Reintroducing the invariant in varied form is allowed.
- Repeating the same examples, definitions without deepening, or case templates without new nuance is not.

## Citation and glossary

### Citation (when prose is drafted)

- Use Pandoc footnote syntax (`[^id]` with `[^id]:` definitions).
- Stable, chapter-scoped IDs.
- Never fabricate references; mark unverified sources as "verify source".

### Glossary (when used)

- Maintain `back-matter/glossary.md` only if domain terms accumulate.
- Bold glossary terms only at first occurrence in manuscript reading order.

## Drafting checks

Before marking a unit approved, ask:

1. Does this reinforce the core invariant?
2. Does this advance the arc, not circle it?
3. Is tone diagnostic rather than prescriptive?
4. Are claims proportionate to evidence at this draft stage?
5. Does this respect that interviewees speak for themselves, not for groups?
