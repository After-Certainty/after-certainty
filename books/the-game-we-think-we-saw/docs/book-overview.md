## Short premise

Sports gives us some of the clearest outcomes in public life. A score becomes final, a ruling takes effect, and one side advances. But the result cannot fully explain what produced it, why it should count, what it is allowed to justify, who carried its cost, or what the event will eventually mean. *The Game We Think We Saw* follows those unsettled questions from the scoreboard into the systems, bodies, judgments, and stories surrounding the game.

## Governing claim

> A result can be final while its cause, legitimacy, cost, and meaning remain unsettled.

Sports produces unusually decisive outcomes. The clock expires. The whistle sounds. One team wins and another loses. Yet the certainty of the outcome tempts us to treat the result as a complete explanation.

The scoreboard is not wrong. It is complete only about the question it was built to answer.

## Chapter progression

> What was measured → what caused it → why it counts → what it excuses → what it demands → who may refuse → what it becomes.
