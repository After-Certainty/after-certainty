## Chapter title

Chapter 2 — When the Star Becomes the Explanation

## Governing question

What produced the result?

## Purpose in the book’s progression

**Conceptual role: Causation.** Show how distributed achievement is narratively compressed into individual explanation, and how rescue can block institutional learning.

## Ordinary opening image

Late-clock possession where the ball returns to the same player again.

## Central mechanism

Systems are hard to narrate; people have faces. Repeated individual rescue can preserve outcomes while hiding design failure.

## Primary case or case structure

Tom Brady and Bill Belichick — not to crown one total author of success, but to show the persistent need to choose between them as evidence of the compression problem.

## Supporting cases

- Michael Jordan and the Chicago Bulls
- LeBron James
- quarterbacks who compensate for weak systems
- teams whose stars repeatedly rescue poor design

## Key distinctions

1. A star within a system
2. A system built around a star
3. A star substituted for a system

**Diagnostic question:** Does the star’s presence make the system more capable, or merely make its incapacity less visible?

## Necessary complication or counterexample

Building around rare ability is not inherently dependency. Rational specialization is real.

## Research questions

- Which contemporaneous Brady/Belichick attribution debates are documentable without becoming fan-war reenactment?
- What examples best separate specialization from dependency?
- Which supporting cases are citation-ready vs lore-heavy?

## Evidence risks

Moderate — credit disputes are culturally heated; avoid absolute causal verdicts and unsupported claims about “who really won titles.”

## Possible return image

The same player receives the ball again as the clock runs down.

## Approved compression lines

- Leadership increases the capacity around the leader. Dependency increases the number of situations only the leader can save.
