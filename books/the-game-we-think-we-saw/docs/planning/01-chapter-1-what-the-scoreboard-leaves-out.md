## Chapter title

Chapter 1 — What the Scoreboard Leaves Out

## Governing question

What did the result actually measure?

## Purpose in the book’s progression

**Conceptual role: Measurement.** Open the life-of-a-result sequence by showing that comparison depends on compression, and that every measure creates a remainder.

## Ordinary opening image

A completed box score or ledger of official statistics after a game; or a role (lineman, catcher, defensive midfielder) whose contribution barely registers on the sheet fans trust.

## Central mechanism

1. Existing judgment leaves something out.
2. Measurement makes the omission visible.
3. Measurement earns authority.
4. The sport optimizes toward it.
5. A new remainder appears.

## Primary case or case structure

Comparative measurement history rather than one personality: traditional scouting vs analytics, with Moneyball as one major node among others.

## Supporting cases

- Bill Russell
- offensive linemen
- catchers
- defensive midfielders
- off-ball contribution
- analytics revealing previous blind spots
- new measures creating new blind spots

## Key distinctions

- Instrument vs definition of value
- Visibility gained vs visibility lost
- Correct numbers vs complete explanation of the game

## Necessary complication or counterexample

This is not a “numbers versus the human game” argument. Analytics often reveals what inherited observation ignored.

## Research questions

- Which historical measures most clearly created new blind spots after correcting old ones?
- Which Russell / Moneyball claims need primary verification before prose?
- How to show off-ball contribution without inventing unverified anecdotes?

## Evidence risks

Lower relative risk, but inherited sports lore and pop-Moneyball summaries often oversimplify. Cite league records, scouting/analytics literature, and contemporaneous debate carefully.

## Possible return image

A fan closes the box score. The numbers remain correct. The memory remains larger.

## Approved compression lines

- Every measurement helps us see by deciding what not to see.
- The scoreboard is not wrong. It is complete only about the question it was built to answer.
