# Living in Sediment — Chapter Outline

Authoritative structural skeleton for Phase 1 drafting. See `book-rules.md` for tone and invariant constraints.

---

## Core Thesis

We live inside inherited systems built to solve constraints that no longer exist.

When constraints disappear but structures remain, meaning drifts.

Over time, outputs become norms.

Authority becomes invisible.

The danger is not sediment.

The danger is forgetting it is sediment.

---

## Prologue — September Is Not Seven

Open with the calendar mismatch.

- September meaning seven.
- January inserted later.
- The names never changed.

This becomes the metaphor:

We are always two months off from our origin.

End the prologue with:

> The question is not whether we live in sediment.
> The question is whether we know we do.

## PART IV — Living Among Sediment

This is the philosophical landing zone.

### Chapter 13 — When Justification Fades

Three states of a structure:

1. Necessary
2. Useful
3. Habitual

Habitual structures feel natural.

### Chapter 14 — Stability and Corrosion

Not all fossils are dangerous.

- Some stabilize (weekends)
- Some distort (sorting metrics)
- Some are neutral

Introduce a diagnostic framework (reader-facing prose in Ch 14):

- Does it quietly gather power into fewer hands than it once did?
- Does it simplify something that genuinely needs simplifying, or has it begun simplifying people themselves?
- When decisions are made within it, can someone still explain them, question them, and answer for them?

(Ch 15 adds distributional questions: who benefits from persistence; who pays for inertia.)

### Chapter 15 — Archaeology of the Present

Teach readers how to see sediment.

Questions to ask:

- What problem did this solve?
- Does that problem still exist?
- Who benefits from its persistence?
- Who pays for its inertia?

## Structural Notes

### Tone

- Observational, essayistic—*After Certainty* / *Trust Beyond Similarity* register; Rebecca Solnit adjacency (concrete before abstract, associative but disciplined)
- Calm, almost anthropological
- No outrage posture

### Length

- Compact essay edition—no fixed total word count; chapters scale to the thought
- Clean, tight units; brevity is a feature when the observation lands
- Examples grounded but not partisan

### Audience

- Thoughtful general reader
- Policy-curious but not ideological
- Readers of essayistic nonfiction (Solnit, Arendt, Klein)
