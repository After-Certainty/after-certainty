# Living in Sediment — Bibliography Guide

Working notes for source selection during drafting and the citation pass. Export bibliography lives in [`../back-matter/bibliography.md`](../back-matter/bibliography.md).

---

## What the bibliography serves

> We live inside inherited systems built to solve constraints that no longer exist. When constraints disappear but structures remain, meaning drifts.

The bibliography should reflect **breadth**, not a single academic tradition.

**Core insight (July 2026):** The book is primarily about **historical perception**—learning to look at the present as a future historian would. Sediment is the carrying image; the bibliography should reinforce an interdisciplinary way of looking at the world.

**Target scale for publication:**

- ~60–100 references in export bibliography (current scaffold: ~75)
- ~25–35 **essential** works that establish credibility and give readers exploration paths
- ~8–12 sources appearing repeatedly once footnotes are added; others appear once where prose earns them

---

## Citation discipline

Write observation-driven prose first; use citations to anchor key claims and intellectual lineage—not to drive the argument.

**Footnote timing:** Add Pandoc footnotes (`[^c1-id]`) at structural pivots during the citation pass. Chapter-scoped IDs per [`upcoming/docs/bibliography-pass.md`](../../../upcoming/docs/bibliography-pass.md).

**Blank line before each `[^id]:` definition block** (required for Pandoc/EPUB).

**Foucault — *The Order of Things*:** Use carefully for how classification systems make worlds legible; do not adopt the full archaeological framework wholesale.

**Alexander:** Central for Chapter 8 and the sediment metaphor—environments as accumulated solutions to recurring human problems. *The Timeless Way of Building* is the essential Alexander entry; *A Pattern Language* supports architecture chapters.
