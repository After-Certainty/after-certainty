# **Chapter 7 — Disagreement is Suppressed**

A crack appears in a wall. It’s small. Easy to miss unless you’re
looking for it. A thin line running along the surface, barely
interrupting the structure around it.

Someone notices. They pause for a moment. Then they move on.

The building still stands. Nothing shifts. Nothing fails. Everything
continues as expected.

Later, the crack is still there. Slightly longer now. Slightly more
visible.

It could be addressed. It would not take much.

But it isn’t.

The building does not collapse when the first crack appears. It changes
when the crack is ignored.

The same thing happens in groups. A decision is made. Something feels
off. Someone notices. They hesitate. Maybe it’s not worth raising. Maybe
it’s already been decided. Maybe it will resolve on its own.

Nothing is said. The decision moves forward. At first, this feels like
alignment. The group is moving quickly. There is no friction. Nothing
slows things down. But something else has changed.

**Disagreement is Suppressed.**[^c7-groupthink]

The absence is subtle. There is no visible conflict. No disagreement. No
interruption. And that is the signal.

Before, concerns would surface. Questions would be raised. Alternatives
would appear. Now, they don’t. Not because nothing is wrong. Because it
is no longer being said.

The group is still observing. People still notice what doesn’t fit. They
still see what might go wrong.

But the response changes. They hold it. They soften it. They let it
pass. The moment moves on. And like the crack, it remains.

> The warning is usually silence, not a fight.

This is how erosion begins. Not with failure. With silence.

[^c7-groupthink]: Irving L. Janis, *Groupthink: Psychological Studies of Policy
  Decisions and Fiascoes*, 2nd ed. (Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1982).
