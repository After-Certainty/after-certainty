# **Chapter 8 — Learning Collapses**

A crack that isn’t repaired doesn’t stay the same. It spreads.

Not always in a straight line. Not always in a way that’s easy to
follow. It moves beneath the surface, branching in places that aren’t
immediately visible.

At some point, you can’t tell where it began. Only that something is no
longer aligned.

The same thing happens in groups.

A decision is made. Work moves forward. The plan is executed. Things
continue.

On the surface, everything looks fine. Progress is visible. Updates are
shared. Nothing appears to be failing.

But something has changed. The signal is no longer clear. What’s
actually happening does not move cleanly back to the people making
decisions.

It is delayed. It is filtered. It is softened.

Small issues are described as manageable. Delays are explained as
temporary. Risks are mentioned, but not fully explored.

No one is lying. But no one is saying everything either. The signal
weakens as it moves.

This is the shift.

Before, problems surfaced early. Now, they arrive later—if they arrive
at all. Before, the signal was direct. Now, it passes through layers.[^c8-signal-and-layers]

Each layer adjusts it slightly. To keep things moving. To avoid
unnecessary concern. To present progress. By the time it reaches the
center, it no longer reflects what is actually happening.

**Learning Collapse.**

The people making decisions are still receiving information. But it is
not the same information.

It no longer carries the full weight of reality. At first, this is hard
to notice. Decisions still appear to work. Nothing fails immediately.
The plan still holds together.

But the loop has changed.

Decision. Delay. Partial signal.

The connection is still there. But it is weaker.

Like a crack that runs beneath the surface, the signal hasn’t
disappeared—it’s just harder to trace. The next decision is made without
fully seeing the last. That is not because the group is careless. It is
because the signal is incomplete.

This is where drift begins. Not in a single moment. Across many small
ones.

Each decision is slightly less informed than the one before. Each
adjustment is slightly delayed. Each signal arrives slightly altered.

Individually, none of this is enough to cause failure. Together, it
changes direction. The group continues to move. It is no longer clear how
closely it is aligned with what is actually happening.

> The story at the center and the story on the ground can diverge while both still sound fine.

The structure still stands. Work continues. Progress is reported. But
something important has been lost—clarity. Without clarity, correction
slows. From here, something else begins to take hold.

What was meant to be temporary starts to remain.

[^c8-signal-and-layers]: Charles Perrow, *Normal Accidents: Living with
  High-Risk Technologies* (New York: Basic Books, 1984); Diane Vaughan, *The
  Challenger Launch Decision: Risky Technology, Culture, and Deviance at NASA*
  (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1996); James Reason, *Human Error*
  (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1990).
