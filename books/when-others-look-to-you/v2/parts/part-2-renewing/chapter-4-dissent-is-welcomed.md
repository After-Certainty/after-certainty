# **Chapter 4 — Dissent is Welcomed**

A boat is already in motion. The wind shifts. Not dramatically. Not
enough to force a reaction. Just enough that the sail no longer sits the
same way it did before. At first, nothing happens.

The boat keeps moving. The course looks unchanged. The difference is
small enough to ignore. But the wind has changed.

Someone notices. A slight tension in the line. A change in pressure
against the sail. A feeling that something is just a little off. They
speak up.

“Wind’s shifted.”

It’s a small thing to say. It doesn’t stop the boat. It doesn’t demand a
decision. But it brings the change into view.

The sail is adjusted. The course corrects. The boat continues. Nothing
dramatic happens. Because the change was noticed early. You don’t argue
with the wind. You either adjust, or you drift.

The same thing happens in groups. A problem appears. Not fully formed.
Not yet visible to everyone. Just a signal that something isn’t working
the way it should. A delay. A mismatch. A quiet concern.

At first, it’s easy to ignore. The work continues. The plan still
holds—at least on the surface.

But something has changed.

If the signal moves early, it can still shape what happens next. Someone
says it.

“This isn’t lining up.”

“This might be off.”

“We should take another look.”

It doesn’t need to be complete. It doesn’t need to be certain. It only
needs to be early.

**Dissent is Welcomed.**

When it is, the group stays close to reality.[^c4-psychological-safety] The signal reaches the
point where **Attention Finds a Focus**. It is heard before decisions
are locked in. It arrives while change is still possible.

This is what allows adjustment. If something no longer fits, **Feedback Drives
Change**. The group doesn’t need to defend what was already decided. It
doesn’t need to protect the plan. It can respond to what is actually
happening.

The earlier the signal, the smaller the adjustment. A small shift now
prevents a larger correction later.

When dissent still moves early, something important remains intact.

**Leaders Feel the Consequences.**

The people deciding are still close to what happens next. They see the
effect of their choices. They feel the impact. They adjust in response.

The loop remains tight. Signal. Adjustment. Result. Nothing drifts far
from reality. But this depends on something simple. The signal has to
move. Someone has to notice, speak, and carry it to the place where
decisions are made.

Without that, the boat still moves. But not where it thinks it is going.

> Early signals stay cheap; late signals stay expensive.

From here, the difference is not whether the signal appears. It is what
happens once it does.

[^c4-psychological-safety]: Amy C. Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization:
  Creating Psychological Safety in the Workplace for Learning, Innovation, and
  Growth* (Hoboken, NJ: Wiley, 2018).
