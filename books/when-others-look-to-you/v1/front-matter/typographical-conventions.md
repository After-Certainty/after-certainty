# **Typographical Conventions**

This short section sits after the acknowledgements and before the introduction.
It only names a few recurring layout choices—nothing you need to study before
reading.

### **Headings**

Part titles, bridges between parts, and chapter titles are meant to help you
find your place. Sub-headings mark a real turn in the argument, not decoration.

**Appendix B** lists **Pattern** titles exactly as they appear there; the same
titles show up in the chapters.

### **Pull-quotes**

Most chapters end with a short pull-quote. It compresses the chapter’s
structural point—not a slogan or a personality line.

::: {custom-style="Pull Quote Block"}
When others look to you, your posture becomes part of their world.
:::

### **Pattern blocks**

In places you will see a small block that opens with **Pattern:** and a bold
name (for example **Pattern: Attention Finds a Focus**). That name is the
book’s handle for a recurring dynamic. In ordinary prose the book uses the same
bold titles; it does not keep repeating the word *pattern*.

::: {custom-style="Pattern Block"}
**Pattern: Example Name**

One or two sentences on what is happening in the structure of that moment.
:::

### **Vignettes**

Some chapters use a short scene, set off from the analysis, to make an idea
easier to picture. The prose then picks back up and ties the scene to the
chapter’s claim.

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
The room goes quiet after a mistake is named. No one speaks first.
Then someone says, "Let's move on," and the room accepts it.
:::
