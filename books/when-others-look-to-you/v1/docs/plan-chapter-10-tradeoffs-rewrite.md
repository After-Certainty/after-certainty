## Goal

Rewrite chapter 10 so it matches the **flow** established in chapters 1–9 and in the Part III / Part IV rewrite plans: **concrete, observable material first** (paired vignettes), then patterns, definitions, and cross-references—not abstract framing up front.

Part IV’s arc: **scale and drift** (chapter 9) → **tradeoffs under pressure** (this chapter) → **misjudgment** (chapter 11). Chapter 10 should **own urgency, compression of tradeoffs, and what gets locked in after the spike**—without re-teaching the full harm / effectiveness / legitimacy toolkit from scratch.

## Current gap

The chapter opens with **frame-first** moves: **“When Pressure Reorders Judgment,”** **“Three Lenses, One Moment,”** **“States Under Pressure,”** and **“Crisis Across the Lenses”** **before** the reader reaches the full **Emergency Order** vignette. That **inverts** the see-it-then-name-it order the rest of the book trains readers to expect (same structural issue called out in plans for chapters 6, 7, 8, and 9).

**Strengths to preserve:** the storm / centralized-command vignette’s behavioral detail (speed vs blind spots vs “proven under pressure”); the core tension that urgency compresses tradeoffs and **correction** narrows first; vibrant vs decaying reads under load; links to **Exceptions are Forever**, **Feedback Drives Change**, **Leadership Reproduces Itself**, **Examples Accumulate**; the boundary sequence (temporary → repeated → default); **Restraint Under Urgency**; footnotes (Agamben on emergency precedent; Staw on escalating commitment); closing pull quote (“Pressure clarifies commitments…”)—revisit wording only if the new arc demands it.

## Proposed chapter arc (structure)

1. **Open concrete**
  - **Lead with two short contrasting vignettes** in the same domain: one recovery path (channels reopen; exceptions sunset) and one lock-in path (temporary emergency controls become baseline).
  - Retain **The Emergency Order** as one side of the pair (tighten as needed), then add or adapt a second scene that clearly shows post-spike reopening.
2. **Bridge, then compression**
  - After the scene(s), one short paragraph: what we watched and why “what worked this week” is not the same as “what structure we inherited.” Mirror the chapter 9 plan’s “what scale changes” handoff style: plain language before lens stacking.
3. **Core tension in plain language**
  - Keep “pressure compresses tradeoffs” and “what helps now can damage later” as the chapter spine—**after** the reader has pictured centralization, delayed local correction, and persistence past the storm.
4. **Three lenses, one moment (relocated)**
  - Fold **harm**, **effectiveness**, **legitimacy**, and **correction** moving together **here**, tied to observable moves from the opening (who bears cost, what counts as winning, what becomes acceptable, whether dissent still changes decisions in time). Avoid a dense first-screen list of definitions.
5. **States under pressure**
  - Keep regenerative / adaptive / entrenched / stalled and vibrant vs decaying cost—place **after** the opening stakes so the grid reads as clarification, not cold taxonomy.
6. **Crisis across the lenses**
  - Retain substance (widened harm tolerance, shortened correction window, legitimacy via precedent, template for who leads next). Trim repetition with sections above so “crisis” reads as amplification, not a second introduction.
7. **Pressure and recovery**
  - Keep the recovery question central; cross-links to **Examples Accumulate** and **Exceptions are Forever** stay, but attached to behaviors already shown (rules meant for emergencies becoming everyday).
8. **Restraint under urgency**
  - Keep; optionally anchor with one extra sentence calling back to the vignette’s trade (visible decisiveness vs preserved channels)—without moralizing.
9. **Boundary conditions**
  - Keep the three-step sequence; ensure it lands as observable institutional sequence, not only an abstract list.
10. **What remains possible → handoff to chapter 11**
  - Close the body with a **tight transition**: pressure clarifies trajectories; misjudgment waits when the visible story and the structural story diverge—setting up chapter 11 without previewing its full taxonomy.
11. **Pull quote**
  - Retain unless the rewritten through-line suggests a sharper single sentence.

## Vignette blueprint (parallel build)

Use the same scaffolding for both opening scenes so the contrast reads as structural, not stylistic.

- **Shared setup (2-3 sentences):** same institution, same pressure event, same decision bottleneck.
- **Immediate action (1-2 sentences):** both scenes centralize quickly to move fast.
- **Divergence point (1 sentence):** one explicit choice about whether challenge channels reopen after the spike.
- **Recovery-path vignette (3-5 sentences):** temporary controls sunset, local overrides return, and one plan changes based on new ground signal.
- **Lock-in-path vignette (3-5 sentences):** temporary controls persist, dissent reroutes privately, and “proven under pressure” is used to justify permanence.
- **Mirror sentence check:** both scenes answer the same three reads in near-parallel wording: who pays, what counts as effective, and whether correction can still change decisions in time.

- One closing pull quote still **compresses** the chapter’s core claim.
