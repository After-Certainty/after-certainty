# Reader trial — Chapter 11 (Why We Misjudge Leaders)

## How this trial was run

- **Stance:** Adult reader who has read Parts I–III and Chapters 9–10 in order; first focused pass on Chapter 11 only (no line edits during the trial).
- **Goal:** Notice where attention holds, dips, or races; where the chapter feels like setup vs payoff; where a tired reader would skim.

---

## Overall arc

The chapter delivers a clear promise: misjudgment is not only wrong reads—it **feeds back** and **selects**. The opening and the closing **A Clearer Read** bracket that argument well. The weakest risk is the long **middle** (roughly Intention Trap through Confidence): it can feel like a **psychology textbook sprint** before the plant vignette lands.

## Where a reader might skim or stall

1. **Confidence section (short):** Feels like a subsection many leadership books have; less **specific** to this book’s vocabulary than erosion or the vignette. *Stall risk: low; skim risk: moderate.*

2. **Post–Missed Quarter (three paragraphs before Learning Collapse):** Same mirror idea stated three ways (bias punishes cost; familiar misread has mirror; two directions meet). A reader may **read the first, skim the next two**, and still get the point—or feel the chapter is **explaining twice**. *Consider:* one fewer analytical beat after the vignette (optional cut for a future pass).

3. **What to Watch — four parallel “Watch whether” lines:** Deliberate rhythm per house style; a reader looking for **narrative** may glaze; a reader looking for **traction** may underline. *Not a flaw—know the tradeoff.*

4. **Selection Pressure — line 129 (long sentence):** One breathless conditional; meaning is clear, but a **tired** reader may re-read. *Optional:* break into two sentences.

---

## What works unusually well

- **Inverse outcome** (Missed Quarter + analysis): Unexpected direction; makes the chapter feel **authored**, not cited.
- **Erosion feels rational** paired with **structural blindness** (observer vs leader): Clear **pair** the reader can carry.
- **Dissent misread as disloyalty** (selective followership): High “I’ve seen that” recognition without anecdote overload.
- **Closing:** Sets up partial repair without repeating Ch. 12’s full integration—appropriate tease.

---

## Comprehension checks (honest)

- **“Selection pressure”** appears early and late; a reader who missed the opening may still get it from **Misjudgment as Selection Pressure**—OK redundancy for important term.
- **“The familiar misread”** (post–Missed Quarter) points back to success hiding harm from earlier in the *book*; if not, it still parses as “the usual misread.” Minor **insider** phrasing—acceptable for Ch. 11.

## Verdict

Chapter 11 **holds** as a reader: opening and closing are strong; the vignette and erosion material are distinctive. The main **attention dip** to watch is the **Confidence** sandwich and the **triple analytic stack** after the vignette—not because any line is wrong, but because **density** peaks there before **What to Watch** asks for sustained observational discipline again.
