# Edit: Front Matter Consolidation
 
## Problem
 
The front matter contains four distinct sections before Chapter 1 that are doing overlapping work:
 
1. **Author's Note** — explains why the book was written and how to read it
2. **Preface** — describes the argument and the book's structure
3. **Introduction** — "Attention Finds a Focus" — the actual opening argument
4. **Typographical Conventions** — explains layout choices
 
The **Author's Note** and **Preface** are redundant. Both describe the book's purpose, both sketch the structure, both frame the audience. A reader hits the structural overview twice before reaching the argument. The Typographical Conventions placement also creates a gear-shift between the Introduction and the first bridge.
 
---
 
## Goal
 
Consolidate to three front matter sections:
 
1. **Author's Note** (kept, rewritten to be personal and motivational — *why this book*)
2. **Preface** (collapsed into Author's Note, OR rewritten to be purely intellectual/argumentative — *what this book does*)
3. **Typographical Conventions** (moved — see below)
 
The **Introduction** stays as-is. It is working.
 
---
 
## Changes
 
### 1. Decide: collapse or differentiate
 
**Option A — Collapse into one section**
 
Merge Author's Note and Preface into a single "Author's Note." Keep the personal motivation ("this book begins with attention") and the structural overview. Cut all duplicate framing. Target length: roughly what the Preface alone is now.
 
**Option B — Give each a distinct job (recommended)**
 
- **Author's Note**: Personal register only. *Why I wrote this. What I kept noticing. What the book is trying to do in one paragraph.* No structural map. No audience list. ~3–4 paragraphs max.
- **Preface**: Intellectual/argumentative register only. *The frame, the argument, the five-part structure, what the book is not.* Remove the "Why This Book" and "How to Read It" sections, which duplicate the Author's Note. The Preface becomes a clean statement of the argument before the reader enters it.
 
Option B is recommended because the two sections currently exist — they just haven't been given distinct jobs.
 
### 2. Author's Note: cut or relocate
 
The following subsections in the Author's Note are either duplicated in the Preface or belong in the Introduction:
 
- **"What This Book Tries to Do"** — the four bullets (how influence spreads, how harm is absorbed, etc.) duplicate the Preface's Part I–V overview. Cut from Author's Note.
- **"How to Read It"** — the throughline description (`Attention → Influence → Structure → Renewal or Erosion → Circulation`) appears again in the Introduction. Cut from Author's Note or reduce to one sentence pointing forward.
 
What should remain in the Author's Note after cuts: the opening two paragraphs ("Most books on leadership begin with role...") and a brief closing sentence on why the language is plain.
 
### 3. Preface: cut the audience list
 
The "Who This Is For" bullet list (parents, managers, teachers, etc.) in the Preface is doing the same work as "How to Read It" in the Author's Note. **Cut one of the two.** Recommended: keep the Preface's "Who This Is For" (it's more concrete) and cut "How to Read It" from the Author's Note entirely.
 
### 4. Typographical Conventions: move
 
Currently placed *after* the Introduction, creating a gear-shift. Two options:
 
- **Move before the Introduction** — place it after the Preface and before the Introduction. The Introduction then flows directly into the first Bridge.
- **Move to an appendix** — readers who need it can find it; readers who don't won't hit a speed bump.
 
Recommended: move before the Introduction, after Preface/Acknowledgements. It's short enough that it doesn't cost much, and having it before the argument starts is cleaner than interrupting the flow after the Introduction lands.
 
### 5. Structural map consistency
 
The Preface currently describes a **five-part** structure (Parts I–V). The body of the book uses **bridges** and **chapter numbers** but does not consistently label parts by number. Either:
 
- Add part-title pages to the body that match the Preface's Part I–V labeling, OR
- Update the Preface to describe the structure using the bridge/chapter language the body actually uses
 
Pick one and make them match. The current mismatch means the Preface's map doesn't match what the reader finds.

## Recommended sequence after edits
 
1. Author's Note *(personal, ~3–4 paragraphs)*
2. Preface *(argumentative frame, structure overview)*
3. Acknowledgements
4. Typographical Conventions
5. Introduction — Attention Finds a Focus
6. Bridge — From Attention to Pattern
7. Chapter 1...
