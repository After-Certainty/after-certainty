# **Chapter 7 — Effectiveness and Its Illusions**

### **When the Numbers Look Good**

Two teams finish the quarter above target. In the first, weekly reviews still
reopen decisions when field evidence shifts—a metric may slip, then steady again
on stronger footing.

In the second, the dashboard stays green while objections go quiet. Work still
ships, but more fixes migrate to side channels and private workarounds.

Both teams read as effective from a distance. Only one preserves the conditions
that keep effectiveness real over time.[^c7-performance-paradox]

### **What Results Can Hide**

We rightly judge leadership partly by visible outcomes. That check matters—and it
is still incomplete.

A group can hit its goals while **correction** narrows. It can look coordinated
while trust, learning, and adaptability thin below the surface.

The pair of questions that matters is not only "did this work now?" but "what did
this result do to future capacity?"

### **After the Citation**

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
The public library’s literacy program receives a state citation; the director’s photo runs in the paper, and the board’s annual dinner is a warm night. A children’s librarian mentions afterward, in passing, that the new family intake still asks for documents the district stopped accepting in June; two families have already been turned away at the desk. The director thanks everyone for the year, pivots to dessert, and the room follows.

She does not press it. Nobody ruled her out of order. The celebration just had no place left for that sentence.
:::

The result was real. So was what the room could no longer hold in public.

Together they show the pattern: visible success can grow while channels for
inconvenient signal narrow.

### **Effectiveness Over Time**

**Effectiveness** is about whether the work actually produces results. The
stronger read is whether those outcomes will hold—or whether they were bought by
quietly burning through conditions the group still needs. **Renewal** tends to
spend early to preserve capacity. **Erosion** tends to borrow against the
future—later paid as preventable **harm**, rework, trust loss, and expensive
repair.[^c7-deferred-costs]

### **When Success Narrows Correction**

Success increases authority. It can improve coordination. It can also make
dissent feel optional.

After visible wins, conflicting signals often arrive later, softer, or not at
all. People learn what is easy to raise and what is safer to keep to themselves.[^c7-escalation]

That pattern is a recurring trap. The same run of success that proves capability
can narrow the feedback channels needed to sustain it. That same run also teaches
the next leaders what counts as normal leadership here—**Leadership Reproduces Itself**.

### **Correction as Time-Bound Effectiveness**

Leadership sends influence outward. **Effectiveness** over time depends on whether
feedback comes back while costs are still small.

**Correction** means warnings, dissent, and what people on the ground see can
reach decision-makers and change direction without punishment.[^c7-voice]
**Dissent is Welcomed** means people can still challenge without losing their
place.

When that path stays open, groups can absorb smaller visible costs now to avoid
larger failures later. That read asks whether the group can still change
direction—and whether it is staying healthy or moving toward decay.

Without **correction**, authority can remain while learning slows. In the short
run, outcomes may still look strong. Over time, recurrence, rework, and repair
costs rise, and groups drift toward the **decaying group**.

In **vibrant** groups, warnings and dissent can still move and revise direction.
In **decaying** groups, **correction** narrows. The same care may exist privately,
but the public path for changing a decision narrows until only workarounds
remain.

When **correction** stays open, **deferred effectiveness** becomes possible: patient
judgment that pays a smaller cost now to avoid a larger one later.

### **The Fast Rule Pauses**

::: {custom-style="Vignette Block"}
A regional claims center rolls out a rule that auto-denies incomplete submissions after forty-eight hours. Week one looks efficient. Pending counts drop, managers hit daily closure targets, and the operations report finally reads as stable.

In the second week, a medical-review analyst sees a repeat pattern in escalations from dialysis patients. Documents are arriving, but from clinics that upload in batches, not in the window the rule expects. The denials are technically compliant and operationally wrong.

The claims VP pauses the auto-denial trigger for that category, shifts two teams to same-day manual review, and accepts that month-end closure rates will fall. The drop is immediate and public. Three weeks later, so is what it prevented: appeal volume does not spike, provider complaints stay low, and the unit avoids a broad reopen of closed files.
:::

The vignette illustrates **deferred effectiveness** in practice: accept a smaller
visible cost now so you do not pay a larger failure later.

The pattern steps through in the same order whenever **correction** still works.
First, the week after the choice often looks worse on the metrics people watch.
Second, leaders accept that trade because slowing implementation, reopening
assumptions, or taking awkward public correction is what keeps reliability from
breaking later.

### **Performative Compliance and Real Coordination**

Lead with dashboards and reports. What gets measured can hide
whether people are actually with you.

Watch what people do next. They can produce good-looking counts while
privately withdrawing trust. They can repeat approved language while quietly
routing around decisions they do not believe in.

From the outside, the group still looks coordinated. Underneath, its ability to
act together is already splitting, and the real story can hide behind tidy
paperwork. **Learning Collapse** follows.

When you look closely at effectiveness, selective followership shows up.
Open disagreement can strengthen results. Quiet divergence wears them down over
time.

### **Reading Effectiveness While It Is Happening**

To understand what is really happening, pair outcomes with the conditions
underneath them as the work moves—not only the headline result when it lands.

Keep four checks active while work is underway: whether **correction** stayed
open; whether concerns changed decisions or only decorated meetings; whether
success increased learning capacity or reduced it; whether trust held while
results were achieved.

Without that read, results mislead.

### **Legitimacy Next**

**Effectiveness** is what worked. **Legitimacy** is why people keep following:
how authority forms, hardens, and renews over time, and what makes that
sustainable.

::: {custom-style="Pull Quote Block"}
Performance can certify capacity while masking decay.
