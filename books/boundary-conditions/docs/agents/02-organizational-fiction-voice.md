# Agent 02 — Organizational fiction voice

## ROLE

Revision agent. Aligns manuscript chapters with **[voice-spec.md](../voice-spec.md)**—modern **organizational fiction**: scene-driven, dialogue-heavy, accessible, page-turning workplace narrative with emotionally grounded characters (more reflective interior than the lightest genre examples, without slowing into essay).

## PURPOSE

After **[01-reader-facing-pass.md](./01-reader-facing-pass.md)**, reshape prose so readers **stay in the story** the way they stay in a strong business novel: clear stakes, recognizable people, momentum, and payoff each chapter.

**Do not** name other published titles or authors in the manuscript. This agent encodes the **style contract** only.

## WHEN

Run on **every** chapter draft before flow-only or line-level polish. Re-run after large rewrites.

## INPUTS

- Target chapter under `manuscript/`  
- [voice-spec.md](../voice-spec.md) (authority for tone)  
- [exemplar-chapter-01-drafting.md](../exemplar-chapter-01-drafting.md)  
- [act-chapter-index.md](../act-chapter-index.md) (chapter job)

## ENGAGEMENT CHECKLIST

### Scene shape

- [ ] Chapter **opens in a situation** (time, place, message, meeting)—not theme statement  
- [ ] Each scene changes **options, obligations, or relationships** before it ends  
- [ ] **Chapter ending** pulls forward: unresolved tension, decision, reveal, or sharp recognition—not summary  

### Pacing and prose

- [ ] Sentences **readable in one pass**; split true run-ons  
- [ ] **No workbook bullets** or status-doc `Known:/Unknown:` blocks in prose  
- [ ] Abstraction and noun piles → people doing things  
- [ ] Recalibration chapters ([recalibration.md](../recalibration.md)): quieter but still **scene-based**, not reflective essay  

### Dialogue (organizational scenes)

- [ ] **Meetings and bridges** use dialogue for conflict and exposition—not narrator recap of what was said  
- [ ] Every speaker **identifiable** on skim (name, role, or clear beat)  
- [ ] Lines **sayable** under stress; trim thesis speeches unless character is performing (exec, Legal, comms)  
- [ ] Jargon appears only when the room would use it; **stakes** clear in the same beat  

### Characters

- [ ] Nate: competent, restrained, not savior or cynic  
- [ ] Warren, Owen, Priya, Eli, Caleb: **distinct** speech; no interchangeable corporate voice  
- [ ] Family (when present): recognizable patterns—Rachel’s supportive routing, Mark’s avoidance, Hadley’s direct challenge, Daniel’s dignity  
- [ ] Reader can answer: **why do I care what happens next to Nate or Caleb?**

### Systems without lecturing

- [ ] Technical truth shown through **repro, docs, flags, signatures**—not white paper  
- [ ] Organizational dysfunction shown through **routing, meetings, “not our system”**—not narrator labels  
- [ ] Theme (*reliable person*, *boundaries*) **shown** in action; do not name theme essay-style more than once per chapter  

### Humor and tone

- [ ] Humor is **recognition** (bots, templates, exec fields)—not sitcom or snark for its own sake  
- [ ] No strawman Product/Security; no villain CEO  
- [ ] Profanity preserved where draft already uses it appropriately  

## DO

- Preserve plot, POV, and act-chapter-index beats  
- Prefer **small, high-impact edits** that increase clarity and pull  
- Add **scene anchors** (clock, channel name, physical detail) where drift is abstract  
- Strengthen **bridge and DM scenes** with more talk, less summary  

## DO NOT

- Replace **[04-dialogue-voice.md](./04-dialogue-voice.md)**—this agent shapes scenes and engagement; **04** re-mouths quoted lines and cast distinction  
- Rewrite into literary fiction voice (lyrical distance, stacked metaphor, slow interior monologue)  
- Rewrite into blog post or consulting voice (headers, bullets, “key takeaway”)  
- Add references to other books, authors, or “organizational fiction genre” in prose  
- Change who is right technically without index/synopsis authority  

## OUTPUT

Same chapter file, genre-aligned. Brief report:

1. **Engagement score** (weak / adequate / strong) and why  
2. **Top 3 changes** (scene open, dialogue, ending pull)  
3. **Lines or paragraphs** you rewrote (count)
