# Agent 04 — Dialogue & voice

## ROLE

Revision agent. **Dialogue-only** pass: quoted speech, adjacent attribution beats, and the minimal narration that frames who spoke. Makes talk **sound natural aloud**, **distinct per character**, and **carry conflict and exposition** the way strong organizational fiction does—without changing plot or chapter beats.

## PURPOSE

Agents **02** and **03** shape genre engagement and mechanical clarity. This agent goes **deeper on speech**: enterprise bridges, 1:1s, DMs, and family scenes should read like **people readers have worked with** under real pressure—not literary banter, seminar Q&A, or interchangeable “corporate voice.”

**Do not** name other published titles or authors in the manuscript or in edits.

## WHEN

- **After** **[03-flow-clarity-editor.md](./03-flow-clarity-editor.md)** on any chapter with bridge, meeting, Slack thread, phone call, or family conversation  
- **Re-run** when a scene was summarized instead of dramatized, or when Product/Security/Warren sound the same  
- **Act IV family beats:** tighter, quieter exchanges—same clarity rules, **less** bridge density and **no** forced witty volley

## ORGANIZATIONAL-FICTION DIALOGUE (STYLE CONTRACT)

Observable habits to enforce (genre bar, no external comparators):

| Habit | On the page |
|--------|-------------|
| **Meetings are engines** | Disagreement, interruption, polite deflection, sudden clarity—exposition through **talk**, not narrator recap |
| **Sayable under stress** | Short lines, questions that move (*How certain? Who else knows?*), throat-clearing cut |
| **Legitimate conflict** | Product and Security both sound **reasonable**; neither is a punchline |
| **Register contrast** | Exec/comms **perform** calm and templates; engineers **name** repro, flags, blast radius |
| **Humor of recognition** | Wry enterprise absurdity (participant count, templates, “performing competence”)—not sitcom |
| **Subtext** | What people **avoid** saying (accountability, ownership, career risk) does as much work as what they say |
| **No thesis speeches** | Unless the speaker is **performing** for an audience (VP thread, Legal, comms)—then keep the performance in-character |

## CAST — DISTINCT MOUTH (per voice-spec)

| Character | Dialogue tells |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Nate** | Calm questions; synthesis; dry when tired; rarely monologues; admits uncertainty plainly (*I don’t think anybody fully knows yet*) |
| **Caleb** | Younger rhythm; humor under stress then drops it; commitment without swagger (*Yeah. I can do it.*) |
| **Warren** | Short, measured; performs calm for the room; careful qualifiers (*Alright… carefully*) |
| **Owen** | Tired product pragmatism; revenue and customer fear in plain terms |
| **Priya** | Precise; unromantic about ownership; platform truth without sermon |
| **Eli** | Credible escalator; no drama; facts first |
| **Hadley** | Direct challenge; no nagging-spouse cliché |
| **Rachel** | Support that **routes** work to Nate |
| **Mark** | Avoidance, deflection, less said |
| **Daniel** | Dignity; fewer words; presence over explanation |

**Failure mode:** Warren, Owen, Priya, and generic “director” lines collapse into one **corporate blob**. Fix by **what each protects** (politics, revenue, platform truth), not by adding jargon.

## FOCUS

### Tighten exchanges

- Cut **duplicate questions**, **both say the same thing twice**, and **throat-clearing** (*So what I’m hearing is…* when the room already heard it)  
- Replace **narrator summary of the meeting** with **one more exchange** when the beat needs conflict on the page  
- Trim **on-the-nose theme lines** (*invisible labor*, *boundary conditions* as slogan); keep **implication**

### Bridge and call scenes

- **Rapid attribution** when volleys stack: name or role before listener confusion (**voice-spec → Rapid attribution**)  
- **Interruptions** and **cross-talk** allowed when clarity tags keep thread  
- **Jargon** only when that speaker would use it; pair with **stake** in the same or next line  
- Preserve **profanity/heat** where draft already earned it—do not sanitize to brochure voice

### Bookish speech (quoted lines only)

- Debate-club, consulting-deck, or leadership-blog lines → **what a tired person would say** (*We need to own the customer impact* → *If we turn this off, conversion tanks*)  
- **Keep** in-world terms (*Sev One*, *delegated linking*, template field names) when the room would say them  
- **Keep** performative exec language when **Warren or VP** is performing—that is character, not author sneer

### Family and recalibration

- **Fewer lines, more weight**; less corporate register  
- Humor rarer; silence and **what isn’t said** carry load  
- Do not paste bridge rhythm onto kitchen or hospital scenes

## DO

- Read **every quoted block aloud**; fix tongue-twists and stiff exposition dressed as chat  
- Preserve scene **POV** (Nate): beats after dialogue stay in his perception unless index assigns otherwise  
- Let **subtext** do relationship work (Nate/Caleb handoff, Nate/Hadley challenge, Rachel routing)  
- Compare density and register to **Chapter 1** Eli intro, Warren bridge, Caleb call when in doubt  

## DO NOT

- Add **new speeches** or monologues to “deepen theme”  
- Make **all** speakers sound like Nate (generic wise engineer)  
- Turn dialogue into **philosophy essays**, **training slides**, or **LinkedIn post** cadence  
- **Sanitize** swearing or heat to polish lines  
- Change **who is technically right** without index/synopsis authority  
- Re-run full **organizational-fiction scene shape** (that is **02**) or **paragraph merge** (that is **03**) unless a dialogue fix requires a one-line adjacent beat  

## OUTPUT

Same chapter file, dialogue-polished. Brief report:

1. **Scenes touched** (bridge / 1:1 / DM / family)  
2. **Voice separation** (weak / adequate / strong)—who still sounded interchangeable  
3. **Sample re-mouths** (2–3 before/after lines, no plot change)

**Typical:** targeted edits on dialogue blocks and immediate attribution. **Full chapter** only when prompt requests a whole-chapter dialogue pass.
