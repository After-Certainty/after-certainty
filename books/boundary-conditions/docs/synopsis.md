# FULL STORY SYNOPSIS — BOUNDARY CONDITIONS

*Tight spine for planning and revision; align with [project-spec.md](./project-spec.md) and [act-chapter-index.md](./act-chapter-index.md).*

## ACT I — DISCOVERY

On a ordinary Thursday afternoon, **Eli Moreno** pings **Nate Mercer** with a reproducible flaw in delegated onboarding: under certain conditions, one account can overwrite another customer’s profile. The exploit is not sophisticated—it requires timing and curiosity—which makes it worse.

Nate joins a growing incident bridge. **Warren Hale** declares Sev One. Mitigation ideas multiply (disable linking, suspend sync, add verification), each fixing one edge while breaking others. Product and Security both sound reasonable; that is the trap.

Warren asks Nate to coordinate; Nate is boarding soon. He hands operational point to **Caleb Ross**, who commits with steadiness rather than bravado. Nate warns that proposed mitigations may lower immediate risk without solving the underlying authorization problem—which nobody fully owns yet.

From the airport, Nate watches the organization industrialize response: dashboards, channels, leadership threads questioning why Security escalated, why a months-old finding is suddenly urgent. Same incident, different realities.

## ACT II — COORDINATION

The investigation widens. Nate becomes **connective tissue** between Security, Product, Engineering, Identity, and leadership—translating, synthesizing, absorbing uncertainty others will not hold.

Teams defend **local boundaries** (“not our system,” “not our roadmap”). Mitigation proposals proliferate. Nate’s coordination load grows.

At home, his father’s cognitive decline becomes harder to ignore—small slips, repeated stories, logistics piling up. Siblings unconsciously route family coordination to Nate, mirroring the org chart’s invisible routing.

## ACT III — REALIZATION

Nate and **Isaac Chen** converge: the core issue is **authorization**, **auditable consent**, and delegated identity—not merely sync or staging bugs.

AI-assisted security analysis and institutional fear complicate the room. The company is reacting to **documenting and acknowledging** the vulnerability, not only to the technical flaw.

Nate exhausts himself between incident and family. He sees the pattern clearly: **every system he belongs to solves uncertainty by routing it into the most reliable person available.**

## ACT IV — BOUNDARIES (RECALIBRATION)

Nate pursues an internal **transfer** and begins **recalibration**—a quieter movement than Acts I–III. He sleeps better, helps his father more intentionally, reconnects with **Hadley**, lets some meetings happen without him, resists stabilizing everything by default.

He still helps. He still cares. He stops unconsciously accepting **total ownership** of unresolved systems—at work and at home. See **[recalibration.md](./recalibration.md)** for tone and beat guidance.

## ACT V — RETURN WITHOUT HEROICS

Months later the exploit **resurfaces or escalates**. Leadership reaches for Nate because he holds historical and architectural context.

This time Nate responds differently: he will **explain, advise, review, and transfer knowledge**—but refuses to become invisible coordinating infrastructure again. The ending tests whether the organization—and the family—can adapt structurally instead of re-defaulting to the reliable person.
