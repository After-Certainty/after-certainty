# About This Book

Boundary Conditions began as a way for me to think through a period of my own life.

Not the exact events of this story, but something emotionally adjacent to it.

Like Nate Mercer, I found myself trying to hold together too many systems at once:

work,
family,
responsibility,
expectations,
and the quiet pressure that reliable people often place on themselves long before anyone else asks them to.

Over time, what begins as helping can slowly become absorbing.

People begin depending on the person who consistently steps forward. Organizations adapt around them. Families do too. Eventually the reliable person discovers that the line between responsibility and self-erasure has become difficult to see clearly.

This novel grew out of my attempt to better understand that boundary.

In many ways, I am still learning the same lessons Nate is learning in this story. I suspect most people encounter some version of these moments eventually, even if the setting looks very different from incident bridges, escalation calls, and organizational charts.

The pace of real life is slower and messier than fiction. Problems linger. Conversations repeat. Systems adapt unevenly. Progress rarely arrives in a clean arc. Writing this novel became a way to give shape and language to experiences that often feel difficult to explain while you are still living inside them.

This book is also part of a broader body of work connected to my ongoing project, [After Certainty](https://after-certainty.com).

That project exists for a simple reason:
to create language for patterns that many people recognize but struggle to describe clearly:

responsibility routing,
institutional drift,
communication breakdown,
invisible emotional labor,
coordination under pressure,
and the strange ways systems quietly shape the people inside them.

My hope is that readers might recognize parts of their own lives in these stories and ideas, and perhaps even contribute to the conversation themselves. I expect the project to evolve over time, including more collaborative participation from others.

I also want to acknowledge that this novel was written with significant assistance from AI systems. I used AI not to replace the emotional reality behind the story, but as a collaborative tool for shaping structure, dialogue, pacing, revision, and fictional transformation.

Many elements of this novel were inspired by real emotional experiences, but the characters, organizations, events, and situations have been altered, recombined, fictionalized, and expanded substantially in order to create a work of fiction rather than a direct retelling of real events.

More than anything, I hope this book helps readers feel a little less alone inside the systems they are trying to navigate.
