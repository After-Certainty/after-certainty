# Trust Beyond Similarity — Bibliography Guide

Working notes for source selection during drafting. Export bibliography lives in [`../back-matter/bibliography.md`](../back-matter/bibliography.md).

---

## Thesis the bibliography serves

> People trust similarity because it reduces uncertainty.
>
> But trust becomes most valuable when it allows us to benefit from perspectives we do not share.

Curate around that claim—not a generic diversity or management reading list.

**Bibliography clusters:**

1. Social trust
2. Epistemic humility
3. Perspective-taking
4. Group decision making
5. Collaboration across difference
6. High-trust societies
7. Complexity and distributed knowledge

---

## Citation discipline

Write observation-driven prose first; use citations to anchor key claims and intellectual lineage—not to drive the argument.

**Target density:** About 8–12 sources appearing repeatedly across the manuscript; others appear once or twice where they naturally fit. Do not force every entry into the book.

**Footnote timing:** Add Pandoc footnotes (`[^c1-id]`) at structural pivots during the citation pass. Chapter-scoped IDs per [`upcoming/docs/bibliography-pass.md`](../../../upcoming/docs/bibliography-pass.md).

**Blank line before each `[^id]:` definition block** (required for Pandoc/EPUB).

## Notes on specific titles

**Svendsen — *Trust*:** Published in the Reflections series (Aarhus University Press, 2014 English ed.). Often discussed in press coverage as Denmark's high-trust culture; motto in the book: "Control is good, but trust is cheaper." Do not confuse with Fukuyama's *Trust*—different argument, complementary use in Ch. 1 and 9.

**McPherson et al.:** Standard homophily citation when the chapter names the pattern explicitly.

**Page — *The Difference* vs. *The Diversity Bonus*:** *The Difference* for thesis-level claims; *The Diversity Bonus* for when conditions and team composition matter in prose.
