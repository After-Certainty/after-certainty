# **Epilogue — Holding the Lens**

Philip opened the shared plan one more time before the review.

The assumption was still gone. The milestone had been adjusted. His line from an earlier meeting sat in the summary block—clean, legible, reasonable—without the pause that had preceded it in the room. Someone had pasted a photograph of the whiteboard into the deck: a crossed date, layers of erasure and rewrite, more history than any single person had meant to put there.

He had not made every call the document attributed to him. He had made enough of them, late enough, with enough authority in the room, that the difference no longer mattered for coordination. The organization was already moving on what the record said.

His cursor hovered over the summary block. He could edit it—add the pause back, mark the decision provisional, make the tentativeness visible for the next ten people who would inherit the plan without having been in the room. That would mean reopening a question the calendar had already spent.

He closed the edit window without saving.

Nora had left a comment on the dependency line weeks ago—*Unresolved as of last session—room did not return*—still sitting there, unanswered. The document sat between them now, containing both truths, fully preserving neither.

Philip closed the document. He did not correct the summary block. He did add one line to the agenda for the next meeting—a single visible question about what the room believed had been decided.

Once you have a lens, you want to aim it at other people. You want to explain them. Name them. Win with your new vocabulary. Holding the lens is simpler than that, and harder: noticing that you already moved from hearing to verdict, staying in the unfinished part even when finishing the story would feel better.

No one masters this. People who coordinate work, love, repair, and power will sometimes hurt each other without malice, simply because meaning moves faster than contact.

The work is not to keep meaning from moving. It will move.

The work is to leave it somewhere to return.
