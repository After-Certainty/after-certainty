## Second scene — expand (priority)

Replace summarized check-in with slowed scene. Author sample dialogue:

> “I thought we took it out,” someone said.  
> Philip looked toward Nora. “Did we?”  
> Nora turned her laptop slightly, as though the document itself might answer.  
> “You said we should revisit it,” she said. “Then everyone started planning without it.”  
> “That isn’t the same as deciding.”  
> “No,” Nora said. “But it is what the room did.”

**Nora’s perspective:** Not merely mechanism that corrupted Philip’s meaning. She may understand something he does not: organizations cannot preserve every layer of tentativeness indefinitely. Her compression may be coordination work she believed was required—not necessarily careless.

**Dramatic goal:** Two partial truths protecting different realities (Philip: intent; Nora: what the room did; document: both, fully preserving neither).

## Middle exposition — consolidate (~20–25% cut)

Replace procession of conceptual paragraphs with **three movements**:

### Movement A — Authority gives language consequence

Merge material from “That is not a story about a careless leader…” through “…before slow correction can catch it.”

- Replace “authority changes the physics of a sentence” with plainer: **“Authority changes what a sentence is allowed to remain.”** (Philip’s sentence not allowed to remain provisional.)

### Movement B — Records turn interpretation into inheritance

Move legitimacy + institutional-record material **adjacent to shared document**. Central insight: overread becomes recorded, distributed, treated as historical fact.

- Replace “A minute is also a compression machine” with essayistic register, e.g.:

> Meeting minutes preserve a meeting by reducing it. They remove the hesitations, glances, abandoned sentences, and changes in tempo that made the words mean what they meant in the room.

### Movement C — Power creates obligation to reopen

Combine restraint, employee voice, responsibility into one progression ending:

> He cannot control every interpretation. But once he sees the weight his words carry, he becomes more responsible for creating visible permission to question them.

**Refine central claim** — replace:

> The more power someone holds, the less control they have over how people hear them.

With (author preference, second option):

> Authority gives people more power over what happens and less freedom to pretend their words will land as lightly as they felt while speaking them.

## Core principle — author-approved close

```markdown
### Core Principle: Meaning Reinforces Itself

What locked in yesterday quietly trains what people notice tomorrow. Under authority, that reinforcement speeds up. Early reads become defaults. Defaults become records. Records become the story new people inherit before they have heard a single live voice.

Each settled interpretation makes the next one easier to settle, until correction requires contending not only with a person’s memory but with a trail of reasonable documents and reasonable decisions that all point in the same direction.

Seeing this clearly does not give leaders a new script. It makes one obligation harder to dodge. The person whose words carry the most weight has the least room to pretend that weight arrived only in the listener’s imagination—and the most reason to leave meaning somewhere it can still be revised before it becomes the plan.
```

**Cut:** middle principle paragraph “not malice and not stupidity” (defense already made in scene).
