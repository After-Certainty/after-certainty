# Quick Pass Card - Chapter Edits

Use this card for fast chapter-by-chapter passes. Default target voice is
Part I: Chapter 1 (Signal), Chapter 2 (Compression), Chapter 3 (Restraint).

## 1) Voice check (first read)

- Does this read like calm social observation, not theory explanation?
- Does the tone stay serious, restrained, and human?
- Would a reader think "I recognize this" instead of "I am studying a model"?

## 2) Abstraction check

- Replace abstract noun chains with observable actions when possible.
- Translate model wording into human wording:
  - interpretation/narrative/sensemaking -> what people assume or decide
  - certainty/context stabilization -> what starts feeling true
  - acceleration/mechanism language -> people settling faster under pressure
- Keep core terms only where they are structurally necessary.

## 3) Grounding check

- After any abstract claim, add a recognizable human moment quickly.
- Favor ordinary settings: work, relationships, text threads, authority,
  disagreement, repair attempts.
- Prefer what changed in the conversation over what changed in the framework.

## 4) Progression check

- Remove adjacent paragraphs that explain the same mechanism twice.
- Keep section movement: claim -> observable consequence -> next pressure.
- Stop early when a line lands; do not over-explain.

## 5) Compression + restraint language check

**Compression**

- Prefer:
  - "people settle on what they think happened"
  - "the story starts feeling true" / "stops feeling chosen and starts feeling obvious"
  - "people trade depth for speed"
- Avoid:
  - dense process/mechanism exposition
  - academic synthesis cadence
  - corporate, self-help, or therapy tone

**Restraint** (re-ground moral or abstract passages)

- Prefer behavior and cost: delay, not replying yet, tolerating not knowing, carrying discomfort.
- Prefer: "leave more room for revision," "without the protection certainty usually provides," "no rule can fully automate restraint."
- Avoid: "proceduralized," schematic without/out contrast strings, philosophy-default stacking.
- Hinge sentences allowed sparingly; do not chain aphorisms.

## 6) Rhythm check

- Mix short and medium sentences for calm readability.
- Avoid long runs of one-sentence paragraphs.
- Avoid repeated sentence openings and repeated abstract keywords.

## 7) Architecture check

- Preserve chapter structure, pattern blocks, vignette blocks, citations, and
  core thesis.
- Preserve book progression (keep it felt, not lecture-y):
  - signal shapes what arrives before words settle
  - compression settles meaning quickly
  - restraint delays closure so contact can hold
- Improve readability without rewriting framework intent.

## 7b) Vignette check (when present)

- Strongest when nothing dramatic happens, conflict stays subtle, cost is social or internal.
- Analysis stays outside the block; scene does the emotional calibration.

## 8) Final lock question

If a sentence sounds "correct" but not lived, rewrite once more.

## 9) Attentional architecture (audiobook / long listen)

Full guidance: `docs/book-rules.md` → **Attentional Architecture**.

Quick checks:

- **Endings:** Are several sections landing with the same “does not X /
  explains Y” or “problem is not X / is Y” shape? Rotate closure types (image,
  hard stop, paradox, consequence, shorter line—see book-rules).
- **Openings:** Does every chapter open with the same framing→clarify arc?
  Consider vignette-first, contradiction-first, or concrete failure-first where
  one chapter in a part can carry the variation.
- **Headings:** Are “What X Reveals” / “Makes Unavoidable” repeating across
  chapters? Rename a few to substance-led titles (pressure, scene, turn).
- **Abstract traffic:** In systemic or institutional passages, break chains of
  contact/connection/meaning with rooms, calendars, bodies, threads, meetings—without
  losing defined terms where they matter.

## 10) Between vignettes

If the chapter has vignettes, does exposition **advance pressure** between
them—or only clarify the same idea again? Add consequence or stakes earlier,
not only refinement.

## 11) Publishable-tier polish (optional final pass)

Full guidance: `docs/book-rules.md` → **Advanced Polish (Publishable-Tier Pass)**.

- **Landing:** After a two-sentence claim that already landed, is there a
  third sentence that only re-stabilizes? Try cutting it.
- **Vignette landing:** After a hard emotional stop (a short line that already
  landed), is there still another sentence *inside the same block* spelling out
  the implication? Cut it; let the silence do the work.
- **Tonal contour:** Any long stretch of “reflective diagnostic” without a
  compression spike, hard stop, sensory beat, or shorter line? Add **one**
  strategic contrast per section where the ear might go flat.
- **Late sections:** Do multiple chapters end with the same “synthesis →
  implication → restraint” shape? Vary: image, vignette-as-closer, cold line,
  contradiction, very short stop.
- **Abstraction:** After several pages of structural vocabulary, have you
  touched room, body, or timing once in plain terms?

## 12) Over-trust, over-cite, over-stack (late pass)

Full guidance: `docs/book-rules.md` → **Advanced Polish** (Over-resolution;
Phenomenological weight; Citation rhythm).

- **Third stabilizer:** After a good insight, are you still explaining, then
  explaining the explanation—especially to pre-empt moral misread? Cut one
  layer; trust the reader.
- **Phenomenology:** In synthesis paragraphs, can one beat be *felt* (pressure,
  room, body) instead of only named (labels)?
- **Footnotes:** In a dense paragraph, are multiple notes interrupting the ear?
  Consider one composite note, moving a cite to a calmer neighbor, or dropping
  a redundant cite when the claim is now common knowledge for this readership.

## 13) Damage prevention (late-stage; book largely locked)

Use when recursive coherence is already working—ideas reinforce without
feeling repetitive—and **over-optimization** is the bigger risk than
underdevelopment.

- **Local abstraction only:** If `meaning`, `restraint`, `contact`, `ambiguity`,
  `certainty`, `legitimacy`, and `connection` pile into the same sentence or
  breath, thin **locally** (swap one term for plain speech or a concrete beat).
  Do not chase density management across whole sections unless readability is
  clearly suffering.
- **Vignettes:** Prefer uneven length, pacing, and texture over identical scene
  rhythm. Do not polish vignettes toward one template; keep them observed and
  slightly asymmetrical.
- **Sharp edges:** Keep some blunt, plain lines for contrast (short stops, hard
  facts, moral weight without varnish). Not every sentence should reach the same
  “elegant cadence.”

**Prefer these questions on a late pass:**

- Does this make the prose more **alive**?
- Does it preserve **tension** and **rhythm variation**?
- Does it preserve **restraint** (the habit of not over-finishing)?
- Does it preserve **humanity**?

**Avoid treating these as primary goals:**

- More polished / more complete / more explained—unless something is still
  materially weak.

## 14) Surgical preservation pass (do not “improve”—prevent degradation)

Use when the manuscript is already coherent and further editing risks sanding
away texture. Goal: **accidental degradation**, not expansion.

**Only three rules:**

1. **Remove explanations after emotional landings** — especially an extra
   sentence *inside the same vignette block* after a line that already landed.
   Selective cuts only; trust the reader.
2. **Preserve rhythm variation** — do not normalize sentence length or symmetry.
   Protect short, quiet, asymmetrical lines (including stark Part openers and
   cold stops).
3. **Touch nothing else** unless it is **obviously weak** — resist smoothing,
   tightening, or “one more clarifying sentence.”

**Also protect:**

- **Non-prescriptive stance:** no new frameworks, checklists, tactics, or
  promises of conversational mastery—the book’s refusal to promise mastery is
  part of its credibility.

**Psychological bar:** accept **incompleteness**; a book about unfinished
interpretation can tolerate unfinished edges.

**Thesis echo (optional, ~2%):** Phrases like *meaning outruns understanding*
or *certainty feels stabilizing* recur on purpose—they accrete a worldview.
Vary one only if two stock lines land very close *and* the second adds no new
information; otherwise repetition is a feature, not a bug.
