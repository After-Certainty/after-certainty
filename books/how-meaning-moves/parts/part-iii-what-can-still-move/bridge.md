# **Part III — What Can Still Move**

Meaning can harden before a relationship ends.

The meeting continues. The project still has to ship. Two people wake in the same house the next morning. A sentence becomes a record, a judgment, or a remembered injury, yet the lives it entered remain connected by work, care, dependence, history, or consequence.

That connection does not guarantee repair. People can remain close while becoming unreachable to one another. They can continue coordinating through increasingly narrow stories about what the other person means. A team can keep meeting after doubt has gone underground. A family can preserve its routines while old interpretations do half the speaking.

But sometimes the story has not closed completely. A later question still has somewhere to land. A correction can settle a fact without settling the worth of the person who raised it. A record can preserve enough uncertainty for the next room to understand that action was taken before knowledge was complete.

This is the possibility the final part follows—not reconciliation in every case, not agreement, not the recovery of some untouched moment before misunderstanding began. Meaning changes what happens, and some consequences cannot be withdrawn simply because people later understand them differently.

The chapters ahead begin after someone is right—in a kitchen, a spreadsheet review, or a parent meeting. They ask what correctness teaches the people who watched it arrive, what allows people to remain consequentially connected after their versions of events diverge, and what restraint can still make possible when a decision cannot wait.

The question is no longer whether meaning will move.

It already has.

The question is whether it still has somewhere to return.

These final chapters move with less exposition because the mechanism is already familiar. What remains is what can still change.
