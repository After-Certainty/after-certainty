# **Part I**

## **Why We Crave Absolutes**

The introduction named a discomfort many people already feel: certainty still functions as social glue, but it frays under scale, speed, and exposure. Part I asks why absolutes remain seductive even when they fail—before the book turns to patterns, probability, institutions, and leadership.

Two chapters follow as one argument, not two topics.

**Psychological comfort** comes first. Certainty reduces cognitive and moral load; under stress, a clean answer can feel safer than a true one. Chapter 1 follows that mechanism in everyday scenes—shortages, reorganizations, rooms where standing depends on sounding sure.

**Abstraction** comes second. Clean models and categories promise control; they also tempt us to treat summaries as the whole. Chapter 2 traces how abstraction becomes seductive—and when it becomes a substitute for judgment rather than a tool for it.

Across Part I, one discipline holds: maturity under incomplete information does not begin by shaming the desire for closure. It begins by understanding why closure sells—and what it costs when reality returns variance.

Chapter 1 opens with the psychological comfort of certainty itself: why absolutes feel like safety, and why that feeling is rational before it becomes dangerous.
