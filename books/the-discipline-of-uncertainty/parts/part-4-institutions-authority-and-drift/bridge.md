# **Part IV**

## **Institutions, Authority, and Drift**

Part III grounded judgment in probabilistic seriousness. Part IV asks what happens when **systems** must live with uncertainty—especially systems built to signal, audit, and punish.

Two chapters examine institutional drift toward absolutes.

**Warning systems.** Organizations install metrics, thresholds, and alerts to surface trouble early. Chapter 7 follows a cruel irony: success can **incriminate** the warning system itself—quiet periods read as proof the watch was unnecessary, until drift returns louder.

**Collapse into absolutes.** When pressure rises, structures and individuals often **simplify** blame—culture, bad apple, leadership failure—because distributions feel like abdication. Chapter 8 traces how institutions and people jointly collapse into verdict language.

Part IV’s stake is practical: authority without disciplined uncertainty becomes either paralysis or prophecy. The chapters do not offer a checklist for “better governance.” They name mechanisms leaders already live inside.

Chapter 7 begins with warning systems that incriminate their own success—and why institutions forget the very signals they paid to install.
