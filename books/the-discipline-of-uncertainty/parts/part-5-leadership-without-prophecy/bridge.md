# **Part V**

## **Leadership Without Prophecy**

Parts I–IV diagnosed appetite, patterns, probability, and institutional drift. Part V turns to **leaders**—the people whose speech is heard as orientation even when they know less than the room demands.

Two chapters.

**Pressure into certainty.** Boards, publics, and teams reward decisive tone. Chapter 9 maps the incentives that push leaders to speak in verdicts—and the reputational cost of sounding conditional when others sound sure.

**Discipline as practice.** Uncertainty is not a mood; it is a set of habits: revision, proportion, refusal of false prophecy. Chapter 10 treats leadership as **practice under incomplete information**—not “comfort with ambiguity” branding.

Part V does not romanticize hesitation. It asks for speech that can **carry responsibility** without pretending to see around corners. The bridge hands off to leaders who must decide anyway.

Chapter 9 opens with why leaders are pressured into certainty—and what that pressure does to institutional memory and trust.
