## 1. Manuscript-wide rhetorical patterns

### Short standalone emphasis sentences

- **Description:** One-line paragraphs used for hinge emphasis (“We infer it.” / “Character does not.”).
- **Representative locations:** Introduction; Chapters 1, 3, 5, 10, 16; Epilogue. Densest in Chapter 16 (~33 hits in scan) and Chapter 15 / Chapter 1.
- **Disposition:** Preserve climactic and object-return hits. Reduce local stacks of three or more consecutive one-liners where the third merely re-punches the second.
- **Editorial rule:** Keep the strongest occurrence on the page; fold weaker nearby hits into adjacent prose without homogenizing syntax.

### “It does not…” runs

- **Description:** Parallel negated claims in successive sentences or paragraphs.
- **Representative locations:** Chapter 3 (cost/sanctify; test limits); Epilogue (lock + character negations); Chapter 2 (trophy incompleteness); lighter uses in Chapters 1, 7, 13.
- **Disposition:** Preserve Chapter 3’s “Cost changes the weight… It does not sanctify it,” Part III / Intro “less unknowable / not known,” and the epilogue lock opening.
- **Editorial rule:** One strong run per local section; convert nearby repeats into ordinary embedded negation.

### “Sometimes…” paired reversals

- **Description:** Paired or triadic “Sometimes X. Sometimes Y.” flips.
- **Representative locations:** Introduction (formal / emotional / ordinary); Chapter 2 (“Sometimes it did”); Chapters 6–7 (patience/courage variation); Chapters 8, 13.
- **Disposition:** Preserve Intro triad and Chapter 2 pair; keep one strong dispositional variation pair in Chapter 7.
- **Editorial rule:** Avoid stacking pairs in adjacent paragraphs; prefer a single earned flip.

### “Not X. Not Y.”

- **Description:** Twin fragment negations for compressed refusal.
- **Representative locations:** Introduction (“Not certainty. Not destiny.”); Chapter 6 (“Not every need… Not every problem…”); Chapter 16 (“Not disappearing… Not being replaced…”; “Not Mara's… Not a character she could declare.”).
- **Disposition:** Preserve Intro pair and one Chapter 16 succession pair.
- **Editorial rule:** Do not stack Not-pairs in adjacent Chapter 16 paragraphs; soften the weaker of two nearby pairs.

### “Someone may…” / inventory constructions

- **Description:** Multi-clause catalogues of possible persons, traits, or failures.
- **Representative locations:** Introduction and Chapter 1 openings; Chapter 7 habit-of-vice; Chapter 8 apology failures; Chapter 10 trust domains; Epilogue; Chapter 14 question block.
- **Disposition:** Preserve Chapter 10 object-of-trust architecture and Chapter 14’s intentional question inventory (social climax). Compress overlapping person-may lists in Introduction / Chapter 1 / Chapter 8 / Epilogue where the distinction is already known.
- **Editorial rule:** Keep inventories that introduce architecture; cut inventories that re-list earned distinctions.

### Claim → qualify → warn → restate

- **Description:** Thesis stated, immediately hedged, warned against misreading, then restated narrowly—often again at chapter close.
- **Representative locations:** Chapters 3, 4, 5, 8, 12, 16; many chapter endings.
- **Disposition:** Preserve one full CQWR cycle per chapter thesis.
- **Editorial rule:** Prefer cutting the second restatement (especially end-of-chapter summaries) while keeping the concrete scene that earned the claim.

### Stacked rhetorical questions / antithesis

- **Description:** Question sequences and antithetical couplets used for emphasis.
- **Representative locations:** Chapter 1 repair questions; Chapter 10 trait questions; Chapter 14 permission questions; Chapter 16 ownership questions.
- **Disposition:** Keep Chapter 14 block; keep repair questions in Chapter 1 **or** Chapter 8 at full strength, not both; keep Chapter 16 ownership questions inside the boiler scene.
- **Editorial rule:** Convert redundant question stacks into labeled nouns or ordinary prose when the chapter has already asked the same question earlier.
