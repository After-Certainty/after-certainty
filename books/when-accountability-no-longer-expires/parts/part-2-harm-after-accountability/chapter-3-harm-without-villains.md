# **Chapter 3**

## **Harm Without Villains**

The rule was simple.

No exceptions.\
No discretion.\
No case-by-case judgment.

Everyone understood why it existed. It was meant to be fair. It was
meant to prevent abuse. It was meant to remove favoritism and
inconsistency.

For a while, it seemed to work.

Then a case arose that no one could ignore.

The policy was applied exactly as written.\
The outcome was devastating.\
No one involved believed the harm was deserved.

And yet, the decision stood.

### **When necessity replaces judgment**

Zero-tolerance rules are not created casually.

They usually emerge after something has gone wrong:

- Inconsistent enforcement

- Perceived favoritism

- Scandals that undermine trust

The promise of the rule is clarity. Everyone knows the standard.
Everyone is treated the same.

From the inside, this feels responsible.

But the strength of the rule—its rigidity—is also what makes harm
procedurally unavoidable.

When judgment is removed, context disappears.\
When context disappears, harm becomes procedural.

No one has to want that outcome for it to occur.

### **Harm without malice**

This is the kind of harm this chapter examines.

Not harm driven by cruelty or indifference.\
Not harm pursued for personal gain.

But harm produced by **faithful adherence** to a system that values
consistency, order, and protection.

In these cases:

- The policy is believed to be necessary

- The decision-makers feel constrained rather than empowered

- The outcome is regretted but defended

The harm is real.\
The intent is not malicious.

Both can be true at the same time.

### **Why no one feels responsible**

One of the most disorienting features of this kind of harm is how
responsibility disappears.

The rule required it.\
The process demanded it.\
The system left no alternative.

Each person involved can point to something outside themselves:

- The policy

- The precedent

- The obligation to be consistent

Responsibility is everywhere and nowhere at once.

This is not avoidance. It is one way systems distribute moral weight.

### **Correct decisions with devastating outcomes**

Similar patterns appear in other domains.

A church disciplines a member “out of love,” following established
guidelines meant to protect the community.\
A corporation lays off employees to ensure survival, citing fiduciary
responsibility.\
A court issues a ruling that is legally correct and morally shattering.

In each case:

- The decision follows the rules

- The justification is coherent

- The harm is acknowledged

And the institution continues.

What matters is not whether the decision was defensible.

What matters is that **defensibility does not prevent damage**.

### **Why this kind of harm is so stable**

Harm without villains is unusually durable.

Because no one intended it, there is no obvious place to intervene.\
Because the rule was necessary, questioning it feels risky.\
Because the outcome followed procedure, interruption feels unjustified.

The system learns something important in moments like these.

It learns that it can survive harm without revisiting its foundations.

### **Sincerity as a stabilizer**

In many cases, the sincerity of the people involved actually strengthens
the system.

They:

- Express regret

- Offer care where they can

- Acknowledge pain

And then continue.

These responses are not insincere. They often reduce immediate
suffering.

But they also allow the system to move forward without placing authority
itself at risk.

Harm becomes something to be managed, not something that forces
reconsideration.

### **What this chapter establishes**

This chapter does not argue that such policies are always wrong.

It establishes something narrower:

> Harm can be produced by systems acting exactly as intended.

When legitimacy is secure, those systems do not need to deny harm in
order to endure. They need only to explain it.

That explanation is often convincing.\
It is often careful.\
And it is often enough.

### **Where this leads**

The next chapter examines how harm is **distributed** once it becomes
survivable—who bears it immediately, who absorbs it quietly, and who is
protected by distance or time.

Harm without villains is not the end of the story.

It is the beginning of a pattern.[^c3-mashaw-bureaucratic][^c3-arendt-eichmann]
