# **Conclusion**

## **What It Would Mean for Legitimacy to Expire**

Imagine a different rule.

Imagine that legitimacy truly expired after harm—not automatically, not
dramatically, but genuinely.

Not every harm.\
Not every failure.\
But harm that persisted without resolution.

What would we lose?

### **The cost we rarely name**

If legitimacy expired more easily, many of the things we rely on would
weaken.

Continuity would become fragile.\
Expertise would lose insulation.\
Institutions would fracture more often.

People would have to decide sooner and with less guidance.\
Authority would offer fewer guarantees.

Stability, as we know it, would be harder to maintain.

That loss would not be abstract.

It would be felt in:

- Slower coordination

- Greater uncertainty

- More visible conflict

- Fewer shared narratives to lean on

These are not trivial costs.

They are the reason legitimacy is protected so carefully.

### **Why endurance feels necessary**

Throughout this book, endurance has appeared again and again—not as
denial, but as strategy.

Legitimacy persists because people depend on it to:

- Make sense of complexity

- Absorb disagreement

- Avoid repeated rupture

When harm is unresolved, allowing authority to continue can feel like
the least dangerous option.

This judgment is often rational.

It is not made lightly.

### **What expiration would interrupt**

If legitimacy were genuinely revocable, some familiar patterns would no
longer hold.

Correction would require consequence.\
Learning would risk authority.\
Patience would lose its moral privilege.

Institutions would have to face a harder question more often:

*Do we still deserve to continue as we are?*

That question is destabilizing.

It offers no guarantee of improvement—only the possibility of change.

### **Why this is not a recommendation**

This book does not argue that legitimacy should expire more often.

It does not propose thresholds, mechanisms, or reforms.

It asks a quieter question:

> What do we protect when legitimacy never expires—and what do we
> sacrifice?

There is no universal answer.

Different institutions exist for different reasons.\
Different harms carry different weight.\
Different communities tolerate different risks.

Any attempt to formalize expiration would create new dangers.

### **Choosing without certainty**

Allowing legitimacy to expire would not produce moral clarity.

It would produce uncertainty.

Authority would weaken before alternatives were ready.\
Communities would fragment before they could regroup.\
Responsibility would shift to individuals sooner than most are
comfortable with.

This is why non-expiring legitimacy is so attractive.

It postpones that uncertainty indefinitely.

### **The unresolved tension**

The central tension of this book remains unresolved on purpose.

Non-expiring legitimacy allows harm to persist without collapse.\
Expiring legitimacy risks collapse without guarantee of renewal.

Both options carry cost.

The danger is not choosing endurance.\
The danger is forgetting that it is a choice.

### **What remains after certainty**

If legitimacy can persist without resolution, then certainty is not what
sustains authority.

What sustains it is reenactment:

- Continued participation

- Continued deference

- Continued patience

Recognizing that does not tell us what to do.

It tells us where responsibility actually lies.

Not in the past.\
Not in origins.\
Not in warnings.

But in the present moment, where legitimacy is either renewed—or quietly
allowed to continue.

### **Closing reflection**

This book has not argued for rupture.

It has argued for clarity.

If legitimacy never expires, harm can be tolerated indefinitely.\
If legitimacy can expire, stability becomes costly.

Between those two realities lies the world we inhabit.

Understanding that tension does not resolve it.

But it does change how we live with it—after certainty no longer carries
the weight we hoped it would.
