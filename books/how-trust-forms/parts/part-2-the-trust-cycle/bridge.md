# **The Trust Cycle**

The neighbors were no longer strangers—not because uncertainty had disappeared, not because every question had been answered. The uncertainty remained. What had changed was the amount of evidence both families now carried.

The package had stayed dry. The borrowed tools had returned. The driveway had been shoveled during the storm. Small actions had produced visible outcomes. Those outcomes had quietly reshaped expectations. Something important had happened: the next interaction no longer began where the first one had.

This is the point where trust becomes easier to recognize—and more difficult to understand.

The first part of this book examined how trust gets built: participation before certainty, beliefs visible through action, outcomes that become evidence. What follows asks what that accumulation makes possible. Trust starts influencing behavior rather than merely responding to it.[^p2-luhmann] Evidence gradually becomes expectation; participation becomes easier, more frequent, and more ambitious.

The result is a cycle. Beliefs shape actions. Actions create outcomes. Outcomes influence trust. Trust enables participation. Participation changes beliefs. The process begins again.[^p2-dewey]

The chapters in this part follow that cycle—from what trust enables, to how participation reshapes belief, to how cooperation scales beyond the porch.

[^p2-luhmann]: Luhmann, *Trust and Power: Two Works*, trans. Howard Davis, John Raffan, and Kathryn Rooney (Chichester, UK: John Wiley & Sons, 1979).

[^p2-dewey]: Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct: An Introduction to Social Psychology* (New York: Henry Holt, 1922).
