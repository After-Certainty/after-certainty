# How Trust Forms — Bibliography Guide

Working notes for source selection during drafting. Export bibliography lives in [`../back-matter/bibliography.md`](../back-matter/bibliography.md).

---

## Citation discipline

Write observation-driven prose first; use citations to anchor key claims and intellectual lineage—not to drive the argument.

**Target density:** About 8–12 sources appearing repeatedly across the manuscript; others appear once or twice where they naturally fit. Do not force every bibliography entry into the book.

**Footnote timing:** Add Pandoc footnotes (`[^id]`) at structural pivots during the citation pass, not while drafting anchor stories.

---

## By unit

### Introduction — The New Neighbor

**Primary**

- **Luhmann — *Trust and Power*** — Trust reduces social complexity; participation despite uncertainty is essentially a Luhmann question.
- **Baier — "Trust and Antitrust"** — Vulnerability, dependence, moral dimensions of trust.

---

### The Architecture of Trust (Part I)

**Primary**

- **Luhmann — *Trust and Power*** — Trust as structure, not emotion; sets up Part I.

---

### The First Leap

**Pattern:** Trust Begins Before Verification

**Primary**

- **Baier — "Trust and Antitrust"** — Trust as vulnerability; strongest fit for this chapter.
- **Luhmann — *Trust and Power*** — Action before complete information; near the chapter thesis.

---

### Beliefs Become Visible

**Pattern:** Actions Reveal Beliefs

**Primary**

- **Goffman — *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*** — Signals, presentation, behavior, social interpretation.

**Optional**

- **Berger & Luckmann — *The Social Construction of Reality*** — Shared realities through repeated interaction.

---

### Outcomes Create Evidence

**Pattern:** Outcomes Teach Trust

**Primary**

- **Hardin — *Trust and Trustworthiness*** — Why trust becomes justified; strongest fit.
- **Dewey — *Human Nature and Conduct*** — Learning through consequences; experience as feedback.

---

### The Trust Cycle (Part II)

**Primary**

- **Dewey** — Recursive nature of experience.
- **Luhmann — *Trust and Power*** — Trust creating possibility for further action.

---

### Trust Enables Participation

**Pattern:** Trust Enables Participation

**Primary**

- **Ostrom — *Governing the Commons*** — Cooperation without central authority; participation sustained through trust.

**Optional**

- **Putnam — *Bowling Alone*** — Social capital; community participation.

---

### Participation Changes Beliefs

**Pattern:** Participation Updates Beliefs

**Primary**

- **Dewey — *Human Nature and Conduct*** — Action → learning; participation → understanding.
- **Berger & Luckmann — *The Social Construction of Reality*** — Shared reality formed through interaction.

---

### Trust at Scale

**Pattern:** Trust Scales Through Abstraction

**Primary**

- **Luhmann — *Trust and Power*** — Trust beyond personal familiarity; likely his strongest chapter in this book.
- **O'Neill — *A Question of Trust*** — Institutional, professional, and public trust.
- **Scott — *Seeing Like a State*** — Abstraction, distance, institutional simplification.

**Optional**

- **Weber — *Economy and Society*** — Bureaucracy, legitimacy, authority.

---

### What Trust Makes Possible (Part III)

**Primary**

- **Arendt — *The Human Condition*** — Action and public life.
- **Dewey — *The Public and Its Problems*** — Collective participation.

---

### Trust and Leadership

**Pattern:** Trust Converts Attention Into Legitimacy

**Primary**

- **Weber — *Economy and Society*** — Legitimacy, authority, charisma; likely the Weber chapter.
- **MacIntyre — *After Virtue*** — Communities and moral practices.

Cross-link: *When Others Look To You*.

---

### Trust and Meaning

**Pattern:** Trust Slows Distortion

**Primary**

- **Berger & Luckmann — *The Social Construction of Reality*** — Meaning formation; shared reality.
- **Fricker — *Epistemic Injustice*** — Credibility; who gets believed; trust and testimony.
- **Goffman — *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*** — Interpretation and signaling.

Cross-link: *How Meaning Moves*.

---

### Why Trust Matters

**Pattern:** Trust Makes Cooperation Possible

**Primary**

- **Ostrom — *Governing the Commons*** — Coordinating without perfect agreement; may be the chapter's most important citation.
- **Putnam — *Bowling Alone*** — Social capital; community health; participation.
- **Arendt — *The Human Condition*** — Plurality and public life.
- **Dewey — *The Public and Its Problems*** — Collective action under uncertainty.
