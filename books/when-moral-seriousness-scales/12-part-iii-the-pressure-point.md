# Part III — The Pressure Point

Distance stretches consequence. Asymmetry redistributes exposure.
Incentives begin to favor movement over deliberation. Over time, these
shifts become perceptible.

Actors notice when restraint feels selectively costly. They notice when
speed accumulates advantage. They notice when reciprocity no longer
stabilizes behavior automatically.

This is the pressure point.

It is not collapse. It is not destiny. It is the moment when structural
thinning becomes visible enough to influence choice. The system does not
force escalation. It alters the field in which judgment must operate.

Part III examines that field—where acceleration begins to feel rational,
where restraint begins to require intention, and where the possibility
of drift emerges. The question is not whether pressure exists. It is how
actors respond to it.
