# Part III — The Pressure Point

The regional scheduling decision revealed a difference in pace: approval could proceed through an established route, while a local challenge required evidence, coordination, and time. Amir and Nina found a way to share some of that burden, but the arrangement depended on choices the wider system had not made inevitable.

What happens when more people begin noticing the disadvantage of carrying costs others can avoid? The temptation may not be to dominate. It may be to stop being the only person expected to make room for disagreement.

This part examines that temptation and the judgments still available inside it. Pressure can be real without being a verdict. Resistance can be principled without always being wise. The difficulty is deciding which limits deserve to be kept when keeping them has a price.
