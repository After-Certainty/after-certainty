# Part IV — What Remains Possible

It would be comforting to end with a simple choice: restore the intimacy of small communities or design a larger institution that never loses sight of those affected by its decisions. Neither option is available.

Local knowledge is incomplete. Central coordination is necessary. Reviews and appeals can help and can also become rituals. Deliberation has costs, including costs borne by people waiting for a decision.

The question now is whether a large system can make its authority answerable to consequences it cannot observe directly. That calls for more than conscientious leaders and more than procedures that look good on paper. It calls for ways that information, challenge, and responsibility can change the terms of a decision.

This final part considers those limited possibilities without treating them as a program that guarantees success.
