# Part II — What Scale Changes

A missed pickup can reach a neighborhood coordinator through a conversation at the door. Across a regional network, a similar difficulty may reach decision-makers as a statistic—or not reach them at all.

Large systems need shared schedules, categories, and procedures. Those tools are not evidence of indifference. They let strangers coordinate and make commitments they can rely on. Yet every translation of lived consequence into organizational information creates choices about what is recorded, who can respond, and how much of a decision remains open.

This part follows three linked changes. **Distance** alters what can be seen. **Asymmetry** alters who bears the consequences. Together they can create an **incentive gradient** in which a decision is easier to complete than an objection is to make useful. The point is to understand how those changes occur without treating efficiency or scale as moral defects.
