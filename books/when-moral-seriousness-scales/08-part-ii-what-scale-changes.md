# Part II — What Scale Changes

The human-scale model does not disappear as systems grow. The
commitments of proximity, reciprocity, and contestability remain
intelligible. But the conditions that once reinforced them begin to
thin.

Proximity stretches. Decisions reach people who are never encountered.
Reciprocity becomes uneven as some actors gain insulation from
consequence. Contestability requires greater coordination to exercise,
and disagreement becomes more complex to absorb.

These shifts do not immediately destabilize moral seriousness. They do
not invalidate restraint. They alter its operating environment.

To understand how moral seriousness functions under scale, we must
examine what distance introduces, how asymmetry emerges, and why certain
behaviors begin to move faster than others. The change is gradual, but
it is structural.
