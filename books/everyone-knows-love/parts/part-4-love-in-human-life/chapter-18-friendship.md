# Friendship

A woman ran into someone she had not seen in years at the grocery store.

They had been close once—long talks, shared crises, the easy shorthand of people who had watched each other's lives change shape. Then work, then distance, then the slow drift that no one announces. Each of them had meant to call. Each had felt, at odd moments, a small shame about not calling.

In the cereal aisle they stopped, awkward at first, then one of them laughed and said, "This is ridiculous. We're doing the thing where we pretend we weren't important to each other."

They talked for twenty minutes beside the carts. Not about everything they had missed—there was too much for that—but about enough. A hard month at work. A parent's health scare. The ordinary weather of lives that had kept moving while the friendship sat quietly in the background, still there, still real, still waiting to be picked up.

Nothing was repaired because nothing had exactly broken. Something was simply resumed.

That was friendship, or something close enough that most of us recognize it: how often a bond can survive interruption without demanding explanation. A marriage can hold this resumption too—not only friends beside the cereal aisle.

## Welcome back

How can two people stop talking for years and still feel like friends?

Life happens. Children happen. Jobs happen. Moves happen. Years happen. And somehow real friendship often says *Welcome back* instead of *Where have you been?*

The college roommate you have not seen since graduation. The coworker from another city. The person you kayak with once a summer. You start talking—and within minutes you are back. Not because nothing changed. Because the bond was built to tolerate drift.

You have seen other relationships ask for more after long silence. Friendship, at its best, can sometimes pick up where it left off—the bond sitting quietly in the background for seasons, still waiting beside the cereal aisle.

## When you pick it back up

Someone says three words at the reunion and both of you laugh while everyone else waits for the punch line. Half-finished stories. Nicknames from a summer nobody else remembers. Words that only mean something inside that friendship—you built a small world together, and twenty minutes beside the carts is enough to step back inside it.

## When friendship deepens

When friendship deepens, you remember what your friend is actually facing—not only the version they post or the version they offer in public. You return the call late but you return it. You keep a confidence. You tell the truth without using it as a weapon. You show up when showing up is inconvenient, without keeping score.

*Get in here* at the door after too many months away—no lecture, no inventory of absence, just the door held open. One person may carry more for a season. Another season may reverse that.

## When friendship thins

Friendships can also become performative, competitive, or conditional—the friend who is excellent in crisis and absent in ordinary weather, the friend who keeps score, the friend whose loyalty arrives with expectations attached, the friend who wants the version of you that is easy to admire. The same gestures can come from generosity or strategy. The difference is not always visible from outside. It is often felt from within.

Perhaps you have had a friendship that deepened your life without becoming central, and another that became central without ever becoming simple. Both can be real. Both can matter. You cannot fully explain why one friendship becomes indispensable and another remains pleasant. You can only notice what happens when someone says *Want to grab coffee?* after years of not calling—and the answer is yes without a trial.

## This voice, this shorthand

None of this explains why you became friends with this person rather than the person beside them in the cereal aisle. Friendship lives inside particularity—this voice, this willingness to resume after drift, this twenty minutes beside the carts that felt, for a moment, like coming home. Some friendships cannot survive long silence; others simply wait, and pick up again when someone says *Want to grab coffee?*
