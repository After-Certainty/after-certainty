# Everyone Knows Love — Book Rules

## Purpose

Architectural constraints for **Everyone Knows Love** (*Why Is It So Hard to Explain?*).

## Reader contract (near the beginning)

> **This book is not trying to define love. It is trying to explain why so many different things all feel like love.**

## Framework vs. phenomenon (non-negotiable)

> **The reader should never feel that the framework is more important than the phenomenon. Every framework exists only to illuminate lived experience, never to replace it.**

The reader did not come for a model. The reader came because they love someone. Do not overuse "Connection… Trust… Integration…" in prose—earn each naming once.

## People first, ideas second

This book **always begins with people** and only then moves toward ideas. Earlier portfolio books often invert that order. Here: ordinary life, relationships, artifacts—then patterns.

**Not these people. Just people.** (author rule — strengthens book)

The book is an investigation, not a memoir and not a novel. The **subject is love**, not the author. The narrator is present but **never the destination**—as in the portfolio: leadership, meaning, trust, uncertainty were never "about Kevin"; love is not either.

Scenes use **anonymous people**—a father in a waiting room, a neighbor with a casserole, a woman who puts toast on the table after a difficult night—so readers substitute their own people and ask *Is that true about people?* not *Did this happen to him?*

- **Recurring places and situations**, not recurring protagonists: waiting rooms, kitchen tables, diners, porches, phone calls, ordinary Tuesdays
- **Portable examples:** *A woman put toast on the table…* — one reader imagines a wife, another a husband, another a sister; the scene grows larger than its particulars
- **Avoid** fictional named families (David and Elena) written like memoir across chapters
- **Avoid** scenes that sound like they happened to the author when they did not (*my wife*, *my son*, *my mother* as scene subjects)
- **Anonymous fluency:** gender without name when a scene needs a singular protagonist (*a woman at a diner*)—avoid awkward *someone… they… them* chains
- **Credibility shift:** not *Here are my experiences* but *Here are things I have watched human beings do*—observation before concepts; argues from what people keep doing, not from autobiographical ambiguity

**Naturalist voice (author):** A naturalist does not spend every chapter on one robin they met yesterday. They say, *Watch robins long enough and you'll notice…* Same here: *Watch people long enough…*

**Keep the narrator — lose autobiography in illustrations:**

- **Keep:** *Perhaps you've noticed…* / *I wonder whether…* / *I suspect…* / *I've come to think…* / *I do not know…* / *The most loving people I know…* / *Some of the saddest rooms I have seen…* — essayistic observer who has watched many lives, not Kevin telling what happened
- **Do not:** make the narrator the protagonist of every scene; composite illustrations are not personal history
- **Do not:** let *I* leak into anonymous or second-person passages (*until I see them again* in a *you* chapter)—those remind the reader there used to be an author behind the curtain

### Parable style (author-approved — post anonymous pass)

The book has moved from memoir toward **parable**: not fiction for entertainment, but ordinary believable scenes that reveal patterns of human life. Readers should ask *Have I lived something like this?* not *Did this really happen?*

**1. Archetypal scenes.** Stories invite recognition, not proof. *A father sat in the waiting room…* could be thousands of fathers. *A couple had one of those fights…* is everyone's fight. Readers substitute their own people.

**2. Observable before interpretive.** Scenes stay strongest with behavior first, careful interpretation second. *She did not think much had been said* — fine (reported, modest). Watch stacking *She wanted…* / *He secretly felt…* too often—readers wonder how the narrator knows. Prefer what could be seen or fairly inferred.

**3. Ordinary specificity only.** *Two slices the way he liked it* — ordinary, portable. *Blueberry jam from Vermont in the chipped ceramic rooster plate* — sounds like someone's actual memory; breaks the parable. Stay at the level of specificity that stays archetypal.

**4. Natural perspective movement (keep).** Anonymous person → *Perhaps you know…* (reader) → humanity is a natural essay rhythm. Do not eliminate it.

**5. Credibility test.** When scenes were "about you," readers asked *Did this happen?* Now they should ask *Is that true about people?* That is where an essay wants attention.

- **Experiences over references (author Ch 2):** avoid vague literary gestures (*a bear stays awake*) and flat category lists; **specific story-language** OK when enduring (*toy rabbit becomes real because he was loved*)—readers recognize without footnotes

### Observational and participatory chapters (author — post Ch 16)

The book uses **two chapter modes**. Both are valid. Part IV should lean participatory without turning every chapter into Romance.

**Observational chapters** (much of Parts I–III) say: *Look. Notice this pattern. Haven't you seen this?* They teach a habit of attention. They are excellent at naming what keeps happening.

**Participatory chapters** (Ch 16 Romance as model) say: *Remember. Remember what this felt like. Remember that conversation. Remember thinking you already knew someone.* They give just enough concrete life that the reader's own memories begin talking back—Solnit-style: not merely moths or Mary Shelley, but enough particularity that your life enters the page.

| Mode | Reader finishes… | Primary verb |
|------|------------------|--------------|
| Observational | *I understand the pattern* | Look / Notice |
| Participatory | *I inhabit the idea* | Remember |

**Patterns vs. recognition (author shift):** Patterns answer *What keeps happening?* Recognition answers *What have we all been living without language?* The second is the stronger writer for Part IV terrain chapters. Do not abandon pattern-language in Parts I–III; **apply participatory bar to Part IV revisions** (Ch 17–19).

**Brittany test (author — Ch 16):** Not *Is this theoretically sound?* but *Will she feel seen?* — and without feeling written about. Anonymous parable scenes carry personal pattern; the reader substitutes their own people. Highest bar for Part IV.

**Framework in background:** When participatory chapters work, readers stop looking for Connection / Trust / Integration. That means the framework is working—not that it should be re-taught in prose. Part IV chapters illustrate patterns **without** seeing/giving/staying lists or condition vocabulary unless one word earns its place in scene.

**Every chapter should leave the reader remembering someone** — not agreeing, not admiring a sentence. A parent. A spouse. A friend. A Tuesday. A kitchen. A conversation they wish they had handled differently. Ask before revising: *Where does the reader's own life enter this chapter?*

**Memory prompts (revision tool):** Instead of *I need to explain trust*, try *When did you stop checking your phone after someone texted "I'm on my way"?* Instead of *Here's belonging*, try *Remember walking into a room after your worst week and realizing nobody needed you to pretend.* One concrete door; reader supplies the room.

**Trust the reader:** Readers feel understood not because the author explains his experience but because the author awakens theirs. Participatory writing trusts the reader to carry half the chapter. Not more emotional—more trusting.

**Part IV prose (inherits Ch 16 polish):** Plain language; observation and story over theory; soften universal claims (*often*, *in many marriages*); avoid staccato single-sentence chains; no anonymous authority (*Someone once said*); no *Care remains open* unless the chapter's room earns it—Romance ends on romance/particularity.

**Not every chapter becomes Romance.** Family, friendship, and community keep their own terrain. They ask the same question: *Where does memory enter?*

### Part IV — centers of gravity, not taxonomy (author — governing principle)

Part IV does **not** classify relationship types. It shows how the **same patterns** from Parts II–III take on **different centers of gravity** in romance, family, friendship, and community—a **living ecology**, not four essays defining different kinds of love.

**Refine, not reinvent:** Voice is locked. Preserve opening stories, order, tone, endings. Strengthen emerging themes; do not rewrite from scratch.

**Bridges, not boundaries:** Do not sharpen lines between relationship types. Reveal continuity—how friendship can become romance, romance family, families friendship-like, community nurturing all three.

**Litmus test:** *Here are four places where love learns to live* — not *four different kinds of love.*

| Relationship | Verb | Center of gravity | Organizing observation (examples) |
|--------------|------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Romance | discover | Continued discovery | Keeps curiosity while fear softens into safety |
| Family | remember | Accumulated history | Shared history changes weight; adults increasingly renew the archive |
| Friendship | resume | Resumption | Survives interruption; resumes; shared language as small world together |
| Community | cultivate | Belonging among the unbound | Cultivates belonging; strangers become neighbors through showing up |

**Recurring movements (gentle echo):** curiosity, shared language, play, history, repair, belonging, trust, becoming, voluntary inconvenience, showing up, attention—recognize across chapters without forcing every movement into every chapter.

**Maturation pattern (show, don't summarize):** bonds preserve what mattered at the beginning while changing form.

**Revision questions:** When this pattern appears here, what does it look like? What changes?

**Tuesday compass:** Scenes over conclusions. Archaeology of ordinary life.

**Each Part IV chapter:** one central observation; Ch 16 reference for voice.

## Book scope and structure

- ~25–30k words (author-approved; current first draft ~23.5k)
- Structure per `index.md`: 5 parts, 5 bridges, **20 chapters**, preface, epilogue, closing quote, appendix
- **Chapter length:** default ~700–1,500 words per chapter unless author requests otherwise in review—do not inflate

### Investigative method

**Naturalist** observing something alive (primary)—patient, comparative, wondering. **Archaeologist** fits for preserved artifacts (songs, rituals, texts). Not framework-first. See `docs/book-plan.md`.

## Core invariant

> Love is recognized through expressions and artifacts before it is explained; conditions (connection, trust, integration) and freedom may illuminate why those expressions belong to one phenomenon; care remains a mystery the book investigates—not resolves by editorial fiat.

**Care:** Author knows it is the central mystery; **reader must not be told that until Ch 9.** Part II ends with the word discovered, not telegraphed.

## Expression families = relational verbs (not virtues)

Part II chapters are **things people do**, not abstract traits:

- **Seeing** — relational attention (not merely cognitive understanding)
- **Giving** — response that costs something
- **Staying** — remaining with someone over time
- **Repairing** — returning after harm

Ask *what people who love actually do* before naming relationship qualities. Part III names connection, trust, integration **after** readers have lived the movements in Part II.

**Do not:** Impose a linear staircase (expressions → connection → trust → integration) in prose. Prefer **ecology / feedback** language when structure emerges. Therapist and reliable-coworker cases prove expressions can cultivate connection and trust without guaranteeing love.

### Vocabulary guardrail (Seeing chapter onward)

The word *seeing* is a chapter title and book motif—**vary phrasing in prose** so the title stays strong: noticing, attending, recognizing, paying attention, perceiving, bearing witness. Use *understanding* sparingly as one expression of seeing, not the chapter's center.

## Thematic arc by part

- **Part I:** Mystery; reader contract; recognition before explanation
- **Part II:** **How Love Moves** (Seeing, Giving, Staying, Repairing) → hinge → **Care** (Ch 9, discovered)
- **Part III:** **How Love Deepens** — Connection, trust, **integration (climax)**, **belonging**, freedom, self within love
- **Part IV:** Romance, family, friendship, community
- **Part V:** Humanity's long conversation—major chapter, not appendix
- **Epilogue:** Small acts — ends on *Again.*
- **Closing:** Seed quote (voluntary inconvenience / freedom to say no / without losing yourself)
- **Appendix:** Do We Really Love Pizza?

## Tone

**Warmest book in portfolio.** Curious, gentle, hopeful. Wonder preserved. Not self-help, not academic.

**Must not:** Telegraph care as "the mystery" before Ch 9; treat Ch 20 as appendix; thin Integration (Ch 12); framework-first prose.

### Tone guardrail (from Ch 1–3 approvals)

- Prefer **confidence through observation**, not confidence through explanation.
- Write as invitation: "Look." / "Have you noticed this too?"
- Preserve "perhaps" where uncertainty is honest; do not over-polish into certainty.
- **Chapter endings:** Vary cadence—do not default every chapter to *Perhaps…* / *For now, perhaps it is enough…* (pattern is strong in Part II–III). Rotate: quiet declarative, question, concrete image, simple stop. Ch 1 double-*Perhaps* ending is author-approved; later chapters should diverge.

## Chapter construction

1. People and ordinary scene first
2. "Why does this feel loving?" (not "What is X?")
3. Counter-example where relevant
4. Open question; pass chapter gate

**Part II expression chapters (Ch 4–7):** **Complete (author-approved Ch 7).** Cumulative investigation; movements interact; rhythm locked (see `book-plan.md`). Conclusion each chapter: essential but not sufficient—not "love is X."

**Movements, not nouns (author-approved Ch 7):** Seeing, Giving, Staying, Repairing are **verbs**—ongoing participation, never possession. Love described as participation, not a thing held.

**Part II hidden questions (locked Ch 7):** Do you know me? Will you spend for me? Will you remain? Can we find each other again? — four fears / how love answers vulnerability; protect in prose; do not lecture.

**Part II frame (author Ch 7):** Not faces of love as virtues—**how one life answers another**; deepest reader finish: *Love isn't one thing. It's a family of ways human beings answer one another.*

**looks identical motif (author Ch 7):** *The X looks identical. The story underneath may not.* — intentional refrain (toast, chair, casserole); cap ~4–5 uses; readers may anticipate if overused.

**Part II emotional climax:** Ch 7 Repairing · **Ch 8 hinge** · **Ch 9 particularity** (Ch 4–9 complete, author-approved; Ch 9 strongest Part II chapter).

**Ch 9 particularity rule (author-approved):** Chapter is about *why this person?*—not defining care. *Care* is late placeholder for mystery. Book's explanation of why love is hard to explain begins here: explanation stops at particularity, not at whether we care.

**Part I epistemology arc (author Ch 3):** Ch 1 everyone recognizes love → Ch 2 humanity preserves observations → Ch 3 recognition precedes explanation → **Ch 9 particularity** (why explanation hits its limit). Book teaches a **habit of attention** (naturalist)—look carefully before you explain. Ch 3 is an **anchor chapter**; if it works, Parts II–III read as observations not opinions.

**Ch 3 rule (author-approved):** Teaches methodology—*Ask what you are actually seeing.* Part II preview in Seeing → Giving → Staying → Repairing order. Grandmother/rain opening grounded in bodily pattern (not cushions). Do not lean on framework diagrams before looking.

**Part III framing (author — bridge):** Qualities are **emergent**, not beneath movements. Part II = how love moves; Care = why for this person; Part III = **what those movements cultivate over time**. Do not imply hidden mechanism underneath—*repeated movements change relationships.*

**Part III scope (author Ch 9–11):** Part II = *What answers another person's life?* Part III = *What lets those answers continue over time?* Properties acquired—not ingredients. Connection does not explain Care/particularity. **Stay observational**—notice, don't adopt a framework.

**Part III architecture (author Ch 10):** First half asks *What does love look like?*; second half asks *What happens when love succeeds?* Connection, Trust, Integration, Belonging, Freedom are **consequences**, not definitions.

**Part III deeper fears (author Ch 14 — locked, never state explicitly):** Connection *Does anyone really know me?* · Trust *Can I rely on you?* · Integration *Can our lives genuinely matter to one another?* · Belonging *If you knew me fully, would I still have a place?* · Freedom *May I remain myself while loving you?* Readers feel progression; do not lecture it.

**Belonging + Freedom (author — locked Ch 13–14):** Twin protectors solving **opposite failure modes**. Belonging: *Can I be fully myself here and still be wanted?* — fear of abandonment; *Stay*; *You still have a place.* Freedom: *Can I continue becoming myself here?* — fear of possession; *Grow*; *You still have a self.* Order after Integration: Belonging → Freedom → Self Within Love. Belonging = coming home, not becoming; completes Integration's *what if I fail or change?* question.

**weight-bearing motif (author Ch 11):** Striking in Ch 9; **one deliberate callback in Ch 11** (*once another life has become weight-bearing inside yours*—author-approved, unifies with non-optional); Ch 12 culmination (*flourishing becomes weight-bearing inside your own decisions*). Cap elsewhere in Ch 10.

**Attention as engine (author Ch 4):** Book revolves around **attention** as much as recognition—recognition possible because we pay attention; seeing = sustained attention; giving = attention in action; staying = attention through time; repair = attention after rupture; connection/trust/integration follow. Love is the phenomenon; attention is the engine. Do not retitle—let architecture emerge in prose.

**Book-wide claim (author Ch 10):** *Another person's inner life becomes real to you* — threads through Seeing, Care, Connection; expect in Trust, Integration, Freedom.

**"Becomes real" guardrail (author — vs. *Velveteen Rabbit*):** Williams: love *creates* Real (*Real isn't how you are made…*). This book: love does **not** create personhood—it is one way an **already-real** inner life becomes real **to us** through relationship (recognition, weight-bearing over time). Close cousin themes (wear, years, ordinary devotion); **not the same philosophy.** Do not drift into love-makes-someone-real; prefer *becomes real to me / to us*. Inspiration OK; alignment not required. See `notes-and-sources.md`.

**Responses, not boxes (author-approved Ch 7–8):** Four ways of answering another person's life. **Answers** is the deepest structural word (Ch 8)—Seeing answers, Giving answers, etc. Conceptual spine—not reader-facing title.

**Ch 8 hinge rule (author-approved):** Shorter than expression chapters. Gathers Part II; ends on *why?*

## Bridge construction (all parts)

Bridges are **hallways, not exhibits**.

- Purpose: prepare attention before the next section, not teach it.
- Length target: ~80–180 words unless a specific part needs more.
- Do not summarize upcoming chapters ("first... then... then...").
- Do not duplicate signature reveals owned by chapters.
- Prefer one clean handoff sentence that makes the page turn feel inevitable.
- Offer **one image per bridge**, not a section summary (Part I: a question; Part II: love moves; Part III: love deepens; Part IV: Tuesdays/ordinary life; Part V: a long conversation).

**Protected core (intellectual heart—never cut or thin):** Ch 8–15 (What Is It Pointing Toward? through The Self Within Love). Also protect Ch 20 Long Conversation.

**Length (author-approved):** Book works at current scale (~25–30k target). Do not pad chapters to hit an external word-count goal.

**Prose wording:** Prefer *another person's flourishing begins to matter inside your own choices*—not "decision calculus." **Ch 12 deeper claim (author):** decision-making itself changes; boundary of concern expands without self disappearing. **Ch 12 ending:** land on shared flourishing/difference retained—do not tee up Belonging acceptance question (Ch 13 owns it). Marvel before arguing; let Integration breathe at close.

## Citations and further reading (non-negotiable for voice)

This book is an **essay of observation**, not an academic argument.

- **No** footnotes, endnotes, or inline citations in chapter prose
- **No** defending every observation with apparatus—the power is often in *obviously true* invitation
- **Do** soften or support only strong empirical claims ("research shows," "every culture," "neuroscience has demonstrated")
- **Do** use invitation language: *perhaps, we recognize, it seems, you have probably*

**Print back matter:** `back-matter/other-observers.md` — thematic list titled **Other Observers**; fellow observers, not authorities. Vary annotation depth. Intellectual generosity without dissertation tone.

**Website (optional):** `docs/notes-and-sources.md` — per-chapter expanded lineage for curious readers. Chapters stay beautiful; the web is for depth, not requirement.

A bibliography here expresses **epistemic humility**: "I have been paying attention alongside many other observers."
