# Further Reading — Other Observers

This book is an essay, not a dissertation. The chapters ask you to look alongside the author—not to evaluate footnotes. For that reason, there are no citations in the body of the text.

But the conversation about love did not begin with this book, and it will not end here. If you would like to keep looking, these observers may be useful companions—not part of the argument, but part of the longer conversation.

## Seeing the world differently

- **Rebecca Solnit** — *The Faraway Nearby* or *A Field Guide to Getting Lost* — on following questions through observation rather than rushing to conclusions. Her work helped shape the observational style of this book more than any single argument about love.

## Recognition before explanation

- **Gerd Gigerenzer** — *Gut Feelings* — on recognizing patterns before we can explain them
- **Michael Polanyi** — *Personal Knowledge* — on knowing more than we can say
- **John Vervaeke** — *Awakening from the Meaning Crisis* (lecture series and related work) — on meaning, relevance realization, and participatory knowing. Some of this book's questions about recognition and attention resonate with that territory, even where the paths diverge.

## Seeing another person

- **Martin Buber** — *I and Thou* — on encounter that treats the other as person, not object
- **Iris Murdoch** — *The Sovereignty of Good* — her claim that loving attention is a moral act shaped many of the questions in this book, even where we ultimately take different paths
- **Simone Weil** — *Waiting for God* (essays) — on attention as love's prerequisite

## Love in ordinary life

- **bell hooks** — *All About Love* — on love as practice, not sentiment
- **Leo Buscaglia** — *Love*
- **Fred Rogers** (interviews and writings) — on noticing children, naming feelings, staying present

## Attachment, trust, and repair

- **John Bowlby** — *Attachment*
- **Sue Johnson** — *Hold Me Tight* — on emotional attunement and repair in close relationships
- **Brené Brown** — *Atlas of the Heart* — contemporary language for connection, trust, and emotional experience

## Meaning, suffering, and what matters

- **Viktor Frankl** — *Man's Search for Meaning* — on what remains when explanation fails
- **Elisabeth Kübler-Ross** — *On Death and Dying*

## Humanity's long conversation

- **C.S. Lewis** — *The Four Loves* — on the many faces of one word
- **Erich Fromm** — *The Art of Loving*
- **Paul Tillich** — *Love, Power, and Justice*
