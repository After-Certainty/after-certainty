# Small Acts

Someone waited at the curb while an elderly neighbor locked the door; someone listened on the phone without trying to fix what could not be fixed; someone carried groceries up the stairs because the bags were heavy and the offer was easy; someone made coffee and left the mug where it would be found; someone said, "I've got you," and meant it for the next hour, not for the rest of life.

No theory was discussed, no framework was named, and no one proved anything about the nature of love—and yet the day changed shape, a little, the way days do when someone answers your life instead of passing it by.

A book like this should end here—not with a final definition, but with attention. Small acts are simply what attention looks like when it reaches another person's life.

If these pages have helped you notice those ordinary gestures a little more often—in your own life, in someone else's, in the rooms you enter tomorrow—then they have done their work.

Perhaps love has never needed the last word—only the next one, spoken kindly, or not spoken at all, only shown, only stayed for, only done.

Again.
