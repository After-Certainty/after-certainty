# Chapter 6 - The Curse

The world came back wrong—worse than the rings had warned.

Cael stood on the outer ring of the Ashring and tried to get his bearings. Stone steps cut across the rings beneath his boots—ring after ring stepping down toward a flat center still empty from where they'd stood. Beyond the rim, the mountain dropped away into grey slopes and haze. The sky hung low and pale over the lip of the Ashring, no shadows, no clear direction. Something heavy pressed on his chest and wouldn't move.

They had moved toward the rim without speaking. The packs waited where they'd left them beyond the scree lip—canvas stiff with sleet, ordinary things against the grey sky. Neither had reached for them yet. Picking them up felt too much like signing something twice. Riven's breathing wouldn't stay separate from Cael's—inhales and exhales tangling until he couldn't tell which lungs were whose.

Cael let go of Riven's wrist—or tried to. His fingers opened too slowly, then all at once. Pins and needles shot up his arm.

The bowl didn't release them all at once. Warmth clung to Riven's boots while cold ran up Cael's spine—both at once, before either could say which was whose.

Riven made a sound, not a word, just discomfort. Cael heard it twice: in his ears and in his head. He wanted to rub his own ears like that would fix them.

"You all right?" Cael asked.

"No," Riven said. The word arrived with the same copper taste already sitting in Cael's mouth. His stomach lurched.

"Sit," Cael said.

"I'm not fainting," Riven said.

"Sit." Cael didn't raise his voice. Riven opened his mouth to argue, then sat down so abruptly it looked like his legs had made the decision for him. Cael dropped beside him. The stone felt cold now—the warmth was gone, or had sunk into the rock below them, he couldn't tell.

His nose was running. He wiped it on his sleeve before Riven could see and hated that he cared.

His hands were shaking. He clenched them into fists. It didn't help. Riven's fear slid into him anyway, immediate and raw, no warning, no distance.

Cael swallowed hard. "Okay." The word sounded thin. "Breathe."

"I'm breathing," Riven said.

The irritation arrived before the words did. Cael felt that too.

"Then slow down," Cael said.

"You sound like Mara," Riven said.

"Good." Cael's throat felt dry. "Mara keeps people alive."

Riven barked out a laugh. The sound stung. Cael pressed a hand against the stone and focused on things he could name—knife, rope, buckle, nail. The thought barely formed.

*Stop listing.*

Cael flinched hard, teeth clicking together.

"What?" Riven's voice came instantly. Too instantly.

"Nothing."

"That wasn't nothing," Riven said. Cael looked up. Riven was staring at him, eyes too wide, too focused.

"Don't." The word came out before Cael knew what he meant.

Riven swallowed. "You thought nail."

Cael froze. "I said nail."

"You thought it first." The fear in Riven's voice wasn't fear of the mountain anymore. "I heard it."

Silence. Cold. The hollow still felt watched.

"Fuck, Cael."

Cael wanted to deny it. The denial never arrived. Instead relief punched through him, sharp and ugly, because if Riven could hear him, then maybe he wasn't alone with whatever was happening. The relief lasted less than a second—long enough. Riven saw it. Felt it. The expression on his face changed immediately.

"You don't get to be relieved."

Cael stared. Neither of them had spoken. Neither needed to. Riven looked sick. Cael probably did too.

"Okay." Riven wiped his nose on his sleeve. "That's fucked."

"Yeah."

For a while they sat without speaking. Pieces kept breaking through—dock planks, herring on his gloves, Mara counting coins, Bran catching a coin—not his memories, but he was seeing them anyway.

Riven saw the lower dock: handcart, herring, boss shouting for crates. Cael flinched because the herring smell wasn't his shift.

Cael saw Mara's kitchen door—the hinge tapping, warm smell through the gap—and flinched because it wasn't his door, wasn't his hinge, wasn't his memory to have.

He jerked his head away.

"Sorry." Riven sounded horrified.

"You didn't do it."

"Didn't I?" Cael almost said no. The honest answer was worse. "I don't know."

Riven looked down at his hands. "So that's it?"

"I don't know." That earned a short laugh. Neither of them enjoyed it.

After a moment Cael pushed himself to his feet. Motion felt easier than sitting still. His knees almost buckled. Riven was standing before Cael could offer a hand. They both noticed. Neither mentioned it.

Wind crossed the rings, thin and directionless. Cael raised one hand slowly, palm out—a test, nothing more. Riven matched him immediately. Their hands stopped an inch apart, heat between them, steady in a way that made no sense. Neither hand shook.

Cael thought *knife* and felt Riven flinch before the word formed.

Riven thought *rope* and Cael's fingers curled without permission.

For a few seconds they stood there. Then Cael lowered his hand. Riven's followed the same motion, same speed, same hesitation. The sight of it turned his stomach.

"Fuck."

Riven nodded once. "Yeah."

Cael turned away from the center on purpose—toward the trail, toward the slope they'd climbed, toward anything that wasn't the rings. The path dropped away in tight switchbacks below them. Gravel spilled over the edge in frozen slides. Shadows were already gathering in the cut.

"Pack," Cael said.

"Pack," Riven echoed, then winced. "I didn't mean to do that."

"I know," Cael said.

Their packs sat where they'd left them near the rim—canvas, straps, ordinary things. Cael headed toward his. Riven headed toward his. Their hands reached for the straps at the same moment. Both stopped—the same hesitation, the same inch of space. Neither had touched the other.

Cael forced his hand onto his own strap. The canvas bit his palm. Good. His.

Riven shouldered his pack wrong—left strap twisted, right too loose. Cael reached to fix it. Stopped. Fixed his own instead. Riven watched both motions and looked sick.

"This is bad," Riven said. "Like actually fucking bad."

"Yeah." No maybe this time.

Cael grabbed his pack strap and held on. The rough canvas helped a little. Not enough.

The pamphlet called it Heartbridge. Cael thought set your feet and move—and felt Riven go still, not startled anymore. Used to it. That was almost worse.

Riven rubbed both hands across his face, then stopped. Cael pressed a hand against his thigh until the muscle ached—something solid, something his.

"We walk."

"Down."

"We can't stay up here." Riven adjusted his blanket roll, then adjusted it again, then stopped pretending that helped. Together they shouldered their packs.

The moment they started down the trail, things got worse—each step arriving early, balance shifting before gravel moved, fatigue crossing between them before either body stumbled. The first steep stretch should have been difficult. Instead they moved in sync. Cael hated it immediately.

Riven's pack had been packed wrong since the rim—left heavy, right light. Cael had noticed at the seep yesterday and not said anything. Now the wrong weight pulled at both of them. Riven shifted the strap. Cael felt the relief before the strap moved.

"Christ," Riven said. "Goddamnit."

"Yeah."

Halfway down the first switchback, Riven's foot slipped on loose stone. Cael's knee buckled at the same instant—before Riven fell, before the gravel moved. They caught themselves on the same handhold. Same knuckles. Same split skin.

"Stop." Riven's voice cracked.

Both of them stopped—the same foot, the same instant. Silence. Wind. A long drop beside the trail.

"We have to stop doing that," Cael said.

"That," Riven said. Cael knew exactly what he meant. "I tried to break step."

"So did I," Cael said.

Neither of them liked the implication. Riven's breathing started to speed up. Cael felt the panic before he saw it. He turned and put a hand on Riven's shoulder, heavy and deliberate.

"My hand," Cael said. "Your shoulder. My breath."

Riven swallowed. "Your hand. My shoulder. Your breath."

A laugh escaped him, halfway to a sob. "This is completely fucked."

"Yeah."

They stood there until the shaking eased, or until they got tired of standing there—maybe both. The descent got slower after that, ugly on purpose. Every few steps one of them would hesitate. The other would feel it. Neither could fully stop it.

At the next bend Riven tried to walk on the outside edge—on purpose, stupid, proof he still could choose. Cael felt the tilt before Riven's foot found gravel. His hand shot out. Not the bond. Habit. Riven jerked away.

"Don't."

"I'm not—" Cael stopped. "I felt it before you moved."

"I know." Riven's voice cracked. "That's the problem."

They walked single file after that. Riven on the inside. Cael ahead. Ugly on purpose—out of sync when they could manage it, which wasn't often.

Halfway down the bench trail, Cael tried an experiment—one word, nothing important.

*Cabbage.*

Riven snorted, the sound immediate and hopeless. "Why cabbage?"

Cael almost smiled. "First thing that came to mind."

"I heard it." Riven kicked a stone off the trail. It bounced twice and vanished. "I heard it before you—" He stopped. Swallowed. "Doesn't matter."

"Neither do I," Cael said.

They tried again at the next bend—Riven with *rope*, Cael with *knife*. Same instant. Same failure. Riven sat on a flat stone until his breathing slowed. Cael sat beside him without being asked. Their knees almost touched. Neither moved away.

"If we think at the same time—" Riven started.

"We hear it," Cael said.

"If we think different things?" Cael stared at his boots. "We heard cabbage."

"That's not—" Riven laughed, helpless. "That's not an answer."

"It's the one we've got." He picked at a burr stuck to his cuff—a stupid problem, normal-sized, almost comforting. "At least cabbage doesn't bite."

The wind crossed the slope, cold and thin. Cael watched his breath fog and vanish. Silence was getting harder. That scared him more than the cabbage test.

Riven kept his eyes on the trail—not on Cael, not on the Ashring behind them.

"This is the curse."

The word hit between them—*curse*—and Cael hated it on contact. Songs and pamphlets and market bullshit in one neat package.

Riven stumbled—not from the trail, from something crossing the bond. His head snapped around.

"Don't."

Cael looked away. "I didn't say anything."

"You didn't have to."

The anger hit first. Then fear. Then something worse—disappointment. Riven had caught him hoping.

"You already sound like you're explaining it."

"I'm trying to get it."

They walked.

At the next bend Riven stopped without warning. Cael stopped too—same foot, same instant.

"Chalk map," Riven said. "You had the seep wrong."

"I had it before dusk."

"You said noon."

"On the step I said before dusk." Cael kept his eyes on the trail. "You heard noon because you were angry."

Riven's anger arrived in Cael's chest before Riven spoke. "I heard what you wrote."

"You heard what you expected." Cael's voice stayed level. Too level. "Same as the fork marker. Same as the left road we didn't take."

Riven kicked gravel off the edge. "Fine. You were wrong. I was wrong. We're still walking."

"Yeah." Cael hitched his pack. "That's the part that matters."

The trail narrowed. Silence helped exactly as much as talking.

"I'm not saying this is good," Cael said.

"Could've fooled me," Riven said.

"You heard *maybe*." Cael kept his eyes on the trail. "That wasn't yes."

"Wasn't no," Riven said.

They stopped walking. Wind pushed at their coats. The mountain fell away beside them. Far below, a bird cried late—or early. The sound arrived after the bird had already turned.

"We didn't ask for this," Cael said.

"You walked us onto the rings." Riven's voice stayed level. Too level. "You held out your hand. You fucking held it out."

Cael's jaw tightened. "You grabbed it."

"I know." Riven opened his eyes. "That's what I'm saying. Shit."

Cael didn't answer. Because Riven was right. Because he wasn't. Because both things could be true.

The fear underneath Riven's anger hit his gut—trapped, stuck, the same claustrophobia he'd felt on the rings. Cael bent forward and put his hands on his knees until his vision steadied.

"How far to the tree line?" Riven asked.

"Hours." Cael straightened. "Maybe less if we don't fall."

"What?" Riven said.

"To the trees." Cael hitched his pack. "You asked."

"I know what I asked." Riven's boot skidded on wet needles. "I just wanted to hear it out loud."

Riven barked out a laugh. The sound carried down the slope. "You really don't quit."

"No," Cael said. That at least was easy.

Riven shook his head. "Fuck you."

"Fair," Cael said.

They started walking again. Lower down, the mountain softened. Pines returned. The wind sounded like wind again. Almost.

Twice on the descent Riven tried to hum—nothing, just noise to fill his head. Cael heard the tune anyway. Old bond story. Market square. Wrong mountain for it. Riven stopped humming.

Cael found a flat spot beneath a stand of pines and dropped his pack. Needles gave under his boots; the slope fell away to the east, treeline opening on the pale Ashring above the ridge. Neither discussed it. Riven let his pack slide off one shoulder. The relief crossed between them immediately. Both of them winced. Riven muttered, "Still fucking hate that."

Above the treeline the Ashring showed pale on the ridge—grey stone against cloud, no warmth left in it.

Cael gathered deadwood and built a fire—anything with steps felt easier than thinking. Split kindling. Stack. Spark. Flame. Pine sap hissed. His hands knew the work. Riven's hands knew it too—Cael felt the same motions ghosting across his own fingers before Riven touched the wood.

"Don't look at me like you're happy," Riven said. He stared into the fire. Ash crept up the kindling stack.

"Are you?" Cael asked.

Cael watched the flames climb.

"I'm scared." He waited for Riven to use it against him. Riven didn't.

Riven nodded. "But."

There it was—the word neither of them could leave alone.

Cael sighed. "Yeah."

Riven looked away immediately. "Of course you did."

"Stop saying that," Riven said.

"It's still true," Cael said.

Riven rubbed both hands over his face, tired and angry and frightened. Cael felt all three.

They ate because they had to. The food tasted wrong—metallic, ash and salt. Neither finished much.

Riven set the half-eaten bread aside. "Mara would've charged us for that."

"She still will," Cael said.

Riven almost laughed. Almost.

"You're scared," Cael said. It wasn't a question.

"Yeah," Riven said.

"But you keep thinking it's worth it," Cael said.

Cael didn't deny it fast enough. Riven felt the hesitation cross a beat late.

"Stop," Riven said.

"I'm stopping," Cael said.

"You're not." Riven stared into the coals. "You're already planning tomorrow's miles."

"Someone has to," Cael said.

"Plan them quiet," Riven said.

Cael looked at his hands. "I'll try."

Later they lay down back to back beside the fire like they always did on cold nights. Firelight threw orange across Riven's profile and died at the tree line. Every shift of Riven's shoulders arrived in Cael's body too. The fire burned low to coals. Thoughts kept breaking through—dock planks, coins, the monument, fear—and none of it stayed private.

Once Cael thought about turning around. Walking back to Greyhaven alone. The thought crossed whole—route, gate, Mara's board—and Riven stiffened before Cael finished it.

"Don't even think it," Riven said to the dark.

"I wasn't—" Cael started.

"You were," Riven said.

Cael shut his eyes. "I'm not going."

"Good." Riven's breath hitched. "Because I can't tell if that was you or me wanting it."

"Cael." Riven's voice came quietly.

"Yeah," Cael said.

A long pause. Then: "I'm still me."

The words were so small they almost disappeared into the dark.

Cael swallowed. "Me too." He wasn't completely sure. Neither of them were.

Riven picked a bit of gristle from between his teeth and flicked it into the fire. "Mara's stew had more bone than meat. Should've charged us double."

"She did charge us double."

"Then she was right." Riven stared at the coals. "Hate when that happens."

"Her turnips were worse."

"Turnips aren't food."

"Agreed." Cael poked the fire with a stick. "Bran ate mine anyway."

"Bran eats everything."

"True."

The fire popped softly. Somewhere between waking and sleep:

We hold.

The answer came back immediately—not spoken, not hidden.

Yeah.

The fire settled into coals. Neither pulse sounded entirely separate anymore.

Cael woke once in the dark to Riven already awake—or the other way around; the distinction had stopped mattering. The coals glowed. The Ashring was a pale absence on the ridge above them.

"Sleep," Cael said.

"Trying," Riven said.

"Try harder," Cael said.

Riven laughed once, silent. "Fuck you."

"Fair," Cael said.

Dawn came grey and cold. Cael woke to Riven already packing—or Riven woke to Cael already packing; the order didn't settle. Coals still held a dull red in the ash ring. The Ashring sat pale on the ridge above them, rings and rim sharp against the grey morning air. They checked the fire twice. Checked the trail once. Checked each other's straps without speaking.

"Food," Riven said.

"Half an oatcake."

"Quarter." Riven held up the oatcake wrap—they'd eaten through one at the fire and split the last wrong. "Quarter if you're counting honest."

"Half," Cael said, and tied the oatcake wrap. His hands knew the knot. Riven's knew it too. They both hated that.

They walked east downslope because the north road home would have meant deciding to go back, and neither of them could tell anymore whether that decision would be shared.

At the first stream they stopped to wash. The water tasted wrong—metallic, faintly warm though the air was cold. Riven spat. Cael spat too. They looked at each other and didn't laugh.

"Still us?" Riven asked.

"Ask me at the next town."

"There isn't a next town."

"Then ask me tonight." Cael shouldered his pack. "Same question."

Behind them the Ashring stayed pale on the ridge. Ahead, more road. More mistakes. That much, at least, was familiar.

Riven walked behind Cael for a while, then beside him, then behind again—trying to find a distance that didn't feel shared. Every arrangement failed. The bond didn't care about formation.

At midmorning they passed a collapsed waymarker—post snapped, arrow buried in scree. Cael stopped long enough to read the carved initials. Not Greyhaven. Not anywhere he knew.

"Left would've been longer," Riven said. "Your map said left at the fork."

"My map said check the cloth." Cael stepped over the broken post. "There was no cloth."

"So we're walking blind."

"We're walking east." Cael kept his eyes on the downgrade. "Blind is when you don't know you're wrong."

Riven snorted. "Feel better?"

"No." Cael's boot skidded on wet pine needles. Riven's ankle turned at the same instant—before the skid, before the needles. They caught themselves on the same trunk. Same bark. Same split skin.

"Christ," Riven said.

"Yeah."

They didn't stop long. Stopping made the sharing worse.

"Cael."

"Yeah."

"If I say stop thinking, does it help?"

"No."

"Didn't think so." Riven kicked a stone. "Just checking."

Cael was quiet long enough that Riven felt the quiet too. Then: "Ask me tonight again."

"Same question?"

"Same question."

The road bent east. Greyhaven stayed behind the ridge. The curse stayed in their chests. Walking was still walking—for now, that had to be enough.
