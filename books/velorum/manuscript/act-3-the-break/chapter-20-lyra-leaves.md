# Chapter 20 - Lyra Leaves

On the second day after the square, the infirmary annex had more cots and fewer answers. One long room off the main hall, high north windows looking down on the yard. Cots now filled the center where there had been walking space before the attack. Lyra had turned two walk-ins away at dawn—crush bruises, nothing broken—and sent them to Merrow's compensation line because every cot was spoken for.

Prep table and jars along the west wall. The square still lived in Riven's palms when he closed his hands—splinters under bandages, wet stone that had not been wet, Halverin's horn cracked on the step.

Yesterday—the first day after the demonstration—he had hauled splintered timber until his shoulders burned and the bond carried Cael's fatigue on top of his own. Merrow had assigned him to debris because hands were needed and symbols were dangerous. Paste crews worked the magistrate wall in strips—not replacing, scraping. Halverin's clerk tried twice for signatures on a correction broadside. Merrow's runner took both sheets to the command hall and filed them under *suspended*. Riven watched paste curl in brazier smoke and went back to hauling.

At the debris pile the talk was not about timber. A guard on lunch break said the bond had failed because the pair loved each other wrong. A cooper said Halverin had failed because he counted bodies like beams. A fishwife said the crowd had failed because Kestrel Gate believed paste more than neighbors. Nobody agreed. Everybody kept working.

Riven heard all three versions before noon and felt Cael hear them too through the bond—fatigue, irritation, a clamp of shame when the guard's version landed too close to the canal memory. Riven did not comment. Commenting would make it real in the room between them. He had commented his way through every argument since Greyhaven. He was running out of sentences that reached Cael on the other side of a slate.

At the debris pile a cooper stacked timber beside his nephew with rope burns. They lifted on the wrong beat twice and both winced. The third time they matched without looking and the bond went quiet for six breaths—long enough to feel how loud it had been before.

Merrow passed at noon with a slate and marked them both present. "Hands not throats," she said, and kept walking.

Yesterday at midday Cael had come down from the wall roster and joined the debris cord without announcement. Riven had seen him coming and felt his own shoulders tighten before Cael felt it back.

"You don't have to," Riven said.

"Merrow assigned me second shift on debris instead of east wall." Cael picked up a splintered board. "Unless you'd rather I hold a story somewhere."

They worked in silence long enough for the cooper's nephew to leave for water. Then Riven said, "People think we failed the city."

"People think a lot of things." Cael stacked timber on the wrong beat. Both winced. "Some of them think Halverin failed the city. Some think the city failed itself."

"Which do you think?" Riven said.

Cael was quiet while a debris wagon rolled past. "I think we failed in public and the city saw it," he said. "I think that's different from failing the city. I think Halverin wanted those to be the same sentence."

Riven looked at him. "You understand me."

"I understand you," Cael said. "I still signed his slate."

"I know," Riven said.

"I'd sign Merrow's slate again."

"I know that too." Riven lifted a beam end. "That's what scares me. Not that you disagree. That you're right about which slates matter and I still can't follow you there without feeling like I'm abandoning something."

Cael's jaw worked. Through the bond Riven felt grief—not for Halverin, for the version of them that had thought one rhythm could fix a city if they just counted hard enough.

"We're not fixing a city today," Cael said.

"No," Riven said. "We're stacking proof."

The nephew returned with water. They drank. They worked. The bond stayed loud but not ricocheting—fatigue and irritation and something like respect that neither of them would name because naming it felt too much like hope.

---

That morning patrol routes shifted—east wall thin, debris detail thick. A guard at the basin gate argued with Halverin's aide about who held the square roster. "Captain's until the magistrate files otherwise," Merrow's runner said from the lane. The guard chose Merrow's slate and waved Riven through.

The spice seller from the south lane—saffron stain still ghosted in the stones where her table had sat—opened a tray at the market edge with nothing on it but a Merrow compensation stub and a sign that said *paid at last, still angry*. She sold spice by the pinch to customers who asked why she was smiling. "Because paperwork finally lost," she said. "Don't make me a symbol either."

Above the magistrate office windows, shutters stayed closed. Riven heard from the debris pile that Halverin's senior clerk had spent the morning rewriting the demonstration report three times—*crowd noise*, then *the pair mismatched*, then *the bond went wrong*—while a council aide read each draft aloud and shook his head. By afternoon the clerks had stopped agreeing with each other and started filing everything under *pending council*, which was Merrow's word for *suspended* with better shoes.

He had slept in fragments the night before and woken with Cael's dreams still moving in his chest. Lyra had treated three crush injuries and a guard with a rope burn and had not come to the east annex except once, at noon, to leave broth and a note that said *don't sign*. He had eaten the broth. He had burned the note so clerks would not find it.

Halverin's clerk came to the annex door twice with a correction broadside. Merrow's guard turned him away both times—first with a spear shaft flat side, second with an injury slate held up like a shield. The clerk left ink on the threshold and no signature on either sheet.

He had known she was leaving before she said it. Her kit had shrunk. Her name was on a south ward manifest Merrow's runner had dropped at the command hall. Greyhaven's outer clinics needed needles more than Kestrel needed another witness to the bond.

He had told himself he would argue when the moment came. He had also known, with the same tired accuracy Lyra had always carried, that argument would be for him, not for her.

The bond had been loud since the demonstration, not sharp spikes anymore, a constant low static of mood and fatigue crossing without permission. He could tell when Cael ate. He could tell when Cael pretended to sleep. He could not tell where his own irritation ended and Cael's began.

Riven came in at dusk with split knuckles from hauling debris and a headache that had started in Cael's shoulders at noon and never left.

The annex smelled of boiled cloth and the sharp clean of Lyra's soap. Two orderlies moved cots along the far wall—Lyra's cot row now held eight filled and one spare where her kit had been. A man with a bandaged forearm watched Riven enter and looked away, as if eye contact might pull him into the story.

In the main hall an orderly turned away a woman with a crush bruise from the square. "Annex full. Try Merrow's compensation line or come back when a cot clears." The woman sat on the steps with her wrist wrapped in a dishcloth and waited anyway.

Two clinic orderlies argued in the corridor over whether Lyra's departure meant the bond was contagious or the city was finally telling the truth about cost. One said Greyhaven would send another medic. The other said Greyhaven would send another story. Lyra's voice cut through from the prep room: "Both. Now move a cot."

Lyra was at the prep table washing blood off her forearms. She looked up once and pointed at the stool.

"Sit before you fall on someone," she said.

He sat. She wrapped his knuckles without asking what happened. Her hands were steady. Her face was not angry. That was worse.

"You came to say goodbye," Riven said.

"I came to finish a bandage," Lyra said. "But yes."

The word landed in his chest and in Cael's at the same time. Across the courtyard Cael flinched over a supply slate. Riven felt it and hated that even this moment had a witness inside his own skin.

An orderly stacked folded cloth on the prep table where Lyra's kit had been. Needles in a roll. Salve jars counted twice. Merrow's runner checked the manifest at the door and initialed without looking at either of them.

"You don't have to watch the gate," Lyra said. "You have debris until second bell."

"I know," Riven said.

"Then don't make Cael watch for you."

"He'll go anyway."

"Yes," Lyra said. "Don't make it clean when he does."

Riven's throat tightened. "It isn't clean."

"No," she said. "It isn't clean."

She tied off the cloth.

She washed her forearms again though they were already clean, like she needed the motion. Riven watched water pinken and drain. He had seen that color in the square on someone's bandage. He had seen it again in the medic tent when Lyra washed his palms after the demonstration without saying *I told you*.

Lyra set the salve down. "The roof was dry," she said, so quiet he almost missed it. "You remember that?"

Riven's throat closed. "Yeah."

"So do I." She picked the salve back up. "That's not why I'm leaving."

Merrow's runner knocked once and left a south ward manifest on the bench without entering. Lyra glanced at it and kept washing. Riven saw Greyhaven's outer clinics on the header line.

"I wanted—" He stopped. Started again. "I thought if we survived that fucking square, there'd be room for something that was mine."

Lyra met his eyes. "There isn't."

"I know." His throat hurt. "I just needed you to hear it."

"I heard it before you said it," she said. "So did he."

Riven closed his eyes. "I can't fucking sneeze without him feeling it."

Lyra set the basin aside. "I told you before you walked out there."

"You told me the truth. I didn't want it."

"I know." She dried her hands on a cloth. "I didn't want it either."

That stopped him. "What?"

"Not the bond." She folded a clean cloth once, precise, like control still mattered in small things. "The roof. The canal. What we had before the square." She looked at his bandaged knuckles. "I don't get to be private for you. Neither does he. If I stay, I'm just more weight on the cot rail."

"You could stay and hate us," Riven said. "People do."

"I could," Lyra said. "That's not the same as staying and helping."

She glanced at the corridor where Cael's presence pressed on the bond. "He's listening," she said.

"I know," Riven said.

"Then listen back," she said. "Not to me. To what happens when you split again. I won't be the wedge."

Riven stood. The stool scraped. "So you leave because we're broken."

"I leave because staying would be a lie," she said. "I'd stand next to you bleeding in two directions and call it love. I won't."

He wanted to argue, wanted to grab her wrist and prove he could hold one feeling in one body if he tried hard enough—and the bond answered with Cael's spike of jealousy and shame before Riven's hand moved. Lyra saw it. "There."

"I'm not—" Riven said.

Lyra's voice stayed tired, not cruel. "You are." "Every time you reach for me, he feels it. Every time he clamps down, you feel it. I won't be the reason you split again."

Riven's hands hung at his sides. "What am I supposed to do with that?"

"Live," she said. "Stop calling it progress. Stop waiting for Halverin to invent a prettier fucking word." She capped the salve jar. "And don't chase me to make the bond quieter. I can't quiet it."

He laughed once, broken. "You think I'd chase you for that?"

"I think you're desperate for one room that isn't shared," she said.

He had no answer that wasn't true.

She picked up a small bundle from the bench, rolled cloth and two needles in leather. "South ward's short-handed. I'm going with the supply train to Greyhaven's outer clinics. Merrow signed it."

"Greyhaven," Riven said.

"Far enough," Lyra said.

The bond tightened. Cael was moving toward the annex door. Riven felt his steps before he heard them, anger and grief in the same stride.

"Don't," Riven said to the air. Not to Cael. To whatever part of him still believed he could control the goddamn room.

Cael came through the doorway anyway. He stopped when he saw Lyra's bundle.

"You're leaving," Cael said.

"Yes," Lyra said.

Cael looked at Riven. Riven looked back. The bond carried everything neither of them would say in front of her.

"I'm not asking you to stay," Cael said to Lyra.

"I know," Lyra said.

"If you need escort to the gate—"

"I have Merrow's guards," Lyra said. "Use your hands on the wall."

She looked at Cael's bandaged palms, then at Riven's knuckles. "Stop calling it alignment when it hurts."

Cael's jaw tightened. "Fine."

She shouldered the bundle. At the door she stopped and looked at Riven one last time.

"I'm not rejecting you," she said. "I'm leaving before I start lying to us both."

"Yeah." Riven looked at the door. His chest hurt in a way that had nothing to do with the bond for once—or everything, he couldn't tell. "I wanted the roof back. I wanted one morning where you knocked and it was only you."

"I know." Her voice broke on the second word. She covered it by shouldering the bundle. "Don't follow the wagon. Merrow already said that. I'm saying it because I mean it."

She left. The annex door shut. Footsteps faded down the corridor and out into the yard. Riven heard a guard greet her by name. Heard a wagon wheel turn. Heard none of it through his own ears only.

Merrow's runner checked the south ward manifest at the yard gate and initialed each crate line. Halverin's clerk tried to attach a correction broadside to the manifest stack. Merrow's runner took the sheet, read *change the run*, and handed it back. "File under *suspended*. Gate clears on my slate, not his."

Cael stood in the corridor with his back to the wall. The bond carried Lyra's footsteps leaving and Riven's grief and Cael's own shame in one braid. Cael did not follow Riven into the annex room. He went to the yard instead and signed Merrow's gate roster with a hand that shook.

He understood why Riven wanted one room that did not travel.

An orderly entered without knocking, moved a cot into the space Lyra's kit had occupied, and left. The cot legs scraped stone. Riven felt Cael flinch in the corridor at the sound.

For a minute he stood where she had stood and looked at the prep table. Pink water cooling in the basin. Bandage scraps. The fold in the cloth where her fingers had pressed the edge flat.

Gray evening through the high windows. He listened to the bond carry Cael's grief and his own until they were the same sound.

Cael did not speak for a long time. Then: "I'm sorry."

"Don't," Riven said. "Don't make it about your fucking sorry."

"What do you want," Cael said.

Riven looked at the stool Lyra had pushed toward him. At the basin with pink water. At the bandage scraps on the table. "I want one feeling that doesn't travel," he said.

Cael's face tightened. "I can't give you that."

"I know," Riven said.

"She's not rejecting you," Cael said. "She said that."

"I heard her." Riven's voice stayed flat. "I also heard the wagon."

They stood on opposite sides of the room like strangers who shared one chest. Outside the city kept moving. Debris wagons. Gate counts. Inside, something had ended without a fight and without a reconciliation, the way real endings often did.

Riven washed his knuckles in the basin though Lyra had already wrapped them. Pink water. He poured it out and refilled from the jug. Cael watched and felt Riven's hands as his own for three breaths before the bond eased.

Lyra's folded cloth still sat on the prep table edge—the crease where her fingers had pressed flat. Riven did not touch it. Cael did not ask him to.

Cael set his cup down untouched. "Halverin's clerk came today."

"I know. Merrow turned him away."

"He'll come again."

"Yeah," Riven said. "Everything comes again until it doesn't." Cael flinched. Somewhere in the yard a clerk called counts that nobody answered.

Riven picked up his coat. "I'm going to the wall."

"Riven—"

"Don't follow me to be noble. I can't talk right now without you hearing every goddamn word before I say it."

He left. The bond followed anyway, thinner but inescapable, Cael's hurt and his own braided down the stairwell and out into cold air.

The yard was busy in the ordinary way. A team hitched to the supply train, trace chains clinking. Merrow's guards stood at the gate lane with spears vertical, work-ready, not parade-ready. A driver cursed at a tangled trace. Someone laughed once and stopped, remembering the square.

A fish seller had shortened her hours—shutter half down at the yard mouth, tray going home unsold. Two clinic orderlies loaded crates marked *needles—south ward* onto the second wagon. Lyra checked each knot herself. Riven kept his eyes on the ground until he was past the wagons. He did not want to look at the second wagon bench. He saw anyway. Lyra sat beside the driver with her bundle at her feet, talking to Merrow's guard about clinic stores, voice practical, already gone in the way that mattered.

Halverin's clerk stood at the command hall steps with a stack of forms and nowhere to file them. Merrow's runner walked past without taking the stack. The clerk sat on the bottom step and wrote *demonstration suspended* on the top sheet, then crossed it out and wrote *pending council review* underneath. A fish seller paused to read over his shoulder and told him both phrases meant the same thing. The clerk did not look up.

Merrow passed Riven at the yard edge without stopping. "South ward manifest is signed. Don't chase the wagon."

"I'm not chasing," Riven said.

"Good." Merrow looked at his bandaged knuckles. "Wall duty tomorrow. Hands still work." She paused. "She signed the manifest herself. Don't make her trip longer by standing in the lane."

Riven's step faltered. The bond tightened with Cael's surge from the annex stair. Grief. Restraint. Something like permission. Riven did not know which was worse.

He kept walking to the wall.

At the top of the east wall he stood alone and watched the supply train roll toward the gate—hooves, wheel ruts, guard calls from the lane. Lyra's bundle was a dark shape on the second wagon bench. She did not look back.

Riven did not wave.

Below, Kestrel Gate's watchfires were being fed one by one along the ramparts. The square where they had broken in public stayed empty. Broadsides were gone from the wall. Chalk marks faded in the traffic. From the merlon he could see the basin where sound had lagged. Halverin's horn still on the stair, mouth cracked. A paste crew scraped the last strip from the north wall before the light failed. A cooper's nephew hauled splintered timber to the debris cord without looking at the empty platform. Merrow's runner filed another magistrate sheet under *suspended* at the command hall door while Halverin's clerk sat on the steps with his stack and wrote nothing useful.

Riven pressed his forehead to cold stone and breathed.

Hooves on stone. A guard's call answered from the outer bend. Then the noise thinned to distance and gone.

A guard on the next merlon cleared his throat. "You all right?"

"Fine," Riven said.

"You don't look fine."

"Nobody looks fine this week."

The guard almost smiled. "Fair." He looked toward the gate lane where the last wagon had gone. "She's good at clinics. South ward needed her before the square needed a witness."

Riven did not answer. The guard took that as enough and went back to his count.

When he climbed down, Cael was waiting at the stair foot with two cups of water and no speech.

"You didn't chase the wagon," Cael said.

"You told me not to make it clean," Riven said.

"I meant for her." Cael handed him a cup. "I didn't mean you had to do it alone."

Riven took the water. "I know what you meant." He drank. "I also know what the bond does when you mean something else."

Cael flinched. The bond carried it both ways. They stood on the stair foot long enough for a guard shift to change below and nobody to salute either of them.

Merrow passed behind them with a gate count slate and did not stop. "Manifest clears at second bell," she said to the guard at the foot. "Hands on the wall after."

Riven took one cup. They drank. They walked back to the annex side by side, not touching, the bond carrying the walk anyway.

"She won't come back for a while," Cael said.

"I know."

"Greyhaven outer clinics. Far enough."

"Yeah." Cael stopped at the annex door. "I'm sorry I felt it when you—"

"Don't," Riven said. "If you apologize for the bond one more time I'll walk into the wall headfirst."

Cael shut his mouth. The bond carried his hurt anyway. Riven did not soften. He also didn't move away from the wall, which was petty and he knew it.

The infirmary corridor was quiet. Lyra's prep table was clean. The basin was gone. An orderly had already moved a cot into the space her kit had occupied.

Riven lay on his cot without removing his coat fully. Cael lay on his. The bond hummed low, static and grief and fatigue braided.

No one fixed anything.

Riven stared at the ceiling until the square noise through the wall thinned. Horn lowered. Lyra's bundle on the second wagon. One feeling that did not travel, and he would not have it tonight.

In the yard after second bell a guard shift changed at the basin gate. The outgoing sergeant handed Merrow's injury slate to the incoming guard and kept Halverin's sequence card in his fist. "Captain's square until the magistrate files otherwise," he said. The incoming guard initialed Merrow's column and posted at the debris cord instead of the platform steps.

Before the yard went fully quiet he heard the supply train clear the outer bend—wheel ruts on the road, guard call fading. Riven turned on the cot. Cael felt the turn in his own shoulder. "She's gone," Riven said.

"I know," Cael said.

They lay in the dark with the bond still open between them, static and grief and the ordinary fatigue of bodies that would work debris at first gray. No one fixed anything. No one signed anything. Merrow's slate would still say *wall duty* in the morning. Riven closed his eyes. Cael counted breaths until numbers replaced names again.

---

At first gray the morning after Lyra left, Merrow's runner knocked once on the annex door and left a wall roster—Riven's name, Cael's name, second watch, ordinary ink. Riven got up. The bond carried Cael's relief that work still existed and Riven's refusal to feel relieved about anything yet.

Riven stood at the debris cord while the city decided what it believed.

A tanner hung a charm string on his stall post—woodcut pairs painted red, three for a copper. A widow cut it down and told him to sell soap instead. The tanner said soap did not keep raiders out. The widow said neither did charms and took the string to Merrow's runner, who filed it under *confiscated* with the cooper's nephew tray from yesterday.

Two guards at the basin gate read competing handbills aloud to each other for practice. *VELORUM DEMANDS DISCIPLINE.* *THE PAIR HELD UNTIL THE CROWD DID NOT.* One guard said both could be true. The other said that was how cities died—holding two truths until neither held a gate.

Riven stacked timber and listened. Cael was on east wall second watch. Through the bond Riven felt him hear the same argument secondhand when a merchant repeated it at the gate seal line. Cael did not answer the merchant. Riven felt that too—the clamp of a man who had run out of sentences that could fix anything.

Merrow passed at noon. "Hands not throats," she said, same as always. Then, quieter: "City's still deciding. Don't help it decide on your back."

"I'm hauling," Riven said.

"Good," Merrow said. "Hauling is a decision too."

By dusk the fish seller had opened both shutters for an hour and sold out. The tanner sold two charm strings anyway from his apron pocket when Merrow's runner was on the other side of the square. The story spread faster than scrape crews.

Tam opened both shutters at midmorning and kept them open until the fish ran out—a small tray, sold by noon, nephew handling the scale while Tam scraped the board between customers. He did not go to the square. He did not sign Halverin's correction sheet when the clerk came a third time. Merrow's runner filed the clerk away with a compensation stub for *harassment at stall* and Tam initialed it without looking grateful.

Riven passed the stall on the way back from the wall. Tam nodded once. "Still missing," he said. "Still open." He handed Riven a wrapped fish he had not paid for. "Don't make it a symbol. Eat it."

Riven ate because bodies required it. The bond carried Cael tasting salt on the stair and Riven let it happen because refusing would be another fight they did not have room for.

Riven understood, with the tired clarity Lyra had always carried, that Kestrel Gate was not smaller than before the demonstration—it was louder inside its own walls, and the noise was not going to stop when he and Cael walked east toward Velorum.

He still loved him. That was the part the curse could not file under *the pair mismatched* or *suspended*—the part that made walking east feel like leaving someone behind even when the someone was still in his chest.

He went back to the annex. Cael was already there, boots on the wrong side of the door again, roster in hand. They did not talk about Lyra. They did not talk about the handbills. They ate bread Merrow had left and felt each other eat and went to sleep in fragments because bodies required it even when stories did not.

That was all he had for now. Enough for tomorrow.
