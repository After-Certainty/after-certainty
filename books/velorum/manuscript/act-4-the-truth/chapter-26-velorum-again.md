# Chapter 26 - Velorum Again

Ashring looked smaller than Cael remembered and older, stone bowl cracked where winter had worked the seams. Wind came up the slope bare and cold. No stalls. No pamphlets. No crowd. No chalk arrows for children to copy.

Only pressure in the air.

Cael stood at the rim with Riven beside him and the Seeker three paces back, hood down now, face plain in bad light. The bond was so tight Cael felt his own heartbeat twice, once in his chest and once in Riven's ribs where yesterday's fall still lived.

He remembered Ashfen: smaller pressure, colder water, the first time the bond had opened the wrong direction. Ashring was Ashfen grown teeth.

They had climbed through the night because the Seeker set pace and the land pushed at their backs. Neither had slept. Both had dreamed in pieces that crossed before either woke. Riven had surfaced once with Cael's memory of wooden supports and splintered pins. Cael had woken to the taste of scree dust and blood that wasn't his.

The last mile had been the worst. The ring ahead pressed behind Cael's eyes—not visibly, but in the weight. Riven had gripped the belt at his hip until the leather creaked.

At the rim they stood long enough for breath to steady. It didn't.

Cael stepped into the Ashring. Gravel shifted under his boots. Riven followed. The air thickened until breath felt borrowed.

Each step down increased the weight behind his eyes. By the fifth step Riven's vision greyed. By the tenth, Cael's hand found his sleeve and stayed there. Neither spoke. Speaking would have cost air they didn't have.

Halfway down the slope Cael's skin prickled the way it had at Ashfen. This time he did not fight for a private lung. He let the pressure find both of them.

"Don't," Riven said. "Christ. Don't."

"I know," Cael said.

Riven's hand found the belt buckle. Leather creaked. Anchor again. The bond tightened until Riven's vision narrowed. He breathed through it. Cael breathed with him. Two sets of lungs trying to claim the same air.

The stone ring was the same and not the same. Seams wider. Dust in the grooves. Just weight behind the eyes. Cael counted the standing stones: eleven intact, two cracked at the base, one fallen and half-buried in gravel. Someone had tried to repair the fallen stone once. The patch mortar had crumbled to powder.

"Velorum," Cael said.

Pressure answered. Not words. Weight behind the eyes, in the teeth, along the spine.

Riven's vision greyed at the edges. He tasted copper. Cael tasted it too. The bond didn't distinguish whose mouth bled.

"We want release," Riven said.

"We want out," Cael said.

The words came fast now.

"We want the fuck out," Riven said.

He thought of Halverin's unsigned sheet. Merrow's gate pass. Lyra's clinic wagon. All the doors that had closed behind them. This was the last door. It wasn't opening.

The pressure tightened. Not anger. Not refusal. Something older than either.

Images slid through Cael before he could separate them from memory.

River stairs. The square. Lyra climbing into a wagon without looking back. A practice yard in Greyhaven. Children drawing chalk arrows on stone. The same arrows washed away by rain. Gone. Drawn again.

A bridge at Heartbridge. Timber creaking under crowd weight. A wall at Kestrel Gate with empty support slots. A pair in a woodcut margin, line between their wrists. Each image arrived with the same weight in his teeth.

Then older images, not his: a pair on a road like this one, belt between their wrists; a crowd at a shrine lane; a wall failing where a support had been removed. No faces he recognized.

A woman mending a net. A boy carrying water up steps. A magistrate reading names into rain. None of it his life.

Riven flinched beside him. The bond carried confusion in both directions.

"No," Cael said.

The word left him before he knew he was speaking.

The pressure deepened. Not a voice. A certainty. The same certainty a beam carried under weight.

Not cure.

Structure.

Cael's stomach turned.

Beside him, Riven grabbed his sleeve. "Say it."

Cael couldn't. The answer sat behind his teeth. Heavy. Wrong. Already known.

Cael's knees hit stone. He hadn't meant to kneel. The ring didn't care about intention. Weight wanted contact. His palms found cold gravel. Riven swore and grabbed his shoulder, pulling against the pressure and failing.

"There isn't one."

The words tasted like rust.

Riven stared at him. "No. Fuck. No."

Cael wished he could argue. He wished the answer were a place they could leave, a god they could bargain with, a cure that would give Riven his room back. None of that was on offer. Only the weight still sitting between them.

The pressure shifted. A bridge. Not a specific bridge. The idea of one. Weight crossing. Weight held. Weight lost.

Then the image broke apart. People running. Mud. Water. A wall failing where something should have carried load and didn't.

"What does that have to do with us?" Riven shouted. "What the fuck does that have to do with us?"

The ring did not answer. The images kept coming. Pairs. Roads. Crowds. Not heroes. Not legends.

Riven tried to step back. His boots wouldn't move. Cael tried too. Same result. The ring held them the way a support held timber until the load settled.

Riven saw the square again, not as memory but as structure: bodies where bodies shouldn't be, load where load shouldn't sit, a crowd that had come to watch weight transfer and gotten blood instead. He wanted to argue with the image. The ring wasn't interested in argument.

He saw the east wall where he'd worked before the bond. Empty niches where metal pins should have sat. Somebody had removed them once for a festival. The wall had held anyway. Until it didn't. The image didn't show the until. It didn't need to.

Cael saw Greyhaven's practice yard. Then Kestrel Gate's east wall. Then the wooden support in the basin, pins sheared, wood grey with age. The same problem wearing different faces. Weight. Support. Failure.

"That's not a fucking answer," Riven said.

The pressure sharpened. For a moment Cael saw himself and Riven as strangers might. Not brothers. Not victims. Not chosen. A joint in a frame. Useful because it carried weight. Terrifying because it could fail.

Riven swore. Long. Creative. The ring ignored him. Good. Somebody should.

Cael laughed once despite himself. The sound died quickly.

"No cure," he said. This time he didn't fight the words. "Fuck."

Riven's knees unlocked. He hadn't known they were locked. Cael felt the unlock in his own legs and hated that he did.

The pressure didn't release. It settled. Like a beam finding its seat. Like weight accepting that weight was what it was—not less weight, not private weight, just load that had to be carried together or dropped on someone else.

Riven looked away. Toward the rim. Toward the path back down. Toward anywhere that wasn't the center.

"If we walk away?" Riven asked.

The pressure changed again. Not threat. Consequence. A road washed out. A wall leaning. People improvising around a missing support. Nothing dramatic. Nothing personal. Just weight finding somewhere else to go.

Cael saw Kestrel Gate's east wall with no support where one should have been. He saw the infirmary count still climbing. He saw Lyra's clinic wagon on a road they hadn't taken.

The images didn't ask permission. They didn't explain. They stacked.

Riven's jaw tightened. "Bullshit. That's coercion."

The pressure did not react. Maybe because it wasn't listening. Maybe because it didn't understand the accusation. Or maybe because gravity never argued when somebody called it unfair.

Cael put a hand on Riven's wrist.

Not comfort.

Anchor.

Riven's pulse hammered against Cael's fingers. Fast. Wrong rhythm. Cael counted it without meaning to. Halverin counting. He stopped counting.

The Seeker spoke. The voice sounded rough, as though it hadn't been used in a long time.

"Walk back or walk forward," the Seeker said.

Riven's throat hurt. Not from shouting. From swallowing answers that didn't fit.

Riven looked up. "Gods don't bargain."

Wind moved through the Ashring. For a moment nobody spoke. Then:

"Back to what?" Riven asked.

The Seeker studied him. "Back to the story you tell strangers."

The staff shifted against the stone. "Forward to the story they tell about you."

Riven looked at the ring. At the dust in the seams. At the fallen stone half-buried where someone had tried to patch and failed. Back meant Kestrel Gate's clerks and Halverin's empty stage and a city that would file them under *unresolved.* Forward meant he didn't know what yet. That had stopped being a reason to stop.

Riven swore under his breath. The bond carried grief. And something worse he couldn't name. Cael felt it too.

"Forward," Riven said. The word sounded tired.

"Forward," Cael said.

The pressure eased. Not approval. Not forgiveness. Less resistance. The seams of the ring dimmed. Wind crossed the Ashring again. Ordinary wind. Ordinary cold. The most ordinary thing either of them had felt in days.

Riven's ears still rang. Cael's hands wouldn't stop trembling until he pressed them flat against stone and counted breaths the way he'd counted loads on a bridge crew. One. Two. Three. Not Halverin's count. Load count. The ring had answered with structure, not mercy—not a place, not a cure, the act of carrying when partition failed. They would have to learn what that meant on the road home, if home still existed as anything other than a filing category.

Riven's legs didn't work for a moment. Cael's didn't either. They stood in the center of the ring until the Seeker turned toward the rim.

Cael's legs shook. He didn't sit. Sitting was surrender to something other than Velorum. Riven's hand was still on his sleeve. Neither removed it.

They climbed out without speaking. Each step uphill cost more than body weight. Riven stopped twice. Cael stopped with him. The Seeker didn't look back from the rim.

At the lip, Riven bent over and dry-heaved. Nothing came up. Cael's stomach turned anyway. The bond didn't distinguish dry from wet.

Halfway up, Riven touched the rim of the ring. Just once. Then kept moving.

His fingers came away grey with dust. Cael didn't ask why he'd touched it. The bond already knew: Riven had needed to confirm the stone was real. That the weight had been real. That none of it had been a fever dream from the scree slope.

At the top, the Seeker turned toward the scrub hills. No farewell. No blessing. No explanation.

The wind had changed. Ordinary wind. It tugged at Cael's coat the way wind was supposed to. He hadn't realized how wrong the air had felt until it stopped.

"Where do you go?" Cael called.

The Seeker paused. "Somewhere else."

"That's not an answer," Cael said.

The Seeker looked back. For the first time, something that might have been amusement crossed their face.

"No." Then they started down the slope. After a few steps they added: "Neither was yours."

And disappeared among the rocks.

Riven stared after them. "Well, shit."

"Yeah," Cael said.

They stood at the rim long enough for breath to steady. It didn't. Riven's hands shook. Cael's didn't, until the bond caught up.

Cael opened his pack. Merrow's note was still folded inside. The paper had softened at the creases. Another scrap had worked loose from the map pocket: Halverin's gate pass, stamped and expired two days ago. Nobody at the outer gate would care now. Nobody at any gate would care.

He tore the pass in half and let the wind take it. Riven watched the pieces spin downslope. For once the wind arrived on time.

*If you come back, come back useful.*

He read it once. Folded it again. Across the bond, Riven felt the words anyway.

Riven pulled the ruined map from his pocket. The woodcut was gone. The north road ink had run. He kept the scrap anyway, not for navigation, for proof they'd been filed somewhere once. Proof mattered less than it had in the square. He tucked it back without showing Cael. Cael felt the tuck anyway and said nothing.

Riven was still staring at the ring below.

"Think we are?" Riven asked. "Useful?"

Cael looked north. Then south. Then toward the road home.

"I don't know," Cael said.

They started down the slope together. Not following anyone now. Just walking. The road below split: one branch toward Kestrel Gate, one toward the broken hills and whatever came after. A mile marker someone had knocked flat lay in the grass, numbers scraped off. Someone else had carved over the post in fresh chalk—*pairs don't return useful.* Riven scraped at it with his knife until his knuckles bled. Cael took the eastern cut without discussing it. Riven fell in beside him.

"You believe it?" Riven asked. Not the ring. Velorum.

Cael thought of the images. Pairs. Functions. Weight shared because there was no other honest geometry.

"I believe we don't get a cure," he said. "I believe we don't get a room."

"That's not what I asked," Riven said.

"I know." Cael capped the skin. "Forward anyway."

Riven nodded. Forward anyway. No other direction on the map.

A shepherd appeared on the eastern cut, two dogs and a flock moving slow. The shepherd stared at their wrists, at the faded grooves, at the way they stood too close and too far apart at once. "Bond pair," he said. Not a question.

"Yeah," Riven said.

The shepherd spat into the dust. "Velorum's that way." He pointed south, then laughed at their faces. "Joke's on you. Velorum's wherever the load sits wrong." He drove the flock on without waiting for thanks.

Riven spat rust. "Forward tastes like shit."

"Yeah." Cael shifted his pack. "Keep walking."

They made camp that night without discussing it. No fire. Hard ground. Riven's boot wire snapped at the toe. He retied it with line from Merrow's pack. Cael held the boot without being asked. Small work. Real work.

Riven woke once in the dark and reached for a thought that stayed his—Lyra's clinic, the annex door, one feeling in one chest. The bond carried it before he finished the shape. Cael was already awake. "Still there," he said. Not comfort. Fact. Riven let go of the thought and slept in pieces. The forward had no ledger for what they'd become. Only the road east, where nobody in Kestrel Gate would count them unless they came back useful or came back proof.
