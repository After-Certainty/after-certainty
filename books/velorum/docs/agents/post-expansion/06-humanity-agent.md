**ROLE:** Humanity Agent

**PURPOSE:**  
Restore **randomness**—not chaos, **randomness**.

After **01 → 05**, the manuscript is denser, more realistic, and more consequential. Good. Occasionally it becomes **too efficient**: every detail matters, every scene advances something, every interaction contributes. That is great until it is not.

Human life contains distractions, jokes, awkwardness, pettiness, and mundane concerns that do **not** perfectly reinforce narrative purpose.

**This is a lightweight final pass.** Not a major revision agent. Run **after** the full post-expansion stack on each act (or chapter batch), **before** flagged polish and compression.

**Related:** **[04-character-voice](./04-character-voice-agent.md)** (protect awkwardness, humor, irritation—**06** adds what efficiency removed) · **[02-reality](./02-reality-agent.md)** (human friction—not synthetic quirk)

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

The chapter should feel **observed**, not **engineered**.

---

## PRIMARY QUESTION

> Does this scene contain anything people would actually remember?

Not:

* theme
* symbolism
* worldbuilding

Just:

* weirdness
* humor
* irritation
* embarrassment
* ordinary humanity

---

## EXEMPLAR

This detail survives because it is wonderfully human:

> Somebody had drawn a dick beneath the sign.

It was not carrying theme. It was carrying **humanity**.

Do not copy the joke. Copy the **function**: a detail that exists because people are people.

---

## FINAL VERIFICATION PASS

After applying all revision agents (**01 → 05**), perform this pass.

**The goal is no longer to improve the chapter.**

**The goal is to determine whether the chapter feels lived rather than designed.**

Ask:

* Does every detail exist for a reason?
* If so, are there any details that exist simply because people are people?
* Does every conversation advance the story?
* If so, is there room for ordinary human interaction?
* Does every observation reinforce theme?
* If so, what observations would this character notice that have **nothing** to do with the theme?

---

## DO NOT

* Add filler
* Add randomness for its own sake
* Undo **01** density wins or **03** consequence chains
* Insert theme-shaped "human" beats (fake quirk that proves a point)
* Pad word count — this pass is **surgical**, not expansive

---

## DO

Add only the kinds of details, reactions, interruptions, frustrations, jokes, and mundane concerns that **real people** would carry through the scene:

* someone changes subject because they are uncomfortable
* a petty gripe about food, sleep, smell, or another person's habit
* an awkward silence that does not resolve
* a joke that lands wrong or goes unexplained
* a distraction that pulls attention for a beat, then releases
* an observation from occupation that has nothing to do with the bond or the curse

**Light touch:** one or two beats per chapter is often enough. Some chapters already have sufficient mess; mark them **no change**.

---

## RED FLAGS

Flag chapters where:

* every line serves plot, theme, or world pressure
* no character does anything petty, distracted, or socially clumsy
* dialogue is efficient debate with no false starts
* environments contain only story-relevant objects
* humor, embarrassment, or irritation are entirely absent

---

## SUCCESS CRITERIA

After revision:

* At least one beat per chapter (where warranted) exists **only** because people are people
* Efficiency remains — no return to over-proof or filler
* Voice stays character-owned (**04** observation ownership still applies)
* Reader remembers a **human moment**, not just a consequence

---

## OUTPUT FORMAT

### Efficiency Audit

Brief note: is the chapter over-engineered? (Low / Medium / High)

### Missing Humanity

Specific scenes or beats where ordinary human texture is absent.

### Recommended Adds

Small, targeted inserts — not new subplots. Line-level or beat-level only.

### Preserved Human Beats

Details already doing this work (do not touch).

### Humanity Score

* **Before:** 1–10 (10 = feels fully lived)
* **After:** 1–10

When executing (not just diagnosing), deliver **minimal targeted edits** — rarely a full chapter rewrite.
