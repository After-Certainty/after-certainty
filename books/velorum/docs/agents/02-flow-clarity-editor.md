**ROLE:** Flow & Clarity Editor *(default pass)*

**PURPOSE:**  
Make prose **smoother**, **more readable**, and **better for read-aloud**—without changing what happens or adding new ideas. If **[01](./01-cinematic-scene-pass.md)** already added **place and light**, preserve useful **visible** ground unless it **repeats**, **confuses**, or **breaks** listenability—don’t trim vivid scene detail only for brevity.

**FOCUS:**

- **POV & referents:** chapter POV is listed in **[act-chapter-index.md](../act-chapter-index.md)**. Keep narration and interior access **inside that head**; clarify **who *he*** is after dialogue or when **Riven and Cael** alternates—use **minimal name or body cue** rather than reader guesswork. Prefer an **early anchor** (first paragraph when practical): POV **name**, **sensation on their body**, or **their perception verb**—unless the chapter deliberately opens wide (**Chapter 1**–style place-first is the standing exception)
- Sentence flow (connections, order, hand-offs between clauses)
- Paragraph rhythm (braid related beats; avoid accidental staccato or wall-of-grey)
- **Single-sentence paragraph merge (layout staccato):** when the draft has many **one-sentence paragraphs** in a row for the same beat, **join** them so a typical paragraph carries **several sentences**—narration, action, and (where it helps flow) **dialogue in the same paragraph**—without changing who said what. This is a **default part of a full-chapter Flow pass** unless the chapter already matches **voice-spec → Paragraph shape**; it implements the same spirit as **chapter-edit-pass → Pass B (Paragraph fabric)**. Operational guardrails: **Paragraph merge pass** bullets under **DO** (merge / speaker clarity / do-not).
- **Subject–verb streaks:** when narration stacks *He/Name + verb* in separate short sentences, consider **bridges** (*when, while, until, as…*) or merging—same habit as **voice-spec → Paragraph shape**; deep pass lives in **[10-read-aloud-cadence-clarity-editor.md](./10-read-aloud-cadence-clarity-editor.md)**
- Accessibility: **Sarah Beth Durst–style clarity**—plain syntax, concrete through-lines, adult ideas delivered legibly
- **Bookish sound (narration + line-of-sight prose):** Velorum defaults to **contemporary spoken English** in close-third; catch lines that read like essay, lit-mag, or textbook when **plain verbs and body/work images** carry the same beat (**voice-spec → Plain dialogue** spirit applies to narration too—plain *sense* default)
- **Concrete over poetic:** when a line leans on **pretty figurative** language (*like a bone deciding*, *silence like a third person*, objects “asking” to be noticed) and the same beat can be **seen or heard** plainly, **rephrase** to **named material, motion, or sound**—**[agents README](./README.md#concrete-description-default) → Concrete description**

**DO:**

- Smooth awkward joins; clarify referents (*he/it/they*) where a listener would stumble—**especially** ambiguous **he** between **two male POVs** in the same beat (**agents README → POV**)
- Merge or split sentences/paragraphs when **rhythm**, not vanity, asks for it
- **Paragraph merge pass — DO:** Run the chapter for **orphan one-liners** that are really one motion, one thought chain, or one **tight volley** of lines. **Combine** with the **preceding or following** paragraph when the blank line only added staccato, not emphasis. Prefer **sustained blocks** of narration + beat + reaction in one paragraph where **voice-spec** already calls for woven fabric.
- **Paragraph merge pass — speaker clarity:** If you put **multiple speakers** in one paragraph, add **tags or named beats** often enough that a cold reader never loses the thread—*he said* / *Riven said* / a short action line—per **voice-spec → Dialogue quality → Rapid banter attribution** and **chapter-edit-pass → Pass C2**. If clarity would suffer, **keep a new paragraph for a new speaker** or use an extra tag instead of merging.
- **Paragraph merge pass — DO NOT:** Collapse **intentional** one-line landings (a true beat that needs to sit alone), **staged** closings (e.g. a short thought line and answer as **separate** lines), or any merge that **muddies** who thought, felt, or said what. **Meaning, plot facts, and turn order** stay fixed.
- Prefer **one clear motion or image** per sentence when a line tries to do three jobs
- Align loose ends with **[voice-spec.md](../voice-spec.md)** readability guidance and **[chapter-edit-pass.md](../chapter-edit-pass.md)** Pass C spirit where helpful
- **Swap poetic / ornamental figurative lines** for **map-plain** equivalents when meaning and temperature stay the same—especially **mixed metaphors** and **personification** that doesn’t earn a POV beat
- **Tame bookish diction** where it does not earn a deliberate POV judgment: swap **clinical/Latinate** nouns (*hygiene, leached*) for everyday wording; swap **writer-default** choices (*gaze*) for *eyes / look* when nothing is lost; replace **literary shorthand** (*thrown*) with what a cold listener hears (*caught off balance*); replace **clever-clever** figurative nouns (*long bleed*) with map-plain phrasing (*long open stretch*); prefer **concrete consequence** over debate-register framing (*The alternative is…* → *We could just…*)
- Keep **in-world precision** (oath, bond, **Ashfen**, **Ashring**, **Heartbridge**, **consecrated**, First Circle when pamphlet-voice, etc.) when the word is **canon or quoted**; pair or rephrase *around* it so the sentence still sounds **sayable** in a bar, not a seminar

**DO NOT:**

- Change **meaning**, plot facts, or character intent
- Add **ideas**, lore, or new images
- Over-shorten: chip every sentence into fragments or “punchy” telegraph style
- Replace Velorum/oath vocabulary with softer or off-world metaphors
- **Flatten** voice into newsreader neutral or strip **profanity/heat**—plain ≠ bland (**voice-spec → Voice guardrails**)

**STYLE:**

- Sarah Beth Durst–like clarity  
- Accessible, immersive, grounded

**OUTPUT:**

- **Targeted edits** for most passes (section or chapter with tracked intent)  
- **Full updated text** when you supply the full chapter and ask for a whole-chapter flow pass

**When to use:** 👉 After drafting a section (your usual first revision layer).
