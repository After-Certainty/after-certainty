## What Act IV and the Act I craft template share (storytelling)

These are **movable bones** for Acts II, III, and V—not a plot template.

### 1. Consequences before mechanics

Chapters are about **what people do after something lands**, not about explaining magic.

- **Bad:** telepathy proof loops (*I heard it* → *You heard it?* → *I really heard it*).  
- **Good:** **It happened → they react → the story moves forward.**

Bond beats read as **boundary violation** (private thought exposed, relief caught, shame shared)—not **resonance lectures**.

### 2. Emotional target: adaptation, not novelty

The unsettling beat is not *oh no, magic happened* but *oh no, we're already adapting*.

**Exemplar:** ch. 6 — *Used to it. That was almost worse.* (Heartbridge thought; Riven not startled anymore.)

### 3. Two-level dialogue

Surface argument and underneath argument differ.

| Surface | Underneath |
|---------|------------|
| Are you okay? | Did you secretly want this? |
| Is the curse real? | Was what we wanted worth the cost? |
| We walk. | I'm scared / Maybe. |

**Thematic spine on the page:** *Was the thing we wanted worth the cost?* — not cosmology debate.

### 4. Four-movement chapter shape *(when the beat supports it)*

**Chapter 6** is the clearest template:

1. **Realization** — nail, relief violation, memory bleed  
2. **Experimentation** — hand test, synced movement, cabbage  
3. **Argument** — curse, meaning, Greyhaven, cost (*We wanted something that mattered* / *We didn't ask for this*)  
4. **Acceptance** — campfire *Maybe*; *I'm still me* / *Me too*; *We hold* / *Yeah*

Not every chapter needs all four; use as **diagnostic** when a draft feels repetitive or front-loaded.

### 5. Verification discipline

Keep **one** shock proof (nail), **one** human test (cabbage), **one** character-cost beat (glad/scared, *Maybe*, belt line in Act IV). **Compress** middle proofs that repeat the same information (old ch. 6 bridge telepathy exchange → one carpenter beat + adaptation horror).

### 6. Pressure valves

Exhausted friends need **absurd or funny** beats under load (cabbage; *Fair* after *Fuck you*). Without them, panic stacks read monotonous and AI-smooth.

### 7. Prose shape

- **Woven paragraphs** default; **earned isolates** rare (*The world came back wrong.*, *It's real.*, *We hold.*).  
- **Re-braid staccato** after snippet merges—dialogue on own lines; narration sustained.  
- **Cut thesis closers** when the scene already demonstrated the beat (*road only seemed to know one answer*, retrospective Heartbridge summaries, quote-ready aphorisms).  
- **Direct camera + plain dialogue** per **voice-spec**; profanity **restored** in dialogue under stress.

### 8. Character (revision insight)

**Cael** is not only *competent guy who wants answers*—he is someone who **wanted meaning badly enough to walk into danger**. Riven catches him **hoping** (*At least it makes sense*, *It's real*, cost argument). That conflict drives Act I ch. 5–6 and should persist in Halverin-era rationalization (Acts II–III) and surrender (Act V).

### 9. Bond on the page (shared burden)

Per **voice-spec → Shared burden guardrails**:

- Fatigue, pain echo, panic spread, emotion leak, anticipatory wrongness  
- **Not:** mirror puppeting, hivemind, permanent lockstep as aesthetic  
- Synced movement is **horror**, not cool fight choreography—cut or shorten after first landing

**Rule of thumb:** If a scene proves the same bond fact twice, **cut one proof** and **deepen consequence** instead.

### Craft swaps (Acts II–III pipeline → Act I bar)

| Pipeline habit | Act I / IV bar |
|----------------|----------------|
| Repeated bond-exposition dialogue | One proof + **violation / cost** |
| Synced combat as power fantasy | **Redistributed load** with **intrusion** and **aftermath cost** |
| Thesis closing paragraphs | **Concrete last image** or **two-word bond beat** (*We hold*) |
| Abstract Halverin / alignment speeches | **Smooth menace** in **short transactional lines** |
| Long verification chains | **Funny or ugly** one-beat confirmation |

### Workflow (proven on Act I and IV)

1. User **prose snippets** (or scene-level rewrite brief)  
2. Merge + **re-braid staccato** into sustained paragraphs  
3. **Feedback pass** — cut repetition, thesis lines, duplicate proofs  
4. **Word-count / POV** gate; update progress doc  
5. Commit → draft PR  

Agents **03, 13, C2, F, H** still apply; **skip** expansion agents (01, 11) unless a chapter is **under** ~1,200 words and missing a mandatory beat.
