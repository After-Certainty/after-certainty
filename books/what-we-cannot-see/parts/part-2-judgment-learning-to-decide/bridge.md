# Part II — Judgment: Learning to Decide

> What is judgment?

Part I traced how finite perspectives are **built**—through attention, memory, interpretation, trust, and the structures that carry sight to scale. Each layer solved a real problem before it left a remainder. None of that work paused the world. Leases expire. Tumors grow. Votes are called. **Judgment** is what happens when partial sight must become a decision someone can answer for.

This movement is not a debiasing manual. It does not ask you to erase your vantage point and decide from nowhere. It asks what responsible judgment looks like when nowhere does not exist—when certainty is unavailable, when others bear costs your default view may never include, when waiting is also a decision and the world keeps moving anyway.

Part I asked why we see differently. Part II asks what we owe one another when we do.

The chapters stay close to ordinary pressure: the clinician who cannot offer neutral certainty, the emergency manager who must act inside the fog, the committee learning that good faith was not the whole of responsibility. The goal is not to excuse harm. The goal is to make **answerability** visible under finitude.

> What is judgment?

That is where this movement begins.
