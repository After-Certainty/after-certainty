# What We Cannot See — Integration Framework

Part III concepts. Planning document for architectural consistency before prose.

**Part III subject:** Integration **and** designed epistemologies—how societies organize knowing when individuals cannot see everything. See [`process-design.md`](process-design.md) for the four-level model and process examples.

## Core distinction

**Integration is not compromise.**

Integration is constructing a larger model that explains why multiple perspectives each noticed something important.

**Process design is not debiasing people.**

Process design integrates partial perspectives and creates feedback that reduces unchecked dominance of any one view—without assuming unbiased individuals exist.

**Integration is not collaboration.**

Collaboration is people working together. **Integration** is perspectives becoming something none of them could produce alone—collective wisdom, institutions as epistemic technologies. Part III must reveal something surprising here, not “collaboration is good.”

**Early foreshadow (locked):** Intro names *integrating* and *civilization exists because no one perspective was sufficient* before Part III prose.

## Culminating question

Given that individuals have finite perspectives, how should we organize ourselves so that we can know more together than we can alone?

## Key questions (Part III)

- Why do other people keep seeing things I miss?
- How can incomplete perspectives produce better understanding?
- How do institutions preserve both wisdom and blind spots?
- How do designed processes improve collective judgment without eliminating individual bias?

## Concept map

| Concept | Is | Is not |
|---------|-----|--------|
| Integration | Constructing a larger model that explains why multiple partial views each noticed something real | Compromise, averaging opinions, false balance |
| Process design | Deliberate structures that integrate perspectives and create error correction | Debiasing training for individuals |
| Collaboration | One integration venue | The whole of integration or process design |
| Science | Designed epistemology: replication, peer review, error correction across partial observers | Neutral view from nowhere |
| Engineering / medicine | Review, failure analysis, safety reporting across partial observers | Perfect safety through single expert |
| Democracy | Institutional integration under disagreement; checks and balances | Majority rule as truth |
| Organizational learning | Preserving and updating collective partial sight through feedback | Best-practice copying without context |
| AI | Another partial perspective to integrate | Unbiased oracle or inevitable harm |

## Chapter moves

### Ch 9 — Why Others See What I Miss (Collaboration)

**Level:** Collective integration.

Integration begins when we recognize others notice what our partial perspective obscures.

**Relation to portfolio:** Distinct from *Why Collaboration Is So Hard* (coordination fragility). WWCS focuses epistemic model-building and partial sight.

### Ch 10 — The Method of Many Eyes (Science)

**Level:** Institutional process design.

**Author note:** Open with the Part I payoff—centuries of deliberate compensation for finite perspective—before science-as-designed-epistemology. Land: humans did not wait for unbiased minds; they built methods that integrate partial observers.

Science as designed epistemology: replication, peer review, adversarial testing—not unbiased individuals.

**Process examples:** replication, peer review, randomized trials, double blinding, adversarial collaboration.

### Ch 11 — When Failure Teaches the System (Engineering / medicine)

**Level:** Institutional process design.

Paired case studies: design review, postmortems, morbidity conferences, aviation checklists, safety reporting.

### Ch 12 — Institutions That Remember and Forget (Democracy / org learning)

**Level:** Institutional process design.

Democratic checks and balances, appeals, organizational memory—and how institutions accumulate blind spots.

**Process examples:** legal appeals, separation of powers, periodic reassessment, learning loops.

### Ch 13 — Another Kind of Partial Perspective (AI)

**Level:** Institutional process design + integration.

Models as partial perspectives: training data, objective functions, human integration of machine outputs.

**Ch 13 guardrail (author feedback — locked):** AI is not “LLMs are biased.” AI is **another finite perspective** that inherits different adaptations (attention, memory, interpretation, trust, structure)—same architecture as human partial sight, different substrate. Apply the book’s full stack; do not collapse into debiasing handbook.

## Integration and process mechanisms (cross-chapter)

- Disagreement as signal, not obstacle
- Method and review that survive partial observers
- Failure as teacher when **systems** learn
- Feedback loops that return outcomes as information
- Institutions as memory, forgetting, and designed correction
- Human judgment over machine partiality
- Distributed cognition across roles, artifacts, and time

## What Part III requires

- Willingness to explain why each partial view noticed something real
- Models large enough to hold contradiction without false equivalence
- Processes that update, not merely accumulate
- Individual judgment that participates in collective sight
- Clear distinction: improve **process**, not pretend to fix **people**
