# What We Cannot See — Writing Style

## Primary rhythm (locked for this book)

The introduction established a movement closer to **Atul Gawande at his best** than to Solnit— not in prose texture, but in structure:

Observation → Question → Pattern → Reframing → Implication

Less essayistic wandering; more readable diagnostic progression. Walk beside the reader; do not lead through manifesto.

Rebecca Solnit remains a reference for observational curiosity and indirect discovery—borrow the spirit, not the syntax.

## Secondary influences (what to learn, not copy)

| Author | Borrow |
|--------|--------|
| Hannah Arendt | Judgment under plurality; thinking without banisters |
| James C. Scott | How structures shape what becomes visible |
| Atul Gawande | Cases that illuminate mechanism without becoming anecdotal |
| John McPhee | Structural clarity across domains |
| Gary Klein | Recognition-primed judgment; expertise as partial sight |

## Voice

Curious. Thoughtful. Restrained.

Diagnostic, not prescriptive. Observational, not preachy. Clear, not academic.

## Anti-patterns

- Preaching or manifesto voice
- Mocking readers or subjects
- Heroes and villains framing
- Culture-war fuel
- Management-blog tone
- Debiasing handbook register
- Named bias encyclopedia
- Tech-bashing in Structural/Technological chapters

## Prose habits

- Short sentences by default; one clear claim per sentence unless parallelism is deliberate
- Concrete verbs over abstract noun chains
- Ground observable behavior before naming concepts
- Default 2–4 sentences per paragraph; reserve single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis
- Subheads name the movement of thought, not outline labels

## Opening scenes

Use history, science, engineering, medicine, psychology, nature, everyday life.

Do not define concepts immediately. Allow readers to notice the pattern before naming it.

## Chapter rhythm

See [`chapter-template.md`](chapter-template.md). Every chapter is one continuous inquiry.

## Intellectual honesty

Optimize for intellectual honesty over rhetorical certainty.

Proportion claims to evidence. Mark unverified sources. Never fabricate references.
