## Movement map

- **African National Congress (ANC)** — underground, exile, and negotiation wings
- **Umkhonto we Sizwe (MK)** — armed wing (1961–); Rivonia defendants
- **National Party government** — de Klerk; CODESA negotiations (1991–93)
- **Inkatha Freedom Party (IFP)** — Zulu nationalist; violence in Natal/transvaal
- **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** — Tutu chair; amnesty-for-truth framework
- **COSATU / UDF** — mass democratic movement in 1980s
- **Springboks / rugby establishment** — 1995 World Cup as nation-building symbol

## Controversy map

- **MK armed struggle** — sabotage and violence vs moral iconography of non-racial democracy
- **TRC limits** — amnesty without prosecution; economic apartheid largely untouched
- **Persistent inequality** — post-1994 poverty, land, service delivery gaps undermine “rainbow nation”
- **Mandela centrality** — negotiators (Ramaphosa, Mbeki, others) and mass struggle minimized in popular narrative
- **1995 rugby symbolism** — praised as bridge; criticized as appeasing white minority sport culture
- **Single term** — restraint admired; succession politics (Mbeki era) later contested

## Chronology strip

- **1960** — Sharpeville; ANC banned
- **1961** — MK founded
- **1962** — Mandela arrested
- **1963–64** — Rivonia Trial; life sentences
- **11 Feb 1990** — Mandela released
- **1991–93** — CODESA negotiations
- **27 Apr 1994** — first democratic elections (~19.7–20M voters)
- **10 May 1994** — Mandela inaugurated president
- **24 Jun 1995** — Rugby World Cup final, Ellis Park
- **1996–98** — TRC public hearings
- **1999** — Mandela leaves office after one term
