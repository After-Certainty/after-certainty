## Movement map

- **Charter 77 signatories** — dissident writers, musicians, workers (~240 initial; rotating spokespeople)
- **Samizdat networks** — underground publishing; foreign broadcast (Radio Free Europe)
- **Theater world** — Havel’s plays; banned performances; cultural resistance
- **Civic Forum (Občanské fórum)** — 1989 umbrella; student and theater strikes
- **Communist Party / normalization regime** — Husák; police, StB surveillance, prison
- **Student leaders (1989)** — Jan Opletal anniversary → November strikes
- **Slovak counterparts** — Public Against Violence; federal transition

## Controversy map

- **Havel as symbol vs collective** — Charter 77 was broad; Velvet Revolution multi-actor (students, workers, Forum)
- **Greengrocer simplification** — essay is allegory; lived dissent included imprisonment, factory work, splits
- **Post-1989 governance** — economic shock therapy; nationalism; disillusion with liberal transition
- **Minority questions** — Roma, Slovak separation (1993) less visible in Havel-centric narrative
- **Art vs politics** — intellectual leadership distanced from industrial workers’ daily grievances
- **Nostalgia for normalization** — later polls on “order” vs freedom complicate triumph narrative

## Chronology strip

- **1968** — Prague Spring crushed; normalization begins
- **1975** — Havel open letter to Husák
- **Jan 1977** — Charter 77 published
- **Oct 1978** — “Power of the Powerless” circulated (samizdat)
- **1979–83** — Havel imprisoned (among multiple arrests)
- **17 Nov 1989** — Police beat student demo; strike spreads
- **24–29 Nov 1989** — General strike; CP resigns leadership
- **29 Dec 1989** — Havel elected president of Czechoslovakia
- **1993** — Velvet Divorce; Havel president of Czech Republic until 2003
