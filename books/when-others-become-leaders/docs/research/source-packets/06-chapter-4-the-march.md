## Movement map

- **Montgomery Improvement Association (MIA)** — King president; Jo Ann Robinson/WPC groundwork
- **Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC)** — King, Shuttlesworth, Abernathy
- **Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC)** — Baker mentor; sit-ins, Freedom Rides, SNCC field staff
- **Local hubs** — Birmingham ACMHR; Selma DCVL; Memphis AFSCME Local 1733
- **Coalition planners** — Randolph, Rustin, Wilkins (NAACP), Young (Urban League), Lewis (SNCC)
- **Black church networks** — mass meetings, fundraising, transportation
- **Federal actors** — Kennedy/Johnson administrations; FBI surveillance (context)

## Controversy map

- **King-centric narrative** — Robinson, Baker, Bevel, local women organizers precede and surround him
- **Nonviolence tactics** — children’s marches in Birmingham (strategic debate); white liberal discomfort
- **“I Have a Dream” vs radical late King** — Vietnam, poverty, structural critique underplayed in popular memory
- **FBI COINTELPRO** — harassment of King; moral authority vs personal conduct allegations
- **Gender in movement** — women’s labor often uncredited (Robinson, Baker, Nash, Height)
- **March compromises** — Rustin’s sexuality marginalized; speech revisions; limited legislative follow-through

## Chronology strip

- **1 Dec 1955** — Rosa Parks arrest
- **5 Dec 1955** — Boycott begins; King elected MIA head
- **1956** — Browder v. Gayle; boycott ends
- **1960** — SNCC founded, Raleigh/Shaw
- **1963 (Apr–May)** — Birmingham campaign; “Letter from Birmingham Jail”
- **28 Aug 1963** — March on Washington
- **7 Mar 1965** — Bloody Sunday, Selma
- **1967** — “Beyond Vietnam” (Riverside Church)
- **12 Feb–Apr 1968** — Memphis sanitation strike
- **4 Apr 1968** — King assassinated; strike settled Apr 16
