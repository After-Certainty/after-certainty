# Part III — How Influence Endures

The first two parts traced how influence begins and moves. This part turns to endurance: what keeps producing agency after the originating leader is absent or no longer central.

A house can outlast its founder if guests become stewards. A neighborhood can persist on a screen when attention becomes teachable. A bracelet can circulate when a culture no longer waits for instruction from the stage. None of these forms announces a program for succession. Each reveals how places, relationships, and cultural practices carry influence forward when the person who first gathered attention can no longer occupy the center.
