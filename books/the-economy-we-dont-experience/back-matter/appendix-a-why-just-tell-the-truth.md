# **Appendix A**

## **Why "Just Tell the Truth" Isn't a Strategy**

After credibility crises, a moral reflex appears: leaders should **just tell
the truth**. More transparency. More facts. Less spin. The reflex is
understandable. It is also incomplete.

Truth is necessary. It is not sufficient under compression and uneven
experience.

> Truth does not execute itself under conditions of compression and uneven
> experience.

### **1. Truthful explanations that still fail**

Explanations can be **truthful and fail** because audiences hear them through
trust filters built by prior mismatch. A correct inflation report does not
automatically repair the memory of prior forecasts that felt wrong in the
kitchen.

Failure is not always lying. It is often **channel mismatch**.

### **2. High-dimensional truth, low-dimensional speech**

Economic truth is **high-dimensional**. Public speech is **low-dimensional**.
Something must be omitted. Omission feels like deception to those living in
omitted dimensions—even when speakers believe they are being responsible.

### **3. Lived experience defeating abstraction**

**Lived experience** routinely defeats abstraction in selection of belief,
not because people cannot understand charts, but because experience carries
moral immediacy charts lack.

Telling the truth without naming whose experience is centered can sound like
talking past the hurt.

### **4. Truth sounding dismissive**

Under compression, truth can sound **dismissive**: "Actually, wages are up,"
when the listener's rent rose faster. Technically true sentences become
social insults when they appear to deny felt reality.

### **5. More information, more distrust**

**More information** can increase distrust when people believe information
systems are gamed: revised definitions, selective windows, expert class
capture. Transparency without credibility repair can feel like more noise.

### **6. Moral comfort of the slogan**

"Just tell the truth" offers **moral comfort** to elites and critics alike:
a simple virtue in a messy environment. Simplicity is attractive. It avoids
structural questions about compression, pain scaling, and resonance.

### **7. Legitimacy as interpretive**

**Legitimacy is interpretive**, not merely factual. Institutions are trusted
when their summaries are heard as seeing people, not only measuring them.

A strategy—not a slogan—would pair truthful claims with:

- explicit distributional acknowledgment,
- restraint from confidence theater,
- memory of guardrails linked to felt stakes,
- and channels that reward repair when summaries miss.

Without that pairing, truth remains necessary and still not enough.
