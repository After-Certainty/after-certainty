---
title: "Miss Maybe & Mister Sure"
slug: miss-maybe-mister-sure
languages: ["en"]
---

[Intro — both, spoken over banjo and brass]


[Mister Sure]
Now, darling, there are certain things a gentleman simply knows.


[Miss Maybe]
Is that so?


[Mister Sure]
Absolutely.


[Miss Maybe]
Oh, this oughta be good.


[Verse 1]


[Mister Sure]


I know the moon comes up at night,
I know a wrong man from a right,
I know a wink means come on through,
And honey, I know what you meant too.


[Miss Maybe]


You know the moon, you know the stars,
You know the saints from corner bars,
You know my mind from one small clue—


[Mister Sure]


Precisely, dear.


[Miss Maybe]


Well, bless your shoe.


[Chorus — alternating]


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe yes—


[Mister Sure]
Certainly!


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe no—


[Mister Sure]
Obviously!


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe the truth took off its hat
And slipped out through the kitchen flat.


[Mister Sure]
There’s no great mystery, I assure—


[Miss Maybe]
Says Miss Maybe—


[Mister Sure]
And Mister Sure!


[Verse 2]
[Miss Maybe]


I said, “That coat looks mighty fine,”
You heard, “Come home and make me thine.”
I said, “The evening sure is warm,”
You bought a ring and raised alarm.


[Mister Sure]
A lady’s words are subtle things,
They flutter soft on hidden wings.


[Miss Maybe]
Or maybe words are words, mon cher,
And all those wings came from your chair.


[Brass break]


[Mister Sure, spoken]
You are a difficult woman.


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe.


[Verse 3]
[Mister Sure]


I know when silence means disdain,
I know when laughter hides the pain,
I know when “fine” means “go away”—


[Miss Maybe]
You’ve built yourself a busy day.


[Mister Sure]
You hesitate, therefore you doubt.


[Miss Maybe]
Or maybe I’m just thinking it out.


[Mister Sure]
You see? You prove my point once more.


[Miss Maybe]
What point was that, Professor Sure?


[Chorus — both]


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe yes—


[Mister Sure]
Certainly!


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe no—


[Mister Sure]
Obviously!


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe your facts got dressed too soon
And strutted proudly round the room.


[Mister Sure]
A thing is settled once we’re sure—


[Miss Maybe]
Who’s “we,” exactly, Mister Sure?


[Bridge — slower, sly duet]


[Miss Maybe]
You like the door shut nice and tight.


[Mister Sure]
A gentleman must choose what’s right.


[Miss Maybe]
You like the ending neatly tied.


[Mister Sure]
It keeps the troublesome things outside.


[Miss Maybe]
But every lock forgets a key.


[Mister Sure]
That sounds suspiciously like philosophy.


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe, darling.


[Mister Sure]
...Don’t start.


[Verse 4 — both, trading faster]


[Mister Sure]
You said you’d never fall for me.


[Miss Maybe]
I said, “We’ll have to wait and see.”


[Mister Sure]
That’s practically the same affair.


[Miss Maybe]
Only if you supply half there.


[Mister Sure]
You kissed me once behind the band!


[Miss Maybe]
A fact at last! Now ain’t that grand?


[Mister Sure]
So you admit—


[Miss Maybe]
I kissed you, sure.


[Mister Sure]
Aha!


[Miss Maybe]
Don’t get excited, Mister Sure.


[Final Chorus — full band]


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe yes—


[Mister Sure]
Most certainly!


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe no—


[Mister Sure]
Now wait and see—


[Miss Maybe]
Well look at you, you learned a phrase!


[Mister Sure]
Don’t make a habit of your praise.


[Both]
The world keeps spinning, that’s for sure,
But what it means—


[Miss Maybe]
Well, maybe—


[Mister Sure]
...I’m not sure.


[Outro — music stops]


[Miss Maybe]
Did you just say you’re not sure?


[Mister Sure]
I most certainly did not.


[Miss Maybe]
Maybe I heard you wrong.


[Mister Sure]
Exactly.


[Miss Maybe]
Oh, hush.


[brass sting]
