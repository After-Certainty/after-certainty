---
title: "Mr. Bell Keeps the Weather"
slug: mr-bell-keeps-the-weather
languages: ["en"]
---

[Verse 1]
Mr. Bell lives over the laundromat
where the dryers shake the floor
Keeps seventeen broken barometers
nailed above his door


There’s a photograph of Cleveland
there’s a tooth inside a jar
there’s a postcard from a woman
who never said where they are


Every night at half past eleven
someone asks him, “Think it’ll rain?”


He looks up like the sky owes him money
and says,
“Could go either way.”


[Verse 2]
He used to read the weather
on Channel Number Nine
Back when men wore narrow neckties
and the forecast had a sign


They gave him forty-seven seconds
to explain the shape of wind


He said, “There’s something coming westward.”
They said, “Just tell the people when.”


So Bell pointed at the blue part
and promised them the storm


And somewhere fifty miles north
a little girl was born
beneath a sky
that never changed at all.


[Chorus]
Oh, Mr. Bell keeps the weather
in boxes by the stairs
Thunder in the dresser drawer
fog tangled in the chairs


He says,
“The sky don’t break a promise, son.
The promise wasn’t his.”


Then he listens to the pipes knock twice


like they know something
we missed.


[Verse 3]
There’s a notebook in his kitchen
bound in peeling leather skin
called Weather That Did Not Occur
with the years all written in


June the third: no tornado
August twelve: no hail
November twenty-seven:
wind predicted, wind failed


I asked him why he kept them,
all those storms that never came


He said,
“Everybody keeps what happened.


Somebody ought to keep
what almost did.”


[Piano Interlude]
[Slow, enormous piano chords; bowed bass; the room itself seems to hum]


[Verse 4]
Last winter Bell went missing
around the first hard freeze
His landlord found the windows open
and snow across the keys


The television whispered
to nobody in the room:


Ninety percent chance of snowfall
continuing through noon.


His coffee cup was empty
His old brown coat was gone


Every barometer pointed somewhere
different before dawn


And taped across the kitchen wall
in letters black and small:


DON’T CONFUSE THE ODDS
WITH WHAT HAPPENS
AFTER ALL.


[Final Chorus]
Now Mr. Bell keeps the weather
somewhere out of town


Maybe underneath the river
Maybe six feet underground


And when somebody tells me
they know exactly what comes next


I hear that old piano upstairs
and Bell clearing his chest:


“The sky don’t owe you certainty.


It barely owes you rain.”


[Outro]
Could go either way.


Could go either way.


Could—


[piano chord]


—go either way.
