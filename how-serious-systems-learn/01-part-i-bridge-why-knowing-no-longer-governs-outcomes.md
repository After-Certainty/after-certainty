# Part I Bridge: Why Knowing No Longer Governs Outcomes

Part I establishes the book's central diagnosis: modern systems can become
more informed while becoming less corrigible. As knowledge improves,
confidence scales, commitments harden, and corrective loops weaken under
pressure.

These chapters do not argue against intelligence, data, or planning.
They show why those tools no longer guarantee responsible action when
reversal becomes socially and structurally expensive.

The argument here is foundational rather than prescriptive. By the end of
Part I, the reader should see why post-certainty systems need disciplines
designed not for permanent correctness, but for survivable error and
timely correction.
