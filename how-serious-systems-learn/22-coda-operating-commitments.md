# Coda: Operating Commitments

After certainty, commitment must shift from claiming control to preserving
corrigibility. That shift is less a single decision than a recurring
discipline: keeping systems revisable when pressure rewards closure,
keeping authority interruptible when momentum rewards certainty, and
keeping consequence visible when abstraction rewards speed.

What this book calls seriousness can be held as a small set of operating
commitments organized around action, authority, narrative, and
responsibility.

Action must stay reversible as commitment deepens. Confidence claims need
explicit revision conditions, and interventions should remain bounded
enough that error is discoverable before it becomes irreversible harm.
When systems lose this reversibility, they usually replace learning with
defense.

Authority must remain correctable in practice, not only in principle.
Dissent has to change decisions rather than simply be heard. Slack must
be preserved where interruption is necessary, and success must not become
permission to harden power against feedback.

Narrative must remain provisional. Coherence is useful for coordination,
but dangerous when treated as sovereignty over evidence. Methods should
be re-tested against changing conditions before they harden into doctrine,
and local wins should never stand in for full consequence accounting
across system boundaries.

Responsibility must remain continuous. Prefer bounded error now over
concentrated failure later. Re-enter judgment repeatedly rather than
outsourcing it to procedure, precedent, or momentum. The point is not to
avoid uncertainty, but to keep accountability alive inside it.

None of these commitments eliminates uncertainty. They do something more
necessary: they preserve responsibility under uncertainty. They make it
possible to act without illusion, revise without collapse, and continue
without pretending that continuation is the same as control.
