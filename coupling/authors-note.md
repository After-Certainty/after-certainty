# Author's Note

This book began as a practical question from lived work: what happens when decisions and consequences stop touching the same people.

I chose a cross-domain frame because the same failure pattern appears under different names in software, organizations, and institutions. Terms change. Incentives and abstractions change. But the recurring shape is familiar: ownership diffuses, feedback delays, and responsibility thins under scale.

This manuscript is written as an inquiry, not a manifesto. It is meant to be argued with, improved, and stress-tested.

Where claims are strong, they should be grounded. Where limits appear, they should be named directly.

If this work is useful, it will leave the reader with better questions, cleaner distinctions, and a more serious standard for institutional and technical design.

The preface that follows explains how the argument is organized and how the sections build on one another.

## About the Author

Kevin Steffensen has worked in the software industry for more than 20 years in a range of leadership roles.

He is also the author of the *After Certainty* series, including *When Authority Outlives Accountability* and *How Meaning Moves*. Across that body of work, the core books offer lenses for evaluating moral leadership and communication under constraint.

His writing synthesizes philosophy, psychology, ethics, and civics, using analogies from systems thinking, software methodology, and software architecture to clarify recurring patterns in complex subject matter.

His current focus in *Coupling* is tracing how authority, ownership, and consequence become misaligned across technical and institutional systems, and how design can restore alignment.
