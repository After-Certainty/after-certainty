# Acknowledgements

This book was shaped through conversation, challenge, and quiet
encouragement over a long period of time. I am grateful to the people
who read early drafts, tested ideas, asked difficult questions, or
simply took the work seriously when it was still unsettled.

I want to thank **Jeremy Smith**, **Dee Grey**, **Alistair Cockburn**,
**Anderson Shen**, **Herb Collins**, and **Tyler Chapman** for their
time, curiosity, and thoughtful engagement. Each contributed in
different ways—through conversation, feedback, or example—and helped
sharpen what this book is trying to say by refusing easy agreement.

I am especially grateful to my wife, **Brittany**, whose patience,
honesty, and steadiness made the writing of this book possible. Her
presence shaped not only the conditions under which the work was
written, but the moral seriousness with which I approached it. This book
is better—and more careful—because of her.

I also want to acknowledge my parents, whose presence—both steady and
complicated—shaped how I came to understand responsibility long before I
could articulate it.

Finally, I want to acknowledge my children. Much of what this book is
concerned with—harm, responsibility, restraint, and what is passed
forward—has become clearer to me through the experience of caring for
them. I choose not to name them here, out of respect for their privacy,
but they remain central to why this work matters to me at all.
