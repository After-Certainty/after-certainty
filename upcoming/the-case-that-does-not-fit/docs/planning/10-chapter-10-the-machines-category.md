## Chapter title

Chapter 10 — The Machine’s Category

## Governing question

How can automated classification remain correctable?

## Ordinary opening image

A computer screen displaying a classification, confidence score, and recommended action.

## Case / terrain

AI systems will increasingly classify applications, claims, risks, misconduct, eligibility, performance, and threat. They may process cases more consistently and quickly than human workers. They may also make the initial category feel more objective, less visibly authored, and more difficult to challenge. The enduring problem is institutional uncorrectability.

## Conceptual movement

Distinguish this book from No Time to Think: focus on architecture of reconsideration (explainability, contradictability, real human authority), not temporal conditions of judgment.

## Questions to preserve

- Who can explain why the system classified the case as it did?
- What evidence can contradict or overturn the classification?
- Can affected people correct the data and assumptions behind the decision?
- Does faster classification come with proportional review capacity?
- When should a person be entitled to human reconsideration?
- Does the human reviewer possess real authority, or merely approve the machine’s recommendation?
- Can the system preserve uncertainty rather than converting it into a false binary?
