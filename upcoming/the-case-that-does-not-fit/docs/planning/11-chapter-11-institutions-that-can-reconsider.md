## Chapter title

Chapter 11 — Institutions That Can Reconsider

## Governing question

How can institutions preserve both protection from arbitrary judgment and protection from mistaken categories?

## Ordinary opening image

A flowchart containing a visible path labeled “Review.”

## Case / terrain

Lessons to develop: clear rules; visible discretion; independent review; comprehensible reasons; reachable appeals; bounded exceptions; due-process guardrails for local judgment; consequence visibility; matched automated decision and reconsideration capacity; revision as competence.

## Conceptual movement

Every safeguard has a shadow. Documentation can become burden. Review can become delay. Discretion can become bias. Consistency can become blindness. The design problem is to preserve both protections at once.

## Questions to preserve

- How can review remain independent without becoming endless delay?
- How can exceptions identify purpose, boundaries, evidence, and review conditions?
- How should automated decision capacity be matched by reconsideration capacity?

## Return image

The flowchart does not promise that the first decision will be correct. It promises that the first decision is not the last place reality may enter.

## Central tension

Preserve both protections: from arbitrary judgment and from a rule that misunderstood the person.
