## Chapter title

Introduction — The Blank Line

## Governing question

When does formal access become fictional access?

## Ordinary opening image

The line marked “Other” on an application form.

## Case / terrain

On July 31, 2026, a federal contract supporting legal services for unaccompanied migrant children expired. Reporting indicated that approximately 20,000 children could lose access to legal orientation or representation through the affected network. The immigration system still contained statutes, hearings, eligibility categories, notices, and possible forms of relief. But many children could no longer rely on someone capable of translating their particular experience into the language the process recognized.

## Conceptual movement

Due process is not merely the existence of a hearing. A person must be able to enter the process, understand what it asks, and present the facts that make the case different from the category initially assigned.

## Questions to preserve

- When does formal access become fictional access?
- Which people are expected to translate themselves into institutional language without help?
- Is representation an exception to the system, or part of what makes the system legitimate?

## Return image

The form is complete. Every box has an answer. Nothing on the page shows whether the institution has understood the person.

## Durability check

The chapter remains valid if legal assistance is restored, replaced, or found unnecessary in some cases. Enduring question: whether a formal procedure is meaningful when the person subject to it cannot understand, enter, or make their particular circumstances legible.
