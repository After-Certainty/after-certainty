# The Game We Think We Saw — Research Plan

This is a structured roadmap for research. It is not full chapter research and does not claim source completeness. **No chapter prose should be drafted from this document alone.**

## Evidence standards

- Prefer primary or institutional sources for specific factual claims.
- Verify memorable anecdotes before using them.
- Distinguish documented facts from interpretation in notes and prose.
- Avoid invented interiority.
- Quote sparingly; paraphrase accurately with source tracking.
- Track controversies and limitations honestly.
- Maintain a claim-support ledger for each chapter’s major factual assertions.
- High-risk chapters require dossiers before any narrative drafting.

## Four evidence layers (required distinction)

For every central case, separate:

### 1. What happened (documented events)

Use:

- official records
- original rulebooks
- governing-body explanations
- game footage
- direct interviews
- transcripts
- official disciplinary records
- court or investigative documents where applicable

### 2. How it was interpreted at the time (contemporary interpretation)

Use:

- contemporaneous reporting
- commentary from multiple viewpoints
- polling where available
- broadcasts
- team and league statements
- sponsor or institutional responses

### 3. How the meaning changed later (retrospective interpretation)

Use:

- serious biographies
- historical scholarship
- later interviews
- retrospective reporting
- institutional changes
- documentaries with appropriate caution

### 4. What the pattern reveals (author analysis)

This is the author’s analysis. It must be clearly distinguishable from the documented factual record.

## Source hierarchy

1. Official game records, rulebooks, league statements, transcripts, investigative and court documents
2. Direct athlete / coach / official interviews and contemporaneous primary statements
3. Reputable contemporaneous reporting and multi-outlet corroboration
4. Peer-reviewed or serious historical scholarship; serious biographies
5. Retrospective journalism and documentaries (usable with dating and caution)
6. Cultural reputation, memory, and secondary commentary without citation trail — **not sufficient** for central claims

## Preliminary research questions

### Cross-book

- Where does finality diverge from completeness in sports discourse?
- Which compressions are necessary for the game to function, and which overreach?
- How do leagues, media, fans, and athletes differently inherit unsettled remainders?

### By chapter (starter list)

- **Ch 1:** Which measures historically created new blind spots while correcting old ones?
- **Ch 2:** When does star concentration enable a system versus conceal its incapacity?
- **Ch 3:** How do clear-and-obvious / review standards settle meaning before settling pixels?
- **Ch 4:** What contemporaneous complaints existed during peak success, and how were they answered?
- **Ch 5:** Where does moral language surrounding toughness leave room for refusal?
- **Ch 6:** How were institutional knowledge and athlete knowledge framed in real-time coverage?
- **Ch 7:** What is the documented sequence from original concern through later canonization?

## High-risk dossier requirements

### Chapter 4 — Bobby Knight

Before drafting:

- Chronology of documented incidents, sources, and outcomes
- Contemporaneous responses from institutions, media, players
- Distinctions among demanding coaching, disputed methods, and documented harm
- Claims ledger with verification status for every factual pivot

Do not rely on cultural reputation or memory.

### Chapter 7 — Colin Kaepernick

Before drafting, build chronology for:

1. Original concern
2. Original form of protest
3. Consultation and change in form
4. Kaepernick’s direct explanation
5. NFL and team responses
6. Media framing
7. Opposing public compressions
8. Supporting public compressions
9. Material consequences
10. Later reinterpretation and partial canonization
