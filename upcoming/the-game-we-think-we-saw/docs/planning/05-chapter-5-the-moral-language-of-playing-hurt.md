## Chapter title

Chapter 5 — The Moral Language of Playing Hurt

## Governing question

What are people persuaded to carry in pursuit of the result?

## Purpose in the book’s progression

**Conceptual role: Institutional incentives and embodied cost.** Trace how institutions translate needed behaviors into virtues athletes are expected to possess.

## Ordinary opening image

Begin before the result: an athlete on a training table, or an injury being wrapped before an important game.

## Central mechanism

Moral translations that convert institutional need into character:

- pain becomes commitment
- concealment becomes courage
- rest becomes selfishness
- caution becomes weakness
- departure becomes betrayal
- bodily sacrifice becomes loyalty

## Primary case or case structure

Cross-sport institutional incentive map rather than one exclusive personality; select 2–3 well-documented systems after research.

## Supporting cases / systems

- concussion culture in football and hockey
- pitch counts and arm injuries
- gymnastics and normalized pain
- load management
- return-to-play pressure
- injury disclosure
- protective versus extractive performance analytics

## Key distinctions

- Freely chosen sacrifice vs coerced virtue language
- Protective analytics vs extractive monitoring
- Toughness as practice vs toughness as insulation from refusal

## Necessary complication or counterexample

Sacrifice is real and may be freely chosen. The argument is not that competitive risk or toughness should disappear. The question is whether the moral language surrounding sacrifice leaves room for meaningful refusal.

## Research questions

- Which institutional statements and medical/return-to-play protocols best document incentive pressure?
- Where has load management been defended as wisdom vs denounced as selfishness?
- Which cases show protective vs extractive use of performance data?

## Evidence risks

Moderate — medical and concussion literature must be dated carefully; avoid pop-culture medical claims and invented locker-room dialogue.

## Possible return image

The athlete says, “I’m good.” Everyone nearby has an interest in believing it.

## Approved compression lines

- An institution becomes morally powerful when it can describe the behavior it needs as the character a good person should possess.
