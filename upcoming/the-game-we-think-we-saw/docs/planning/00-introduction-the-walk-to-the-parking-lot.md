## Chapter title

Introduction — The Walk to the Parking Lot

## Governing question

How can people agree on the result and disagree about the game?

## Purpose in the book’s progression

Establish the book’s central tension before the chapter progression begins: sports appear unusually objective, yet agreement on the score does not settle explanation, legitimacy, cost, or meaning.

## Ordinary opening image

Two spectators walking from the arena toward the parking lot.

## Central mechanism

Shared result → divergent narrative. The scoreboard answers one question; conversation immediately expands into causation, fairness, blame, pride, and memory.

## Primary case or case structure

No single celebrity case required. Use the paired walk as a recurring structural device that can later host briefly named shared moments of a game without becoming a full case study.

## Supporting cases

Deferred. Introduction may preview the chapter progression with light touch only after architecture review; avoid a chapter-by-chapter summary dump.

## Key distinctions

- Result vs explanation
- Finality vs completeness
- Necessary compression vs interpretive overreach
- Agreement on score vs agreement on “the game”

## Necessary complication or counterexample

Sports outcomes are real and consequential. The introduction must not imply that results are arbitrary or that every interpretation is equally valid.

## Research questions

- Which opening-walk details keep the scene ordinary rather than theatrical?
- How much of the progression should be previewed vs discovered in chapters?
- What minimal examples, if any, should appear before Chapter 1?

## Evidence risks

Low factual risk if the scene stays generic. Risk rises if specific games, quotes, or fan demographics are invented or asserted without documentation.

## Possible return image

They reach their cars. The score has not changed. The game already has.

## Approved compression lines

- A result can be final while its cause, legitimacy, cost, and meaning remain unsettled.
- The scoreboard is not wrong. It is complete only about the question it was built to answer.
