## Chapter title

Chapter 4 — What Winning Is Allowed to Excuse

## Governing question

What does the result retroactively justify?

## Purpose in the book’s progression

**Conceptual role: Moral legitimacy.** Show how winning casts a moral halo backward over process, authority, and harm.

## Ordinary opening image

Begin after success: a championship photograph or trophies invoked in response to criticism.

## Central mechanism

Structural sequence:

1. Concerns are visible.
2. Success makes them harder to raise.
3. Critics are told they do not understand excellence.
4. Effectiveness becomes a defense.
5. Harm is reframed as the price of winning.
6. When winning stops, the same conduct becomes newly visible.

## Primary case or case structure

Bobby Knight — **only after** a citation-quality evidence dossier. Do not rely on cultural reputation or memory.

## Supporting cases

- Michael Jordan
- Lance Armstrong as a limited contrast
- successful coaches whose conduct was interpreted differently once victories diminished

## Key distinctions

- Effectiveness vs desert
- Demanding coaching vs unjustified harm
- Contemporaneous concern vs retrospective condemnation

## Necessary complication or counterexample

High standards, conflict, discipline, sacrifice, and demanding coaching are not automatically abusive or unjustified.

## Research questions

- What contemporaneous complaints and institutional responses are documentable at peak success?
- Which incidents have primary or investigative documentation vs lore?
- How to use Armstrong only as limited contrast without collapsing the chapter into doping history?

## Evidence risks

**High.** Central biographical and conduct claims require verified sources, claims ledger, and chronological dossier before any narrative prose. Unsupported “everyone knew” phrasing is prohibited.

## Possible return image

A championship photograph remains on the wall while what happened outside the frame becomes harder to name.

## Approved compression lines

- A victory can prove that a method produced a result. It cannot prove that the method deserved to.
