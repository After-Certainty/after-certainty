# Author’s Note

This is not a book about settling famous sports debates.

The cases that follow are not included because they offer simple verdicts about who was right, who was wrong, or who deserves the most credit. They are here because sports gives us unusually clear outcomes and unusually unsettled meanings.

A score can tell us who won. It cannot fully explain what produced the result, why the result should count, what success is allowed to excuse, what the pursuit of victory asks people to carry, or what an event becomes once it enters public memory.

The book stays inside sports deliberately. It does not pause to translate every pattern into business, politics, or leadership. Those connections may be present, but they are not the subject here.

The subject is the game itself: the scoreboard, the star, the whistle, the injured body, the refusal, the gesture, and the stories that gather around them.

Sports gives us outcomes clear enough to share and complicated enough to keep interpreting.

The chapters that follow begin where the result stops.
