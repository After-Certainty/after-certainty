# What We Cannot See — Chapter Template

Every chapter follows the same progression. The book should feel like one continuous inquiry.

## Six-beat scaffold

### 1. Genuine human question

Open with a blockquote containing the chapter's central question.

Do not answer immediately. Let the question orient the reader.

### 2. Observations

Start with concrete observations drawn from:

- History
- Science
- Engineering
- Medicine
- Psychology
- Nature
- Everyday life

Do **not** define concepts immediately. Allow readers to notice the pattern before naming it.

### 3. Pattern reveal

Name what the reader has been noticing—without yet fully systematizing.

The pattern should feel discovered, not delivered.

### 4. Conceptual framework

Introduce the conceptual framework: bias family, judgment move, or integration mechanism.

This is where terms earn their names.

### 5. Implications

Explore what each partial perspective reveals and obscures.

Adaptive ↔ dangerous. Individual ↔ collective.

### 6. Natural handoff

End by naturally raising the next chapter's question.

Document handoffs in [`chapter-questions.md`](chapter-questions.md).

## Structural notes

- Subheads (`###`) are content-specific, not outline labels
- No "Chapter N" in reader-facing headings
- No cross-references by chapter number—refer by theme
- Central question blockquote at open; handoff question at close (optional second blockquote)

## Outline vs prose

Phase 0 scaffolds may use outline labels internally. Replace with content-specific headings when drafting prose.

## Essay compression

For essay-length editions, merge chapters marked in `chapter-questions.md` without changing the six-beat rhythm within each surviving unit.
