# What We Cannot See — Judgment Framework

Part II concepts. Planning document for architectural consistency before prose.

## Core distinction

**Judgment is not the absence of bias.**

Judgment is the responsible use of limited perspectives.

Individuals are responsible for decisions, not for becoming unbiased.

## Key questions (Part II)

- What is judgment?
- How should we decide when certainty is impossible?
- What responsibilities accompany judgment?

## Three chapter moves

### Ch 6 — Judgment Is Not Neutrality

**Concept:** Judgment names the work of deciding under partial sight—not achieving neutrality.

| Term | Meaning in this book |
|------|---------------------|
| Judgment | Responsible decision using limited perspectives |
| Neutrality | Myth of perspective-free seeing |
| Responsibility | Owning decisions despite incomplete information |

**Guardrail:** Adaptive bias ≠ exculpation. Part II must carry moral weight.

### Ch 7 — Deciding Without Certainty

**Concept:** Decision methods under uncertainty—without pretending certainty is available.

| Move | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Uncertainty as condition | Not failure of effort or character |
| Provisional commitment | Acting without final proof |
| Revisability | Willingness to update when evidence shifts |
| Proportionality | Matching confidence to evidence |

**Relation to portfolio:** Distinct from *The Discipline of Uncertainty* (probabilistic leadership). WWCS focuses cognitive architecture of partial sight.

### Ch 8 — Perspectives We Do Not Naturally Possess

**Concept:** Wisdom grows by intentionally incorporating perspectives we do not naturally possess.

| Move | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Foreign perspectives | Views we do not default to—not exoticism |
| Intentional incorporation | Practice, not automatic diversity |
| Individual responsibility | Still accountable for how we decide |
| Limits of individual wisdom | Sets up Part III bridge |

**Handoff:** Even excellent individual judgment remains incomplete → integration is necessary.

## What judgment is not

- Debiasing to neutrality
- Relativism ("all views equal")
- Deferring all responsibility to groups
- Certainty-seeking as moral virtue

## What judgment is

- Responsible use of partial perspectives
- Deciding when certainty is impossible
- Incorporating what we would not naturally see
- Owning outcomes while remaining revisable
