# What We Cannot See — Recurring Metaphors

Candidate through-lines for architectural consistency. Not all must appear in final prose—select 2–3 primary metaphors.

## Primary Bias

### The attention budget

Finite minds operate on limited attention currency. Every notice costs something else unseen.

**Use in:** Part I opener, Ch 1 anchor.

### Selective aperture

Perspective as aperture—not lens flaw. The aperture chooses what enters; it does not corrupt what is real.

**Use in:** Introduction, thesis articulation.

### What the light catches

Observational metaphor (Solnit-adjacent): we see what the angle of light reveals; shadow is not deception.

**Use in:** Part I bridges, chapter openings.

## Judgment

### Deciding in fog

Not ignorance as failure—fog as condition of movement.

**Use in:** Part II, Ch 7.

### Borrowed sight

Incorporating perspectives we do not naturally possess—temporary use of another's partial view.

**Use in:** Ch 8, handoff to integration.

## Integration

### Many sketches, one terrain

Partial drawings that together approximate terrain no single sketch captures.

**Use in:** Part III bridge, Ch 9.

### Model-building

Integration as constructing a larger model—not averaging sketches.

**Use in:** Part III throughout; distinguish from compromise.

### The blind men and the elephant (careful use)

Classic but risk of mockery. If used, reframe: each partial contact is real; failure is claiming the part is the whole.

**Use in:** Overflow only—may feel too familiar.

## Cross-book caution

- Do not reuse *Living in Sediment* calendar metaphor
- Do not reuse trust cluster community-center anchor
- Keep metaphors observational, not clever
