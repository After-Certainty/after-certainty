# What We Cannot See — Book Promises

Every chapter begins with a question.

Every concept is introduced through observation before definition.

Every bias is presented as both adaptive and limiting.

Every historical example exists to illuminate a pattern.

Every chapter should leave the reader with another question.

The goal is not to convince readers they are biased.

The goal is to help readers recognize that every perspective—including their own—is necessarily partial.

## Reader contract

- **Discovery, not lecture:** The reader should feel they are uncovering a hidden structure, not being told what to think
- **Observation before naming:** Patterns emerge before concepts are defined
- **Adaptive and dangerous:** Every bias family gets both sides
- **Continuous inquiry:** the book feels like one inquiry, not isolated essays
- **No debiasing agenda:** partiality is structural, not moral failure

## Chapter-level promises

- Introduction establishes the central question and reader contract
- Part I establishes bias as adaptive allocation, not defect
- Part II establishes judgment as responsible use of partial perspectives
- Part III establishes integration as model-building, not compromise
