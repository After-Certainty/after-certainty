# What We Cannot See — Integration Framework

Part III concepts. Planning document for architectural consistency before prose.

## Core distinction

**Integration is not compromise.**

Integration is constructing a larger model that explains why multiple perspectives each noticed something important.

## Key questions (Part III)

- Why do other people keep seeing things I miss?
- How can incomplete perspectives produce better understanding?
- How do institutions preserve both wisdom and blind spots?

## Concept map

| Concept | Is | Is not |
|---------|-----|--------|
| Integration | Constructing a larger model that explains why multiple partial views each noticed something real | Compromise, averaging opinions, false balance |
| Collaboration | One integration venue | The whole of integration |
| Science | Error-correction through partial observation + method | Neutral view from nowhere |
| Engineering / medicine | Failure-driven learning across partial observers | Perfect safety through single expert |
| Democracy | Institutional integration under disagreement | Majority rule as truth |
| Organizational learning | Preserving and updating collective partial sight | Best-practice copying without context |
| AI | Amplifier and distorter of partial perspectives | Unbiased oracle or inevitable harm |

## Chapter moves

### Ch 9 — Why Others See What I Miss (Collaboration)

Integration begins when we recognize others notice what our partial perspective obscures.

**Relation to portfolio:** Distinct from *Why Collaboration Is So Hard* (coordination fragility). WWCS focuses epistemic model-building.

### Ch 10 — The Method of Many Eyes (Science)

Science as institutional integration: many partial observers, shared method, error correction.

### Ch 11 — When Failure Teaches the System (Engineering / medicine)

Paired case studies: how high-reliability domains integrate partial perspectives through failure analysis.

### Ch 12 — Institutions That Remember and Forget (Democracy / org learning)

How institutions preserve wisdom and blind spots simultaneously—memory, mandate, and drift.

### Ch 13 — Another Kind of Partial Perspective (AI)

Models as partial perspectives: training data, objective functions, and human integration of machine outputs.

## Integration mechanisms (cross-chapter)

- Disagreement as signal, not obstacle
- Method that survives partial observers
- Failure as teacher when systems learn
- Institutions as memory and forgetting
- Human judgment over machine partiality

## What integration requires

- Willingness to explain why each partial view noticed something real
- Models large enough to hold contradiction without false equivalence
- Institutions that update, not just accumulate
- Individual judgment that participates in collective sight
