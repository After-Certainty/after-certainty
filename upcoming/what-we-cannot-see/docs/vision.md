# What We Cannot See — Vision

## Purpose

Help readers understand why every perspective is necessarily partial, how individuals make responsible judgments despite those limitations, how integration combines partial perspectives, and how societies design processes that improve collective judgment—without assuming unbiased people exist.

This is **not** a book about eliminating bias from individuals.

It is a book about perspective—selective cognition, responsible judgment, integration, and the institutional processes humanity has developed because no one sees everything.

## Central question

If no one can see everything, how can individuals and societies make wise decisions?

## Culminating question (Part III)

Given that individuals have finite perspectives, how should we organize ourselves so that we can know more together than we can alone?

## Core thesis (summary)

Every perspective is partial. Finite minds inhabit finite perspectives.

Bias is not primarily a flaw to eliminate from individuals. It is the selective work through which finite perspectives become possible—one way situated cognition takes shape.

Every bias reveals something. Every bias obscures something.

Judgment is the responsible use of limited perspectives.

Integration is how many incomplete perspectives become more complete understanding.

Process design is how societies reduce the unchecked effects of individual bias through feedback, review, and distributed cognition—without pretending anyone achieves objectivity.

See [`thesis.md`](thesis.md) and [`process-design.md`](process-design.md) for full articulation.

## Four levels

1. Individual cognition (Part I)
2. Individual judgment (Part II)
3. Collective integration (Part III)
4. Institutional process design (Part III culmination)

## Reader experience

The reader should never feel lectured.

The reader should feel like they are slowly discovering a hidden structure that has always existed.

The goal is not agreement.

The goal is that readers begin noticing these patterns in everyday life—and recognize that improvement lives in **process**, not in fantasies of unbiased individuals.

## Tone

Curious. Thoughtful. Restrained.

Never preach. Never mock. Never reduce complex issues to heroes and villains.

Always assume people are adapting to limited information.

## What this book is not

- A debiasing handbook for individuals
- A fatalist claim that because bias is unavoidable, nothing can be improved
- Implicit "bias = bad" morality tale
- Culture-war ammunition
- Management consulting for "unconscious bias training"
- A trust or diversity guide (those are application domains, not this book's scope)

## What this book is

- Foundational epistemics: why partial sight is universal
- A movement arc: perspective → bias → judgment → integration → process design
- Designed epistemologies: science, engineering, medicine, democracy, and organizational practice as answers to finite perspective
- Observational discovery, not rhetorical certainty
- One continuous inquiry across the manuscript
