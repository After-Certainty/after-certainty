# When Others Become Leaders — Book Rules

## Purpose

Architectural constraints for **When Others Become Leaders** (*What Enduring Influence Leaves Behind*).

These rules protect the book from flattening into biography recap, leadership tips, or framework dumping.

## Book scope and structure

### Scope

- Intro + three parts + nine chapters + epilogue.
- Essays centered on how influence circulates through people and institutions, especially after a leader is absent.
- Chapter-level inquiry uses ordinary objects or scenes as entry points.

### Structural baseline

- Canonical chapter ordering and chapter fields live in [`outline.md`](outline.md).
- Reusable chapter movement lives in [`chapter-architecture.md`](chapter-architecture.md).
- Voice and paragraph rules live in [`voice-guide.md`](voice-guide.md).

## Core invariant (non-negotiable)

The book must show, not merely assert, that the most enduring leadership creates more capable people and communities rather than permanent dependence on one central figure.

## Guardrails

### This book must not become

- A ranking of great historical figures.
- A collection of mini-biographies.
- A business/consultant leadership framework.
- A moral equivalence claim across radically different contexts.
- A defense brief that erases harms, controversies, or limitations.

### This book must remain

- Essayistic, reflective, and discovery-led.
- Historically grounded and morally honest.
- Interested in differences among cases, not only similarities.
- Open to mixed legacies: remarkable in one dimension, limited or harmful in another.

## Chapter construction rules

1. Start with an ordinary object, place, gesture, or social mystery.
2. Let the deeper chapter question form before introducing the central figure.
3. Delay naming the chapter pattern until late.
4. Include tensions, criticism, and context before synthesis.
5. End with resonance and sharpened recognition, not leadership tips.

See detailed template in [`chapter-architecture.md`](chapter-architecture.md).

## Historical and ethical constraints

- Do not collapse a movement into one person; include surrounding communities and institutions.
- Do not invent interior states or dialogue.
- Distinguish documented facts from interpretation.
- Verify memorable anecdotes before use.
- Use controversy as explanatory context, not ornamental takedown or avoidance.

Research standards and source mix are in [`research-plan.md`](research-plan.md).

## Relationship to other books

- The book is related to *When Others Look To You* but stands alone.
- If cross-book relation appears in manuscript prose, keep it brief and non-dependent.
- Planning detail and unresolved decisions are tracked in [`book-overview.md`](book-overview.md) and [`open-questions.md`](open-questions.md).
