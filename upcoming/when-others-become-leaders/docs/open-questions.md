# When Others Become Leaders — Open Questions

Deliberate unresolved decisions. Keep these visible rather than settling them implicitly in draft prose.

## Structure and scope

1. Is nine the right number of central figures?
2. Should chapters remain in the current three-part ordering or move toward a more associative sequence?
3. How directly should the epilogue name the recurring architecture versus leaving it mostly implicit?
4. Should the introduction explicitly mention the relationship to *When Others Look To You*, or remain fully standalone?

## Voice and method

5. How much personal narrative should appear, and where does it clarify versus distract?
6. Should the manuscript use the term “circulation” explicitly, or mostly show it through scenes and cases?
7. How can chapters preserve essayistic wandering without losing question clarity?

## Ethics, comparison, and controversy

8. How should controversies for each subject be integrated without turning chapters into prosecution or defense briefs?
9. How can the book distinguish moral influence from celebrity without dismissing cultural leadership?
10. How can the book avoid implying that influence is healthy merely because it spreads?
11. What criteria prevent accidental moral equivalence among radically different contexts and consequences?

## Evidence and historical method

12. What minimum evidence threshold should be set before including a memorable anecdote?
13. What does “enduring” mean for a living contemporary figure such as Taylor Swift?
14. Which claims require strongest primary-source corroboration before drafting begins?

## Process checkpoints

- Revisit these questions at the end of Phase 1 research architecture.
- Mark any resolved items in [`status.md`](status.md) with rationale and date.
