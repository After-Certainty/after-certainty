## Movement map

- **Twelve and wider disciples** — sent pairs; travel without purse or staff (Synoptic mission discourses)
- **Women patrons and witnesses** — Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Susanna (Luke 8); empty-tomb traditions (all four gospels)
- **Meal companions** — tax collectors, “sinners,” Pharisees, Zacchaeus (Luke 19), Simon the Pharisee (Luke 7)
- **Post-Easter communities** — Jerusalem assembly (Acts 2); Pauline house churches; Corinthian disputes over table fellowship
- **Second Temple Judaism** — purity, Sabbath, honor codes shaping meal boundaries
- **Roman provincial order** — occupation, taxation, client elites (context for “wrong” table partners)

## Controversy map

- **Historical Jesus limits** — gospel narratives are faith documents; reconstructions vary (Meier’s criteria vs maximalist readings)
- **Institutionalization** — later church hierarchy, sacramental theology, and empire sponsorship complicate “multiplication through service”
- **Gender and authority** — women as first witnesses vs later ecclesial restriction of leadership roles
- **Anti-Judaism in reception** — table scandal often misread as Jesus vs “the Jews”; Levine and Sanders caution against supersessionist framing
- **Violence in Christian history** — crusades, coercion, and exclusion contradict inclusive meal memory

## Chronology strip

- **c. 4 BCE–30 CE** — Jesus’s life in Roman Judea/Galilee (approximate scholarly range)
- **30–33 CE** — crucifixion and reported resurrection appearances (tradition)
- **30s–50s CE** — Paul’s letters; Corinthian and Galatian community disputes
- **c. 70–100 CE** — canonical gospels written/edited in post-Temple context
- **2nd century+** — ecclesial structure, liturgical meal → Eucharist institutionalization
