# **Chapter 1**

## **The Psychological Comfort of Certainty**

A resident reads conflicting guidance about a medication during a drug
shortage. One article says continue; another says substitute; a relative
says stop. The resident feels a spike of anger—not only at confusion, but
at the **loss of orientation**. Certainty would end the spike. Even wrong
certainty might end it faster than nuanced truth.

> Certainty feels like safety.

### **1. Absolutes reduce cognitive and moral load**

**Absolutes reduce load**. They collapse complex distributions into yes/no,
safe/unsafe, us/them. The collapse is cognitively cheap. It also reduces
moral labor: if the category is clear, responsibility feels assignable.

Under stress, cheap orientation is rational. It is also fragile when reality
returns variance.

### **2. Identity, belonging, and orientation**

Certainty provides **identity and belonging**. Shared absolutes signal who
is in the group and what the group stands for. Ambiguity threatens belonging
because it permits too many interpretations.

Orientation is not only intellectual. It is social: what can I say in this
room without losing standing?

### **3. Ambiguity as threat, not neutrality**

For many people, **ambiguity feels like threat**, not neutral uncertainty.
Threat triggers vigilance, moralization, and hunger for closure. Institutions
that speak in calm qualifiers can sound like they are hiding something—even
when they are being careful.

### **4. Certainty feels moral**

A key distinction runs through this book: **certainty often feels moral even
when it is epistemic**. The feeling of moral clarity ("I know what is right")
rides along with the feeling of factual clarity ("I know what is true").
Collapsing the two makes revision look like betrayal.

### **5. Why discipline must start here**

Any discipline of uncertainty must account for the **comfort** certainty
provides—not lecture people out of craving it. The craving is human. The
task is to build practices that offer orientation without counterfeit
inevitability.

The next chapter examines abstraction—one of certainty's most powerful tools,
and one of its most dangerous disguises.
