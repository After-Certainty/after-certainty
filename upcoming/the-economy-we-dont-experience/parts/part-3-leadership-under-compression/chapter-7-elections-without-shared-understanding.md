# **Chapter 7**

## **Elections Without Shared Understanding**

Economic voting used to be discussed as if voters shared a rough model:
inflation hurts incumbents, unemployment hurts incumbents, growth helps.
Those heuristics still operate. They operate inside **fragmented
interpretive frames** where the same print means betrayal to one coalition
and progress to another.

Democracy depends not only on shared outcomes, but on shared understanding
of what outcomes mean.

### **1. Economic voting without shared frames**

Voters can respond to **felt conditions** while disagreeing completely on
causes, responsibility, and what policy would fix them. Elections then
become competitions between **credibility stories**, not between technical
programs voters could evaluate identically.

### **2. Housing as a split screen**

In a swing **suburb**, one voter opens a mortgage statement with a payment
that finally stabilized and hears "inflation victory." Another opens a
rental renewal with a double-digit increase and hears "betrayal." The same
CPI shelter component sits behind both experiences; the campaigns offer
different compressions—who saved you, who abandoned you.

Neither voter needs to be insincere. They are living in different tails of
the same distribution. Elections under interpretive stress reward the
candidate who **names the tail** without calling the other tail fake.

### **3. Credibility gaps outweighing policy gaps**

In eras of thin economic speech, **credibility gaps** often outweigh policy gaps: the
candidate who "gets it" beats the candidate with detailed plans. Plans are
still necessary for governance. They are not always decisive for selection.

### **4. Resonance outperforming explanation politically**

**Resonance** outperforms explanation politically for the reasons Chapter 5
named: lower trust, higher pain scaling, faster media. Campaigns invest
accordingly—not only cynically, but rationally within the environment.

### **5. Small business on the stump**

**Main-street owners** appear in ads because they pass the relational
credibility test: "I sign the checks." Their stories are partial—one firm,
one town—but they answer the public's question after years of aggregate
speech that felt like signaling. Policy detail still matters in office.
On the trail, recognition scales faster than white papers.

### **6. Vibes-based voting as rational**

So-called **vibes-based voting** is often rational signaling: choose the
candidate whose alignment reduces perceived betrayal risk, even if policy
details are thin.

### **7. Elections as expressive signaling**

**Expressive signaling** uses votes to say who we are. Economic conditions
become symbols in culture war narratives. This frustrates policy professionals.
It is also predictable when economic speech fails to orient.

### **8. Erosion of stabilizing legitimacy**

Legitimacy requires more than winning votes. It requires enough **shared
meaning** that losers accept outcomes as interpretable. When meaning erodes,
losers accept procedures while believing the game is rigged—a brittle form
of stability.

### **9. Procedures without shared meaning**

**Democratic procedures can persist while shared meaning erodes**: courts
function, ballots count, transitions occur—alongside collapsing trust in
economic narratives that once bound factions.

Institutional strain shows up as permanent campaign mode for economic truth:
every release is a battle, every revision a scandal.

### **10. Governing after the credibility election**

Winning on resonance is not the same as **governing with trust**. An administration
elected because it "gets" housing pain still faces the two-clock problem: national
prints and local renewals will diverge again. The first policy communication
after victory is often where credibility is spent or rebuilt—whether leaders
continue to name tails or revert to aggregate victory laps.

Elections without shared understanding thus produce mandates that are
**expressive**, not interpretive: voters know whom they rejected; they may not
agree what they purchased.

The final chapter of the argument turns from communication breakdown to
material stability—resilience, reform, and guardrails we forget until they
are gone.
