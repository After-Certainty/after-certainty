# **Chapter 5**

## **Resonance Without Understanding**

A short video explains inflation using animated charts. A creator in a
pickup truck explains inflation using the price of lumber and diesel. Both
reach millions. The truck video wins on shares—not because the charts are
wrong, but because the truck **resonates** with viewers who already feel
priced out of the story experts tell.

In compressed, low-trust environments, **resonance often determines
credibility before explanation begins**.

### **1. Resonance replacing explanation**

**Resonance** is the felt sense that a messenger sees your world. Explanation
is the cognitive sense that a model maps causal structure. When trust is
high, explanation can build resonance. When trust is low, resonance becomes
a shortcut—sometimes the only available one.

### **2. Messengers over models**

People increasingly choose **messengers over models**: follow the person
whose tone, biography, and enemies align with their identity, then adopt
that person's economic story. This looks irrational from a textbook view.
It is often **expressive rationality**: minimize betrayal risk in an
information environment where models feel weaponized.

### **3. Expressive economic interpretation**

**Expressive interpretation** uses economic language to say who we are and
whom we oppose. Slogans outperform analysis because slogans bind groups.
"Let people build" and "tax the rich" may be policy programs; they are also
tribal coordinates.

### **4. Narrative versus factual coherence**

**Narrative coherence** (the story hangs together emotionally) competes
with **factual coherence** (claims survive scrutiny). Under compression,
narrative coherence is cheaper to verify socially: do people like me believe
this? Factual coherence requires time and trust.

### **5. The rationality of vibes**

Colloquial **"vibes"** are not random feelings. They are heuristics for
trust under bandwidth limits. Vibes fail when captured by bad actors. They
also succeed often enough that dismissing them as mere stupidity misreads
the environment.

### **6. Alignment without understanding**

**Alignment** can substitute for shared understanding: we know which side
we are on without agreeing what would count as evidence. Alignment stabilizes
action in the short term. It erodes democratic capacity to revise beliefs
together in the long term.

### **7. Emotionally legible narratives**

**Emotionally legible** stories—villains, betrayal, stolen futures—
outcompete analytically complete accounts because legibility survives
retweets, clips, and hostile audiences. Completeness dies in the middle
sentence.

Institutions that want explanation to scale again must earn resonance first—
not through performed folksiness, but through credible recognition of pain
and constraint. Without that, explanation arrives as condescension.

> In compressed environments, resonance often determines credibility before
> explanation begins.

Part III turns to leaders and elections operating inside this environment—
where certainty often outperforms humility at scale, and shared meaning
can erode even when procedures hold.
