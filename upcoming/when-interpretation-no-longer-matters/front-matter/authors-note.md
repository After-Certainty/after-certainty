# **Author's Note**

In this book, interpretation does not mean personal opinion, private
belief, or individual comprehension. People can understand something
fully and still lack interpretive leverage over it. Interpretation, as
used here, refers to a **shared public process**—the process by which
meaning is evaluated, contested, and made consequential for authority.

Interpretation is what allows explanations to matter. It is what
connects what leaders say and do to legitimacy, accountability, and
repair. When interpretation is functioning, reasons can weaken
authority, contradictions can demand response, and outcomes can alter
standing. Authority is not guaranteed, but it is interpretable.

This book examines what happens when that process weakens—or disappears.
