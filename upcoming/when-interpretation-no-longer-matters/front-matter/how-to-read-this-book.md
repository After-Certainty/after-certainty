# **How to Read This Book**

Some terms and cases in this book may feel unfamiliar at first.

- The absence of prescriptions is deliberate. Diagnosis comes before
  remedy, and sometimes remedy is not available.

- Difficulty does not signal failure of comprehension. It often signals
  proximity to a boundary the book is trying to name.

If at any point the argument feels unsatisfying, pause before assuming
disagreement. Ask instead what kind of explanation the book is refusing
to give. Often the discomfort comes not from confusion, but from
encountering a limit that explanation itself cannot cross.

This book does not ask you to suspend judgment.

It asks you to locate it more carefully.

Reading slowly, revisiting sections, or setting the book down and
returning later are all appropriate ways to engage it. The work it is
doing is not additive. It is clarifying. And clarification often takes
time.
