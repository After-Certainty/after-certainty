# **Chapter 11**

## **Why Judgment Feels Impossible**

Some authority regimes render judgment simultaneously obvious and
useless.[^c11-ref-1-source] This chapter examines why—showing how moral
clarity can increase without restoring leverage when interpretation no
longer mediates authority.

Readers coming from essay-length work on **judgment without finality**
will recognize the feeling: condemnation that is morally serious but
structurally inert. Here the limit is not incomplete understanding but
displaced interpretation—judgment as witness rather than repair.

In these conditions, condemnation does not fail because it is
inaccurate. It fails because it no longer connects to consequence.
Judgment becomes expressive rather than corrective.[^c11-ref-2-when-authority-outlives-accountability]

This chapter explains why readers often feel moral exhaustion or
helplessness when encountering such cases—and why that reaction signals
a structural limit rather than a personal failure.[^c11-ref-3-hannah-arendt-the-origins]

## **The Event in Brief**

Pol Pot (1925–1998), leader of the Khmer Rouge, took control of Cambodia in April 1975. The regime sought to create a radically agrarian society by dismantling urban life and abolishing existing institutions.[^c11-ref-4-ben-kiernan-the-pol]


Cities were emptied. Currency was eliminated. Schools, media, religious
institutions, and professional roles were dismantled. Intellectuals and
perceived political enemies were imprisoned, tortured, or executed. The
regime established prison centers such as S-21 (Tuol Sleng), where
forced confessions and systematic violence occurred.[^c11-ref-5-david-p-chandler-voices]

Between 1975 and 1979, approximately 1.5 to 2 million people died from
execution, forced labor, starvation, and disease.

The system did not merely suppress dissent. It dismantled the
institutional and linguistic structures through which interpretation
could coordinate resistance or evaluation.

Moral clarity about suffering remained possible. Mechanisms for
consequence did not.

## **Leader Focus: Pol Pot**

This is a case where the conditions for interpretation were destroyed,
leaving judgment without consequence.

## **What Replaced Interpretation**

Interpretation was replaced by obliteration of evaluative space.[^c11-ref-8-arendt-origins]

Authority no longer required belief, alignment, or performance. It
required only compliance within a system that eliminated conditions for
interpretation to matter. Meaning was neither debated nor enacted; it
was overridden.[^c11-ref-9-michel-foucault-society-must]

Judgment could be made—but not applied.[^c11-ref-10-how-meaning-moves]

## **How Authority Reproduced Itself**

Authority reproduced itself through systematic destruction of
mediation.[^c11-ref-11-orlando-figes-the-whisperers]

Institutions that enable interpretation—education, media, civil society,
professional networks—were dismantled. Without shared language for
evaluation, authority reproduced itself through terror and isolation
rather than persuasion or spectacle.[^c11-ref-12-foucault-society-must-be]

Reproduction occurred through elimination of alternatives rather than
reinforcement of meaning.[^c11-ref-13-when-authority-outlives-accountability]

## **What Still Functioned**

Minimal private understanding persisted.[^c11-ref-14-chandler-voices-from-s21]

Individuals recognized suffering, danger, and injustice. What did not
persist was any pathway for interpretation to coordinate response or
constrain authority.[^c11-ref-15-kiernan-pol-pot-regime]

What remained operative was awareness without agency.[^c11-ref-16-jrgen-habermas-the-theory]

## **What Was Lost**

What was lost was judgment with consequence.[^c11-ref-17-when-authority-outlives-accountability]

Moral clarity no longer translated into leverage. Condemnation could not
accumulate pressure. Interpretation ceased to link recognition to
action.[^c11-ref-18-how-meaning-moves]

This explains why judgment feels both urgent and futile in such cases.[^c11-ref-19-arendt-origins]

## **Why This Case Matters Structurally**

This case demonstrates the limit condition of post-interpretive
authority.[^c11-ref-20-arendt-origins]

Unlike alignment-based, identity-saturated, or performative regimes,
total destruction of interpretive mediation leaves no internal or
external pathway for correction. Judgment remains accurate but inert.[^c11-ref-21-when-authority-is-misread]

The case is structurally diagnostic rather than morally explanatory. It
shows why some authority failures cannot be addressed through better
interpretation, stronger condemnation, or clearer moral language.[^c11-ref-22-chapter-4-when-authority]

> **When interpretation cannot connect judgment to consequence,\
> moral clarity no longer restores leverage.**
