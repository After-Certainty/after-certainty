## **What Replaced Interpretation**

Interpretation was replaced by selective credibility.⁸

Claims were no longer evaluated through shared standards. They were
filtered by proximity to leadership, access to information, or perceived
loyalty. Meaning was managed rather than contested.⁹

Interpretation did not vanish. It became conditional.¹⁰

## **How Authority Reproduced Itself**

Authority reproduced itself through restricted access and reputational
borrowing.¹¹

Secrecy was framed as necessity. Endorsements substituted for
verification. Each delay in independent testing increased both the cost
of dissent and the appearance of legitimacy. Authority reproduced itself
through postponement rather than repair.¹²

Reproduction occurred through deferral of evaluation, not elimination of
it.¹³

## **What Still Functioned**

Interpretation continued to function in limited domains.¹⁴

Employees, partners, and observers reasoned about feasibility, risk, and
credibility. Understanding persisted, but it was fragmented and
siloed.¹⁵

What remained operative was interpretation without coordination.¹⁶

## **What Was Lost**

What was lost was interpretation as a shared corrective mechanism.¹⁷

No single challenge accumulated sufficient pressure. Evidence failed to
converge publicly. Interpretation lost its capacity to force
resolution.¹⁸

This explains why warning signs were visible yet ineffective.¹⁹

## **Why This Case Matters Structurally**

This case demonstrates how authority can drift across the boundary
without announcing the shift.²⁰

Transitional systems retain the appearance of interpretability, which
makes them difficult to diagnose in real time. Interpretation still
exists—but it no longer governs legitimacy.²¹

The case is structurally diagnostic rather than legally explanatory.
Authority can become post-interpretive by narrowing the channels through
which interpretation is allowed to matter.²²

> **When interpretation narrows before it collapses,\
> warning signs remain visible—but powerless.**

# Part IV — After Interpretation

This part addresses the limits of judgment and repair once
interpretation no longer governs authority. Moral clarity may increase
while practical influence declines, leaving judgment more certain but
less effective.

- **Judgment failure** — the condition in which moral clarity increases
  without restoring interpretive traction or practical leverage.

- **Irreparability** — the point at which transparency, dialogue, or
  accountability no longer function because interpretation itself has
  been displaced.

# Chapter 11: Why Judgment Feels Impossible

Some authority regimes render judgment simultaneously obvious and
useless.¹ This chapter examines why—showing how moral clarity can
increase certainty without restoring leverage when interpretation no
longer mediates authority.

In these conditions, condemnation does not fail because it is
inaccurate. It fails because it no longer connects to consequence.
Judgment becomes expressive rather than corrective.²

This chapter explains why readers often feel moral exhaustion or
helplessness when encountering such cases—and why that reaction signals
a structural limit rather than a personal failure.³

## **The Event in Brief**

Pol Pot (1925–1998), leader of the Khmer Rouge, took control of Cambodia in April 1975. The regime sought to create a radically agrarian society by dismantling urban life and abolishing existing institutions.⁴


Cities were emptied. Currency was eliminated. Schools, media, religious
institutions, and professional roles were dismantled. Intellectuals and
perceived political enemies were imprisoned, tortured, or executed. The
regime established prison centers such as S-21 (Tuol Sleng), where
forced confessions and systematic violence occurred.⁵

Between 1975 and 1979, approximately 1.5 to 2 million people died from
execution, forced labor, starvation, and disease.

The system did not merely suppress dissent. It dismantled the
institutional and linguistic structures through which interpretation
could coordinate resistance or evaluation.

Moral clarity about suffering remained possible. Mechanisms for
consequence did not.

## **Leader Focus: Pol Pot**

This is a case where the conditions for interpretation were destroyed,
leaving judgment without consequence.
