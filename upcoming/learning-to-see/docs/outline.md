## 1. Book identity

### Working title

**Learning to See**

### Subtitle candidates

1. *Practices of Attention, Humility, and Wisdom Across Traditions* (working placeholder)
2. *How Traditions Form the Knower*
3. *Science, Spirit, and the Cultivation of Perception*
4. *What Becomes Scarce When Answers Become Abundant*
5. *Comparative Practices for Honest Knowing*

### Book promise

This book explores how science, philosophy, psychology, and spiritual traditions have developed different practices for helping human beings perceive more clearly, confront self-deception, learn from others, preserve wisdom, and live with uncertainty. It is a comparative inquiry into the **formation of the knower**—the argument that the quality of what we know depends partly on the kind of knower we are becoming. It compares practices without claiming that traditions are secretly identical, and it culminates in an age when artificial intelligence makes information abundant without automatically producing attention, judgment, humility, character, or wisdom.

### Intended audience

- Curious general readers willing to follow essayistic inquiry across traditions
- Believers, skeptics, scientists, philosophers, and people between categories
- Readers of the After Certainty series seeking the **formation** question (how knowers are shaped) rather than only the **limit** question (why perspective is partial)
- Readers interested in inherited culture, spiritual practice, and epistemic humility without conversion narrative or debiasing handbook tone

### What this book is

- Essayistic comparative epistemology of **practice**
- A personal doorway (Utah, LDS inheritance, scripture experiment) widening into cross-tradition conversation
- An argument that information can change what we know while **repeated practices** help form who we become
- A study of cultural sediment, institutional distortion, and integration without reduction
- An AI-age culmination framed as **scarcity**: what becomes precious when answers become cheap

### What this book is not

- A conventional theology book or comparative religion survey
- An argument that science and religion are fundamentally the same
- A debiasing handbook, mindfulness productivity guide, or wellness catalogue
- A conversion narrative or memoir-only project
- A claim that observable benefits prove metaphysical claims
- A reduction of spirituality to psychology or epistemology to scientific method

### Central question

> Why have traditions that disagree profoundly about reality often developed analogous practices for addressing recurring human limitations?

Those limitations include: distraction; overconfidence; self-deception; isolation; tribalism; forgetfulness; inherited assumptions; unresolved conflict; suffering; the need for meaning; the pressure to reach certainty too quickly.

### Central thesis (related claims)

1. Human beings do not simply see reality. We learn what to notice.
2. Families, cultures, professions, institutions, and spiritual traditions train attention in different ways.
3. Information can change what we know, but repeated practices help form who we become.
4. Wisdom is not merely accumulated information. It is a cultivated capacity to recognize what matters under conditions that are incomplete, relational, and morally consequential.
5. Every way of seeing reveals something and obscures something.
6. Communities preserve insight that no individual could rediscover alone, but they also preserve blind spots and obsolete adaptations.
7. Ancient practices may remain important in the age of AI because AI can make information and analysis abundant without automatically producing attention, judgment, humility, character, or wisdom.

### Governing distinctions

> These traditions are not the same, but they sometimes develop similar patterns in response to similar human problems.

Preserve throughout:

- Christian prayer is not Buddhist meditation.
- Repentance is not scientific updating.
- Confession is not peer review.
- A congregation is not a research community.
- Tradition is not merely documentation.

The analogies matter. So do the places where they break.

### Target length

~55,000–70,000 words total (~3,500–4,500 per chapter unit; introduction ~1,200; epilogue ~1,500; bridges ~800–1,200 each).

## 2. Narrative arc

### Movement

**Personal experiment** (inheritance made visible through an honest attempt the author had avoided) → **formation vs information** (answers are not enough to form a knower) → **limits of solitary seeing** (why mirrors and introspection fail) → **six practice domains** (attention, humility, community, memory, conflict/repair, suffering/meaning) → **cultural inheritance** (sediment, archaeology, integration without reduction) → **AI-age scarcity** (what cheap answers cannot supply) → **modest epilogue** (teachability without a new system).

### Why AI is the culmination, not an appendix

The book's spine is that **information does not automatically form knowers**. Parts I–II establish that practices cultivate capacities—attention, humility, community, memory, repair, moral imagination—that propositional knowledge alone does not guarantee. Part III shows how inherited practices carry both wisdom and blind spots; evaluation requires archaeology without contempt. Part IV asks what becomes **scarce** when generated answers become abundant: judgment, discernment, attention, courage, responsibility, character, meaning, trustworthy community, willingness to revise oneself.

AI is therefore the **stress test** of the prior argument, not a separate topic. The author's ChatGPT scripture experiment (Chapter 1) returns in Chapter 13: AI as a companion that surfaces available information without conferring wisdom, lived context, or formation. Chapter 14 then asks whether ancient practices answer **persistent** human limitations—not because the past was purer, but because the problems (distraction, self-deception, isolation, forgetfulness) persist.

## Editorial assessment

### Three strongest elements

1. **Personal LDS / ChatGPT doorway** grounds comparative inquiry in stakes without conversion narrative; AI callback in Ch 13 pays off the opening honestly.
2. **Part II limitation-by-limitation architecture** with explicit "where the analogy breaks" guardrail keeps comparison respectful and specific.
3. **AI culmination as scarcity inversion** makes the technology thread a natural stress test of the formation thesis, not a trend-chasing appendix.

### Three important risks to address before drafting

1. **Scope creep across traditions** — mitigate with named schools/texts, one dominant anchor per Part II chapter, and consultation flags before prose.
2. **Overlap with *What We Cannot See* and *Living in Sediment*** — mitigate with differentiation table, cross-links, and discipline in echo passes.
3. **Flattening practices into wellness techniques** — mitigate with five-layer framework and institutional distortion in every Part II chapter; Sabbath/meditation/confession examples must show commodification danger.
