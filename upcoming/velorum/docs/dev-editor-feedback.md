---

## Constraints (non-negotiable)

- Preserve manuscript length (**90k–100k** words; baseline ~95k).
- Do **not** solve pacing primarily through cutting.
- Prefer **differentiation** over compression.
- Preserve **working-class perspective**.
- Preserve **philosophical ambiguity**.
- Avoid adding **lore, mythology, or exposition**.
- Focus on **distinctiveness, emotional specificity, scene-level memorability**.

---

## One-sentence assessment

**Velorum is a mature, thoughtful, working-class mythic fantasy with a distinctive thematic identity and strong character relationships, but it still occasionally trusts its ideas more than its dramatic movement.**

The book is strongest when ideas emerge from people solving problems. It is weakest when the story pauses so ideas can catch up.

---

## What is working (protect)

1. **Riven and Cael** — friendship engine; protect.
2. **Greyhaven** — labor, logistics, repair, trade; inhabited.
3. **Working-class fantasy perspective** — biggest differentiator.
4. **Lyra** — necessary; novel less balanced when she leaves the page.
5. **Merrow** — hidden MVP; do not expand much; never cut.
6. **Halverin** — trapped, neither secretly evil nor secretly right.

---

## Priority issues

### 1. Repetition through variation (biggest)

Narrative repetition by act — not prose repetition:

| Act | Pattern |
|-----|---------|
| II | Problem → solve → mythologize (×3+) |
| III | Preparation → preparation → demonstration |
| IV | Strange terrain → stranger terrain |
| V | Hold line → hold bigger line |

**Revision goal:** Not cutting chapters — **increasing distinctiveness**. Every chapter should do something no other chapter does.

### 2. Cael agency

Riven, Lyra, Halverin, Merrow grow; Cael grows least. Often conscience/ballast/endurance rather than agency. Need one moment: *"Only Cael could have solved that"* — insight, not endurance.

### 3. Velorum vs journey

Road to Velorum (delayed echoes, lagged reflections, wrong gravity, fractured timing) more vivid than Velorum itself. Destination should slightly overshadow journey — **via behavioral specificity**, not spectacle.

### 4. Symbol trust

Monument, crack, injury counts, rope/load imagery — occasionally explained after they land. Trust symbols sooner.

### 5. Messy humanity (small)

Characters believable but often emotionally coherent. Occasional pettiness, embarrassment, selfishness, irrationality in quieter scenes.

---

## What NOT to do

- Add more lore, worldbuilding, mythology, explanations, or length.
- Cut chapters for pacing.
- Turn Velorum into visual spectacle (crystals, impossible sky, Marvel set piece).

---

## Market pitch (reference)

> Working-class mythic fantasy about two laborers bound together by a supernatural burden that gradually destroys privacy while transforming them into a civic legend.

---

## Revision decision hierarchy

When trade-offs arise:

> When choosing between strengthening theme and strengthening character, **choose character**.
>
> When choosing between strengthening symbolism and strengthening scene specificity, **choose scene specificity**.
>
> When choosing between explanation and implication, **choose implication**.
>
> **Preserve ambiguity wherever possible.**

---

## Beta reader success criteria

Define before recruiting readers.

**Success:**

- 70%+ finish rate
- Multiple readers mention Lyra
- Multiple readers mention the monument
- Readers understand the ending

**Warning signs:**

- Act 2 repeatedly cited as slow
- Confusion about Velorum
- Readers indifferent to Cael
- Readers stop before Act 3

Use rubric to filter feedback — reopen revision only if several independent readers hit the same warning sign.
