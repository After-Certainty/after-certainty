# Velorum — expansion guardrails

**Purpose:** When expanding Velorum toward **[target-length-spec.md](./target-length-spec.md)** word targets, prioritize **concrete experience over explanation**.

The goal is **not** to make themes clearer.

The goal is to make readers **feel** themes through events, choices, behavior, and consequences.

**Related:** **[expansion-progress.md](./expansion-progress.md)** (tracker) · **[act-1-calibration-standard.md](./act-1-calibration-standard.md)** (craft bar) · **[voice-spec.md](./voice-spec.md)** (dialogue / bond) · **[agents/initial-drafting/01-chapter-expansion-depth-pass.md](./agents/initial-drafting/01-chapter-expansion-depth-pass.md)** (expansion agent) · **[agents/revision/08-reality-agent.md](./agents/revision/08-reality-agent.md)** (believability audit)

## Core rule

Every chapter should answer **one concrete story question**.

Examples:

- What happens when the bond helps?
- What happens when people start believing the story?
- What happens when institutions discover the bond?
- What happens when privacy disappears?
- What happens when the bond causes failure?

Do **not** write chapters whose primary purpose is exploring a theme.

Themes should emerge from events.

---

## Moment before meaning

When choosing between:

- explaining an idea
- showing a moment

**always choose the moment.**

| BAD | GOOD |
|-----|------|
| Riven realized he was losing his identity. | The canal memory wasn't his anymore. |
| The city was transforming tragedy into symbolism. | "They always put symbols there." |

Readers should infer the meaning.

---

## One idea, three moments

Each chapter should contain:

- **1 central idea**
- and at least **3 concrete moments**

**Example — Chapter 11:**

| | |
|--|--|
| **Idea** | Loss of privacy |
| **Moments** | Roof argument · Canal memory · *It was mine not to tell* |

The moments carry the idea.

**Never explain the idea after the moments already demonstrated it.**

---

## Characters are not philosophers

Characters should speak from their **role and experience**.

| Character | Speaks like |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Lyra** | A healer; someone who sees wounds |
| **Halverin** | An administrator; someone managing systems |
| **Merrow** | A crisis commander |
| **Riven** | Someone protecting personhood |
| **Cael** | Someone searching for responsibility and purpose |

Do not allow characters to deliver author essays.

Their beliefs should emerge from their **work**.

---

## Trust the reader

Avoid:

- explaining emotional reactions
- explaining symbolism
- explaining themes
- explaining what readers should think

If a scene already demonstrates something, **do not explain it afterward**.

Cut the explanation.

---

## No "theme dialogue"

Avoid dialogue that sounds like:

- quotes for social media
- philosophy essays
- perfectly polished wisdom

Allow:

- interruptions
- uncertainty
- incomplete thoughts
- frustration
- repetition

People rarely sound like finished books.

---

## Expand through consequences

When increasing word count:

**DO NOT add:**

- lore
- exposition
- philosophy
- worldbuilding essays

**Instead add:**

- aftermath
- reactions
- practical problems
- relationships
- unintended consequences
- emotional fallout

The book grows through **consequences**, not explanations.

---

## Systemic reality

The world should behave like a **living system**.

Show:

- exhausted workers
- contradictory orders
- missing inventory
- bad information
- delayed responses
- people making mistakes
- shortages, repairs, queues, labor strain, rumor spread

Prefer **recognizable human problems** over **invented texture**:

| Prefer | Avoid as expansion filler |
|--------|---------------------------|
| wrong crate called twice | invented infrastructure (*fish stairs*) |
| paid extra, still shorted | quirky inventory (*running out of chalk*) unless plot-critical |
| boss contradicts yesterday's order | fantasy-market label stacks (*bond singers*) |
| guard waves you through without looking | abstract strain (*administration was strained*) |

Concrete **human** signals beat abstract descriptions—and beat **glossary-dependent** "worldbuilding" that expansion passes invent under pressure.

**Post-expansion:** Run **[08-reality-agent](./agents/revision/08-reality-agent.md)** to replace placeholder texture with connectable friction. See **Scene anchors** in that doc.

---

## The curse

The curse is **not** a superpower.

The curse is a **boundary violation**.

When writing curse / bond scenes, prioritize:

- loss of privacy
- emotional bleed
- memory bleed
- judgment bleed
- identity erosion

over:

- combat advantages
- tactical coordination

The curse should increasingly feel **useful and unbearable at the same time**.

Aligns with **[voice-spec.md](./voice-spec.md)** → Shared burden guardrails and **[act-1-calibration-standard.md](./act-1-calibration-standard.md)** → bond as intrusion/cost.

---

## Riven and Cael

The central conflict is not whether they survive.

The central conflict is whether they **remain themselves**.

Every major scene should affect **identity, autonomy, obligation, responsibility, or connection** in some way.

---

## Litmus test

Before finishing any scene, ask:

> **What specific moment will readers remember six months later?**

If the answer is:

- a sentence of explanation
- a theme
- a speech

→ **rewrite the scene.**

If the answer is:

- a memory
- an action
- an image
- a choice

→ the scene is probably working.

---

## Expansion pass checklist

Use with **[expansion-progress.md](./expansion-progress.md)** per-chapter checklist:

- [ ] One concrete story question for this chapter (written in Notes column if helpful)
- [ ] At least three concrete moments carrying the central idea
- [ ] No theme explanation after demonstration
- [ ] Dialogue from role, not essay
- [ ] Word count added via consequences (aftermath, logistics, fallout)—not lore or philosophy
- [ ] Bond scenes = boundary violation, not power fantasy
- [ ] Litmus test passed for new / expanded scenes
- [ ] **Pass I** / **[08-reality-agent](./agents/revision/08-reality-agent.md)** — new material uses **human friction** (mistakes, bad info, missing inventory), not invented glossary texture; people and institutions behave believably; scenes have plain **place + problem** anchors
