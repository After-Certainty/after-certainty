# Chapter 5 - Velorum

The stone under Riven's boots was warm.

Not from the sun. The sun hadn't shown itself all day. This warmth came up through the soles of his boots and settled in his legs until they felt loose beneath him.

The bowl spread wide around them, ring after ring cut into pale stone, grey sky overhead, no shadows, no movement. Behind him, the rim hid the trail they'd climbed. Ahead, the rings narrowed toward a pale center that looked no different from the rest of the rock.

Riven tried to count the rings. Lost track at seven. Started again. The numbers wouldn't stay in order.

Cael still hadn't let go of his wrist.

"You're crushing my hand."

"Let go, fuck."

"No."

"You are."

"Good."

Riven almost laughed. Almost. The sound died before it reached his throat. Something about the place swallowed noise.

He tried to speak again. His voice came out flat, wrong distance, like the bowl was listening from inside his chest.

The wind should have been moving through the carved channels. It wasn't. His own breathing sounded wrong, too loud, then too far away, then loud again.

The air pressed against him, not fog, not smoke, pressure like standing too deep underwater.

His pack strap dug into his shoulder—the same worn seam from Mara's loft—but the pain arrived a heartbeat late, like somebody else had felt it first. Riven shook his head once. The lag didn't clear.

Sweat gathered at his hairline though the air was cold. He wiped it with his free hand and smelled salt and iron. Wrong smell for a mountaintop. Wrong smell for anywhere that wasn't the harbor.

The carved channels in the stone should have carried runoff. They sat dry. Riven watched a leaf—or something like a leaf—skitter across the center without wind. It stopped. Started again. Wrong direction.

"Cael." His voice came out thin. Wrong. "We should really go."

"Maybe." Again. The same answer.

Something tightened beneath Riven's ribs. His skin prickled. The warm stone seemed to breathe beneath his feet. Then something moved through him, not a thought, not a voice, a realization, sudden, cold, absolute. It landed so hard his eyes watered.

*You don't get to stay untouched.*

Riven tried to step back. His foot lifted. Stopped. The warmth in the stone climbed higher, past his ankles, into his knees.

The clouds overhead shifted, not drifted, shifted, one moment there, the next somewhere else. His stomach dropped. His heart stumbled. For a second he couldn't tell whether the extra beat came from his chest or Cael's.

"Tell me you saw that."

"Yeah." The word came quietly. Too quietly.

Riven licked his lips. His mouth felt dry. The weight settled deeper, not around him, inside him, like somebody had stepped into the space where his thoughts usually formed. No voice. No words. Just presence, the certainty that something else was there, watching, waiting.

His pulse jumped. The feeling didn't. It stayed exactly where it was. Patient.

The patience was worse than anger would have been. Riven had dealt with angry bosses, angry crowds, angry weather. He knew the shape of those. This was waiting without a face—like the dock at dusk when the work was done and the silence asked who remembered.

The pamphlet flashed through his head: Heartbridge, First Circle, the Ashring, cheap ink and old stories. For the first time since leaving Greyhaven, he wished he'd never picked the damn thing up.

He thought about Mara's board—his name in chalk, already scrubbed or not yet, he couldn't know. Thought about the handcart and the crate list called twice and the dick drawn under the square sign. Ordinary things. They felt very far away.

Beside him, Cael's grip tightened, not much, enough. "I'm here." Riven couldn't tell whether the words were meant for him or for Cael himself.

Riven sucked in a breath. The air barely seemed to reach his lungs. "Did you feel that?"

"Yeah." The answer came immediately. "What was it?"

For the first time since they entered the bowl, Cael looked away from the center. Looked directly at him. Fear sat plain on his face, not panic, not confusion, fear. "I don't know." He didn't sound uncertain. "But it's real."

It kept building. Neither of them let go.

They had crossed deeper without deciding to—past the outer rings, past the place where Riven's knee had buckled on the climb in. The warmth climbed with each step—not comfort, wrongness wearing a friendly face. His boot scuffed the stone and the sound arrived late, like the bowl was listening from behind a door.

Cael's stride hitched. Riven felt the hitch in his own knee before he saw it. Neither fell. Falling would have meant letting go.

"Cael."

"I'm here." Same words as before. Different cost.

It dragged old fears to the surface and held them there: Mara's chalkboard, dock planks at dusk, the feeling of finishing a day and knowing nobody would remember who had done the work.

The dock came vivid—not memory exactly, more like somebody had turned the image up too loud. Wet planks. Empty quay. His own hands on a crate rope. Then Cael's hands on the same rope, same grip, wrong wrists. Riven jerked his head sideways. The image didn't leave.

Riven wanted it to stop. The worst part was that he didn't, not entirely. The wanting was still there, buried underneath the fear.

He tried to name what he wanted—not Velorum, not legend, something smaller. To be seen. To be counted on a board that wasn't Mara's. To finish a day and have somebody remember which crates he'd moved. The bowl held those wants up like wet laundry and let them drip.

A laugh escaped him, or maybe a choke, hard to tell.

"Riven." Cael's voice sounded farther away than it should have.

"I'm fine."

"No."

"I'm standing, aren't I?"

"Barely."

Riven tried to roll his eyes. The effort felt enormous.

The center of the bowl remained empty. That bothered him more than if something had been standing there. His mind kept trying to force a shape into the space, a face, a body, anything. Nothing stayed.

Once he thought he saw Mara's kitchen—the strip of light under the door, coins clicking—and the image dissolved before he could blink. Wrong place. Wrong moment. Memory arriving uninvited.

Velorum. The name kept surfacing anyway, like it had been waiting for him to stop fighting it.

Riven's pack strap bit his shoulder. He shifted it without letting go of Cael's wrist. The shift hurt Cael too—Riven felt the flinch cross before he saw it.

Inside the pack, the bread count was wrong in his head. Two loaves at the gate. One gone at the seep. Half a bar left. Numbers he could trust on a dock. Up here the numbers kept sliding, like the boss's short count that became long count when Riven stacked the empties.

He tried to remember whether he'd tied the iron wrap or left it loose at the hollow camp. Cael's memory of the knot arrived before his own—tight, double, Mara-style. Riven jerked.

"Stop."

"I wasn't—"

"You were in the pack." Riven's voice cracked. "That's my count."

Cael's jaw worked. "I know."

"Sorry."

"Don't." Cael's eyes stayed on the center. "Don't apologize for my shoulder."

"Christ."

"Yeah."

Beside him, Cael drew a slow breath. "If this is it," he said quietly, "we hold."

Riven looked over. "We hold what?"

"I don't know." The answer surprised both of them. Cael shook his head once. "Whatever comes."

"That's not a plan."

"No." The corner of Cael's mouth twitched, not quite humor, not quite fear. "It's what we've got."

Riven wanted to spit the taste of metal out of his mouth. Couldn't. His tongue felt thick. He watched Cael run a thumb along his lower lip and felt the motion an instant before it happened—wrong delay, wrong body.

"Stop," Riven said.

"What?"

"Standing like the bridge holds if you will it hard enough."

"Maybe it will."

"Listen to us." Riven's laugh died in his throat. "The square fiddler hits a wrong note and people jeer. Up here we talk like we're already dead."

"We're not dead."

"Feels close." Riven's boot shifted on the warm stone. The shift pulled at Cael's balance too. Neither fell. "That's new."

"I know."

The air seemed to lean closer. Riven felt the hairs rise along his arms. Cael lifted his free hand toward the center of the bowl, not dramatic, not brave, just stubborn, the same look he got before testing a bridge everyone else thought would collapse. "We hold."

The words landed strangely. Riven felt them settle inside his own chest, not agreement, not obedience, recognition.

"Don't."

Cael didn't look away from the center. "If you're leaving, leave."

Riven almost laughed again. The bowl had already made that impossible. His feet stayed where they were. Cael still had his wrist. The road back to Greyhaven suddenly felt very far away—the north gate, the lower dock, Mara's chalk, all of it behind a ridge that might as well have been another country.

He tried to picture Heartbridge from here. Couldn't. The monument stayed in Greyhaven like a stain on a pamphlet.

He tried to picture the yard hinge instead—the tap, tap, tap in the wind. The image arrived with Cael's memory of the same sound layered underneath. Riven jerked.

"Don't," he said.

"I wasn't—"

"You were." Riven's throat tightened. "That's mine."

Cael looked at him, finally. "I know."

"I'm here." The admission slipped out before he could stop it. "And I hate it."

"Yeah." Cael finally glanced at him. "Me too."

That caught him off guard. For one stupid second all Riven could think about was the loose thread hanging from Cael's cuff. He wanted to pull it, just to prove he still could.

Then something shifted, a flicker, small, fast, relief, not his, Cael's. Riven felt it anyway. The emotion hit him with the same certainty as his own heartbeat. Before he could stop himself, anger followed, sharp, immediate.

*You don't get to be relieved.*

The thought landed between them, not spoken, shared.

Cael's head snapped around. Riven froze. They stared at each other.

"What was that?"

Neither of them moved. Neither let go. The bowl's weight vanished from Riven's attention. Something more frightening had replaced it.

Riven tried to think of something private—the loft rafters, the yard hinge, the coin count at Mara's table—and felt Cael brush against each image like a hand through smoke. Not taking. Not yet. Just touching.

"Stop," Riven said aloud, because saying it felt like building a wall.

"I am stopping."

"You're not." Riven's pulse hammered. "You're in my—" He couldn't finish. There wasn't a word that wasn't wrong.

Cael's jaw worked. "I know."

They stood in the rings while the bowl listened. Riven counted breaths. Cael counted with him—not on purpose, or on purpose, impossible to tell. At ten, Riven's shoulders dropped a fraction. At twelve, Cael's did too. Shared rhythm. Shared mistake.

Somewhere behind the rim their packs waited where they'd dropped them before stepping onto the outer ring—canvas stiff, straps wrong, ordinary things they'd trusted at Mara's gate. Riven tried to picture the handcart instead. The crate list. The herring smell. Ordinary work with ordinary failures.

The image arrived with Cael's dock dusk layered underneath—empty quay, nobody remembering who moved what. Riven shook his head once. The bowl didn't care about shaking.

"Cael." His voice came out rough. "When we get off these rings—"

"We check water." Cael didn't look back toward the rim. "We check bread. We don't assume we packed right."

"Since when do you assume?"

"Since the seep." Cael's thumb moved against Riven's wrist. "Since you felt my strap."

Riven swallowed copper. "Fine."

"Fine," Cael echoed, and Riven felt the echo before he heard it and hated both.

"What was that?" Cael asked again, quieter.

Copper flooded the back of Riven's tongue. The clouds overhead jumped. The bowl tilted, or maybe he did. Suddenly he felt weight shift, a stumble, the sensation hit before he saw it happen. His stomach dropped. His knee buckled. At the exact same moment, Cael caught himself.

Both of them froze, breathing hard.

"What the fuck was that?"

This time neither of them had an answer.

Riven tried to let go of Cael's sleeve. His fingers wouldn't open. Cael tried the same. Same failure. They stood locked—wrist, sleeve, heat—while the bowl waited.

"Count," Cael said.

"What?"

"To five. Out loud. Separate."

Riven swallowed. "One."

"Two." Cael's voice on three. Riven's on four. They both said five at once and heard the overlap like a bad echo in a thin room.

Riven's fingers loosened. Not free. Looser.

"What the fuck was that?" he said again, because once wasn't enough.

This time neither of them had an answer that helped.

Weight snapped inward beneath Riven's ribs. His lungs refused a full breath. He grabbed a fistful of Cael's sleeve, hard. Cael didn't pull away.

Riven's vision narrowed to Cael's face—fear there, plain, and underneath it something worse: relief. The same relief Riven had felt on Heartbridge when he'd said *fine* too fast.

"If we stop now—"

"We're not stopping." Cael answered too quickly.

Riven heard it. So did Cael.

"That's not what I was going to say."

For a moment neither spoke. The bowl waited around them: stone, warmth under their boots, the thing at the center that wasn't a thing at all, just attention, just waiting.

Riven tried to list what they'd need when they stepped off—water, bread, rope, iron, the patch kit he'd bought at the tinker stall and hadn't used yet. The list arrived in Cael's head first, complete and infuriating. Riven gritted his teeth.

"Stop inventorying me."

"I'm inventorying us." Cael's grip tightened. "Same pack. Same mistake if we walk off stupid."

"We're already stupid."

"Then we check twice." Cael's voice stayed rough. "Once with hands. Once with eyes. No trusting the bowl to keep count."

Riven swallowed. "Mara would charge extra for the lecture."

"She'd be right."

Cael's grip tightened. "I'm in." The words came out rough, certain. "Whatever this is. Whatever it costs."

Riven heard his own breath catch—not Cael's, his. Or both. He couldn't tell.

The answer arrived immediately, not a voice, not a sound, recognition.

Yes.

Heat climbed through Riven's jaw. Shame hit first, then gratitude, unwanted, uninvited, not entirely his.

"Don't." The word barely made it out.

Cael didn't look away. "Say it."

Riven laughed once, short and hopeless. "Say what?"

"The truth."

Riven wanted to lie, wanted to say he'd been dragged here, wanted to say this was Cael's fault, wanted to say he'd been trying to leave since they stepped into the bowl. His mouth wouldn't do it. Not here. Not now.

He thought about turning around one more time. Gate. Board. Handcart. List called twice. Small failures he understood.

"I'm not leaving you up here alone." The admission hurt, more than the fear, more than the weight in his chest. "I'm not giving you that story."

Cael swallowed. Something moved across his face, gone before Riven could name it.

"That's not a yes."

"It's not a no."

"Riven."

The presence leaned closer. His heartbeat stumbled. For a second he couldn't tell whether the second rhythm belonged to him or Cael. Maybe it didn't matter anymore.

Riven's mouth tasted like copper and old rain. He wanted to spit. Couldn't.

The words came out raw. "I'm in." He closed his eyes, opened them again. "Fuck." A breath. Then: "I'm in."

For a heartbeat nothing changed. Then the stone beneath them hummed, low and deep, more felt than heard. The presence pressed closer—not approval, not welcome. Acknowledgment. Like Mara marking a name on the board: *present*.

Riven had the sudden terrible feeling that there was nowhere left to hide, not a thought, not a memory, not a fear. Everything felt exposed. Seen.

He tried one last time to step back. His heel lifted half an inch. Stopped. The warmth held him the way a crowd holds you when the crush starts—not hands, just mass.

"Cael." His voice came out rough. "If we die here, Mara charges somebody."

"She charges everybody." Cael's mouth twitched. Not quite a smile. "Write that on the board."

Riven laughed once. Broken. Real.

The warning arrived cold and absolute, not words, meaning:

*What happens to one of you starts happening to both.*

His eyes burned. Hot tears gathered before he could stop them. No wind. No excuse.

Riven wiped his face with his free hand. The tears kept coming anyway—or maybe they were Cael's. He couldn't tell.

Beside him, Cael squeezed his hand once, hard. "I know."

Riven looked over. Cael's eyes were wet too. That frightened him more than anything else, because for the first time he couldn't tell whether the grief in his chest belonged to him or to both of them.

The bowl fell silent. No wind, no birds, no scrape of stone, just breath, just heartbeats, just the feeling of something listening.

Riven tightened his grip on Cael's sleeve until his knuckles hurt.

Whatever came next had already heard their answer.

The bowl did not release them. It held—attention, warmth, the second rhythm in his chest—while the grey sky failed above the rim. Riven counted breaths again because counting was something bodies did without asking permission. At twenty his count tangled with Cael's. At twenty-one he stopped.

Cael's thumb moved once against Riven's wrist. Apology or anchor, impossible to tell.

"We should—" Riven started.

"Yeah," Cael said. "I know."

Neither moved yet. Moving meant the rest of it—the packs, the trail down, the world where mistakes had names like *short count* and *wrong crate* instead of *Yes*.

Riven swallowed copper. "Mara's going to kill us."

"Maybe."

"For leaving without paying ahead."

"Definitely." Cael's mouth twitched. "Add it to the list."

Riven laughed once despite everything. The sound stayed small. The bowl swallowed the edges of it but not the center.

They moved toward the rim without deciding to—pressure easing a fraction with each ring outward, warmth thinning, breath returning in uneven pieces. Riven's boot found the trail edge. Cael's found it a heartbeat later. Same stone. Wrong timing.

The packs sat where they'd left them. Riven's left strap still twisted wrong. Cael's water skin still lighter than it should've been. Ordinary mistakes waiting like they'd never left.

Neither reached yet. Reaching felt like signing something twice.

Riven looked back once at the bowl's empty center. "Still nothing."

"Still there," Cael said.

"Mara's going to kill us for the bread count alone."

"Add it to the board." Cael's mouth twitched. "Under *gone north*."

Riven almost laughed. The sound stayed small. The ridge wind took the edges but not the center.

That, at least, still felt like his—until Cael felt it too, and neither of them could tell where the laugh stopped.
