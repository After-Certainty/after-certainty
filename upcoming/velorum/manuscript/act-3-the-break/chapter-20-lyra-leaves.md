# Chapter 20 - Lyra Leaves

Two days after the square, the infirmary annex had more cots and fewer answers.

Riven came in at dusk with split knuckles from hauling debris and a headache that had started in Cael's shoulders at noon and never left. The bond had been loud since the demonstration, not sharp spikes anymore, a constant low static of mood and fatigue crossing without permission.

Lyra was at the prep table washing blood off her forearms. She looked up once and pointed at the stool.

"Sit before you fall on someone," she said.

He sat. She wrapped his knuckles without asking what happened. Her hands were steady. Her face was not angry. That was worse.

"You came to say goodbye," Riven said.

"I came to finish a bandage," Lyra said. "But yes."

The word landed in his chest and in Cael's at the same time. Across the courtyard Cael flinched over a supply slate. Riven felt it and hated that even this moment had a witness inside his own skin.

"Don't," Riven said.

"Don't what."

"Don't make it clean."

Lyra tied off the cloth. "It isn't clean."

He stared at the high windows. Gray light. Dust in the bands. Outside, someone shouted for buckets.

"I wanted—" He stopped. Started again. "I thought if we survived the square, there'd be room for something that was mine."

Lyra met his eyes. "There isn't."

"I know." His throat hurt. "I just needed you to hear it."

"I heard it before you said it," she said. "So did he."

Riven closed his eyes. "I can't sneeze without him feeling it."

"That's the bond," Lyra said. "That's what I told you before you walked out there."

"You told me the truth. I didn't want it."

"I know." She set the bowl aside. "I didn't want it either."

That stopped him. "What?"

"Not the bond. The shape of what we could have been if the world weren't chewing you both in public." She folded a clean cloth once, precise, like control still mattered in small things. "I don't get to be a private door for you. Neither does he. If I stay, I become part of the load. Another person the bond uses to hurt you both."

"That's not fair to you."

"It's accurate," Lyra said.

Riven stood. The stool scraped. "So you leave because we're broken."

"I leave because staying would be a lie," she said. "I'd be standing next to you while you bleed in two directions and calling it love. I won't."

He wanted to argue. He wanted to grab her wrist and prove he could hold one feeling in one body if he tried hard enough. The bond answered with Cael's spike of jealousy and shame before Riven's hand moved.

Lyra saw it. "There."

"I'm not—"

"You are." Not cruel. Tired. "Every time you reach for me, he feels it. Every time he clamps down, you feel it. I won't be the reason you split again."

Riven's hands hung at his sides. "What am I supposed to do with that?"

"Live," she said. "Stop calling it refinement. Stop waiting for Halverin to invent a prettier word." She paused. "And don't chase me to make the bond quieter. I can't quiet it."

He laughed once, broken. "You think I'd chase you for that?"

"I think you're desperate for one room that isn't shared," she said.

He had no answer that wasn't true.

She picked up a small bundle from the bench, rolled cloth and two needles in leather. "South ward's short-handed. I'm going with the supply train to Greyhaven's outer clinics. Merrow signed it."

"Greyhaven," Riven said.

"Far enough," Lyra said.

The bond tightened. Cael was moving toward the annex door. Riven felt his steps before he heard them.

"Don't," Riven said to the air. Not to Cael. To whatever part of him still believed he could control the room.

Cael came through the doorway anyway. He stopped when he saw Lyra's bundle.

"You're leaving," Cael said.

"Yes," Lyra said.

Cael looked at Riven. Riven looked back. The bond carried everything neither of them would say in front of her.

"I'm not asking you to stay," Cael said to Lyra.

"I know."

"If you need escort to the gate—"

"I have Merrow's guards," Lyra said. "Use your hands on the wall."

She shouldered the bundle. At the door she stopped and looked at Riven one last time.

"I'm not rejecting you," she said. "I'm refusing a shape that can't exist."

"Yeah," Riven said. "I get it."

He did. That was the worst part.

She left. The annex door shut. Footsteps faded down the corridor and out into the yard.

Riven stood in the empty room and listened to the bond carry Cael's grief and his own until they were the same sound.

Cael did not speak for a long time. Then: "I'm sorry."

"Don't," Riven said. "Don't make it about your sorry."

"What do you want."

Riven looked at the stool Lyra had pushed toward him. At the bowl with pink water. At the bandage scraps on the table.

"I want one feeling that doesn't travel," he said.

Cael's face tightened. "I can't give you that."

"I know."

They stood on opposite sides of the room like strangers who shared one chest. Outside the city kept moving. Inside, something had ended without a fight and without a reconciliation, the way real endings often did.

Riven picked up his coat. "I'm going to the wall."

"Riven—"

"Don't follow me to be noble. I can't talk right now without you hearing every word before I say it."

He left. The bond followed anyway, thinner but inescapable, Cael's hurt and his own braided down the stairwell and out into cold air.

At the top of the east wall he stood alone and watched the supply train lanterns move toward the gate. Lyra's bundle was a dark shape on the second wagon. She did not look back.

Riven did not wave.

He understood why she had to go. Understanding did not make it less final.

Below, Kestrel Gate's lights came on one by one. The square where they had broken in public stayed empty. Broadsides were gone from the wall. Chalk marks faded in the traffic.

Riven pressed his forehead to cold stone and breathed until the bond slowed enough to let him think one thought alone, or close to alone: *This is loss.*

Not refinement. Not alignment. Loss.

He stayed on the wall until the lanterns vanished beyond the outer bend. When he climbed down, Cael was waiting at the stair foot with two cups of water and no speech.

Riven took one cup. They drank. They walked back to the annex.

No one fixed anything. The quiet between them was not peace. It was the space after the last private door closed, and both of them knew it.
