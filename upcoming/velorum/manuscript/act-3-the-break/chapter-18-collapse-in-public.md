# Chapter 18 - Collapse in Public

The square did not break all at once. It broke in layers.

Riven had Cael's grip on the mock frame and Halverin's counts in his ears when someone on the north rope shouted that an awning was coming down. Real or rumor, it did not matter once bodies moved. Sun had bleached the broadside ink on three walls. *STEADINESS RETURNS.* Lyra had stayed at the south tent with her sleeves rolled and her face closed. Riven had felt that in his chest before the bond even tightened.

The morning had started with chalk and counts and the clean lie that discipline could be watched. Cael had stood on the east mark with his jaw set and his hands loose because Halverin said clenched fists read as fear. Riven had stood three paces left and tried to feel nothing the crowd could use. For six steps it had worked. For one hold it had worked. Then Halverin had called margin increase and the guards had stamped harder and the square had leaned in like a single body waiting to be lied to.

Riven remembered the third lift, the frame catching wrong, Cael's splinters and his own. He remembered Halverin saying *You're not two men. You're one pair* as if saying it could make it true. He remembered Lyra's arms folded at the south tent and the sentence she had given him in the annex: *invasive dependence.* He had walked out here anyway because Cael had asked and because the city had asked louder.

Then the square got loud and the bond got honest.

Fear hit the bond faster than thought. Halverin was still calling counts from the platform. The numbers did not match what Riven's body was doing.

"Hold the line!" Halverin shouted.

Riven tried. Cael tried beside him. For three breaths they moved like the road again, bracing the mock timber while guards stamped and people pressed the rope. Load shared clean. His name in the margin where everyone could read it. A boy in the front row had shouted Cael's name like a song on the third lift. Riven had missed half a count and felt Cael's irritation cross before he could swallow his own.

The guards' drill had looked stupid from the platform. It did not look stupid when boots stamped close enough to shake the chalk. Shields rose and fell in pattern. Halverin called breath. *In on my voice. Out on my voice. You do not carry alone.* Riven had breathed on the wrong beat twice and felt Cael adjust for him both times, shame crossing each time like a slap.

Then a woman in the front row went down and someone screamed that she could not breathe.

Heat and noise and sweat and paste. A man in a baker's apron climbed the rope line. A child cried because the drum had stopped. The fountain basin threw back sun hard enough to hurt the eyes. Merrow's medics moved at the west lane. Lyra's tent stayed shut until it did not.

It crossed into Riven through the bond before he could sort it. Cael's fear. His own. The crowd's on top of both. His hands on the timber slicked with someone else's sweat. The frame bit through gloves he did not remember putting on. Splinters found the same spots on Cael's palms three feet away.

"Step!" Halverin called.

Riven stepped late. Cael's shame hit him hard. His shame hit Cael. The frame jerked. Sound over the fountain basin arrived a beat behind mouths moving. A guard shouted an order Riven heard twice, once from the man's lips and once from inside his skull.

Riven saw Halverin's hand rise with the horn. He saw the magistrate's mouth form words that did not land in the noise. Same coat as the stairs briefing, buttons polished, hair combed for ink. He looked ready for a city that would behave.

"Follow my instruction," Halverin said, louder. "You're resisting again. Stop."

"We're not resisting," Riven said. His voice came out raw. "We're fucking drowning."

Cael grabbed the frame edge. "On me. Now."

They moved together, not on Halverin's count, on the rhythm they had earned in Ashfen and on berms and at river stairs where failure had already taught them the price. Riven felt it catch for half a second. Weight distributed. Breath aligned. The bond went quiet the way it had when they carried a wounded man off the berm without dropping him.

The crowd leaned in. Riven saw faces he knew from the lower market and faces he did not. The fishmonger who had lost his brother at Kestrel Gate. A cooper's apprentice who had watched them climb Heartbridge hill as children. A magistrate clerk with paste on his cuffs. All of them waiting for the ground to hold because a broadside said it would.

Then pain came back wrong.

Not shared load. Ricochet. Cael's old guilt from the stairs, Riven's anger at the broadsides, Lyra's name unspoken in both their throats, Halverin's voice insisting discipline could cure invasion. It bounced between them too fast to brace. Each feeling picked up the last and threw it harder.

Riven saw the fishmonger from the lower market grab the rope as if he could steady the air. He saw the magistrate clerk drop his slate. He saw the boy who had shouted Cael's name cover his mouth with both hands. The crowd was not one feeling. It was hundreds, and the bond tried to drink all of it at once.

His knees buckled. Cael's did not, which was worse. Cael stayed upright through sheer stubbornness and pulled Riven down by the shared load until they were both on the stone with the frame between them like a broken brace.

Riven's vision grayed. The square tilted. Stone looked wet when it was dry. Shadows lagged behind bodies. He tasted copper. Cael made a sound like a man hit in the gut.

"Stabilize!" Halverin shouted. "I said stabilize!"

Riven laughed once because there was nowhere else for the sound to go. "You don't know how, you arrogant bastard."

Halverin's face changed. The performance cracked. He stepped down one stair, hands out, still giving orders even though his voice had gone small.

"Do as I tell you," he said. "Trust the path."

"There is no path," Riven said. "There's a leak."

The bond answered with force. Pain spiked in Riven's palms and in Cael's at the same time. Riven heard himself make a sound he did not recognize. Cael staggered. The mock frame tipped and hit stone hard enough to splinter. A shard skittered across the basin lip and dropped into the water.

The crowd pulled back in a wave. Someone knocked over a rope post. A child wailed. Merrow shouted for space at the west lane. Lyra's tent flap flew open and she was moving before Riven could find her face in the blur. She had told them what would happen if they performed obedience in public. She had been right, and people would hate her for it.

The air over the square went wrong.

Sound lagged. Dust hung without settling. A guard's shield rang and the ring arrived late. Riven saw a pigeon freeze mid-wing above the east roofline and then lurch forward as if time had hiccuped. The fountain water in the basin shivered without wind. Broadside paper flapped on the magistrate wall and the ink seemed to run though the sun was dry.

Riven went to one knee. Cael went down beside him, not mirroring, pulled. They grabbed each other by the forearms because it was the only thing left that was not Halverin's voice.

"Stop fighting me," Cael gasped.

"I'm not fighting you," Riven said. "I'm fighting the goddamn room."

"Then let it move."

They let it move.

Not obedience. Not performance. The load went where it wanted when they stopped pretending they could each keep a private share. Riven felt Cael's pain and his own and the square's fear braid into one pressure. His skin buzzed. His ears rang. A woman in the second row crossed herself. A clerk dropped his slate and did not pick it up. The boy who had shouted Cael's name started crying and could not stop.

For a moment Riven understood what Halverin had wanted and why it could never work in public. The bond was not a display. It was a leak. When the room pressed hard enough, everything private became visible whether you performed or not. Cael's guilt. Riven's want. The crowd's hunger for proof. Halverin's need to be right. It all ran through the same channel.

Riven stopped trying to sort it. He let the load move and felt the square's fear thin at the edges as bodies stopped pressing the rope. The fountain water settled. Sound caught up with mouths. A guard lowered his shield without being told.

The cost did not stop. His palms burned. Cael's breath hitched in Riven's ribs. The bond stayed loud, but the ricochet eased when they stopped fighting each other for privacy that no longer existed.

Halverin kept talking. "Stand. Follow. You are almost—"

"Shut the hell up," someone in the crowd shouted.

Another voice joined. Then another. Not cheers. Not jeers. A low furious noise of people who had been sold steadiness and watched it turn into visible harm. Riven had seen riots in Ashfen. This was different. People were angry because they had believed the poster.

Halverin raised the horn again. His hand shook. "This is a correction. Stay calm."

Riven wanted to stand. His legs would not. Cael's wouldn't either. They stayed on the stone with the splintered frame between them and the crowd's noise pressing from every side.

A merchant Riven did not know shouted, "You said it would hold!"

Halverin turned toward the voice. For a second he looked like a man who believed his own poster. Then the second passed and he looked like what he was: someone who had sold a city a story about steering invasion.

"Step back," Halverin told the guards. "Clear the center."

The guards did not move fast enough. Merrow did.

Merrow cut through the rope line with two guards and a medic. She did not look at Halverin. She looked at the ground where Riven's knees had scraped stone.

"Back. Give them air."

Riven could not tell if she meant the crowd or him and Cael. Both, probably. The medic knelt on Riven's other side and checked his pulse with two fingers, professional, no drama. A guard tried to lift the splintered frame and Merrow said, "Leave it. Evidence."

Halverin said, "This is not—" and Merrow cut him off without raising her voice.

"You're done in my square today."

Halverin's mouth opened. Nothing useful came out.

Lyra reached them first. She did not touch Halverin. She knelt at Riven's side and checked his pupils with two fingers.

"Look at me," she said.

"I'm looking."

"Good. Breathe with me. In. Out."

He tried. The bond still pulled, but slower now. He could feel Cael's heartbeat in his own wrist. He could feel the square's fear thinning at the edges as people stopped pressing forward.

Cael's breath matched on the fourth count. Not Halverin's count. Theirs.

A man in the front row said, loud enough to carry, "They said it would hold."

No one answered him. The answer was on the ground in splinters and on their faces.

The square went quiet in stages. First the center. Then the side streets. People stood with their mouths open. Broadsides flapped on the wall with their names still centered in bold. A paste boy stood with his bucket, not sure whether to run. Two guards looked at Merrow and then away, as if eye contact might assign them blame.

Halverin stood on the platform with the horn lowered. His face was pale. His coat was still neat. Nothing about him looked magistrate now. He looked like a man who had bet a city on a story and lost in public.

"I can adjust the sequence," he said, voice smaller. "We can run it again."

No one answered.

Riven got one foot under him. Cael helped without speaking. They stood together in the splintered frame and the wrong air. Riven's palms stung. Cael's jaw was tight. Through the bond Riven felt shame and anger and something underneath that felt like relief so ugly he did not want to name it.

He looked up at Halverin. No triumph. Just the sick wash of everybody seeing the same thing.

"This isn't refinement," Riven said, loud enough for the front rows. "This is loss."

Whispers started. Someone repeated it. *Loss.* A woman near the rope line said it again, softer, like she was testing whether the word would burn her tongue.

An old man in a cooper's apron spat on the step below the platform. Not at Halverin. At the ground, like the stone had failed him personally. Two women behind the north rope argued in whispers about whether the pair were cursed or heroes. A guard told them to shut up and did not sound sure which side he was on.

Halverin opened his mouth. Closed it. His aides were gone from the platform edge. The speaking horn lay on the step where he had set it down, mouth cracked from when he had dropped it without noticing.

Riven turned away from the platform. Cael turned with him. The bond was still tight, still painful, but Halverin's counts were not in it anymore.

Behind them the square held its breath.

Merrow's voice carried, flat and practical. "Clear the basin. Medics to west lane. Someone fetch slate for injuries." Orders that assumed the demonstration was over. Orders that did not include Halverin's sequence.

A clerk tried to speak to Halverin. Halverin waved him off without looking. He stood alone on the platform with the horn at his feet and the crowd's silence pressing in from every side.

Then, slowly, people began to move. Not cheering. Not fleeing. A mother pulled her child back from the rope. Two men lifted the woman who had gone down and carried her toward the medic tent. The paste boy ran. A guard who had stood at the rope all morning turned his back to the platform and helped a medic lift a cot.

Riven walked toward the west lane where Lyra had pointed without speaking. His legs shook. Cael matched pace without touching him. Moving together because there was no other way through the square, not because a magistrate had counted them into place.

Merrow met them at the lane edge without ceremony. She looked at Riven's palms, at Cael's color, at the bond between them the way she looked at a cracked wall brace.

"Can you walk?" she asked.

"Yes," Riven said.

"Then walk. South tent. Not the magistrate's office." Her eyes flicked to the platform. "He doesn't get you tonight."

Halverin was still up there. Riven heard his voice once more, thin against the quiet. Sequence. Correction. No one in the lane answered.

Riven looked back once. Halverin had not descended. He stood in the wreckage of his own authority as if standing might bring it back.

It would not.

Lyra was at the tent flap when they arrived. She did not say *I told you*. She checked Riven's pupils the way she had in the square and handed Cael a cup of water he drank without tasting.

"Sit," she said. "Both of you."

They sat on the bench inside the tent. Canvas filtered daylight to flat gold. Outside, square noise came muffled through cloth. The city sounded quieter, as if someone had turned the volume down. A boy peered in and Merrow waved him off. Riven's hands shook when he tried to set the cup down. Cael's shook too. Through the bond they felt each other's tremor and could not stop amplifying it until Lyra pressed two fingers to Riven's wrist and said, "Breathe. Count. Not his count. Yours."

They breathed. Four in. Four out. The bond eased a fraction.

Lyra washed Riven's palms with clean water and wrapped them without comment. Cael's hands next. Her touch was professional and steady. Riven felt Cael's shame spike when she worked and his own answer it before he could stop either.

"How bad?" Riven asked.

"Bad enough to see," Lyra said. "Not bad enough to die. That's the category you're in now."

He almost laughed. "Comforting."

"It's accurate," she said.

"How many?" Lyra asked Merrow through the flap.

"Three down hard. One maybe worse. No dead." Merrow's voice was flat. "Halverin's still on the platform."

"I know," Lyra said.

She did not look at Riven when she said it. She looked at the bandage knot.

"The square saw it," Riven said.

"I know that too."

Cael stared at the cup in his hands. "We tried his counts."

"You tried," Lyra said. "That's not the same thing as it working."

"He'll run it again," Cael said.

"Not today," Merrow said through the flap. "Maybe not this week. The square won't have it."

Silence inside the tent. Outside, someone shouted for a medic. A cart wheel squeaked. Normal city noise with a hole in the middle where steadiness used to sit.

Riven looked at his bandaged palms. "They heard me."

"They heard you," Lyra said. "They also saw you on your knees. Both things will travel."

"Which one wins?"

She met his eyes. "Neither. They'll carry both until something else happens."

The bond still hurt. The city still watched. Halverin had lost the square in front of everyone, and no broadside was bold enough to paste over that.

When they left the south tent an hour later, the square had thinned to workers and stares. Cael walked beside him without touching. Riven could not stop hearing the word *loss* in his head.

Merrow passed them at the lane edge with two guards and a slate. She did not stop. "Injury count goes to my office tonight. Halverin's clerk can read it tomorrow if he still has an office." She looked at Riven's bandages. "South annex if the palms swell. Not the magistrate's infirmary."

"We're not going to Halverin," Riven said.

"Good," Merrow said, and kept walking.

Someone in the second row was still saying it softly. *Loss.* Not steadiness. Not refinement. A woman near the basin picked up a splinter and dropped it again like it might bite.

Workers were stacking splintered timber by the basin. A clerk wrote names on a slate Merrow had ordered. Two guards argued quietly about whether the ropes should stay up overnight. The magistrate wall still wore its banners. *STEADINESS RETURNS* in ink that had looked brave at dawn and looked like a lie at dusk.

A paste crew worked at the north wall, not replacing the broadside, scraping it. Riven watched paste come off in gray curls. The woodcut pair underneath was smeared. Names still visible. His and Cael's in bold where everyone could read them.

Cael saw him looking. "Leave it," Cael said.

"Can't," Riven said. "It's already in my chest."

They walked past the platform without climbing the steps. Halverin was gone from the top. His horn still lay on the stair where he had dropped it, mouth cracked. An aide stood at the margin talking to no one in particular, sequence, correction, tomorrow. No one answered him either.

Riven passed the spot where the frame had fallen. Wet stone where there had been no water. Chalk marks scuffed to smears. He bent once and picked up a splinter, then dropped it because Merrow had said evidence and because his palms screamed when he closed his fist.

Cael did not ask why he stopped. The bond carried the reason anyway.

At the annex gate a guard let them through without ceremony. No salute. No jeer. Just a nod like they were men who worked debris now, not symbols on a wall.

Riven did not look back at Halverin again. He had the bond still tight in his wrists, the splinters on the ground, and the plain sight of what everyone had seen.

That would have to be enough for now.
