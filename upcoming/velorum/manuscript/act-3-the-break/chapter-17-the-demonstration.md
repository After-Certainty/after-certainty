# Chapter 17 - The Demonstration

First bell rang over a square packed shoulder to shoulder.

Cael stood on the east staging mark with his boots set on chalk and his hands loose at his sides because Halverin had told him clenched fists read as fear. The crowd filled every rope line and spilled into the side streets. Faces turned up. Banners hung from the magistrate wall with the woodcut pair and their names underneath.

Halverin raised the speaking horn. His voice carried clean across stone.

"Today is not punishment," he said. "Today is proof that what began in Ashfen can be held in public. That shared burden is not chaos when it is directed."

Riven stood three paces to Cael's left, jaw tight, eyes on the guards at the margin. Through the bond Cael felt Riven's dread like a second heartbeat.

"Signal one," Halverin called.

They stepped forward on the count. The bond caught and settled. For six steps it felt like the road again: one rhythm, one load.

"Hold," Halverin said.

They held. The crowd went quiet enough that Cael heard a baby fussing near the north rope.

"Signal two. Pressure."

Guards at the margins moved in a slow drill, shields up, boots stamping a pattern that was supposed to simulate stress without real blades. It looked stupid from the platform. It still worked on the crowd. People leaned in.

Halverin counted breath. "In on my voice. Out on my voice. You do not carry alone."

Cael breathed. Riven breathed half a beat behind, then caught up. The lag scraped through the bond.

"Again," Halverin said. "Tighter."

They ran the sequence: step, brace, turn, hold. Cael focused on the chalk marks and the counts and the feel of Riven's weight moving when it should. Merrow stood at the west lane with two medics and an open wagon. Lyra was not in the front row. Cael saw her anyway, at the south tent flap, arms folded.

Halverin walked down two steps from the platform. "Now we add load."

Two laborers rolled a timber frame into the center space, a mock breach brace like the one at the west berm. Cael and Riven had lifted real timber under smoke. This was cleaner and worse because everyone was watching.

"Lift on three," Halverin said.

They lifted. The bond took the strain and spread it. Cael's shoulders burned. Riven's breath hitched. The crowd made a sound like approval.

"Good," Halverin said. "Set. Again."

They set. They lifted again. On the third lift a boy in the front row shouted Cael's name like it was a song. The shout spiked Riven's attention. Riven missed half a count.

The frame dipped. The bond jerked. Pain lanced across Cael's palms and landed in Riven's at the same time.

"Focus," Halverin said, louder. "You are not two men. You are one system."

Riven's anger hit the bond hot. Cael's shame answered it. The frame dipped again.

"Again," Halverin shouted. "Discipline."

They tried. Cael could feel the rhythm underneath the noise, the same rhythm that had worked on the road when nobody was selling hope from a platform. It was still there. It was thin.

Halverin climbed back up. "Citizens, you will see correction in real time. Watch."

He signaled the guards to increase the margin drill. Boots stamped harder. A horn blew once, off rhythm. Someone in the crowd pushed against the rope and a guard shoved back.

The square tightened.

"Step," Halverin called.

Cael stepped. Riven stepped late. The bond pulled wrong. Cael's knee buckled. The crowd gasped.

"Hold the line," Halverin said. "Do not improvise."

Riven looked at him. Cael saw the refusal in Riven's face before he heard it in words.

"We're improvising every second," Riven said, too low for the crowd, loud enough for Cael.

"Not now," Cael said.

"Now is all there is."

Halverin raised the horn again. "Again. From the top. You will not leave this square until you hold."

Cael felt something in the bond change, not break yet, but strain like timber before snap. The air over the fountain basin looked wrong, a faint lag between motion and shadow.

He looked at the chalk marks. He looked at Merrow's open wagon. He looked at Halverin's face and saw the first crack in the magistrate's certainty, quickly plastered over.

"One more sequence," Cael said to Riven. "Match me."

"I've been matching you," Riven said.

"Then match harder."

They turned toward the center mark. The crowd pressed in. The horn blew. Halverin called the count.

Cael stepped on one. Riven stepped on one. For one breath the square felt like it might hold.

Then someone in the north rope screamed about a collapsing awning two streets over, real or rumor, and the crowd surged. Guards shouted. A child went down in the press.

Fear hit the square like weather.

It hit Cael and Riven at once through the bond, too fast to sort whose was whose. Halverin kept calling counts into noise that no longer obeyed counts.

"Hold!" he shouted. "Hold the line!"

Cael tried. Riven tried. The bond did not settle. It amplified.

The air over the fountain basin thickened. Sound lagged. Cael saw his own hand move and felt Riven's panic a beat later, then earlier, then both at once.

Halverin lowered the horn. For the first time in Cael's hearing, his voice shook.

"Follow my instruction," he said. "Do not—"

Cael stopped listening. He looked at Riven. Riven looked back. Not Halverin's rhythm. Theirs, hard-earned on roads and berms and river stairs where failure had already taught them the cost.

"On me," Cael said.

"On you," Riven said.

They moved together, not for the crowd, not for the magistrate, for the load that was trying to tear through both of them at once. The square was still screaming. The air was still wrong. Halverin was still talking.

Cael knew, with a cold clarity that felt like falling, that the next beat would not be proof.

It would be exposure.
