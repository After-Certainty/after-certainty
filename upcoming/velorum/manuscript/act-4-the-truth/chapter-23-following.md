# Chapter 23 - Following

By the fourth day out of Kestrel Gate, Cael had stopped counting miles. He counted what held.

The Seeker did not speak.

Rock. Staff. Riven still behind him. That was the list now. Everything else had become negotiable.

The trail narrowed between tilted slabs of stone. Wind pushed through them and the rock groaned like timber under load. Riven walked a few paces behind, testing each slab with his staff before committing his weight. Cael had stopped telling him to hurry. That was new too.

"You going to test every rock between here and wherever we're going?"

"If they need testing."

"Christ."

Cael stepped onto the next slab. It held. That was enough.

The night before had been the worst yet. No fire. Hard bread soaked with dew. Riven had woken twice with Cael's nightmare still in his chest: the square, the count, Halverin's voice calling numbers into a crowd that wouldn't stop moving. The second time Cael had woken with Riven's memory of the canal stairs, cold water, somebody else's hands on the rail. Neither of them spoke about it at breakfast. There was no breakfast. They ate while walking.

At a bend in the trail Riven stopped and vomited nothing into the grass. Cael waited, jaw tight, feeling the dry heave in his own throat. When Riven straightened, he said, "Walking anyway." Cael nodded. The rule held.

Once, midmorning, Riven had stopped without warning and pressed both palms against a boulder beside the trail. Cael felt the stone's cold before he saw Riven touch it. The bond carried texture now, grit under skin, vibration in the rock that shouldn't have been there. Riven stood there for ten breaths. Then he walked again. Cael didn't ask. Asking would have been a sentence.

Through the bond, Cael felt Riven stop calculating the walk back. Not all at once. Just a door closing somewhere behind them.

The first week after the square he would have measured everything twice. After Halverin's office, maybe three times. Now he found himself watching the Seeker instead, not because he trusted them, because he was tired. The distinction mattered.

The Seeker's boots left prints that sometimes pointed the wrong direction. Cael had noticed on the second day and stopped mentioning it. Arguing with the ground had stopped being useful somewhere south of the slip.

Midday they passed a milestone someone had tried to reset. The chiseled numbers overlapped: *12* and *12* again, the second cut deeper, as though the carver had hoped repetition would make the count true. Riven spat beside it. "Even the fucking miles lie."

"Keep walking."

"I am walking."

The trail forked around a split boulder. The Seeker tapped the left path with the staff and kept walking. No explanation. No warning. Just a direction.

Cael hesitated. Old habits wanted evidence, a reason, a list. The Seeker disappeared around the bend. Cael followed. Behind him, Riven muttered, "Fucking hell," and came too.

The left path dropped through a slot barely wide enough for shoulders. Wind squeezed through after them, cold and metallic. Halfway down, Cael's staff tip rang against stone and the sound returned from three directions at once. Riven swore. The swear echoed late.

The left path held. Rock. Staff. Riven still behind him. Cael felt his shoulders loosen, only a little, enough to notice.

The country grew stranger the farther north they walked. Streams hesitated. Wind arrived wrong. Shadows sometimes forgot where they belonged. Cael stopped keeping track.

By noon they crossed a narrow shelf above a scree slope. Riven was complaining about something, probably him. The bond carried the shape of the complaint without the words. Cael almost smiled. That surprised both of them.

Below the shelf, scree moved in slow ripples, as though something underground were breathing. A stone dislodged itself and rolled uphill three feet before stopping. Riven watched it. "Don't."

"I'm not doing anything."

"You are."

Riven kicked a stone off the trail. The stone took its time deciding to fall. Neither watched it.

By evening they stopped in a windbreak between two ridges. No fire. No conversation from the Seeker. Just bread, water, and the sound of cooling stone. Cael cut the ration loaf in half. Riven took his share. Across the bond, the familiar ache settled into place, not comfortable, not painful, familiar.

The windbreak was a crack between two tilted slabs, barely deep enough to hide from the wind. Stone still radiated the day's cold. Cael sat with his back against one wall. Riven sat against the other. The Seeker sat in the open, hood up, staff across their knees, facing the dark.

Cael watched Riven eat. Riven watched the Seeker not eat. The bond carried hunger from both directions until food lost taste.

Riven set his bread down. "Can't."

"Same."

They ate anyway. Wrong-tasting calories were still calories. Cael counted the bites Riven took without meaning to. Ledger habit. Some habits didn't die. They just changed what they counted.

The water skin tasted wrong again. Cael drank anyway. Riven made a face and drank too. Out here wrong was still water.

"Why us?" Cael asked.

The Seeker stared into the dark. No answer.

Riven rolled onto his back. "Still asking for sentences."

Cael looked out toward the hills. Maybe he was still asking for sentences. He didn't like any of the ones he had.

"You ever sleep through the night anymore?" Riven asked.

"No."

"Me neither." The bond carried the admission before the words finished leaving his mouth.

For a while neither of them spoke. The wind shifted. Somewhere beyond the ridges, the Seeker's staff tapped stone. Once. Then again.

Riven sat up. "You ever notice the staff taps late?"

"Yes."

"Christ." Riven lay back down. "Even their fucking staff is broken."

Cael didn't argue. The observation was accurate. Out here accuracy was its own kind of comfort.

Once, deep in the night, Riven heard the Seeker's staff tap somewhere beyond the ridge. One strike. Then nothing. He waited for the second tap. It arrived while he was still waiting for the first. Time doing whatever it wanted.

On the fifth day the trail dropped into a valley of bent trees. The trunks leaned against winds that had already passed. Riven stared up at them.

"Fractured."

A branch moved overhead. The leaves answered a moment later.

"Christ. Now explain why my teeth feel wrong."

Cael's teeth felt wrong too, a faint vibration, not pain, just enough to keep him aware of them. He kept walking.

They passed a dead cart overturned beside the trail. No horse. No driver. Cargo spilled: cracked pottery, a coil of rope grey with weather, a child's shoe. Nobody had come back for any of it. Cael didn't stop. Stopping invited questions. Questions invited answers he didn't have.

Riven slowed anyway. Cael felt it through the bond before Riven's boots changed rhythm. "Don't."

"Not touching it."

"Good."

"Just looking." Riven kept walking. "Someone left in a hurry."

"Yes."

"Or something made them leave."

Cael adjusted his grip on the staff. "Keep walking."

They passed a line of stones someone had set along the trail edge, then abandoned halfway. Cael counted eleven. Riven counted twelve. They didn't argue. Out here even counts lied.

The valley floor changed every few yards. Soft ground became hard. Hard ground gave way without warning. Twice his staff punched through crust into cold mud. The third time he stepped wide before testing it. His ankle rolled on hidden stone. Pain shot up his leg. Ahead, Riven stumbled and grabbed a tree.

Riven's hand sank into bark that felt wet though the trunk looked dry. He pulled back fast. Neither spoke about the bark. Some wrongness didn't need commentary.

"Son of a bitch."

"You all right?" Cael asked.

"No." Riven straightened. "Walking anyway."

The answer made Cael smile despite himself. The smile didn't last.

They crossed a stream that forgot which way downhill was. Water slid uphill for a few paces before correcting itself. Cael's stomach lurched. The bond sent the sensation back from Riven a heartbeat later. Neither of them commented.

Cael tested the far bank with his staff before stepping. The staff sank to the ferrule. He tested again six inches left. Solid. He marked the spot with a stone and stepped. Riven followed the stone marker without comment. Timber thinking on broken ground. It was all he had left.

The Seeker waited on the far bank, still, patient. By now Cael had stopped wondering whether the silence was intentional. The silence was the answer.

Half a mile later Riven caught a toe on nothing and went down hard. Pain flashed through Cael's knee before he saw the fall. Mud streaked Riven's trousers when he climbed back to his feet.

Cael reached for him. Riven waved him off. "Don't. I'm—" He stopped. They both knew what he wasn't.

"You all right?"

Riven spat blood into the grass. "No." A beat. "Walking anyway."

Cael nodded. That seemed to be the rule now.

They rested at a seep that ran sideways for six paces before finding downhill. Cael filled both skins. Riven checked the map while Cael checked his knee. The swelling had started. By evening it would be both their knees. The bond didn't schedule injuries politely.

Riven folded the map with hands that shook. "How much farther to that thing?"

"Far enough that arguing won't help."

"Christ." Riven stuffed the map away. "Fine."

Cael almost smiled. Almost. The smile died when Riven's pain crossed the bond again, sharp, honest, shared.

The rain started sometime after midday, no clouds, just cold mist settling into cloth and hair and skin. Water beaded on leaves and refused to fall. Cael stopped looking at the leaves.

His coat was soaked through by the second hour. The iron ration bar in his pack had started to rust at one corner. He'd deal with it at camp. Or not. Out here some problems waited.

"Halverin was right about one thing," Riven said.

That got his attention. "Which thing?"

"We'll die out here if we start treating this like a demonstration."

Cael adjusted his grip on the staff. "We're not demonstrating."

"Maybe." Riven kicked at the trail. "Still feels like some bastard's counting."

Rain beaded on his sleeve and rolled upward once before falling. Cael watched it and said nothing. Watching was enough.

That stayed with Cael for the rest of the climb.

He had spent years believing load could be measured, assigned, carried. Out here load moved without ledgers. The bond was the ledger now. Every step added a line neither of them had written.

Once Cael's foot broke through crust into void. Riven caught balance on nothing useful. Both laughed once, sharp and wrong, and kept climbing.

The valley eventually gave way to open stone. The bent trees fell behind them. The ground behaved a little better, not enough, just enough to notice. By the time they reached the ridge, both of their legs were burning. The Seeker never slowed. Neither did they.

The climb to the crest took an hour Cael couldn't account for. Time had started slipping: a pause that felt like minutes and turned out to be seconds, a stretch of trail that felt short and went on until his thighs shook. Riven felt it too. Through the bond, impatience and exhaustion traded places without either of them speaking.

Cael's staff had developed a wobble near the grip. He shifted his hold every hundred paces. Riven's blister had reopened despite the cloth wrap. Blood showed through at the heel of his boot. Neither mentioned it. Mentioning it wouldn't change the walking.

At the crest the country opened beneath them: broken hills, a distant storm, a pale track winding north, and beyond it, on the highest rise either of them could see, something built from stone. Not natural. Not close. Real.

Riven stood beside him. Neither spoke for a moment. Wind tore at their coats. Cael's staff swayed. He planted it harder.

Then: "That it?"

Cael shaded his eyes. "Could be."

"Christ, I fucking hope so." Riven stared at the distant shape. "Still looks built."

Below them a hawk circled the ring twice. Its cry arrived after it had already landed on stone. Neither watched it land.

The descent off the crest took until midafternoon. Twice the trail vanished and reappeared ten paces left. Cael stopped testing and started trusting the Seeker's prints instead. Trust by exhaustion. It was all he had.

The Seeker had already started down the far side. Cael felt something settle in his chest, not certainty, direction. Beside him, Riven went quiet. Through the bond, Cael felt the same conclusion arriving from the other side, flat, heavy, inescapable.

"We're not going back."

"No."

Even if the answer was bad. Even if Velorum offered nothing. The road behind them had already failed.

They descended the far side in silence. The stone shape ahead grew slowly: not a tower, not a fort. A ring. Standing stones on a rise, pale against the storm line. Cael had seen drawings in Halverin's archive sheets. Drawings lied. This looked smaller and heavier than any drawing.

The Seeker's staff tapped stone somewhere below. Once. Then again. North was enough. For now.

The sixth day was worse. The storm on the horizon didn't move closer or farther. It simply stayed, a grey wall eating the sky. Rain fell upward from the valley floor for an hour. Cael stopped trying to keep his coat dry. Wet cloth chafed. The bond carried the chafing in both shoulders.

Lightning split the grey without sound. Then sound arrived all at once, late, wrong, loud enough to ring teeth. Riven cursed. Cael cursed with him. The Seeker didn't react.

Hail followed, stones the size of knuckles, falling sideways. Riven turned his shoulder. Cael turned his. Bruises formed in both bodies before either saw the marks.

Riven walked ahead for a while. Not leading. Just needing space the bond wouldn't grant. Cael felt the need and gave him three paces anyway. It wasn't privacy. It was courtesy. Out here courtesy counted.

They reached the ring's foothills by dusk. Stone underfoot again. Solid enough to trust for minutes at a time. The ring itself sat above them, pale, patient. Cael counted standing stones from below. Lost count when the light failed. Riven didn't ask the number. Numbers had stopped helping.

They slept in the lee of a boulder. No fire. The Seeker sat facing the ring. They sat facing the Seeker. Sleep came in pieces. Dreams crossed. The ring appeared in both of them before they saw it in the morning.

Before light, Cael woke to Riven muttering numbers. Not coordinates. Counts. Steps. The bond carried the rhythm until Cael shook him once and the muttering stopped.

When grey light came, the ring hadn't moved. It didn't need to. They were moving toward it.

Cael's staff split at the ferrule before breakfast. He bound it with line. The wobble became a sway. Riven watched him test each step like timber load. "It'll hold."

"For now."

"Rule still holds."

At breakfast Riven split the last iron bar with his knife. The edge had dulled on stone. Cael took the smaller half without comment. Hunger crossed the bond plain enough.

The Seeker stood before they finished eating. Cael swallowed the last bite standing. Riven did too. Chasing had become the only meal that sat.

They walked toward the ring's shadow. The shadow arrived before the stones. Riven stopped once and checked the sun. It hadn't moved. Or had moved twice. He kept walking.

By midmorning the fractured ground steadied enough to trust for whole minutes. Cael marked each minute in his head like a ledger entry. Trust: temporary. Staff: failing. Riven: still behind him. Enough.

At a seep that ran downhill correctly for once, they refilled one skin. Riven drank first. Cael second. The bond carried the order and neither fought it. Fighting cost calories.

The ring grew without moving. Cael felt direction settle again, bruised and real. Not hope. A place to walk toward. Riven's stride evened behind him. Neither looked back.

North was still enough.

They ate the last good bread standing up. No fire. No rest. The ring's shadow reached for them before the sun did.

Halfway down the shadowed trail, Riven's boot slipped on dust that hadn't fallen yet. Cael caught balance on his staff. Neither fell. Falling was for tomorrow.

The trail into the ring's shadow took the rest of the morning. Riven tested each stone. Cael watched the Seeker's prints. Neither spoke. Speaking cost breath they needed for climbing.

Wind rose from the Ashring below and carried dust upslope. Riven tasted stone on his tongue. Cael tasted it too. The ring's hum found his teeth before his ears.

At the cut's edge they stopped. Below, the Ashring waited. Above, cloud broke once and showed the standing stones like teeth. Cael felt Riven's pulse through the bond. Fast. Steady. Still walking.

Riven spat dust. "Tomorrow."

"Yeah." Cael shifted his pack. The staff swayed. He ignored it. "Tomorrow."

The Seeker's staff tapped once below the cut. Late. Always late. Cael watched the print fill with dust that fell upward for three breaths, then stopped. Wrong water ran somewhere under the stone. He heard it in Riven's pulse before his ears.

Neither turned back. Turning back would have required a direction they no longer had.
