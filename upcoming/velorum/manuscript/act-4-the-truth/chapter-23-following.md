# Chapter 23 - Following

By the fourth day out of Kestrel Gate, Cael had stopped counting miles. He counted what held.

The Seeker did not speak.

The trail narrowed between tilted slabs of stone. Wind pushed through them and the rock groaned like timber under load. Riven walked a few paces behind.

"You going to test every rock between here and wherever we're going?"

"If they need testing."

"Christ."

Cael stepped onto the next slab. It held. That was enough.

The first week after the square he would have measured everything twice. After Halverin's office, maybe three times. Now he found himself watching the Seeker instead, not because he trusted them, because he was tired. The distinction mattered.

The trail forked around a split boulder. The Seeker tapped the left path with the staff and kept walking. No explanation. No warning. Just a direction.

Cael hesitated. Old habits wanted evidence, a reason, a list. The Seeker disappeared around the bend. Cael followed. Behind him, Riven muttered, "Fucking hell," and came too.

The left path held. Rock. Staff. Riven still behind him. Cael felt his shoulders loosen, only a little, enough to notice.

The country grew stranger the farther north they walked. Streams hesitated. Wind arrived wrong. Shadows sometimes forgot where they belonged. Cael stopped keeping track.

By noon they crossed a narrow shelf above a scree slope. Riven was complaining about something, probably him. The bond carried the shape of the complaint without the words. Cael almost smiled. That surprised both of them.

"Don't."

"I'm not doing anything."

"You are."

Riven kicked a stone off the trail. The stone took its time deciding to fall. Neither watched it.

By evening they stopped in a windbreak between two ridges. No fire. No conversation from the Seeker. Just bread, water, and the sound of cooling stone. Cael cut the ration loaf in half. Riven took his share. Across the bond, the familiar ache settled into place, not comfortable, not painful, familiar.

"Why us?" Cael asked.

The Seeker stared into the dark. No answer.

Riven rolled onto his back. "You're still asking for sentences."

Cael looked out toward the hills. Maybe he was still asking for sentences. He didn't like any of the ones he had.

"You ever sleep through the night anymore?" Riven asked.

"No."

"Me neither." The bond carried the admission before the words finished leaving his mouth.

For a while neither of them spoke. The wind shifted. Somewhere beyond the ridges, the Seeker's staff tapped stone. Once. Then again.

On the fifth day the trail dropped into a valley of bent trees. The trunks leaned against winds that had already passed. Riven stared up at them.

"Fractured."

A branch moved overhead. The leaves answered a moment later.

"Christ. Now explain why my teeth feel wrong."

Cael's teeth felt wrong too, a faint vibration, not pain, just enough to keep him aware of them. He kept walking.

The valley floor changed every few yards. Soft ground became hard. Hard ground gave way without warning. Twice his staff punched through crust into cold mud. The third time he stepped wide before testing it. His ankle rolled on hidden stone. Pain shot up his leg. Ahead, Riven stumbled and grabbed a tree.

"Son of a bitch."

"You all right?" Cael asked.

"No." Riven straightened. "Walking anyway."

The answer made Cael smile despite himself. The smile didn't last.

They crossed a stream that forgot which way downhill was. Water slid uphill for a few paces before correcting itself. Cael's stomach lurched. The bond sent the sensation back from Riven a heartbeat later. Neither of them commented.

The Seeker waited on the far bank, still, patient. By now Cael had stopped wondering whether the silence was intentional. The silence was the answer.

Half a mile later Riven caught a toe on nothing and went down hard. Pain flashed through Cael's knee before he saw the fall. Mud streaked Riven's trousers when he climbed back to his feet.

"You all right?"

Riven spat blood into the grass. "No." A beat. "Walking anyway."

Cael nodded. That seemed to be the rule now.

The rain started sometime after midday, no clouds, just cold mist settling into cloth and hair and skin. Water beaded on leaves and refused to fall. Cael stopped looking at the leaves.

"Halverin was right about one thing," Riven said.

That got his attention. "Which thing?"

"We'll die out here if we start treating this like a demonstration."

Cael adjusted his grip on the staff. "We're not demonstrating."

"Maybe." Riven kicked at the trail. "Still feels like some bastard's counting."

That stayed with Cael for the rest of the climb.

The valley eventually gave way to open stone. The bent trees fell behind them. The ground behaved a little better, not enough, just enough to notice. By the time they reached the ridge, both of their legs were burning. The Seeker never slowed. Neither did they.

At the crest the country opened beneath them: broken hills, a distant storm, a pale track winding north, and beyond it, on the highest rise either of them could see, something built from stone. Not natural. Not close. Real.

Riven stood beside him. Neither spoke for a moment. Then: "That it?"

Cael shaded his eyes. "Could be."

"Christ, I fucking hope so." Riven stared at the distant shape. "Still looks built."

The Seeker had already started down the far side. Cael felt something settle in his chest, not certainty, direction. Beside him, Riven went quiet. Through the bond, Cael felt the same conclusion arriving from the other side, flat, heavy, inescapable.

"We're not going back."

"No."

Even if the answer was bad. Even if Velorum offered nothing. The road behind them had already failed.

The Seeker's staff tapped stone somewhere below. Once. Then again.

North was enough. For now.
