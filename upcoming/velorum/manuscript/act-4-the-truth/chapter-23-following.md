# Chapter 23 - Following

Cael counted miles the way he counted joists: by pressure, by give, by what held.

The Seeker did not speak. The land did.

On the fourth day out of Kestrel Gate the trail narrowed between tilted stone slabs that groaned when wind hit them wrong. Gravel slid under Cael's left boot and did not fall until he had already taken the next step. His reflection in a rain pool lagged. Through the bond Riven's disorientation hit him as nausea.

"Keep your eyes on the path," Cael said.

"I'm looking at the path," Riven said. "The path isn't looking back right."

"Then don't look at pools."

"Helpful."

They followed anyway.

Cael needed the motion. Standing in the square after the collapse had left him with no frame that fit his hands. Halverin's last meeting had closed an office thread, not the hole inside him. Merrow's gate pass was paper. The bond was not.

Following gave him direction again, even if the guide was silence in a hood.

The Seeker climbed without hurry. At each fork the staff pointed once. No debate. No council vote. Cael found his shoulders loosening in spite of himself. Work he could do: walk, breathe, match Riven when the ground demanded it.

Riven walked like a man being towed by inevitability.

Cael felt it through the bond, not words, a flat acceptance under the anger. *This is what happens if you keep going.* Not prophecy. Observation.

At noon they crossed a shelf where the air tasted thin and metal rang wrong when Cael tapped his knife on stone. The Seeker paused long enough to let them drink. Riven knelt at a seep and cupped water with hands that shook.

"How much farther?" Riven asked the Seeker's back.

No answer.

"You're enjoying this," Riven said to Cael.

"I'm surviving it," Cael said.

"Same face."

Cael almost smiled. The bond carried Riven's surprise at the almost-smile and softened something between them for half a mile.

By dusk they camped in a cut where wind could not reach. Cael split ration bread. Riven took half without thanks. The Seeker sat against rock and watched the dark come in.

Cael studied the figure. No insignia. No prayer beads. Not priest. Not soldier. Not magistrate. Someone who walked toward gods the way carpenters walked toward a sagging roof: because someone had to.

"Why us?" Cael asked the fireless dark.

The Seeker did not answer.

Riven lay back on his bedroll. "Stop asking for sentences. You heard Lyra."

"I heard her," Cael said. "I still want one."

"Want harder," Riven said, tired. "The land's already answering."

Cael listened. Wind in the cut. Stone cooling. His own pulse. Riven's pulse a beat off, then syncing when neither forced it.

On the fifth day the trail dropped into a valley where trees grew at wrong angles, trunks bent as if pushed by invisible hands. Leaves rustled a second after branches moved. Riven swore quietly, constantly, like punctuation.

"Fractured," Cael said.

"Named it," Riven said. "Great."

They crossed a stream that ran uphill for three paces before settling. Cael's stomach lurched. The bond amplified and sent the lurch back doubled. He stopped, braced on his staff, breathed until the ground decided to be ground again.

The Seeker waited without turning.

Cael understood then what following meant. Not faith. Not Halverin's discipline. Endurance. Put one foot where the guide put a foot until your body learned the only language left.

Riven caught up. Mud on his knees. "You all right?"

"No," Cael said. "Walking anyway."

"Good."

They walked.

At a ridge crest the country opened: broken lines of hills, distant smear of storm, a pale track winding toward something the map did not name. Cael felt purpose settle in his chest, thin and hard.

Riven stood beside him. Through the bond Cael felt inevitability like weather pressure.

"You see it too," Cael said.

"Yeah," Riven said. "We're not going back."

"No."

"Even if Velorum says no cure."

"Especially then," Cael said. "Lies already failed."

The Seeker started down the far slope without waiting. Cael followed. Riven followed. The bond pulled tight, not splitting, not performing, just present, a line between them the land seemed to test with every mile.

Cael did not call it hope. He called it direction. For now that was enough to keep moving.
