# Chapter 25 - Losing the Line

Riven tried to keep the fear to himself.

It lasted until noon.

Before noon it had been a morning of small wrongness: breath misting before mouths opened, Cael's staff tapping stone and the tap arriving after the impact, Riven's shadow pointing the wrong way until he stopped looking. The bond carried all of it without comment. Comment would have been screaming.

They had left camp before light, the Seeker setting pace as always. No breakfast. Hard bread eaten while climbing. Riven's ribs still ached from yesterday's basin. Cael's shoulder had stiffened overnight. The bond carried both aches in both bodies until neither could tell which wound belonged to whom.

Riven had stopped marking days on Merrow's map. The ink wouldn't hold anyway. The paper had softened at the creases and torn at one corner where sweat and rain had worked it thin.

They were crossing the knife ridge Merrow's clerk had drawn with a shaking hand: wind from both sides, drop swallowed by cloud, stone barely wide enough for a cart axle. Looking down was a mistake. Riven kept his eyes on the Seeker's back instead.

The Seeker walked ahead, staff across their shoulders, hood up, steady pace, never looking back.

Twice already that morning Riven had felt Cael's concentration before a step landed. Twice he had adjusted without meaning to. The bond was doing the managing for both of them.

Riven's boot slipped on loose gravel.

Before he caught himself, Cael had already felt it. The bond snapped tight.

"Slow down," Cael said.

"I'm fine."

"No, you're not." The answer came too quickly. Too certain.

Riven stopped. Wind tugged at his coat. "Don't fucking manage me."

Cael's jaw tightened. "I'm trying not to watch you fall."

"Then stop feeling my knees for me."

"I can't."

The bond proved it. Cael felt Riven's fear as clearly as his own: the drop, the loose gravel, the wrong lag between foot and ground. Riven felt Cael's helplessness right back. Cael couldn't stop managing. Neither could Riven.

Below the cloud, something that might have been a bird or might have been wind crossed the gap. Riven didn't look. Looking was a mistake.

Riven dropped to one knee and pressed a hand against the stone. The ridge spun for a moment.

Cael grabbed his arm. "Riven."

"Let go."

"You'll slide."

"Then pull me. Don't crawl inside my goddamn head."

The words landed harder than he intended. Cael went still. For a moment neither of them spoke. The Seeker waited ahead, staff planted, silent as ever.

Riven got back to his feet. Blood streaked one palm where the stone had cut him.

He looked at the cut. Looked at Cael's palms. Clean. The bond had already shared the sting. Sharing didn't make it fair.

"We need a line."

"We have one."

"Not that line."

Riven unbuckled his belt. Leather cracked from years of use. He wrapped one end around his wrist and held out the other.

"Something real."

Cael stared at it. Then took it.

The leather sat awkwardly between them. Too short. Too tight. Perfect.

Cael's palm was still bleeding from the morning cut. Riven felt it when he took the belt. Felt Cael feel him feel it. The loop again. The leather was still real.

Riven had worn the belt every day since Greyhaven. Same buckle. Same creases. It had held up trousers and tools and once, years ago, a knife he didn't carry anymore. Now he needed something between his wrist and Cael's grip that wasn't skin.

For the first time all morning, Riven felt something stay in his own chest before the bond took it. Not long. Just long enough to notice.

The belt buckle bit into his wrist when he shifted his pack. Good. Pain he could locate. Pain that stayed on his side of the line.

They kept walking. The ridge narrowed. The storm moved closer. Cloud swallowed the valley below. Wind pushed hard enough to lean into. Riven leaned. Cael leaned with him a heartbeat later, bond catching the balance before either could stop it.

"Fuck," Riven muttered.

"Yeah."

Lightning flickered in the cloud below. No thunder. Or thunder arrived late. Riven stopped trying to pair them.

The ridge narrowed to a spine of stone. Riven kept his eyes on the Seeker's hood. Looking down was a mistake. Looking at Cael was worse. The bond showed him Cael's fear anyway.

"Still hate me for Lyra?" Cael asked.

Riven barked out a laugh. "Really? Christ."

"You never answered." The bond already knew the answer. That didn't make saying it easier.

"Sometimes."

Cael nodded. "Same."

"That's not comforting."

"No." A beat. "It's true."

Riven looked at the belt between their wrists. Leather worn smooth where the buckle had rubbed for years. "Lyra wouldn't have wanted this."

"No."

"Doesn't mean I stop missing her."

"No." Cael's voice roughened. "Doesn't mean I stop being sorry I made it worse."

The belt pulled with each step. Riven counted the pulls. One every four paces. Then every three. The ridge was narrowing faster than the map had suggested. Merrow's clerk had drawn this section with a shaking hand. Riven understood why now.

Wind grabbed at the leather between their wrists. Once it lifted the belt enough to snap skin. Riven hissed. Cael felt it before the sound left Riven's mouth. Neither unlinked.

Once the belt snagged on a thorn bush growing from a crack in the stone. Cael freed it without being asked. Their wrists stayed connected. The gesture felt almost normal. That frightened Riven more than the ridge.

They walked another mile before the ridge ended. The slope beyond looked solid. It wasn't. Riven knew it the moment his foot touched it.

The gravel looked packed. It wasn't. His heel broke crust. The layer beneath slid. His arms windmilled. The belt yanked Cael forward. Cael's boot found a stone that rolled. Balance gone. Both of them gone.

They went down together.

Stone slammed into Riven's hip. His elbow. His ribs. Cael hit shoulder first. The impact crossed the bond before either of them could scream. Then both of them did. One sound. Two mouths. The same panic in two bodies.

Riven's pack strap broke. The pack slid ahead of him, pulling him sideways into more scree. Cael grabbed for the belt with his free hand and caught only air. The leather held.

Riven couldn't tell whose lungs were burning. Couldn't tell whose fear was whose. The slope tilted. The world narrowed. The bond broke open.

River stairs. The square. Lyra turning away. Halverin counting. Merrow's map. The slip. The basin. The wooden support groaning free. Everything at once. Everything shared.

Riven tasted blood again. His or Cael's. The bond wouldn't say.

"Fuck." The word belonged to both of them.

They slammed against a boulder and stopped. For several seconds neither moved. The belt still connected their wrists. The leather felt absurd. Wonderful. Real. Something outside the bond. Something they could touch.

"No." Riven's voice shook. He grabbed the belt harder. "No. Fuck. No."

Cael was breathing just as hard. "Breathe."

"I'm trying."

"Together." The words came out ragged. "In."

Riven inhaled. The bond echoed it. "Out."

The panic stayed. The fear stayed. The memories stayed.

They breathed anyway.

Riven heard Cael counting under his breath. One. Two. Three. Not Halverin's count. Breath count. Riven joined on four. Five. The bond echoed the numbers until they were just numbers.

The scree slope below them was still moving. Small stones shifted and resettled. Riven watched one roll past his boot and felt Cael's urge to grab it before it fell. The bond again. Always the bond.

The Seeker stood above them, staff planted in the scree. Wind tugged at the hood. No hurry. No concern either.

Riven's vision cleared enough to see Cael's face. Mud. Blood at the lip. Eyes too wide. His own face probably looked the same.

"Help?" Riven croaked.

The Seeker held out a hand. Dry grip. Strong grip. Cael first. Then Riven.

Riven's wrist burned where the belt had bitten. Cael's burned too. The Seeker's grip didn't care about burns. It cared about upward.

The belt pulled tight between their wrists as he climbed. The leather bit into his skin before the Seeker hauled him the rest of the way up.

Below the cut a repair cart passed on the lower track going south—timber, rope, three men, empty niches on the rack where wall pins should have sat. The driver read their wrists and didn't rein in. "Gate took our metal for demonstration staging," he called. "If your ring falls, don't file it here." The wheels vanished into dust before Riven could answer. Cael wrote *pins gone* on the map margin with charcoal that smeared at once.

No lecture. No explanation.

The Seeker turned and started down the slope.

Riven stared after them. "Helpful."

No answer.

"Shocking."

The Seeker kept walking.

They worked their way down the rest of the scree on hands, knees, and stubbornness. Twice the belt snagged on loose stone. Once Cael freed it with his teeth. Neither of them had enough breath left to complain.

At the bottom Riven sat on a flat rock and couldn't stand for a full minute. Not injury. Refusal. His body had decided the ridge was still falling. Cael stood guard without being asked, staff out, eyes on the slope above. When Riven finally rose, he kept the belt in his fist instead of rebuckling it. Cael didn't comment. Comment would have made the choice smaller than it was.

Riven found his pack twenty paces downslope, strap hanging. Cael's water skin had burst against a stone. The tin cup in Riven's side pocket had crushed flat. Cael picked it up, tried to round it with his thumb, gave up, and dropped it into the scree.

They shared what was left in Riven's skin. One swallow each. The bond made even thirst arithmetic.

Riven's palms were raw by the time they reached solid ground. Cael's knee had swollen enough to show through torn cloth. The Seeker waited at the bottom as though they'd arrived on schedule.

Riven's ribs felt loose inside him. Cael's shoulder wasn't much better.

Riven spat grit. "Thanks for the fucking help."

The Seeker kept walking.

Cael laughed once, a short, painful sound. "You're getting predictable."

"Good."

Half a mile later Riven's pack caught on a root of stone. Cael freed it without being asked. Their shoulders brushed. Neither pulled away. The bond had gone quiet—not peace. Exhaustion deep enough to pass for it.

They walked the rest of the afternoon with the belt back on Riven's hip but not on their wrists. The groove marks on both wrists stayed pink. Riven caught Cael looking at his once. Cael looked away first.

Riven unbuckled the belt and rubbed his wrist. A red groove circled the skin. The same mark ringed Cael's.

"Keep it."

Cael handed the belt back.

Riven looked at him. "It didn't work."

"It helped."

Riven wanted to argue. Didn't. The belt disappeared back onto his hip.

For a while they walked without speaking. The bond hadn't eased. It had changed. The panic spikes were gone. Something slower had taken their place. Not relief. Not acceptance. Just a current they could feel all the time.

Cael's split lip had crusted black. Riven's palm cut had done the same. They looked like they'd been filed under *damaged goods.*

Riven's pack rode wrong without the broken strap. Cael offered to carry half. Riven refused. Refusing was the last privacy he had.

Riven found himself knowing when Cael needed water before Cael reached for the skin. Cael flinched when a crow cried before Riven heard it. The belt on his wrist still felt like his when he tightened it.

At camp they ate in silence. No fire. No stories. The Seeker sat against a rock and watched the dark gather across the cut.

A south-bound peddler crossed their cut without stopping, eyes on their wrists. He left a tin of salt at the trail edge and kept walking. Riven wanted to throw it after him. Cael pocketed it anyway. Salt still counted when iron ration tasted like blood.

Riven rethreaded his pack strap with line from Merrow's supply. His fingers shook. Cael felt the shake and looked away for three breaths before the bond pulled it back anyway.

Riven tore off another piece of bread. Halfway through chewing, somebody else's hunger arrived. He glared across camp.

"Eat your own damn ration."

"I did."

Cael closed his eyes. "I'm trying."

That took most of the fight out of him.

Riven checked his ribs with careful fingers. Bruised, not broken. Probably. Cael's shoulder was worse. The bond carried the assessment both ways until neither trusted their own diagnosis.

Riven leaned back against the stone. For a while he watched the dark. Then he made one last attempt.

*I'm afraid of what we'll become.*

The thought barely made it halfway. The bond carried the rest.

Across the camp, Cael met his eyes.

"Me too."

Riven picked up a stone and turned it in his fingers. Ordinary stone. Ordinary weight. He set it down carefully, as though the ground might notice.

Nothing else followed. Nothing needed to.

The silence sat between them. Heavy. Honest.

Tomorrow they would reach the ring. Tomorrow somebody might finally answer a question. Riven wasn't sure he wanted to hear it.

"Tomorrow we hear it," Cael said.

Riven rubbed a thumb across the belt hanging at his hip. The leather still smelled like sweat and dust. And fear.

"Tomorrow we stop pretending there's a door."

Cael nodded once. "Yeah."

They slept badly.

Dreams crossed all night: the ridge, the fall, the square, the ring. Riven woke with gravel under his nails though he'd slept on stone. Cael woke with bruised ribs though his pack had taken the hit. The bond had stopped sorting who paid for what.

Once Riven woke to Cael counting breath again. One. Two. Three. Riven joined without meaning to. They stopped at ten.

His ribs protested when he sat up. Cael's shoulder screamed when he rolled his pack. They ate the last heel of bread standing. No fire. No rest.

In the morning the Seeker pointed toward the stone ring and started walking faster. The air thickened as they climbed. Even sound felt reluctant. Riven heard gravel crunch beneath his boots a heartbeat after he stepped.

The ring grew with each hour. Not quickly. Relentlessly. Pale stone against grey sky. No banners. No pilgrims. No chalk arrows. Just the ring and the slope leading up to it.

Cael walked beside him now. Not behind. Not ahead. Beside. Close enough to reach. Far enough to breathe.

Halfway up the final climb, Riven stopped to retie his boot. His fingers fumbled the lace. Cael's fingers fumbled too, though Cael wasn't wearing boots that needed tying. The bond had stopped respecting tasks.

"You all right?" Cael asked.

"No." Riven finished the knot. "Walking anyway."

"Rule still holds."

"Christ. Yeah."

"You still want out?" Cael asked.

"Yes." The answer came immediately. "I know what the fuck we'll hear."

"Me too."

Neither said anything after that.

The last mile to the ring's outer slope was the steepest. Riven's ribs protested on every inhale. Cael's shoulder had gone stiff enough that he climbed one-handed, staff across his back. The bond carried the hitch in both strides.

The ring sat ahead of them, pale against the sky. For the first time since Kestrel Gate, it looked close enough to touch.

Below the outer slope, old offerings lay in the dust: clay bowls, snapped sticks, a ribbon so faded it might have been white once. No pilgrims. No clerks. Just objects left by people who had needed the ring to mean something other than weight. Riven stepped around them. Cael did too. Touching other people's hope felt like theft.

They didn't touch the ring that day. The Seeker circled the outer rim without entering and stopped at a cut in the stone where wind dropped. Camp. No fire. No food left but iron bars and a heel of bread Riven split with his knife.

Cael tried to write a note on the back of Merrow's ruined map—coordinates, water, date—and the pencil snapped. He used charcoal from the cold fire pit instead. The marks smeared at the first wind gust. He kept the scrap anyway. If they died out here, somebody might find it and file them under something other than *unresolved.*

Cael's shoulder had gone stiff enough that he couldn't raise his arm above the shoulder. Riven felt the limitation when he tried to shrug his pack higher. The bond translated injury without permission.

"You should rest," Riven said.

"So should you."

"Christ." Riven ate his half of the bread. "Tomorrow then."

"Tomorrow."

The word sat between them. Real. Temporary. Not enough.

Riven lay awake and listened to the ring. Not sound. Pressure. A hum behind his teeth that might have been the land or the bond or both. Cael felt it too. Neither spoke. Speaking would have made it louder.

Once Riven reached for Cael's wrist in the dark. Stopped himself. Reached again. Cael's fingers found his instead. Not the belt. Skin. They let go after a moment. The bond stayed. The choice to let go stayed too.

When light came, the Seeker stood facing the Ashring. Waiting. The ring waited back.

Riven stood beside Cael at the cut's edge. The stone circle below waited. He could still feel the groove on his wrist where the belt had bitten. Cael flexed his shoulder once and stopped when pain crossed the bond.

"Last chance to turn around," Riven said.

"No," Cael said.

"Didn't think so." Riven adjusted the belt on his hip. "Let's get this over with."

Riven's boot wire cut his toe at the lip. He bled into his sock. Cael felt the wetness before he saw it. Neither stopped to rewrap. Stopping invited the ring to pull harder.

They descended into the Ashring behind the Seeker. Pressure found them before the center. Riven's ears popped. Cael's popped too. Neither had altitude to blame.

The eighth day ended where the Ashring began. Riven felt it in his teeth before he saw stone. Cael felt it too.

They paused at the inner rim long enough for Riven to drink. The water tasted of iron and old rain. Cael drank after him. The bond carried the order without argument.

The standing stones ringed the center. Riven counted them once. Cael didn't. Counting had stopped helping somewhere on the knife ridge.

Wind dropped in the Ashring like a lid closing. Riven's breath sounded too loud. Cael heard it twice—once in his chest, once through the bond. The ring waited. So did they.

Riven touched the nearest standing stone once, not for prayer, for temperature. Cold went up his arm and into Cael's shoulder before he pulled back.

"Yeah." Cael flexed his fingers until feeling returned. "Tomorrow."

"Or we do."

Cael didn't answer.
