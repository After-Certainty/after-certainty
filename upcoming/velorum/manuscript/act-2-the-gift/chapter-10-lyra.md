# Chapter 10 - Lyra

The infirmary sat against the inner wall near Kestrel Gate's east barracks, a long stone room with shutters open for air and cots packed shoulder to shoulder. Late afternoon light came through the shutters in narrow bands, crossing the floor between buckets and stool legs. Two oil lamps burned at the far prep table even in daylight, turning steel tools warm gold against the gray stone. It smelled like boiled cloth, pine resin, blood, and old fear.

Riven came in with a split forearm from patrol and a headache like metal behind his eyes. Cael said it could wait; Merrow saw the sleeve and sent him.

"Sit," the woman at the nearest table said without looking up.

Her hair caught the shutter light and flashed cold. Younger than he expected, maybe his age, with dark brows and a scar across one knuckle. Her voice was flat, no patience for nonsense.

"That your best bedside manner?" Riven asked, sitting.

"It's the one that keeps people alive." She tied the bandage, sent the soldier off, then turned to him. Gray eyes. Steady. "Name."

"Riven."

"Lyra." She took his wrist and turned his arm so the cut opened under lamplight. "Hold still."
He tried to make a joke and couldn't find one in time.

He felt the first pull when she cleaned the wound. Not lightning. More like a door opening onto cold air. The sting sharpened, then dropped behind a warmth he had not asked for.

Then the bond carried it to Cael before Riven could name it.

Cael was across the courtyard in logistics. Riven felt dry paper, lantern heat, irritation at a ledger. His reaction to Lyra dropped into that room: surprise, interest, then Cael's defensive clamp.

Lyra glanced up from his arm. "You just flinched before I touched you."

"Not from the cut," Riven said.

"From what, then?"

He almost lied and heard Cael catch the almost.

"Complicated," Riven said.

"Good," Lyra said. "I hate simple cases."

She stitched where she had to, pressed where it bled, spoke only when needed. A child cried three cots down. Someone cursed in the next room. Outside, wagons rattled and the south tower bell rang twice.

A boy with a split ear sat on the next cot trying not to cry while an older woman held his shoulders. Lyra crossed to them without ceremony, checked the boy's pupils, and told him exactly what she was about to do before she did it. Her voice never rose. The boy still flinched. She steadied his head with one hand and worked fast with the other.

Riven watched her hands more than her face. No wasted motion. No soft performance. Just competence in a room full of pain.

When a runner with blood on his boot came through, Lyra pointed to benches, ordered water, and had a canvas screen up before the next stretcher crossed the threshold.

Riven obeyed with everyone else.

"You. Hold this." She shoved a folded cloth into his hand and pressed it over a gash in a cooper's side. "If your hand slips, he bleeds out before I finish the thread."

Riven kept pressure while the cooper bit down on leather hard enough to tear it. Through the bond he felt Cael's attention jerk from his ledger to the spike of panic in Riven's chest, then flatten when Riven held the line.

By the time Lyra tied off the last stitch, blood had dried to rust on Riven's fingers.

"You're one of Halverin's new miracles," Lyra said at last, tying off the final stitch.

Riven snorted. "Is that what he's calling us?"

"Not to your face. He has better words when he's performing."

Riven laughed before he could stop it. The laugh went through the bond; Cael sent back a warning that sounded like jealousy.

Lyra saw him go quiet. "There it is again."

"What?"

"You disappear for half a second like someone yanked a line." She set the needle aside. "Does he hear everything."

Riven looked at her, then at his bandaged arm. There was no point pretending she had not already read most of it.

"Close enough," he said.

"And you hear him."

"Yeah."

She nodded once, like she was confirming a diagnosis she had already made. "That's not two people in sync. That's a boundary injury people are dressing up."

The words hit harder than the needle. Boundary injury. Cael reacted at the same instant, sharp refusal with something darker under it.

Riven stood too fast, and the room tipped. He caught the table edge until the floor settled.

"You should sit five more minutes," Lyra said.
She nudged a stool back toward him with her heel.

"I should get back before Halverin sends someone with a leash."

She wiped her hands and met his eyes again. "If he talks about the path, ask who pays for it."

"You don't like him."

"I don't like men who rename harm so crowds clap."

Riven grinned despite himself. "You always this cheerful."

"Only on bad days."

Before he could answer, another runner shouldered through shouting for dressings. Lyra pointed; three helpers moved before he finished. Riven grabbed a crate and carried it to the far table as the canvas screen snapped half open.

"Put that down and hold pressure there," Lyra said, nodding at a teamster's shoulder.

He stayed through one more patient, palm braced hard while Lyra threaded a needle and gave orders that sounded bored until you noticed everyone obeyed. When she reset a wrist with a crack that made him swear, she said, "Good. Means you still have edges."

She finally stepped back and flexed her hands, knuckles reddened from constant grip. "You can go now."

Riven did not move right away. "How long have you been doing this?"

"Long enough to know hero stories always arrive after the bleeding stops."

He looked at the cots, at the bucket of cloudy water under one bench. "You from the Gate?"

"From nowhere that matters to Halverin." She tossed a bloodied rag into the wash bin. "Which is useful."

That line hit the bond before he answered. Cael caught every word and sent back a hard wall dressed as caution.

"Useful how," Riven asked anyway.

Lyra gave him a tired half smile. "I can still see what's in front of me."

Riven helped reset cots before he left. Lyra did not ask why. She only handed him a stack of folded blankets and pointed where they went.

At the last cot, an old mason with a crushed foot caught Riven's sleeve and squinted up at him. "You one of the paired men."

"Yeah."

"Then tell your other half to stop walking patrol on loose stone like he's invincible. He limps when he turns left."

Riven blinked. "You know Cael?"

"Built half the retaining walls on west berm with him before I got too old for ladders." The mason grunted as Lyra tightened a wrap. "Good worker. Bad at admitting pain."

Riven filed it away without wanting to. Through the bond he felt Cael catch the memory and push back against it, embarrassed and annoyed in the same breath.

By the time he walked back into barracks court, night had come on hard and torch smoke sat low under the arches.

Cael was waiting by the steps, arms crossed, expression controlled enough to be obvious.

"You were gone longer than treatment takes," Cael said.

Riven stopped two steps away. "I helped."

"With who?"

"A healer. Lyra."

He did not need to add anything. Cael already had the quick laugh, the eye contact, the way Riven had wanted one conversation untouched and could not keep it that way.

Cael's jaw tightened. "We have early patrol."

"I know."

"Then sleep."

Riven pushed past him and felt the pressure follow, not anger exactly, but possession trying to pass as concern. In his cot he replayed Lyra's line.

Across the dormitory, Cael turned once on his bedroll and went still. Through the bond, Riven felt him stay awake anyway, working through supply counts and patrol routes like numbers could fence off jealousy.

Riven rolled onto his side and stared at the aisle between cots where moonlight cut in through high slats. For a few minutes he counted breaths, trying to drown both minds in the same simple rhythm.

It worked until he thought of Lyra again.

Then Cael's reaction hit, immediate and involuntary, hurt and anger mixed with fear of what that combo would do next time steel came out.

Riven pressed his face into his forearm and waited for exhaustion to beat both of them into silence.

Somewhere near dawn, a stable hand shouted in his sleep two cots down and kicked free of his blanket. Riven jerked awake at the sound and found Cael already awake too, both of them held at the same sharp edge before it faded.

Riven lay back and counted roof slats.

"We're getting worse," he said into the dark. "Fast."

Cael was quiet for a long beat. "We're under pressure."

"That's one way to say it."

Neither slept again before first call.

When the wake bell hit, Riven sat up with grit in his eyes and Lyra's voice still clear in memory: ask who pays for it. He felt Cael hear that line again and harden around it.

Boundary injury.

For the first time since Ashfen, he wanted something Cael could not stand inside.
