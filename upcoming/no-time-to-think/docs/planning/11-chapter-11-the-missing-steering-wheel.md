## Chapter title

Chapter 11 — The Missing Steering Wheel

## Governing question

When an old control is removed, where does intervention live?

## Ordinary opening image

The empty space where a steering wheel would normally be.

## Case / terrain

In July 2026, federal regulators allowed Amazon’s Zoox to move further toward commercial operation of robotaxis designed without traditional controls such as steering wheels. The approval highlighted a genuine regulatory problem: safety rules written for human-driven vehicles do not map neatly onto vehicles without human drivers.

## Conceptual movement

Removing an old control can be rational when the system no longer uses it. But the physical absence reveals a deeper question: when an automated system encounters something it cannot handle, where does intervention live?

## Questions to preserve

- What replaces the human’s immediate ability to stop or steer?
- Does removing a control eliminate confusion, or eliminate a recovery path?
- What evidence is sufficient before deployment moves from pilot to ordinary infrastructure?
- How should responsibility be distributed among designers, operators, regulators, owners, and the system itself?

## Return image

The vehicle has more room because the wheel is gone. The empty space asks who can interrupt it now.
