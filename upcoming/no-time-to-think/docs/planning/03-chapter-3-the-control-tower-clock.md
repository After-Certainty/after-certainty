## Chapter title

Chapter 3 — The Control Tower Clock

## Governing question

When work requires sustained attention under consequence, what does efficiency actually mean?

## Ordinary opening image

A wall clock above an air-traffic-control console.

## Case / terrain

The FAA’s 2026 workforce plan described longstanding controller shortages and proposed hiring, modern scheduling tools, new staffing models, and modernization. It also acknowledged the need to reduce excessive overtime that can lead to fatigue and burnout while increasing operational efficiency.

## Conceptual movement

Some work cannot be evaluated by units completed alone. Air-traffic control requires sustained attention under consequence. Efficiency may mean reducing unnecessary coordination and improving tools. It can also mean asking a scarce human to remain actively engaged longer.

## Questions to preserve

- What is the difference between unused capacity and recovery capacity?
- When does scheduling efficiency become attentional debt?
- How should fatigue enter a throughput model?
- Can automation reduce complexity without making the remaining human role less interpretable?
