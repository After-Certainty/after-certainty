## Chapter title

Chapter 2 — The Signature

## Governing question

When documentation is generated, what new verification work appears—and who owns it?

## Ordinary opening image

A clinical note waiting for a doctor’s signature.

## Case / terrain

A July 2026 Government Accountability Office technology assessment found that AI tools for medical notes and coding may reduce administrative burden but can be difficult to verify, with uncertain effects on spending and oversight. GAO noted that U.S. clinicians average a 57-hour workweek, including seven hours of administrative work.

## Conceptual movement

Automation may give clinicians time back, which is a real good. But a generated note creates a new form of work: verifying that the summary belongs to the right person, preserves the clinically important detail, and does not quietly convert an error into the permanent record.

## Questions to preserve

- When does saved documentation time become more patient attention?
- Who reviews the machine-generated note, and under what time pressure?
- How does an organization distinguish assistance from autonomous administrative action?
- What happens when the verifier is already exhausted?

## Return image

The signature takes one second. It transfers responsibility for every sentence above it.
